# VC2HG10S06 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Justify responses and develop and evaluate strategies using environmental, economic or social criteria, recommend a strategy and explain the predicted impacts.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Strategies are designed. Criteria make comparisons fair. Predicted impacts come with uncertainty.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop, evaluate and recommend strategies** using **environmental, economic and social criteria** and explain **predicted impacts**.
- You should be able to move from analysis to recommendation.

## Core content

A **strategy** has goals, actions, resources and timelines. **Generate options** including a "do nothing" baseline before choosing. **Criteria** make trade-offs explicit — emissions, biodiversity, cost, equity, feasibility, cultural integrity, time to impact. Some criteria can be weighted.

**Build a matrix**: strategies as rows, criteria as columns, scores in cells. Note assumptions.

**Worked example: reducing heat in a Melbourne suburb.** Strategies: tree-canopy expansion; cool-roof rebates; community cooling hubs; status quo. Criteria: temperature reduction; cost; equity; biodiversity; time to impact. Canopy and cool roofs score strongly on temperature and equity; hubs strong on short-term equity; status quo low across the board.

**Recommend** a strategy (or mix) and **predict impacts** at named time horizons (5, 10, 25 years) with confidence levels. State assumptions and risks. Plan to monitor and revise — most strong plans are **adaptive**.

**Cultural context.** Strong strategies include Aboriginal-led design and free, prior and informed consent; the First Peoples' Assembly is negotiating treaty-based decision-making. Multilingual consultation strengthens evaluation criteria. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Generate multiple options.
- Use explicit criteria.
- Predict impacts with uncertainty.
- Recommend, with monitoring.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Pick the first idea." Generate alternatives first.
- "Predict impacts as certainty." State uncertainty.
- "Strategies are set and forget." Monitor and revise.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Goal vs strategy.* The goal is the destination; the strategy is the route.
- *"Do nothing" baseline.* Provides a comparison point for other strategies.
- *Matrix vs hunch.* Matrix exposes reasoning and invites review.
- *Why adaptive plans.* Conditions and evidence change.
- *Short summary of heat-strategy recommendation.* Mix canopy and cool roofs with cooling hubs for short-term equity; predict cooling at 10 and 25 years with uncertainty about tree survival under climate change.
