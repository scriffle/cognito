# VC2HG10K18 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The role and responses of international and national government and non-government organisations in improving human wellbeing at a local or regional scale.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Many actors work on wellbeing. Coordination matters as much as funding. Local action makes global goals real.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **role and responses** of **international and national** **government and non-government organisations** in improving wellbeing.
- You should be able to read the system from global to neighbourhood.

## Core content

**Layers**: international (UN agencies, multilateral banks, regional bodies); bilateral (country-to-country aid); national (federal departments); subnational (state, local); non-government (NGOs, charities, ACCOs, philanthropy).

**International actors**: WHO (health); UNICEF (children); UNDP (development); UNHCR (refugees); World Bank; Asian Development Bank; ASEAN; Pacific Islands Forum. **Australian actors**: DFAT (aid); federal and state departments; **Aboriginal Community Controlled Organisations (ACCOs)**. **NGOs**: Oxfam, Save the Children, Red Cross, World Vision, Caritas, MSF; locally VACCHO, Foodbank, Anglicare, Brotherhood of St Laurence.

**Worked example: SDG 3 in Vanuatu.** WHO supports public health; DFAT funds system strengthening; Vanuatu Ministry of Health delivers; UNICEF supports immunisation; local NGOs run outreach. Cyclones Pam (2015), Harold (2020) and Lola (2023) tested the system.

**Worked example: Closing the Gap in Victoria.** Federal and Victorian governments partner with ACCO peak bodies; the First Peoples' Assembly leads treaty work; ACCOs deliver services; philanthropy fills gaps.

**Limits**: aid dependency; coordination failures; political interference; reporting burden; funding instability. Strong design includes local leadership, long-term funding and clear accountability.

**Cultural context.** ACCOs are key delivery actors; the Coalition of Peaks represents national ACCO leadership. Multicultural service organisations — AMES Australia, Foundation House, MRCs — deliver settlement, health and education services. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Many actors; many layers.
- Coordination matters.
- Local leadership is essential.
- Global goals need local action.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Government does everything." NGOs and ACCOs are critical.
- "NGOs are neutral." They have values and constraints.
- "Aid is a one-way gift." Partnership beats charity.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why multiple layers exist.* Different scales of problems require different mandates.
- *NGO strength.* Flexibility, community trust, specialisation.
- *Why local Aboriginal leadership.* Decisions sit with those most affected; cultural safety improves outcomes.
- *Short-term funding harm.* Programs and trust collapse when funding ends.
- *Short summary of Vanuatu SDG 3.* International standards plus DFAT funding plus national delivery plus local outreach maintain health under cyclone stress.
