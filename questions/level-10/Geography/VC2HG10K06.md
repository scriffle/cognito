# VC2HG10K06 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10K06. The economic and demographic impacts of international trade in manufactured products on places, including on Australia and Asia.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Geographies of interconnection).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Manufacturing moves where conditions favour it.
  - Trade reshapes work, wages and where people live.
  - Australia and Asia are deeply linked.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Asia and Australia's engagement with Asia; Critical and Creative Thinking; Sustainability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **economic and demographic impacts** of **international trade in manufactured products** on **places**, including **Australia and Asia**.
- You are learning to read trade as a force that reshapes places.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **comparative advantage**, **global value chain** and **deindustrialisation** in your own words.
- Identify three economic impacts and three demographic impacts of manufacturing trade.
- Describe one Australian and one Asian example.
- Discuss who benefits and who bears costs.

## Core content

**1. Why manufacturing moves.** Lower labour costs; access to raw materials; supply-chain clusters; government policy and trade agreements; large domestic markets. *Quick check:* Why does a clothing brand based in Australia often manufacture in Vietnam or Bangladesh?

**2. Global value chains.** A product is designed in one country, components made in several, assembled in another, sold worldwide. An iPhone is the classic case. *Quick check:* What is one Australian input into a global value chain?

**3. Economic impacts.** Lower consumer prices; cheaper inputs for other industries; job creation in manufacturing-receiving countries; job loss in deindustrialising places; growth of services and specialised manufacturing. *Quick check:* Why did consumer electronics prices fall as production moved to Asia?

**4. Demographic impacts.** Rural-to-urban migration in receiving countries; growth of mega-cities; ageing populations in deindustrialising regions; migration corridors between trade partners; cultural exchange. *Quick check:* Why does manufacturing growth in Shenzhen lead to internal migration in China?

**5. Worked example: Australia and Asia.** Australia exports iron ore, lithium, coal, food, education and tourism services; imports manufactured goods (electronics, cars, clothing, machinery) from China, Japan, Korea, Vietnam and others. Australian manufacturing has shrunk (cars closed in 2017); specialised manufacturing (medical devices, defence, food processing) persists. *Follow the thinking:* trade specialises Australia in resources and services; Asian partners specialise in mass manufacturing.

**6. Place impacts in Victoria.** Geelong lost car manufacturing (Ford 2016) and gained logistics, services and advanced manufacturing. Dandenong has a strong manufacturing cluster with diverse migrant workforce. *Quick check:* What replaced car manufacturing in Geelong?

**7. Sustainability and ethics.** Embodied carbon in imported goods; supply-chain labour conditions (Modern Slavery Act 2018); environmental impact of resource extraction; circular-economy responses. *Quick check:* Why does the Modern Slavery Act matter for trade?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Geelong and Dandenong illustrate manufacturing transition. The Port of Melbourne handles most container trade. Victoria has Free Trade Agreements with major Asian partners.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Mining of Aboriginal lands contributes to Australia's exports. The Native Title Act 1993 and Indigenous Land Use Agreements shape consent and benefits. Aboriginal-owned businesses participate in supply chains, including through Supply Nation certification. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Victorian manufacturing has historically depended on migrant labour from Greece, Italy, the former Yugoslavia, Vietnam, China and India. These communities remain central to manufacturing suburbs like Dandenong and Sunshine.

## Memorable takeaways
- Manufacturing moves to where conditions favour it.
- Trade reshapes work, wages and migration.
- Australia and Asia are deeply linked.
- Costs and benefits are unevenly distributed.
- Sustainability and ethics now matter to trade.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Australia makes nothing anymore." Specialised manufacturing is alive in medical, defence and food sectors.
- "Trade is win-win for everyone." Workers in deindustrialising places face real costs.
- "Asia is one place." It is many economies with very different roles.
- "Cheap is harmless." Cheap goods may carry hidden labour and environmental costs.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Clothing manufactured in Vietnam or Bangladesh.* Lower labour cost and established garment clusters.
2. *Australian input into a value chain.* Iron ore for steel; lithium for batteries; wool for textiles.
3. *Why consumer electronics fell in price.* Specialised mass production in Asia cut unit costs.
4. *Shenzhen migration.* Factory work attracts internal migrants from rural provinces.
5. *Replacing car manufacturing in Geelong.* Logistics, services, advanced manufacturing and tertiary education.
6. *Modern Slavery Act importance.* Requires large companies to report on supply-chain risks, increasing accountability.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Headphones value chain.* Designed in California; chips from Taiwan; assembled in China; sold in Melbourne.
2. *Geelong manufacturing transition.* Retraining; investment in new sectors; support for displaced workers.
3. *India FTA briefing.* Economic: exports of education and resources. Demographic: migration corridor. Strategic: regional partnership.
4. *Supplier-disclosure debate.* For: accountability and worker protection. Against: commercial confidentiality and cost. Both sides accept independent audits.
5. *Vietnamese-Australian manufacturer.* Language and trust advantages; cultural protocols; transparent terms.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Product: smartphone.* Designed in California; chips from Taiwan; assembled in China; rare-earth inputs from various countries; sold across Australia. Asian impact: Shenzhen migration and wage rises. Australian demographic impact on Dandenong: maintained migrant manufacturing workforce while pivoting to specialised production. Policy response: invest in advanced manufacturing skills. Sources cited.
