# VC2HG10K10 — Geography

- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Environmental change and management).
- **Big ideas:**

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **human-induced environmental changes** and their **impacts** on the **sustainability of places** and **environmental functions**.
- You are learning to read environmental change as a geographic system.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **environmental change**, **ecosystem services** and **carrying capacity** in your own words.
- Identify three categories of human-induced environmental change.
- Describe one Victorian example and one global example.
- Discuss two responses that protect environmental functions.

## Core content

**1. Environmental functions.** Ecosystems provide **provisioning** (food, water, fibre), **regulating** (climate, flood, pollination), **cultural** (recreation, identity) and **supporting** (soil, nutrient cycles) services. *Quick check:* Why does a wetland matter beyond its appearance?

**2. Categories of human-induced change.** **Atmospheric**: greenhouse-gas emissions, air pollution. **Land**: deforestation, urban sprawl, soil degradation. **Water**: pollution, over-extraction, salinity. **Biota**: habitat loss, invasive species, overharvesting. *Quick check:* Which category does land clearing fall under?

**3. Drivers.** Population growth; consumption patterns; technology; governance and policy choices; values. *Quick check:* Why is "population is the only problem" too simple?

**4. Climate change as master driver.** It amplifies droughts, floods, fires, sea-level rise, coral bleaching and species range shifts. *Quick check:* What is one Australian environment changed by climate change in your lifetime?

**5. Worked example: the Murray–Darling Basin.** A vast river system supplies water to farms and towns across four states and the ACT. Pressures: over-extraction, salinity, blue-green algae, fish kills, climate-driven drought. Responses: the Basin Plan; Sustainable Diversion Limits; environmental water; Aboriginal water-rights work. *Follow the thinking:* a place with many users requires shared rules.

**6. Worked example: Port Phillip Bay.** Pressures: stormwater pollution, nitrogen, microplastics, heat. Functions affected: fishing, recreation, biodiversity. Responses: catchment-management authorities, recycled water, marine parks. *Quick check:* Why does the bay's health depend on what happens 100 km inland?

**7. Sustainability responses.** Regulation; restoration (revegetation, fish ladders); circular economy; carbon farming; First Nations-led land management; Indigenous Protected Areas. *Quick check:* What is one strength of First Nations-led land management?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Murray–Darling Basin (Mallee section); Port Phillip Bay; the Otway Ranges; Wilson's Promontory. Each is monitored by EPA Victoria, Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action, and CSIRO.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Yorta Yorta, Wamba Wemba, Wadi Wadi, Latji Latji and other Peoples have long stewarded Country along the Murray. The Yorta Yorta Nation Aboriginal Corporation and the Murray Lower Darling Rivers Indigenous Nations advocate for cultural flows. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant farming and fishing communities — Vietnamese-Australian, Italian-Australian, Greek-Australian — contribute to both pressure and stewardship.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Environmental change is just climate change." It is broader — water, soil, biota too.
- "Restoration brings places back fully." Often partial; baselines are gone.
- "Aboriginal land management is past tense." It is current, expanding and evidence-based.
- "Nature is separate from people." Australian environments are shaped by people, including over millennia of First Nations stewardship.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why a wetland matters.* Flood buffering, water filtration, biodiversity, cultural significance.
2. *Land clearing category.* Land change (with knock-on effects on biota and water).
3. *Why "just population".* Consumption, technology and policy matter at least as much.
4. *Australian environment changed.* Great Barrier Reef bleaching; Black Summer fire-affected forests; alpine snow decline.
5. *Bay health depending on inland.* Stormwater and nutrients flow downstream into the bay.
6. *First Nations land-management strength.* Long-term knowledge; cultural-burning regimes; community connection.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Algal bloom drivers.* Fertiliser runoff; stormwater; heat.
2. *Mildura Murray infrastructure.* Efficient irrigation; environmental flows; cultural-flow agreements.
3. *Restoration project.* Baseline survey; species selection; long-term monitoring.
4. *Irrigation vs environment debate.* For irrigation: local economy. For environment: long-term system health. Both sides accept Basin Plan rules.
5. *Cultural-flow partnership.* Step 1: Yorta Yorta engagement. Step 2: agreed flow targets. Step 3: monitoring shared with the community.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Environment: Murray near Mildura.* Changes: over-extraction; salinity; invasive carp. Function under threat: provisioning of clean fresh water. Responses: Sustainable Diversion Limits and Yorta Yorta cultural-flow advocacy. Sources cited.
