# VC2HG10K02 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10K02. The environmental, economic and technological factors that influence crop production in Australia and worldwide.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Biomes and food security).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Crop production depends on a mix of conditions and choices.
  - Australia is unusual in some ways and typical in others.
  - Technology has reshaped productivity.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Sustainability; Numeracy; Asia and Australia's Engagement with Asia.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **environmental, economic and technological factors** that influence **crop production** in **Australia and worldwide**.
- You are learning to read crop production as a system.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **yield**, **fertiliser**, **mechanisation** and **commodity** in your own words.
- Identify three environmental factors that influence crop production.
- Identify three economic and three technological factors.
- Compare an Australian and a global example.

## Core content

**1. Environmental factors.** Climate (rainfall, temperature, frost, heat); soil quality and fertility; water availability; pests and diseases; topography. *Quick check:* Why does Australia's variability affect crop choice?

**2. Economic factors.** Commodity prices (global wheat, rice, sugar); input costs (fuel, fertiliser, seeds); labour availability; subsidies; trade access; transport. *Quick check:* What is one trade-policy decision that affects Australian crops?

**3. Technological factors.** Mechanisation (tractors, harvesters); irrigation (drip, sprinklers, water-saving); plant breeding (drought-resistant varieties); precision agriculture (GPS, drones, soil sensors); biotechnology. Each can lift yield. *Quick check:* What is one technology that helps with Australia's water variability?

**4. Worked example: wheat in Australia.** Distribution: across southern wheat-belt (WA, SA, Vic, NSW). Environmental factors: winter rainfall; well-drained soils; variability of seasons. Economic factors: global wheat price; export markets in Indonesia, China, Vietnam. Technological factors: precision agriculture; drought-resistant varieties from CSIRO. Yields have improved over decades; climate change is shifting the wheat-belt. *Follow the thinking:* environment + economics + technology → outcome → trade-offs.

**5. Worldwide comparison.** India's rice and wheat production; Brazil's soybeans; USA's corn-belt. Each shows different environment-economy-technology mixes. *Quick check:* Why is comparison useful?

**6. Risks and constraints.** Climate change; water scarcity; soil degradation; market volatility; labour shortages. Managed via diversification, technology and policy. *Quick check:* What is one Australian risk for wheat production?

**7. Sustainability.** Sustainable crop production maintains soil, water, biodiversity and community over the long term. Australian programs include conservation tillage, integrated pest management, regenerative agriculture, and Indigenous-led knowledge integration. *Quick check:* Why is sustainability part of food-system thinking?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Mallee, Wimmera and Goulburn-Murray are key Victorian wheat and irrigated-cropping regions. CSIRO and Agriculture Victoria publish research.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal-led farming initiatives include native-grass cultivation and bush-food cropping. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant farmers from Italy, Greece, India, Vietnam and China have introduced crops and techniques. Vietnamese-Australian growers in Melbourne supply markets across the state.

## Stretch yourself
Pick one Australian crop. Describe three environmental, three economic and three technological factors influencing it. Compare with one international example. Cite reputable sources. 250 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Yield is everything." Sustainability and quality also matter.
- "Technology solves all." It can also create new problems.
- "Australian agriculture is unique." Shares many features with similar climates globally.
- "Indigenous farming is past tense." It is current and growing.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Variability and crop choice.* Crops must tolerate variable seasons; varieties and timing are chosen accordingly.
2. *Trade policy and crops.* Free-trade agreements with China, Indonesia, India; tariffs affect competitiveness.
3. *Technology for water variability.* Drought-resistant seed varieties; precision irrigation; weather-smart planting.
4. *Why comparison is useful.* Reveals what policy and technology achieve in different conditions.
5. *Australian wheat risk.* Drought; frost; heat at flowering; market price.
6. *Sustainability in food-system thinking.* Long-term land, water and biodiversity must be maintained for future production.
