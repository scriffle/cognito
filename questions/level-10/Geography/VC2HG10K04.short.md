# VC2HG10K04 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The environmental, economic and political constraints, including climate change, on the world's capacity to sustainably feed projected future global populations.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Feeding the world is a system problem. Three constraint families. Climate change tightens all of them.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **constraints** on the world's capacity to **sustainably feed** projected future populations.
- You should be able to read global food security as a coupled system.

## Core content

World population is projected at about 9.7 billion by 2050. Feeding that sustainably requires roughly 50% more food on about the same land. **Environmental constraints**: water scarcity, soil degradation, biodiversity loss, pollinator decline, extreme weather, sea-level rise into deltas, warming oceans. **Economic constraints**: input costs, price volatility, concentration of seed/chemical companies, food waste (about a third), inequality. **Political constraints**: trade rules, tariffs, conflict, displacement, distortive subsidies, export bans, weak governance.

**Climate change is a threat multiplier**: heat at flowering reduces staple yields; growing zones shift; rainfall patterns change; pest ranges expand; deltas flood; oceans acidify.

**Worked example.** Wheat. Major producers include China, India, Russia, USA, Australia and the EU. Heatwaves at flowering reduce yields; drought lowers reserves; export disruptions cascade through prices. Australia's wheat belt is shifting; CSIRO breeds heat-tolerant varieties.

Pathways: research-driven productivity; less waste; diet shifts where appropriate; regenerative agriculture; equitable trade; investment in smallholders; Indigenous and traditional knowledge integration.

**Cultural context.** First Nations food systems — yam daisy, kangaroo, native grains, bush foods — offer insights for sustainable feeding. Australia exports food to Asia, the Middle East and the Pacific. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Environment + economy + politics.
- Climate change tightens every constraint.
- Productivity alone is not enough.
- Australia is part of global food security.

## Watch out for these traps
- "There is enough food; it's just distributed badly." Distribution matters, but production is also constrained.
- "Climate change is a future problem." It is reducing yields now.
- "Trade always helps food security." Usually, but bans and tariffs can hurt.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why "more land" isn't simple.* Most arable land is in use; expansion harms biodiversity and climate.
- *Why food waste matters.* About a third is wasted; reducing it raises effective supply without new land.
- *Why export bans hurt.* They raise prices for importers and erode trade trust.
- *Why climate change is a threat multiplier.* It worsens water, soil, pest and market stresses at once.
- *Short summary of wheat under pressure.* Heat-stressed flowering and trade shocks tighten supply; Australia adapts through CSIRO breeding and shifting growing zones.
