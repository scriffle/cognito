## What you are learning
- You are learning the **environmental, economic and social sustainability of farming** in Australia.
- You are learning to read farming through a sustainability lens.

By the end of this you should be able to:

## Core content

**1. Three sustainability dimensions.** **Environmental**: soil, water, biodiversity, climate. **Economic**: viability, profitability, market access. **Social**: rural communities, workers, mental health, cultural connection. None alone is enough. *Quick check:* Why is "profitable but eroding soil" not sustainable?

**2. Environmental sustainability practices.** Conservation tillage; cover cropping; integrated pest management; rotational grazing; revegetation; water-sensitive irrigation; renewable-energy on farms; biodiversity-focused planting. *Quick check:* What is one Victorian farming practice that supports biodiversity?

**3. Economic sustainability practices.** Diversification across crops and livestock; value-add processing; cooperative arrangements; long-term contracts; risk management (insurance, hedging); succession planning. *Quick check:* What is one risk that affects Australian farming income?

**4. Social sustainability practices.** Supporting rural communities; mental-health services for farmers; workers' conditions; multilingual support for diverse workforce; succession planning; First Nations partnerships. *Quick check:* Why does farmer mental health belong in sustainability analysis?

**5. Worked example: a Goulburn Valley orchard.** Environmental: shade-cloth reduces heat stress; reuses irrigation water; uses integrated pest management; plants native windbreaks. Economic: diversified crops; supplies multiple markets; succession plan in place; participates in a cooperative. Social: employs local and migrant workers under Fair Work conditions; supports local schools; engages with Yorta Yorta Traditional Owners. Each dimension reinforces the others. *Follow the thinking:* environment + economy + society together.

**6. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander farming.** Native-grass cropping, bush-food production, ranger programs, cultural burning all integrate sustainability. Indigenous Land and Sea Corporation supports many. *Quick check:* What is one benefit of Aboriginal-led farming on sustainability metrics?

**7. Climate change and farming.** Climate change is shifting growing conditions; farmers adapt through new varieties, timing changes, infrastructure investment and water-management changes. *Quick check:* What is one climate-adaptation step many Australian farmers are making?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Agriculture Victoria, Landcare Victoria and the National Farmers' Federation publish sustainability data. The Goulburn Valley, Mallee and Gippsland are key regions.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Yorta Yorta, Dja Dja Wurrung, Wadawurrung and other Victorian peoples lead joint-management and farming partnerships on Country. The Indigenous Land and Sea Corporation supports many. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant farmers contribute strongly in market gardening, viticulture and dairy. Multilingual safety and wellbeing support is part of social sustainability.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Sustainability is just environmental." It is also economic and social.
- "Big farms aren't sustainable." Some are; size is not the only factor.
- "Indigenous farming is small-scale." Many initiatives are commercial scale.
- "Certifications guarantee sustainability." They help; honest practice still matters.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *"Profitable but eroding soil" not sustainable.* Short-term profits without protecting soil reduce long-term productivity.
2. *Biodiversity practice.* Revegetation; windbreaks; integrated pest management.
3. *Australian farming income risk.* Drought; market price swings; trade barriers.
4. *Why mental health matters.* Farmer wellbeing affects safety, productivity and family.
5. *Aboriginal-led benefit.* Long-term land care; integrated practice; community connection.
6. *Australian climate-adaptation step.* Drought-resistant varieties; investment in irrigation efficiency; insurance schemes.

**Try these scenarios**

2. *Greater Shepparton farming sustainability.* Water-management workshops; cooperative marketing; succession-planning support.
3. *Mallee sustainability adviser.* Risk profile; current practices; goals for next season.
4. *Mandatory certifications debate.* For: consumer trust and consistency. Against: cost and complexity. Both sides agree on transparent standards.
5. *Yorta Yorta-led partnership.* Step 1: contact the Yorta Yorta Nation Aboriginal Corporation. Step 2: agree shared goals and respect protocols. Step 3: integrate practices over time.
