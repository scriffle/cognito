# VC2HG10K10 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Human-induced environmental changes and their impacts on the sustainability of places and environmental functions.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Human activity changes environments in many ways. Environmental functions underpin life. Sustainability needs repair as well as restraint.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **human-induced environmental changes** and their impacts on the **sustainability of places** and **environmental functions**.
- You should be able to read environmental change as a geographic system.

## Core content

Ecosystems provide **provisioning** (food, water, fibre), **regulating** (climate, flood, pollination), **cultural** and **supporting** (soil, nutrient cycles) services. Human-induced change falls across categories: **atmospheric** (emissions, air pollution); **land** (deforestation, urban sprawl, soil degradation); **water** (pollution, over-extraction, salinity); **biota** (habitat loss, invasive species, overharvesting). Drivers are population, consumption, technology, governance and values. **Climate change** amplifies droughts, floods, fires, sea-level rise and species range shifts.

**Worked example: Murray–Darling Basin.** A vast river system across four states and the ACT. Pressures: over-extraction, salinity, blue-green algae, fish kills, drought. Responses: Basin Plan; Sustainable Diversion Limits; environmental water; Aboriginal cultural-flow advocacy.

**Worked example: Port Phillip Bay.** Pressures: stormwater pollution, nitrogen, microplastics, heat. Responses: catchment authorities; recycled water; marine parks.

**Sustainability responses.** Regulation; restoration; circular economy; carbon farming; First Nations-led land management; Indigenous Protected Areas.

**Cultural context.** Yorta Yorta, Wamba Wemba, Wadi Wadi, Latji Latji and other Peoples have long stewarded Murray Country; the Murray Lower Darling Rivers Indigenous Nations advocate for cultural flows. Migrant farming and fishing communities in Victoria are part of both pressure and stewardship. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Environments provide functions, not just views.
- Human change cuts across air, land, water, biota.
- Climate change amplifies pressures.
- First Nations leadership strengthens sustainability.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Environmental change is just climate change." It is broader.
- "Restoration brings places back fully." Often partial.
- "Aboriginal land management is past tense." It is current and evidence-based.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why a wetland matters.* Flood buffering; water filtration; biodiversity; cultural significance.
- *Why not just population.* Consumption, technology and policy matter at least as much.
- *Bay health and inland.* Stormwater and nutrients flow downstream into the bay.
- *First Nations land-management strength.* Long-term knowledge; cultural burning; community connection.
- *Short summary of Murray pressures.* Over-extraction, salinity and drought stress the river; Basin Plan limits and cultural flows are key responses.
