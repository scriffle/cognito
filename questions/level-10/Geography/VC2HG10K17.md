# VC2HG10K17 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10K17. Reasons for and implications of spatial differences in the wellbeing of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples at local and regional scales.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Geographies of human wellbeing).
- **Big ideas:**
  - First Nations wellbeing varies across Country.
  - Colonisation and policy are structural causes.
  - Strengths and self-determined responses are central.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures; Ethical Capability; Critical and Creative Thinking.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **reasons for and implications of spatial differences** in the **wellbeing of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples** at **local and regional scales**.
- You are learning to read First Nations wellbeing critically — gaps and strengths together.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **self-determination**, **closing the gap** and **structural disadvantage** in your own words.
- Identify three causes of spatial differences in First Nations wellbeing.
- Describe one Victorian and one remote example.
- Discuss strengths-based responses.

## Core content

**1. The picture.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander wellbeing varies sharply by region — urban, regional, remote and very remote — and between Countries. National averages mask wide variation. *Quick check:* Why is one-figure reporting often misleading?

**2. Causes of difference.** **Colonisation and dispossession**; **mission and reserve systems**; **Stolen Generations**; **infrastructure gaps in remote areas**; **discrimination in services**; **labour market access**; **distance from services**. Also: variation in self-government, treaty and corporate frameworks. *Quick check:* Why are colonial-era policies still relevant today?

**3. Strengths.** Cultural connection; kinship; language; Country; community governance; growing Aboriginal business sector; ranger and arts industries; intergenerational resilience. *Quick check:* What is one strength visible in the Mayi Kuwayu study?

**4. Indicators.** Life expectancy; child mortality; school attendance and Year 12 attainment; employment; incarceration; suicide rate; housing overcrowding; language transmission. Also Aboriginal-led indicators of cultural wellbeing. *Quick check:* Why include cultural-wellbeing indicators?

**5. Worked example: urban Victoria.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander populations in Melbourne, Geelong and Bendigo access more services but also face housing pressure, discrimination and identity stress. The Victorian Aboriginal Health Service and Aboriginal Community Controlled Organisations (ACCOs) lead responses. *Quick check:* Why are ACCOs critical in urban Victoria?

**6. Worked example: remote Australia.** Remote communities in the NT, WA and Queensland face long travel to services, housing overcrowding and infrastructure gaps. Strengths include strong language transmission, Country-based economies and ranger programs. The Closing the Gap framework, Local Decision Making and Empowered Communities programs are responses. *Quick check:* Why is connectivity (transport, broadband) so important in remote wellbeing?

**7. Self-determination as response.** Closing the Gap (2020 refreshed agreement) is built around partnership with Aboriginal peak bodies; targets cover health, education, employment, justice and culture. Victorian treaty processes give legal effect to self-determination. *Quick check:* What does self-determination mean in plain English?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria; Yoorrook Justice Commission; Victorian Closing the Gap; Aboriginal Affairs Victoria; Victorian Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisation (VACCHO).
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal-led research and reporting — Mayi Kuwayu, the National Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Social Survey, AIATSIS — drives understanding. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples include long-standing intermarriage with diverse migrant communities. Identity is plural; service design must respect this.

## Watch out for these traps
- "It is all a deficit story." Strengths are part of the picture.
- "Closing the Gap fixes everything." It is a framework; outcomes depend on action.
- "All Aboriginal experience is the same." It varies by Country, place and history.
- "Specific knowledge is for everyone to use." It belongs to communities; cite published, approved material only.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why one figure misleads.* It hides regional and Country variation and strengths.
2. *Colonial-era relevance today.* Land, family and economic effects compound over generations.
3. *Mayi Kuwayu strength.* Strong cultural connection associated with better wellbeing outcomes.
4. *Why cultural indicators.* They reflect what communities value and capture strengths.
5. *Why ACCOs matter in urban Victoria.* They deliver culturally safe services and build community governance.
6. *Self-determination meaning.* People deciding for themselves with authority and resources.
