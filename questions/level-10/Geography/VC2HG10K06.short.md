# VC2HG10K06 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The economic and demographic impacts of international trade in manufactured products on places, including on Australia and Asia.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Manufacturing moves where conditions favour it. Trade reshapes work and migration. Australia and Asia are deeply linked.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **economic and demographic impacts** of **international trade in manufactured products** on places, including Australia and Asia.
- You should be able to read trade as a force that reshapes places.

## Core content

Manufacturing moves toward **lower labour cost**, **raw-material access**, **supply-chain clusters**, **favourable policy** and **large markets**. **Global value chains** spread design, components, assembly and markets across multiple countries.

**Economic impacts**: lower consumer prices; cheaper inputs; job creation in receiving countries; job loss in deindustrialising places; growth of services and specialised manufacturing. **Demographic impacts**: rural-to-urban migration in receiving countries; mega-city growth; ageing populations in deindustrialising regions; migration corridors; cultural exchange.

**Worked example.** Australia exports iron ore, lithium, coal, food, education and tourism; imports electronics, cars, clothing and machinery from China, Japan, Korea and Vietnam. Australian car making closed in 2017; specialised manufacturing (medical devices, defence, food) continues. In Victoria, Geelong lost Ford in 2016 and gained logistics, services and advanced manufacturing. Dandenong's manufacturing cluster relies on a diverse migrant workforce.

**Sustainability and ethics.** Embodied carbon, supply-chain labour conditions (Modern Slavery Act 2018) and resource-extraction impact all matter.

**Cultural context.** Mining of Aboriginal lands contributes to exports; the Native Title Act 1993 and ILUAs shape consent. Supply Nation supports Aboriginal-owned businesses. Migrant labour from Greece, Italy, Vietnam, China and India built Victorian manufacturing. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Manufacturing moves where it pays.
- Trade reshapes work and migration.
- Australia and Asia are deeply linked.
- Sustainability and ethics now matter.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Australia makes nothing anymore." Specialised manufacturing thrives in medical, defence and food.
- "Trade is win-win for everyone." Workers in deindustrialising places face real costs.
- "Asia is one place." It is many economies with different roles.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why clothes are made in Vietnam or Bangladesh.* Lower labour cost and established garment clusters.
- *Australian input into a value chain.* Iron ore for steel; lithium for batteries; wool for textiles.
- *What replaced Geelong car making.* Logistics, services, advanced manufacturing, education.
- *Why Modern Slavery Act matters.* Requires large companies to report supply-chain risks.
- *Short summary of Australia–Asia trade.* Australia exports resources, food, education and services; Asia exports manufactured goods; trade shapes work and migration on both sides.
