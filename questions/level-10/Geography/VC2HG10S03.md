# VC2HG10S03 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10S03. Represent and analyse information and data using a range of formats, including graphs and maps constructed with geospatial technologies.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Geographical inquiry — skills).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Representation reveals or hides patterns.
  - The right chart depends on the data and audience.
  - Maps are arguments.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Digital Literacy; Critical and Creative Thinking.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **represent and analyse information and data** using **graphs and maps** constructed with **geospatial technologies**.
- You are learning to choose formats that match your data and message.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **chart type**, **thematic map** and **map projection** in your own words.
- Match chart types to data types.
- Build a thematic map with a clear legend.
- Read a map critically for what it shows and hides.

## Core content

**1. Match the chart to the data.** **Bar/column**: categories. **Line**: change over time. **Scatter**: relationships between two variables. **Pie**: parts of a whole (use sparingly). **Histogram**: distribution. **Box plot**: spread and outliers. *Quick check:* Which chart suits annual rainfall over 50 years?

**2. Thematic maps.** **Choropleth** (filled regions): rates per area. **Dot/density**: counts. **Proportional symbol**: comparative magnitudes. **Flow**: movement. **Isoline**: continuous variables (contours, isotherms). *Quick check:* Why use rates rather than counts on a choropleth?

**3. Map design.** Every map needs a title, legend, scale, north arrow, source and date. Choose a projection (Web Mercator is the web default but distorts area). Use accessible colour palettes. *Quick check:* Why does the choice of class breaks change the story a choropleth tells?

**4. Geospatial tools.** QGIS, ArcGIS Online, Google Earth Pro, Google My Maps, Leaflet, Datawrapper. Add layers; symbolise by attribute; produce a clean layout. *Quick check:* What does symbolising by attribute mean?

**5. Worked example: SEIFA in Greater Melbourne.** Use ABS SEIFA data as a choropleth at LGA level; class by deciles; use accessible colour ramp; cite source. Add transport network layer to discuss interconnection. *Follow the thinking:* one map, layered for analysis.

**6. Analysis tools.** Summary statistics; trend lines; correlation; spatial-autocorrelation indicators; before-and-after comparisons. *Quick check:* What does a trend line do?

**7. Reading maps critically.** Ask: who made it, when, what is in and out, what classification, what projection? Maps can persuade as much as inform. *Quick check:* Why is a "red = bad" colour choice not neutral?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** ABS SEIFA, BoM climate maps, DataVic, EPA dashboards, Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action spatial datasets.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Maps shape recognition. The AIATSIS map of Aboriginal Australia (Horton 1996) shows language groups; Native Title and Cultural Heritage maps are core legal documents. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** ABS multicultural-data dashboards reveal language and birthplace patterns across Victoria.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Pie charts are always fine." Often hard to read; use sparingly.
- "Maps are objective." Choices shape what they say.
- "Web Mercator is neutral." It distorts polar regions; consider equal-area for global maps.
- "More colours mean more information." Often the opposite.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Rainfall over 50 years.* Line chart (with optional trend line).
2. *Why rates not counts on a choropleth.* Counts highlight where there are many people; rates highlight intensity.
3. *Why class breaks matter.* Different breaks shift visual emphasis and conclusions.
4. *Symbolise by attribute.* Map symbols vary with data values (colour, size, pattern).
5. *Trend-line function.* Summarises direction and magnitude of change over time.
6. *"Red = bad" choice.* Colour carries meaning; some palettes are culturally loaded.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Local rainfall.* Line chart; trend line and decadal averages.
2. *Casey tree canopy.* Choropleth at SA1; accessible green ramp; clear class breaks.
3. *Fire-risk dashboard.* Fuel load; weather index; population vulnerability.
4. *Crime-rate map debate.* For: spatial awareness. Against: reinforces stigma. Both sides accept context and rates.
5. *Country boundaries.* Step 1: cite AIATSIS source. Step 2: note approximation. Step 3: avoid drawing hard lines on Country.
