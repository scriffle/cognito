## Core content

**1. What is an environmental worldview?** A **worldview** is a set of beliefs about humans, nature and their relationship. It shapes what people value, what they fear, and what policies they support. *Quick check:* Why do two people seeing the same forest reach different conclusions about logging?

**2. Major worldviews.** **Anthropocentric**: humans are central; nature is for human use. **Ecocentric**: nature has intrinsic value beyond human use. **Biocentric**: all life has value. **Indigenous custodial**: humans are part of Country with responsibilities. **Stewardship**: humans manage on behalf of future generations or a higher purpose. *Quick check:* Which worldview most matches the term "natural resources"?

**3. Worldviews and policy.** Anthropocentric framings support development and use. Ecocentric framings support protection and rights of nature. Custodial framings support First Nations leadership. Stewardship framings support balanced multi-use management. *Quick check:* What worldview underlies a wilderness area?

**4. Where worldviews clash.** Logging vs old-growth protection. Mining vs sacred sites. Hunting and fishing vs species protection. Development vs cultural heritage. Climate policy vs fossil-fuel industry. *Quick check:* Why does naming a worldview help debate?

**5. Worked example: Djab Wurrung birthing trees.** In 2018–2020, a section of the Western Highway upgrade in Victoria threatened Djab Wurrung sacred trees. Traditional Owners and supporters protected key trees; some were removed and others retained. The debate combined custodial, ecocentric and development worldviews. *Follow the thinking:* worldviews shaped legal, political and on-Country actions.

**6. Worked example: Rights of Nature.** New Zealand's Whanganui River and the Yarra River (Birrarung) in Victoria have been recognised in law as having legal personhood or living-entity status. The Yarra River Protection (Wilip-gin Birrarung murron) Act 2017 reflects Wurundjeri custodial thinking. *Quick check:* What does it mean to give a river legal personhood?

**7. Dialogue across worldviews.** Sustainable solutions often combine worldviews — for example, custodial leadership with ecocentric science with stewardship policy. *Quick check:* Why is "ecocentric vs anthropocentric" sometimes a false binary?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Yarra River Protection Act 2017; Djab Wurrung birthing trees; Great Forest National Park proposals; offshore-wind debates.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Custodial worldviews — articulated across Victorian Country — centre relational responsibility. The First Peoples' Assembly, Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations and individual Aboriginal Corporations are key voices. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant communities bring varied worldviews — Confucian stewardship, Christian and Islamic stewardship, Hindu and Buddhist ecological ethics, secular environmentalism. Victorian multicultural environment networks reflect this mix.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Worldviews are just opinions." They have real policy and behaviour consequences.
- "There is only one correct worldview." Most policy synthesises several.
- "Indigenous worldviews are folklore." They are sophisticated, evidence-rich frameworks.
- "Science is worldview-free." Science methods are; framing is not.
