# VC2HG10S02 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10S02. Collect, process and compare information and data from primary and secondary sources, including fieldwork and secondary research materials, using geospatial technologies and digital tools as appropriate.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Geographical inquiry — skills).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Good geography combines fieldwork and desk research.
  - Geospatial tools amplify human judgement.
  - Compare sources to find pattern and contradiction.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Digital Literacy; Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **collect, process and compare information and data** from **primary and secondary sources**, including **fieldwork**, **research materials**, and **geospatial technologies**.
- You are learning to combine sources for stronger inquiry.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **primary data**, **secondary data**, **GIS** and **remote sensing** in your own words.
- Plan a fieldwork-and-desk research process.
- Use at least one geospatial tool.
- Compare sources for agreement and conflict.

## Core content

**1. Primary vs secondary.** **Primary**: data you collect — surveys, fieldwork, photographs, sketches, interviews, measurements. **Secondary**: data others have collected — ABS, BoM, journals, news, satellite archives. *Quick check:* Which type is a Bureau of Meteorology rainfall record?

**2. Fieldwork.** Plan: site choice, method, safety, ethics, equipment. Collect: measurements, observations, samples, photos. Process: tabulate, code, summarise. *Quick check:* Why does a site reconnaissance visit help?

**3. Geospatial tools.** **GIS** (e.g., QGIS, ArcGIS, Google Earth Pro, Google My Maps): map layers and data. **Remote sensing**: satellite and aerial imagery (Sentinel, Landsat, Earth Observation Australia). **GPS**: location capture. Online dashboards: BoM, EPA, ABS Census. *Quick check:* What does adding a layer to a map let you see?

**4. Digital tools more broadly.** Spreadsheets for cleaning and stats; survey platforms; visualisation tools (Datawrapper, Flourish); coding (Python, R) for larger datasets. *Quick check:* Why is data cleaning often the longest step?

**5. Processing.** Tidy data: one row per observation, clear column headers, consistent units. Recode categories. Calculate derived measures (averages, change, ratios). Document your steps. *Quick check:* Why does documenting steps matter?

**6. Worked example: assessing a Werribee River reach.** Primary: site walks; water-quality measurements; photo logs. Secondary: EPA monitoring data; council reports; satellite imagery. Processing: tidy field data; plot in QGIS over satellite imagery; cross-check with EPA records. *Follow the thinking:* primary + secondary together reveal more than either alone.

**7. Comparing sources.** Look for agreement (triangulation), contradictions and gaps. Where sources disagree, ask who collected the data, how, when and why. *Quick check:* What might explain disagreement between official and citizen-science water-quality data?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** EPA Victoria water-quality data; BoM weather and climate; ABS Census; DataVic; Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action spatial datasets.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Cultural mapping and data sovereignty are central. The Maiam nayri Wingara Indigenous Data Sovereignty Collective and AIATSIS publish guidance. Aboriginal-led monitoring uses both Western and traditional knowledge. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual survey design and culturally informed fieldwork strengthen data quality in diverse communities.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Field data beats secondary." Each has strengths; together they are stronger.
- "GIS does the analysis." It supports analysis; you still need judgement.
- "Citizen data is unreliable." Quality varies; protocols matter.
- "Indigenous data is open." Community consent and protocols apply.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *BoM rainfall record type.* Secondary.
2. *Why reconnaissance.* Spot hazards; refine method; confirm access.
3. *Adding a map layer.* Reveals spatial relationships among variables.
4. *Why data cleaning takes time.* Real-world data is messy — missing, mis-typed, inconsistent.
5. *Why document steps.* Reproducibility and trust.
6. *Citizen-vs-official disagreement.* Different methods, locations, timing or precision.

**Try these scenarios**

4. *Citizen-science alongside official debate.* For: coverage and engagement. Against: quality variation. Both sides accept transparent protocols.
5. *Bunurong cultural mapping.* Step 1: protocols and consent. Step 2: agreed data sovereignty. Step 3: ongoing custody arrangements.
