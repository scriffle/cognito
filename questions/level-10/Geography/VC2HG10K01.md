## Core content

**1. What a biome is.** A **biome** is a large region with a particular climate, vegetation and animal life. Major biomes include tropical rainforest, savanna, desert, temperate forest, grassland, boreal forest (taiga), tundra, freshwater and marine. *Quick check:* Why is a biome different from a country?

**2. Climate shapes biomes.** Temperature and rainfall are the major drivers. Soil, elevation and seasonality also matter. The Köppen climate classification maps onto biomes. *Quick check:* Why is rainfall as important as temperature in defining biomes?

**3. Biomass and productivity.** **Biomass** is the total mass of living material in an area. **Net primary productivity (NPP)** measures how fast plants build biomass. Tropical rainforests have very high NPP; deserts low. Productivity sets the upper bound on food potential. *Quick check:* What is one biome with naturally high productivity?

**4. Worked example: Australia's biomes.** Tropical rainforest in north Queensland; savanna across northern Australia; grasslands and woodlands in central regions; arid deserts in interior; temperate forest in south-east; alpine in Victorian Alps; marine biomes off all coasts. Each has distinctive vegetation and food potential (grazing in savannas; horticulture in temperate regions; fishing in marine). *Follow the thinking:* identify biome → describe climate and vegetation → assess productivity → describe food potential.

**5. Arable land.** **Arable land** is land suitable for crop production. About 11% of Earth's land is arable, with much more under grazing or forest. Distribution is uneven. *Quick check:* What is one biome that produces a lot of food despite low arable share?

**6. Marine biomes and food.** Oceans contribute ~15-20% of animal protein consumed by humans. Coastal upwelling zones (off Peru, Western Australia) are highly productive. Aquaculture is growing. *Quick check:* Why are marine biomes part of food-security analysis?

**7. Climate change effects.** Climate change is shifting biome distributions: boreal forests creeping north; some grasslands drying; coral biomes under heat stress. Food potential shifts with the biomes. *Quick check:* What is one Australian biome under stress from climate change?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Victoria includes temperate forest, alpine, grasslands, sclerophyll forests, coastal and marine biomes. The Atlas of Living Australia maps these.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** First Nations knowledge of Country includes detailed biome management: cool burning, eel aquaculture (Budj Bim), yam-daisy and grain-grass harvesting. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant farmers and food producers bring biome-specific knowledge from many regions; market gardens, dairy, viticulture in Victoria reflect this.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Biome = ecosystem." Biomes are larger than single ecosystems.
- "High NPP = high food production." Some highly productive biomes are poor for agriculture (rainforests).
- "Marine doesn't count." It is major for protein supply.
- "Biomes are fixed." Climate change is moving them.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Biome vs country.* Biomes are defined by climate and biology; countries by politics. Many biomes span multiple countries.
2. *Why rainfall matters as much as temperature.* It determines plant growth potential; deserts and rainforests can share temperatures but differ in rainfall.
3. *Naturally high-productivity biome.* Tropical rainforest; estuarine wetlands; some grasslands.
4. *Food despite low arable share.* Tropical biomes via fruit and root crops; marine biomes via fishing.
5. *Why marine biomes count.* Major protein source; aquaculture growing; climate-vulnerable.
6. *Australian biome under climate stress.* Alpine; Great Barrier Reef; coastal wetlands.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Local biomes.* Temperate forest; coastal; urbanised. Many suburbs sit in modified versions of these.
2. *Surf Coast biodiversity strategy.* Coastal-vegetation restoration; reef-monitoring; community planting.
3. *Junior agricultural scientist.* Climate match for crop; soil characteristics; water availability.
4. *Arable land for food debate.* For: food security. Against: biodiversity and carbon storage. Both sides agree mixed land use is needed.
5. *Gunditjmara Budj Bim discussion.* Step 1: contact the Gunditjmara Aboriginal Corporation. Step 2: visit under guidance. Step 3: integrate biome science with cultural knowledge.

Productivity: low compared with temperate forest. Food potential: limited; mostly grazing and tourism. Aboriginal management: Bogong moth harvesting by Indigenous nations. Sources cited.
