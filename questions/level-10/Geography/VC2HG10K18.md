# VC2HG10K18 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10K18. The role and responses of international and national government and non-government organisations in improving human wellbeing at a local or regional scale.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Geographies of human wellbeing).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Many actors work on wellbeing.
  - Coordination matters as much as funding.
  - Local action makes global goals real.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Ethical Capability; Critical and Creative Thinking; Intercultural Capability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **role and responses** of **international and national** **government and non-government organisations** in improving **human wellbeing** at **local or regional scales**.
- You are learning to read the wellbeing system from global to neighbourhood.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **multilateral**, **bilateral** and **non-government organisation (NGO)** in your own words.
- Identify three international and three Australian organisations and what they do.
- Describe one example of local action linked to global goals.
- Discuss the strengths and limits of each actor.

## Core content

**1. Layers of action.** **International**: UN agencies, multilateral banks, regional bodies. **Bilateral**: country-to-country aid, treaties. **National**: federal departments, agencies. **Subnational**: state, local. **Non-government**: NGOs, charities, faith-based organisations, community-controlled organisations, philanthropic foundations. *Quick check:* Why do all these layers exist?

**2. International actors.** UN agencies: WHO (health); UNICEF (children); UNDP (development); UNHCR (refugees). Multilateral banks: World Bank, Asian Development Bank. Regional: ASEAN, Pacific Islands Forum. *Quick check:* What does WHO actually do?

**3. National Australian actors.** Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) administers Australia's aid program. Federal departments cover health, education, social services. State governments deliver services. Aboriginal Community Controlled Organisations (ACCOs) deliver community-led services. *Quick check:* What is the difference between DFAT and a state health department?

**4. NGOs and charities.** Oxfam, Save the Children, Red Cross, World Vision, Caritas, Plan International, Médecins Sans Frontières. Local: VACCHO, Foodbank, Anglicare, the Brotherhood of St Laurence. *Quick check:* What is one strength of NGOs vs governments?

**5. Worked example: SDG 3 (Good health and wellbeing) in Vanuatu.** WHO supports public-health programs; DFAT funds health-system strengthening; Vanuatu Ministry of Health delivers; UNICEF supports child immunisation; local NGOs deliver outreach. Cyclone Pam (2015), Harold (2020) and Lola (2023) tested the system. *Follow the thinking:* one country's wellbeing depends on coordinated layers.

**6. Worked example: Closing the Gap in Victoria.** Federal and Victorian governments partner with ACCO peak bodies; the First Peoples' Assembly leads treaty work; ACCOs deliver services; philanthropy funds gaps. *Quick check:* Why is local Aboriginal leadership essential here?

**7. Limits and tensions.** Aid dependency; coordination failures; political interference; reporting burden; funding instability. Strong design includes local leadership, long-term funding and clear accountability. *Quick check:* Why can short-term funding harm wellbeing programs?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** DFAT regional office; VACCHO; Brotherhood of St Laurence; Foundation for Young Australians; community foundations.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** ACCOs are key delivery actors. Closing the Gap (2020) requires partnership with Aboriginal peak bodies; the Coalition of Peaks represents national ACCO leadership. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multicultural service organisations — AMES Australia, Foundation House, Migrant Resource Centres — deliver settlement, health and education services across Victoria.

## Memorable takeaways
- Many actors; many layers.
- Coordination matters.
- Local leadership is essential.
- Long-term funding beats short-term.
- Global goals need local action.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Government does everything." Many wellbeing services rely on NGOs and ACCOs.
- "NGOs are neutral." They have values and constraints.
- "Aid is a one-way gift." Two-way partnership produces better outcomes.
- "Global goals are too big." Local action delivers them.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why multiple layers exist.* Different scales of problems and different mandates require different actors.
2. *What WHO does.* Global health norms, surveillance, emergency response, technical assistance.
3. *DFAT vs state health.* DFAT manages international aid and foreign affairs; state health delivers local services.
4. *NGO strength.* Flexibility, community trust and specialisation.
5. *Why local Aboriginal leadership in Closing the Gap.* Decisions sit with those most affected; cultural safety improves outcomes.
6. *Short-term funding harm.* Programs and trust collapse when funding ends; workforce churn weakens delivery.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Charity donation questions.* Where does the money go; what is measured; who leads in the community?
2. *Maribyrnong sister-city.* Local fundraising; preparedness education; respectful messaging.
3. *Pacific health aid.* Climate-resilient infrastructure; workforce training; partnership models.
4. *Aid-cut debate.* For: domestic priorities. Against: international obligations and stability. Both sides accept transparent budgets.
5. *Pacific school exchange.* Step 1: listen to host priorities. Step 2: avoid stereotypes. Step 3: maintain ongoing connection.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Issue: maternal and child health.* International: WHO, UNICEF, World Bank. National: Department of Health, Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade, ACCOs. Local: ACCHOs, maternal-child health nurses, NGOs. Strengths: standards-setting; service delivery; community trust. Limits: coordination, funding cycles, data gaps. Sources cited.
