# VC2HG10K15 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10K15. Ways of measuring human wellbeing in places, and how these can be applied to measure differences between countries.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Geographies of human wellbeing).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Wellbeing is more than income.
  - Measures shape policy.
  - Within-country differences can be larger than between-country differences.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Ethical Capability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **ways of measuring human wellbeing** in places and how these can be **applied to measure differences between countries**.
- You are learning to read wellbeing critically.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **wellbeing**, **GDP per capita**, **Human Development Index (HDI)** and **multidimensional poverty** in your own words.
- Identify three single-indicator and three composite measures.
- Describe what HDI captures and misses.
- Discuss within-country differences in Australia.

## Core content

**1. What is wellbeing?** **Wellbeing** is the quality of life people experience, beyond income. It includes health, education, safety, environment, voice, belonging and culture. *Quick check:* Why is high income not enough on its own?

**2. Single-indicator measures.** **GDP per capita**, life expectancy, literacy rate, infant mortality, access to safe water. Each captures one slice. *Quick check:* What does GDP per capita miss?

**3. Composite measures.** **Human Development Index (HDI)** combines life expectancy, education and income. **Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)** captures health, education and living standards across many indicators. **Inequality-adjusted HDI** reflects distribution. **Gender Development Index**. *Quick check:* Why is HDI more useful than GDP for comparing countries' progress?

**4. New measures.** **Better Life Index** (OECD), **Bhutan's Gross National Happiness**, **Australia's Measuring What Matters framework (2023)**, **OECD How's Life?**. These include subjective wellbeing, environment and trust. *Quick check:* What is one thing Measuring What Matters tracks beyond GDP?

**5. Worked example: Australia vs Indonesia vs Bangladesh.** HDI ranks change interpretation: Australia very high; Indonesia and Bangladesh medium; differences appear smaller when comparing health and education than when comparing income alone. *Follow the thinking:* multiple measures give a fuller picture.

**6. Within-country differences.** In Australia, wellbeing varies sharply by region, postcode and Indigenous status. Closing the Gap reports show ongoing inequalities in life expectancy, child mortality, education and incarceration between Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people and the general population. *Quick check:* Why is "national averages" sometimes misleading?

**7. Limitations of measures.** No measure is neutral. Choosing what to count is a values choice. Data quality varies. Cultural wellbeing is hard to quantify. *Quick check:* Why is "what you measure is what you manage" both useful and risky?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** ABS data, Victorian Population Health Survey, Closing the Gap Victoria, and SEIFA index of disadvantage map wellbeing variation by region.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal-led wellbeing measures include the Mayi Kuwayu study and the National Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Survey. These capture cultural connection, language, kinship and connection to Country alongside Western indicators. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** ABS Multicultural Australia statistics show wellbeing gradients across migrant cohorts, with refugees often the most disadvantaged.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Higher GDP always means better lives." Inequality and environment matter.
- "HDI is the truth." It captures three dimensions; many are missing.
- "Indigenous wellbeing is just a gap." It also has strengths — culture, kinship, identity, connection.
- "Measures are objective." They reflect values choices.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why high income is not enough.* Health, environment, security, belonging and voice all matter.
2. *What GDP per capita misses.* Distribution; non-market work; environment; subjective wellbeing.
3. *Why HDI helps.* Combines health, education and income; less skewed by income alone.
4. *Measuring What Matters element.* Health, security, sustainability, cohesion or prosperity.
5. *Why national averages mislead.* They hide large regional and group differences.
6. *Risk of "measure-manage".* What is measured gets attention; unmeasured matters can be ignored.
