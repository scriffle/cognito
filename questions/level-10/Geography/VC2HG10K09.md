## Core content

**1. What diffusion means.** **Diffusion** is the spread of a phenomenon across space and through populations over time. It can be **contagion** (neighbour-to-neighbour, like a virus) or **hierarchical** (from large hubs down a settlement system, like a fashion). *Quick check:* Which type does a viral TikTok song most resemble?

**2. Why diffusion has accelerated.** Cheap travel, container shipping, internet platforms, social media algorithms and global media compress time and space. *Quick check:* Why did the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic spread faster than the 1918 influenza?

**3. Cultural diffusion: K-pop.** Korean pop music emerged in Seoul in the 1990s and 2000s, diffused first to East Asian neighbours, then through YouTube and streaming to global audiences. BTS broke into the US in 2017. Australian fan communities grew on social media. *Quick check:* Why did digital platforms accelerate K-pop globalisation?

**4. Cultural diffusion: fashion.** Fast-fashion brands compress design-to-shelf time from months to weeks. Trends from Paris, Seoul or TikTok appear in Melbourne stores within weeks. Critics highlight environmental and labour costs. *Quick check:* What is one downside of fast fashion's diffusion speed?

**5. Pandemic diffusion: COVID-19.** Originated in Wuhan, China in late 2019; global flight networks spread it within weeks; local clusters became outbreaks. Australia closed international borders in March 2020. Diffusion patterns followed flight hubs first, then road and household contacts. *Quick check:* Why did flight-hub cities see outbreaks before regional centres?

**6. Financial diffusion: the 2008 Global Financial Crisis.** Subprime mortgage losses in the US spread through interconnected banks and securitised debt; markets fell worldwide; Australia avoided recession through stimulus and bank regulation. *Follow the thinking:* finance, like disease, diffuses through networks.

**7. Why some places are first and others last.** Network position matters more than physical distance. A small global city (Singapore) is exposed earlier than a remote large country (parts of Central Asia). Connectivity, language and platform access shape diffusion paths. *Quick check:* What is one reason Melbourne is exposed to global trends quickly?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Melbourne hosts global music tours, fashion weeks and sporting events; airport connectivity makes it an early diffusion node. The 2020–2021 lockdowns were among the world's longest, shaped by local diffusion analysis.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander music, art and design have diffused internationally — Yothu Yindi, Archie Roach, Baker Boy, Aboriginal art markets. Pandemic protection of remote communities is also a current case. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Diaspora networks accelerate cultural diffusion: K-pop fan clubs in Melbourne; Bollywood film releases; Vietnamese-Australian music tours; African fashion designers in Footscray.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Distance is the main control." Network position matters more.
- "Cultural diffusion is harmless." It can erode local industries and traditions.
- "Pandemics spread randomly." They follow specific networks.
- "Australia is isolated." It is well connected by air and digital networks.
