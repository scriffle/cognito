# VC2HG10S06 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10S06. Justify responses and develop and evaluate strategies using environmental, economic or social criteria, recommend a strategy and explain the predicted impacts.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Geographical inquiry — skills).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Strategies are designed, not just chosen.
  - Criteria make comparisons fair.
  - Predicted impacts come with uncertainty.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Critical and Creative Thinking; Ethical Capability; Sustainability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **justify responses** and **develop and evaluate strategies** using **environmental, economic or social criteria**, **recommend** a strategy and **explain predicted impacts**.
- You are learning to move from analysis to recommendation.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **strategy**, **criteria** and **impact prediction** in your own words.
- Generate three alternative strategies for one issue.
- Score them against criteria.
- Recommend one with reasoning and predicted impacts.

## Core content

**1. Strategy.** A **strategy** is a planned way to achieve an outcome. It has goals, actions, resources and timelines. *Quick check:* What is the difference between a goal and a strategy?

**2. Generate options.** For any issue, brainstorm three or more strategies — including a "do nothing" baseline. Avoid jumping to the first solution. *Quick check:* Why is a "do nothing" baseline useful?

**3. Criteria.** Pick **environmental, economic and social** criteria appropriate to the issue. Examples: emissions, biodiversity, cost, equity, feasibility, cultural integrity, time to impact. *Quick check:* Why are weighted criteria sometimes used?

**4. Compare with a matrix.** Build a table: strategies as rows, criteria as columns. Score each cell (qualitatively or numerically). Note assumptions. *Quick check:* Why does a transparent matrix beat a private hunch?

**5. Worked example: reducing heat in a Melbourne suburb.** Strategies: tree-canopy expansion; cool-roof rebates; community cooling hubs; status quo. Criteria: temperature reduction; cost; equity of access; biodiversity; time to impact. Matrix scores show tree canopy and cool-roof rebates strong on temperature and equity; cooling hubs strong on short-term equity; status quo low across all. *Follow the thinking:* options → criteria → matrix → recommendation.

**6. Recommendation and predicted impacts.** Pick a strategy (or a mix) and predict impacts at named time horizons (5, 10, 25 years), with confidence levels. State assumptions and risks. *Quick check:* Why include time horizons explicitly?

**7. Monitoring and revision.** Plan to monitor outcomes and update the strategy. *Quick check:* Why are most good plans "adaptive"?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Plan Melbourne 2017–2050; Climate Change Strategy 2021; Victorian Aboriginal Affairs Framework; Local Government Act 2020 community-vision processes.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Strong strategies include Aboriginal-led design and free, prior and informed consent. The First Peoples' Assembly is negotiating treaty-based decision-making in Victoria. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual consultation strengthens evaluation criteria reflecting diverse community needs.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You plan to reduce your household's water use. Identify three strategies and three criteria.
2. **Civic.** Your council in Hume plans heatwave response. Suggest three strategies and a matrix outline.
3. **Vocational.** A graduate planner recommends transport priorities in Geelong. Outline three strategies, criteria and a recommendation.
4. **Ethical.** A class debates whether cost should outweigh equity in strategy design. Identify two arguments on each side.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A council partners with Wurundjeri on a cultural-burning strategy. Outline three respectful steps.

## Memorable takeaways
- Generate multiple options.
- Use explicit criteria.
- Predict impacts with uncertainty.
- Recommend, with monitoring.
- Include Aboriginal-led design.

## Stretch yourself
Pick one Victorian environmental or social issue. Generate three strategies. Score them against three criteria. Recommend one (or a mix). Predict impacts at 10 and 25 years with uncertainty. Cite reputable sources. 300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Pick the first idea." Generate alternatives first.
- "One criterion is enough." Trade-offs require several.
- "Predict impacts as certainty." State uncertainty.
- "Strategies are set and forget." Monitor and revise.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Goal vs strategy.* The goal is the destination; the strategy is the route.
2. *"Do nothing" baseline.* Provides a comparison point for other strategies.
3. *Why weighted criteria.* They reflect priorities transparently.
4. *Matrix vs hunch.* Matrix exposes reasoning and invites review.
5. *Why time horizons.* Different strategies pay off at different times.
6. *Why adaptive plans.* Conditions and evidence change.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Household water.* Strategies: leak repair; rainwater tank; behaviour change. Criteria: cost; saving per dollar; ease.
2. *Hume heatwave.* Tree canopy; cool-roof rebates; cooling hubs. Matrix on temperature, equity, cost.
3. *Geelong transport.* Active transport; public transport; road. Criteria: equity, emissions, cost. Recommendation: mix biased to active and public.
4. *Cost-vs-equity debate.* For cost: feasibility. For equity: justice. Both sides accept multi-criterion design.
5. *Wurundjeri cultural-burning strategy.* Step 1: invitation and protocols. Step 2: co-design. Step 3: shared monitoring.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Issue: urban heat in Hume.* Strategies: tree canopy; cool roofs; cooling hubs. Criteria: temperature reduction; equity; cost; biodiversity. Recommendation: mix of canopy and cool roofs with hubs for short-term equity. Predicted impacts at 10 years: 1–2 °C cooling in target precincts; at 25 years: greater cooling and biodiversity gain. Uncertainty: tree survival under hotter climate. Sources cited.
