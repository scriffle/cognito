# VC2HG10S02 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Collect, process and compare information and data from primary and secondary sources, including fieldwork and secondary research materials, using geospatial technologies and digital tools as appropriate.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Combine fieldwork and desk research. Geospatial tools amplify judgement. Compare sources for pattern and contradiction.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **collect, process and compare** data from **primary and secondary sources** using **geospatial and digital tools**.
- You should be able to combine sources for stronger inquiry.

## Core content

**Primary**: data you collect — surveys, fieldwork, photographs, sketches, interviews, measurements. **Secondary**: data others have collected — ABS, BoM, journals, news, satellite archives.

**Fieldwork** requires planning (site, method, safety, ethics, equipment), collection and processing (tabulate, code, summarise). **Geospatial tools**: GIS (QGIS, ArcGIS, Google Earth Pro, Google My Maps); remote sensing (Sentinel, Landsat); GPS; online dashboards (BoM, EPA, ABS). **Digital tools**: spreadsheets, survey platforms, visualisation tools, Python/R.

**Processing**: tidy data (one row per observation); recode categories; calculate derived measures; document steps.

**Worked example.** Assessing a Werribee River reach. Primary: site walks; water-quality measurements; photo logs. Secondary: EPA data; council reports; satellite imagery. Processing: tidy field data; plot in QGIS over satellite imagery; cross-check with EPA records. Primary + secondary together reveal more than either alone.

**Comparing sources.** Triangulate; note contradictions and gaps; ask who collected the data, how, when and why.

**Cultural context.** Cultural mapping and **data sovereignty** are central; the Maiam nayri Wingara Indigenous Data Sovereignty Collective and AIATSIS publish guidance. Multilingual survey design strengthens fieldwork in diverse communities. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Combine primary and secondary.
- Geospatial tools amplify judgement.
- Tidy data; document steps.
- Triangulate sources.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Field data beats secondary." Each has strengths; together stronger.
- "GIS does the analysis." It supports it; you still need judgement.
- "Indigenous data is open." Community consent and protocols apply.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *BoM rainfall record type.* Secondary.
- *Why reconnaissance.* Spot hazards, refine method, confirm access.
- *Adding a map layer.* Reveals spatial relationships among variables.
- *Why document steps.* Reproducibility and trust.
- *Short summary of source comparison.* Combine primary and secondary, layer them in GIS, then triangulate to confirm patterns and explain disagreements.
