# VC2HG10K13 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The influence of people's environmental worldviews on their support for environmental sustainability.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Worldviews shape what counts as "nature". Worldviews shape policy. Different worldviews can work together.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **influence of environmental worldviews** on **support for sustainability**.
- You should be able to read debates through value frames.

## Core content

A **worldview** is a set of beliefs about humans, nature and their relationship. **Anthropocentric**: humans are central; nature is for human use. **Ecocentric**: nature has intrinsic value. **Biocentric**: all life has value. **Indigenous custodial**: humans are part of Country with responsibilities. **Stewardship**: humans manage for future generations.

Worldviews shape policy. Anthropocentric framings support development; ecocentric support protection; custodial support First Nations leadership; stewardship support balanced management. Clashes appear in debates over logging, mining vs sacred sites, hunting vs species protection, and climate policy.

**Worked example.** Djab Wurrung birthing trees (2018–2020): a Western Highway upgrade threatened sacred trees. Custodial, ecocentric and development worldviews collided; some trees were removed and others retained.

**Worked example.** Rights of Nature. The Yarra River Protection (Wilip-gin Birrarung murron) Act 2017 reflects Wurundjeri custodial thinking; New Zealand's Whanganui River has legal personhood.

Sustainable solutions often combine worldviews — custodial leadership, ecocentric science and stewardship policy together.

**Cultural context.** The First Peoples' Assembly and Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations are key voices. Migrant communities bring varied worldviews — Confucian, Christian, Islamic, Hindu, Buddhist and secular environmentalism. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Worldview shapes policy.
- "Natural resources" is a worldview.
- Custodial worldviews are central in Australia.
- Dialogue beats binaries.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Worldviews are just opinions." They have real policy consequences.
- "Indigenous worldviews are folklore." They are sophisticated frameworks.
- "Science is worldview-free." Methods are; framing is not.

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Same forest, different views.* People value forests differently — timber, habitat, culture.
- *"Natural resources" worldview.* Anthropocentric.
