# VC2HG10S05 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Consider ethical values and perspectives to justify conclusions related to a phenomenon, issue or challenge using geographical concepts.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Conclusions carry ethical weight. Different values produce different conclusions. Justification requires reasoning.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **consider ethical values and perspectives** to **justify conclusions** using **geographic concepts**.
- You should be able to defend conclusions with reasoning, not just data.

## Core content

**Ethical values in geography**: equity (fair share); sustainability (intergenerational fairness); autonomy (self-determination); dignity; stewardship; cultural integrity.

**Perspectives.** People bring different perspectives shaped by experience, culture, role and worldview. A Traditional Owner, a farmer, an irrigator, a tourist and a hydrologist may share evidence but interpret it differently.

**Justification** connects evidence to a claim through reasoning, names assumptions and acknowledges trade-offs. Frame justification through space, place, environment, interconnection, change, sustainability and scale.

**Worked example: rezoning a Melbourne suburb.** Evidence: population growth, housing demand, biodiversity, transport capacity. Values in tension: equity, sustainability, cultural integrity, autonomy. Conclusion: rezone with green-cover requirements, cultural-heritage protection and Wurundjeri consultation.

**Worked example: cultural-burning policy.** Evidence: ecological outcomes; Traditional Owner expertise; safety. Values: cultural integrity; safety; sustainability. Justification: support Aboriginal-led cultural burning with agency coordination.

Strong conclusions acknowledge views you do not share and explain disagreements.

**Cultural context.** Cultural integrity, custodial responsibility and self-determination are central ethical values; AIATSIS Code of Ethics and Yoorrook Justice Commission guide practice. Migrant and faith communities bring varied ethical traditions. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Values shape conclusions.
- Justify, don't assert.
- Use geographic concepts.
- Acknowledge other perspectives.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Justifying is just stating your view." It requires evidence and reasoning.
- "All perspectives are equally weighted." Some have more authority on specific questions.
- "Acknowledging others weakens you." It strengthens credibility.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why "data speaks" rarely true.* Framing and values shape interpretation.
- *Opinion vs justified conclusion.* The conclusion ties evidence and reasoning together.
- *Concept for Murray water.* Sustainability and interconnection.
- *Why fair-mindedness strengthens.* It pre-empts objections and shows reasoning quality.
- *Short summary of rezoning conclusion.* Recognise housing equity, environmental sustainability and cultural integrity; rezone with safeguards and consultation; acknowledge resident concerns.
