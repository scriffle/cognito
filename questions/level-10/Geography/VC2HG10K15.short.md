# VC2HG10K15 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Ways of measuring human wellbeing in places, and how these can be applied to measure differences between countries.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Wellbeing is more than income. Measures shape policy. Within-country differences can be large.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **ways of measuring human wellbeing** and how they show **differences between countries**.
- You should be able to read wellbeing critically.

## Core content

**Wellbeing** is the quality of life beyond income — health, education, safety, environment, voice, belonging, culture. **Single-indicator measures**: GDP per capita; life expectancy; literacy; infant mortality; access to safe water. **Composite measures**: **Human Development Index (HDI)** combines life expectancy, education and income; **Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)**; **Inequality-adjusted HDI**; **Gender Development Index**. **Newer measures**: OECD Better Life Index; Bhutan's Gross National Happiness; Australia's **Measuring What Matters** framework (2023); OECD How's Life?

**Worked example.** Australia vs Indonesia vs Bangladesh. HDI gaps are smaller than GDP gaps; MPI shows differences in health and education access; multiple measures give a fuller picture.

**Within-country differences.** In Australia, wellbeing varies by region, postcode and Indigenous status. Closing the Gap reports show ongoing inequalities in life expectancy, child mortality, education and incarceration. National averages hide group differences.

No measure is neutral — choosing what to count is a values choice. Cultural wellbeing is hard to quantify.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal-led wellbeing measures — Mayi Kuwayu study, NATSIHS — include cultural connection, language, kinship and connection to Country alongside Western indicators. ABS Multicultural Australia statistics show wellbeing gradients across migrant cohorts. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Wellbeing is more than income.
- Composite measures help.
- Within-country differences matter.
- Cultural wellbeing belongs in the picture.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Higher GDP always means better lives." Inequality and environment matter.
- "HDI is the truth." Many dimensions are missing.
- "Indigenous wellbeing is just a gap." It also has strengths — culture, kinship, identity, Country.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why high income isn't enough.* Health, environment, security, belonging and voice all matter.
- *What GDP per capita misses.* Distribution; non-market work; environment; subjective wellbeing.
- *Why HDI helps.* Combines three dimensions; less skewed by income.
- *Why national averages mislead.* They hide regional and group differences.
- *Short summary of country comparison.* Australia leads on HDI; Indonesia and Bangladesh are closer to Australia on health and education than on income; multiple measures give a fuller picture.
