# VC2HG10K12 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples' approaches to custodial responsibility and environmental management.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Custodial responsibility is a living practice. First Nations management is evidence-based and current. Country includes people, lands, waters and skies.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander approaches** to **custodial responsibility** and **environmental management**.
- You should be able to read management through First Nations frameworks alongside Western science.

## Core content

**Custodial responsibility** is an ongoing duty of care to **Country** given through Law, kinship and ceremony — not ownership in a market sense. **Country** includes lands, waters, skies, plants, animals, ancestors and people in relationship; it is plural.

**Caring for Country practices.** Low-intensity **cultural burning** to manage fuel, biodiversity and cultural sites; **seasonal harvesting**; **songlines and stories** that transmit ecological knowledge; **ranger programs**; **cultural flows** in rivers; **Sea Country** management. Many Australian parks are jointly managed; **Indigenous Protected Areas** cover the largest share of the conservation estate.

**Worked example.** Dja Dja Wurrung cultural burning in central Victoria, with the Dja Dja Wurrung Clans Aboriginal Corporation, Parks Victoria and CFA, in Greater Bendigo National Park. Outcomes: lower fuel loads, restored grassy woodlands, youth-led cultural transmission.

**Worked example.** Yolŋu Sea Country rangers in Arnhem Land manage marine debris, control crocodile populations and partner with CSIRO.

Environmental crises demand multiple knowledge systems. **Two-way knowledge** combines long-tested, place-specific First Nations knowledge with Western methods.

**Cultural context.** Dja Dja Wurrung, Yorta Yorta, Wadawurrung, Gunaikurnai, Bunurong/Boon Wurrung, Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung, Taungurung, Eastern Maar and Jardwadjali Peoples lead caring-for-Country work in Victoria. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Custody is a living duty.
- Country is plural.
- Cultural burning is current science.
- Two-way knowledge strengthens management.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Aboriginal management is in the past." It is current and expanding.
- "Cultural burning is one technique." It is a knowledge system with many forms.
- "Specific knowledge is for everyone to use." It belongs to communities.
