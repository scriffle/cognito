# VC2HG10K02 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The environmental, economic and technological factors that influence crop production in Australia and worldwide.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Crop production depends on a mix of conditions and choices. Australia is unusual in some ways and typical in others. Technology has reshaped productivity.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **environmental, economic and technological factors** that influence **crop production**.
- You should be able to read crop production as a system.

## Core content

**Environmental factors**: climate (rainfall, temperature, frost), soil quality, water availability, pests, topography. **Economic factors**: commodity prices, input costs, labour, subsidies, trade access, transport. **Technological factors**: mechanisation, irrigation, plant breeding (drought-resistant varieties), precision agriculture (GPS, drones, sensors), biotechnology.

Risks: climate change, water scarcity, soil degradation, market volatility, labour shortages. Sustainability programs include conservation tillage, integrated pest management, regenerative agriculture and Indigenous-led knowledge integration.

**Worked example.** Wheat in Australia. Distribution across the southern wheat-belt. Environmental: winter rainfall; variable seasons. Economic: global price; Asian export markets. Technological: precision agriculture; drought-resistant varieties from CSIRO. Climate change is shifting the wheat-belt.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal-led farming includes native-grass cultivation and bush-food cropping. Migrant farmers from Italy, Greece, India, Vietnam and China have introduced crops and techniques. Vietnamese-Australian growers in Melbourne's Werribee supply markets across the state. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Environment plus economics plus technology.
- Australia is variable; technology helps.
- Comparison reveals options.
- Sustainability is essential.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Yield is everything." Sustainability and quality also matter.
- "Technology solves all." It can also create new problems.
- "Indigenous farming is past tense." It is current and growing.
