# VC2HG10S07 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Explain and evaluate proposed actions, using geographical knowledge and concepts and appropriate methods, and incorporating and acknowledging research findings.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Explanation and evaluation are different jobs. Concepts give structure; methods give credibility. Sources must be acknowledged.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **explain and evaluate proposed actions** using **geographic concepts and methods**, while **acknowledging research findings**.
- You should be able to write clearly and credibly about choices.

## Core content

**Explain** describes how an action works and why. **Evaluate** judges how well it does against criteria. Both are needed.

Use **concepts** (space, place, environment, interconnection, change, sustainability, scale) as scaffolding. Match **methods** to questions — quantitative for measurable change, qualitative for meaning and experience, mixed for both.

**Acknowledge** sources in-text and in a reference list. Distinguish your reasoning from your sources. Don't plagiarise.

**Strong structure**: introduction; explanation (concept-led); evaluation (against named criteria, with evidence); recommendation or judgement; limitations; references.

**Worked example: Birrarung daylighting.** Action: re-opening a covered tributary. Explanation: flood capacity, biodiversity, urban cooling, cultural connection. Concepts: environment, change, interconnection, sustainability. Methods: hydrological modelling; ecological monitoring; Wurundjeri consultation. Evaluation: scores on environment, cost, equity, cultural integrity. Acknowledge Melbourne Water reports, peer-reviewed studies and Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation statements.

Use clear paragraphs, named criteria, signposted reasoning and the right register for the audience.

**Cultural context.** Acknowledge community sources properly; cite Aboriginal Corporations and authors; pay where appropriate. AIATSIS protocols and Maiam nayri Wingara principles apply. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Explain and evaluate.
- Concepts give structure.
- Methods give credibility.
- Cite sources properly.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Describe everything." Explanation is targeted to your question.
- "Score it without reasoning." Evaluation connects evidence to criteria.
- "Treat community input as raw material." It is authored content; credit and, where appropriate, pay.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why explanation alone falls short.* It tells what but not how well.
- *When qualitative strengthens.* When community experience matters as evidence.
- *Plagiarism in plain English.* Using someone else's words or ideas as your own.
- *Why limitations.* Honesty about what you cannot know strengthens trust.
- *Short summary of daylighting evaluation.* Explain function and concepts; evaluate against ecology, cost and cultural integrity with cited evidence; judge with monitoring; note long-term funding limits.
