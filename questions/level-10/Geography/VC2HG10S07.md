# VC2HG10S07 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10S07. Explain and evaluate proposed actions, using geographical knowledge and concepts and appropriate methods, and incorporating and acknowledging research findings.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Geographical inquiry — skills).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Explanation and evaluation are different jobs.
  - Concepts give structure; methods give credibility.
  - Sources must be acknowledged.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Literacy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Ethical Capability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **explain and evaluate proposed actions** using **geographical knowledge and concepts** and **appropriate methods**, while **acknowledging research findings**.
- You are learning to write clearly and credibly about choices.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **explain**, **evaluate** and **acknowledge** in your own words.
- Structure an explanation using geographic concepts.
- Evaluate against criteria with reasoning.
- Cite sources correctly.

## Core content

**1. Explain vs evaluate.** **Explain**: describe how an action works and why. **Evaluate**: judge how well it does against criteria. Both are needed. *Quick check:* Why is explanation alone not enough?

**2. Geographic concepts as scaffolding.** Use space, place, environment, interconnection, change, sustainability and scale to structure your writing. *Quick check:* Pick two concepts to scaffold an explanation of a wetland-restoration project.

**3. Methods.** Match method to question: quantitative for measurable change; qualitative for meaning and experience; mixed for both. Cite methods in your writing. *Quick check:* When does qualitative method strengthen an evaluation?

**4. Acknowledging research findings.** Cite sources in-text and in a reference list (Harvard, APA or the system your school uses). Distinguish your reasoning from your sources. Don't plagiarise. *Quick check:* What is plagiarism in plain English?

**5. Structure of a strong evaluation.** **Introduction**: the action and the question. **Explanation**: how and why it works, using concepts. **Evaluation**: against named criteria, with evidence. **Recommendation or judgement**. **Limitations**. **References**. *Quick check:* Why include limitations?

**6. Worked example: evaluating a Birrarung daylighting proposal.** Action: re-opening a covered section of Birrarung tributary. Explanation: how daylighting works — flood capacity, biodiversity, urban cooling, cultural connection. Concepts: environment, change, interconnection, sustainability. Methods: hydrological modelling; ecological monitoring; Wurundjeri consultation. Evaluation: scores on environment, cost, equity, cultural integrity. Acknowledgement: cite Melbourne Water reports, peer-reviewed studies and Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation statements. *Follow the thinking:* explanation → criteria → evaluation → judgement.

**7. Communicating well.** Use clear paragraphs, named criteria, signposted reasoning and the right register for the audience. Visuals help. *Quick check:* Why does audience shape your writing register?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Department of Education writing standards; Victorian Auditor-General reports as models of evaluation; Plan Melbourne strategies.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Acknowledge community sources properly; cite Aboriginal Corporations and authors; pay where appropriate. The AIATSIS protocols and Maiam nayri Wingara principles apply. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Where evaluations rely on community input, cite multicultural service organisations and named representatives.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You support a local rain-garden program. Write a short explanation and one-criterion evaluation.
2. **Civic.** Your council in Banyule evaluates a green-bridge proposal. Outline three concepts and three criteria.
3. **Vocational.** A graduate at Melbourne Water briefs the executive on a wetland. Outline three sections.
4. **Ethical.** A class debates how to cite Aboriginal community input fairly. Identify two arguments on each side.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A school evaluation includes Bunurong oral submissions. Outline three respectful steps.

## Stretch yourself
Pick one Victorian environmental or social proposal. Explain it using at least three geographic concepts. Evaluate it against three criteria with cited evidence. Make a judgement and note limitations. Acknowledge Aboriginal-led perspectives where relevant. 400 words. Reference list included.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Describe everything." Explanation is targeted to your question.
- "Score it without reasoning." Evaluation requires connection of evidence to criteria.
- "Skip citations." Acknowledge all sources.
- "Treat community input as raw material." It is authored content; credit and, where appropriate, pay.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why explanation alone falls short.* It tells what but not how well.
2. *Concepts for wetland restoration.* Environment and change.
3. *When qualitative method strengthens.* When community experience matters as evidence.
4. *Plagiarism in plain English.* Using someone else's words or ideas as your own.
5. *Why limitations.* Honesty about what you cannot know strengthens trust.
6. *Why audience matters for register.* Policymakers want concise; classrooms want clarity; community wants accessibility.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Rain-garden program.* Explain how it slows stormwater and supports biodiversity; evaluate against the criterion "litres slowed per dollar".
2. *Banyule green bridge.* Concepts: interconnection, place, environment. Criteria: connectivity, biodiversity, cost.
3. *Melbourne Water briefing.* Explanation; evaluation; recommendation.
4. *Citing Aboriginal community input debate.* For: named authorship. Against: privacy and protocol; both sides accept community-set rules.
5. *Bunurong oral submissions.* Step 1: consent for use. Step 2: agreed wording. Step 3: review and acknowledgement.
