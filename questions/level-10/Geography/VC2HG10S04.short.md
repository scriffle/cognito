# VC2HG10S04 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Interpret and evaluate information and data to make generalisations and predictions, analyse patterns and trends, infer relationships and make forecasts.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Patterns are not proof. Correlation is not causation. Forecasts are bets under uncertainty.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **interpret and evaluate** data to make **generalisations, predictions, inferences and forecasts**.
- You should be able to reason carefully from evidence.

## Core content

**Patterns at scale.** Local, regional, national, global — patterns at one scale can hide or contradict another. A **trend** is the long-term direction; **variability** is short-term fluctuation. Both matter.

**Correlation vs causation.** Correlation means variables change together; causation means one drives the other. Confounders, reverse causation and coincidence can mimic causation.

**Inference**. Strong inferences combine observation, model, theory and expert judgement; name assumptions and uncertainty.

**Forecasts** estimate a future state with uncertainty. Use **scenarios** (low/medium/high), **confidence intervals** and clear **time horizons**.

**Worked example.** Heatwaves in Melbourne. Trend: average summer temperatures rising. Variability: individual summers vary. Correlation: heatwaves and mortality, especially among older adults. Causation: heat stresses bodies. Forecast: more days above 35 °C by 2050 (medium emissions).

Be honest about uncertainty; disaggregate by group so vulnerable populations are not hidden in averages.

**Cultural context.** First Nations long-term observation of seasons contributes to local forecasting; cultural calendars (Gariwerd six-season; D'harawal) reflect deep climate knowledge. Multilingual risk communication matters in heatwaves. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Trend vs variability.
- Correlation is not causation.
- Forecasts are ranges.
- Disaggregate; do not hide groups.

## Watch out for these traps
- "One year proves a trend." It doesn't.
- "Forecasts are predictions of fact." They are conditional.
- "Averages tell the whole story." Disaggregate.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Town heat trend vs global.* Local effects can mask or amplify global trend.
- *Ice-cream and drowning.* Both rise with summer; season is the confounder.
- *Why climate forecasts are ranges.* Emissions paths and model differences create uncertainty.
- *Why disaggregate for heat mortality.* Older adults and outdoor workers face higher risk.
- *Short summary of Melbourne heat inference.* Trend rising, variability high; cause is climate change; forecast is more 35 °C+ days by 2050 under medium emissions.
