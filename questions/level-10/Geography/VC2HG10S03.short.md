# VC2HG10S03 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Represent and analyse information and data using a range of formats, including graphs and maps constructed with geospatial technologies.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Representation reveals or hides patterns. Format depends on data and audience. Maps are arguments.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **represent and analyse** data using **graphs and maps** built with **geospatial tools**.
- You should be able to choose formats that match your data.

## Core content

**Match chart to data.** Bar/column: categories. Line: change over time. Scatter: relationships between variables. Pie: parts of a whole (sparingly). Histogram: distribution. Box plot: spread and outliers.

**Thematic maps.** **Choropleth** (filled regions for rates); **dot/density** (counts); **proportional symbol** (magnitudes); **flow** (movement); **isoline** (continuous variables). Every map needs **title, legend, scale, north arrow, source, date** and a chosen **projection** (Web Mercator distorts area). Use accessible colour palettes.

**Tools**: QGIS, ArcGIS Online, Google Earth Pro, Google My Maps, Leaflet, Datawrapper.

**Worked example.** SEIFA in Greater Melbourne. ABS SEIFA as choropleth at LGA level; class by deciles; accessible colour ramp; cite source. Add transport-network layer to support interconnection analysis.

**Analysis tools**: summary statistics; trend lines; correlation; spatial autocorrelation; before-and-after comparisons.

Read maps critically: who made it, when, what is in and out, what classification, what projection?

**Cultural context.** The AIATSIS map of Aboriginal Australia (Horton 1996) shows language groups; Native Title and Cultural Heritage maps are core legal documents. ABS multicultural-data dashboards show language and birthplace patterns across Victoria. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Format matches data.
- Maps are arguments.
- Legend, scale, source matter.
- Class breaks change the story.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Pie charts are always fine." Often hard to read.
- "Maps are objective." Choices shape what they say.
- "More colours mean more information." Often the opposite.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Rainfall over 50 years.* Line chart with trend line.
- *Rates vs counts.* Counts show where many people live; rates show intensity.
- *Why class breaks matter.* Different breaks shift conclusions.
- *Trend-line function.* Summarises direction and magnitude of change.
- *Short summary of map design.* Match the format to the data, label every element, choose accessible colour, and disclose source and date.
