# VC2HG10K07 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The impacts of interconnections through trade, including trade in embodied carbon, on the sustainability of countries.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Trade carries hidden environmental cost. Embodied carbon moves with goods. Sustainability needs system-level accounting.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **sustainability impacts** of **trade**, including **embodied carbon**.
- You should be able to read trade as a sustainability issue.

## Core content

**Embodied carbon** is the emissions used to make and transport a product. When goods are imported, the emissions occurred elsewhere but consumption is here. Most national emissions are reported on a **production basis**; **consumption-based accounting** reallocates them to where goods are used. Australia's consumption footprint is higher than its production footprint for some categories.

**Sustainability impacts of trade**: resource-extraction stress; biodiversity loss in source countries; pollution offshoring; transport emissions; spread of invasive species. Positive flows: green-tech transfer; renewable-energy investment; circular-economy initiatives. **Embodied water and land** also travel with goods — a kilogram of beef carries thousands of litres of water.

**Worked example.** Lithium mined in Australia → cells made in China → batteries shipped back for electric vehicles. Each step adds embodied carbon. Australia's consumption-based footprint thus includes Chinese manufacturing emissions.

**Policy responses.** Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (EU CBAM, fully phased in from 2026); supply-chain reporting (Modern Slavery Act and proposed climate-disclosure rules); circular-economy strategies; product-level carbon labelling.

Australia exports iron ore, coal, gas and lithium. Coal and gas exports add to global emissions even when not counted in Australian emissions.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal communities are affected by mining, climate change and trade decisions on Country; the First Nations Clean Energy Network publishes on these issues. Victorian diaspora communities link advocacy to climate-vulnerable Pacific and Asian neighbours. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Embodied carbon travels with goods.
- Production vs consumption accounting matters.
- Trade can hurt and help sustainability.
- Australia's exports complicate its emissions story.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Emissions only count where made." Consumption matters too.
- "Buying local is always greener." Sometimes; check transport, energy mix and method.
- "Trade is bad for sustainability." It also moves clean tech and finance.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Where steel-making emissions count.* Production-based: in China. Consumption-based: in Australia.
- *Why accounting matters for fairness.* Consuming countries can appear cleaner than they are.
- *What CBAM does.* Charges imports for carbon content so domestic producers are not undercut.
- *Coal-export complication.* Emissions burnt overseas are large but not counted as Australia's.
- *Short summary of lithium-battery flow.* Lithium leaves Australia, returns as cells then batteries; each step adds embodied carbon counted across two countries.
