# VC2HG10K14 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG10K14. Geographical approaches to the management of a selected environmental issue, including how environment, change, interconnection and sustainability can be considered to understand environmental issues.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Geography (Environmental change and management).
- **Big ideas:**
  - The geographic concepts are a working toolkit.
  - Management means action, not just analysis.
  - Place-specific responses outperform one-size-fits-all.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Sustainability; Critical and Creative Thinking; Digital Literacy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **geographical approaches** to the **management** of an environmental issue, using **environment**, **change**, **interconnection** and **sustainability**.
- You are learning to design responses, not just describe problems.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **environmental management**, **adaptive management** and **co-management** in your own words.
- Apply environment, change, interconnection and sustainability to one issue.
- Describe management options at multiple scales.
- Discuss trade-offs in real decisions.

## Core content

**1. The four working concepts.** **Environment**: physical and biological systems. **Change**: trends over time. **Interconnection**: links across places and systems. **Sustainability**: meeting present needs without limiting future ones. *Quick check:* Why does using all four together produce stronger analysis?

**2. Management approaches.** **Adaptive management** treats decisions as experiments and updates them with monitoring. **Co-management** shares authority across agencies and Traditional Owners. **Integrated management** combines water, land and biota in one plan. **Precautionary management** acts before harm becomes severe. *Quick check:* Why is adaptive management suited to climate uncertainty?

**3. Tools.** Regulation, market instruments (carbon pricing), zoning, environmental flows, ranger programs, certification, behaviour change, technology investment, restoration. *Quick check:* What is one strength of market instruments and one weakness?

**4. Worked example: managing Port Phillip Bay.** **Environment**: bay ecology, including seagrass, fish, dolphins. **Change**: pollution rising, climate warming, marine heatwaves. **Interconnection**: stormwater catchments, shipping, tourism, fisheries. **Sustainability**: keep ecosystem functions while supporting livelihoods. **Management**: Port Phillip Bay Environmental Management Plan; Marine and Coastal Act 2018; catchment management authorities; Bunurong and Wadawurrung Sea Country leadership. *Follow the thinking:* concepts → plan → action → monitor.

**5. Worked example: managing the Murray–Darling.** **Environment**: river, wetlands, floodplains. **Change**: drought, salinity, climate change. **Interconnection**: four states plus the ACT; upstream–downstream; cultural flows. **Sustainability**: Sustainable Diversion Limits. **Management**: Basin Plan; Aboriginal water entitlements; environmental water deliveries. *Quick check:* Why is interconnection central to Murray management?

**6. Trade-offs and conflict.** Management often involves choices between users — irrigators, ecological needs, cultural needs, recreation. Transparent processes and Traditional Owner authority help. *Quick check:* Why are written plans not enough on their own?

**7. Communicating management.** Use maps, plans, dashboards, and clear language. Acknowledge uncertainty. Cite reputable sources. *Quick check:* Why do dashboards help adaptive management?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Marine and Coastal Act 2018; Catchment Management Authorities; Parks Victoria; Murray–Darling Basin Authority; EPA Victoria.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Traditional Owners participate in joint management, cultural flows and Sea Country planning. The Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations supports this work. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual community engagement strengthens participation in plans. Migrant farming, fishing and recreation communities are stakeholders.

## Stretch yourself
Pick one Victorian environmental issue. Apply all four concepts. Propose three management responses including one Aboriginal-led approach. Discuss one trade-off and how it could be resolved. Cite reputable sources. 300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Pick one concept." Strong analysis uses several.
- "Plans solve everything." Implementation, monitoring and review matter.
- "Co-management is a paperwork exercise." Done well it changes power and outcomes.
- "Trade-offs can always be avoided." Sometimes they cannot, but they can be made transparently.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why use all four concepts.* Each highlights different drivers and responses; together they reveal the system.
2. *Adaptive management and uncertainty.* Allows decisions to update as conditions change rather than locking in a single answer.
3. *Market instruments strength/weakness.* Strength: efficient signal. Weakness: requires good information and fair design.
4. *Murray and interconnection.* Decisions in one state affect others; cultural and ecological flows cross the system.
5. *Why plans aren't enough.* People, funding and politics implement them.
6. *Why dashboards help.* Up-to-date data supports rapid management response.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Beach clean-up lens.* Environment (litter sources); change (microplastic trend); interconnection (catchment to bay); sustainability (reducing waste).
2. *Werribee River.* Stormwater treatment; revegetation; Wadawurrung cultural-flow advocacy.
3. *Port Phillip Bay brief.* Pressures and trends; current plan and gaps; recommended actions.
4. *Water price debate.* For: efficient allocation. Against: equity and food security. Both sides accept caps for environmental water.
5. *Wadawurrung cultural-flow consultation.* Step 1: Wadawurrung Traditional Owners Aboriginal Corporation contact. Step 2: agreed protocols. Step 3: long-term governance role.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Issue: Werribee River.* Environment: lower-river ecology. Change: stormwater nutrients, drought. Interconnection: catchment, port, market gardening. Sustainability: maintain ecological flows. Responses: catchment-management investment; Wadawurrung cultural-flow advocacy; market-garden runoff standards. Trade-off: irrigation entitlements vs ecological needs — resolve through transparent flow rules. Sources cited.
