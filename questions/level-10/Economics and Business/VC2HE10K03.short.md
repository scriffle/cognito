# VC2HE10K03 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Economic indicators as a measurement of economic performance and their influence on economic decision-making.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** No single number is the economy. Different indicators answer different questions. Indicators are snapshots with limits.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about **economic indicators** as **measurements of performance** and their **influence on decisions**.
- You should be able to read indicators with care.

## Core content

**GDP** measures output; growth in real GDP signals expansion. **CPI** measures consumer prices; **inflation** is the change in CPI. The RBA targets 2–3% inflation on average. **Unemployment** counts those without work seeking work; **underemployment** counts those wanting more hours. **Wage-price index** tracks wages.

Households, businesses, governments and the RBA all watch indicators. Different decisions weigh different indicators. Indicators are revised; some lag (employment), some lead (consumer confidence); they miss unpaid work, environmental impact and distribution.

**Worked example.** A Year 10 student considering career options uses: GDP-growth trends; sector forecasts (Jobs and Skills Australia); CPI for cost-of-living planning; mortgage-rate expectations for housing decisions. Indicators inform; personal context matters.

**Cultural context.** Closing the Gap targets include economic-participation indicators alongside health and education. Multilingual ABS communication supports decision-making across Melbourne's diverse communities. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- No single number is the economy.
- GDP, CPI, unemployment together tell more.
- Indicators are snapshots, with limits.
- Honest analysis names what is missed.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Higher GDP = better life." Distribution and environment matter.
- "Low unemployment = full employment." Underemployment may still be high.
- "Indicators are precise." They are estimates with revisions.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *GDP up, per-person GDP down.* Population grew faster than GDP; output per person fell.
- *Why does unemployment alone mislead?* It misses underemployment and discouraged workers.
- *Indicator a small-business owner watches.* Consumer confidence; sector demand; mortgage rates.
- *Unpaid activity GDP misses.* Caring at home; volunteering; informal community work.
- *Short summary of using indicators for a career choice.* Combine GDP, sector forecasts, CPI and rates; state limits; combine with personal context before deciding.
