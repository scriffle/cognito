# VC2HE10S02 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Locate, select, organise and analyse relevant information and data from a range of sources.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Sources are graded, not equal. Organisation makes analysis possible. Quant plus qual together.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **locate, select, organise and analyse** information and data from a range of sources.
- You should be able to build a working evidence base.

## Core content

Sources include ABS, RBA, ATO, Productivity Commission, Treasury, Auditor-General, industry bodies, NGOs, peer-reviewed articles, news and community-language media. Use five criteria: authority, currency, method, independence, verifiability. Organise data in a spreadsheet with consistent columns (date, value, units, source, notes), one observation per row, raw and cleaned versions saved separately.

**Triangulation** uses multiple independent sources on the same point. Strong analysis combines quantitative (numbers, charts) with qualitative (narrative). Cite by author, year and where accessed.

**Worked example.** Investigating Victorian retail trade, 2019–2024. Sources: ABS Retail Trade (primary); RBA commentary (secondary); peer-reviewed article on COVID-19 consumer behaviour; a Victorian industry report. Organise monthly data; calculate year-on-year changes; visualise as a line graph; combine with narrative on behaviour change.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS ethics guidelines apply to research with First Nations communities. Data from IBA, Supply Nation and the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria provide primary evidence. ABS Census data by birthplace complements multilingual community-media reports. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Start with primary sources.
- Score on five criteria.
- One row, one observation.
- Quant plus qual together.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Numbers do not lie." They can mislead through framing, sampling and scale.
- "Anonymous = reliable." Anonymity removes accountability.
- "Recent is always best." Older primary data still matters.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Primary before news.* Closer to source; news interprets.
- *Most-forgotten criterion.* Methodology.
- *Raw vs cleaned data.* Cleaning errors can be reversed; lost raw data cannot.
- *What charts cannot show alone.* Reasons people behave the way they do.
- *Short summary of the retail-trade analysis.* Primary ABS data plus secondary commentary; monthly chart; combined narrative on the pandemic period and recovery.
