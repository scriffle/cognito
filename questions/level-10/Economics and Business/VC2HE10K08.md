## Core content

**1. Risk and reward.** **Risk** is the chance of loss or worse-than-expected outcome. **Reward** is the gain you accept the risk to chase. Higher-reward options usually come with higher risk. *Quick check:* Why is "guaranteed high returns" a red flag?

**2. Types of risk.** Market risk (asset prices fall); credit risk (a borrower fails to pay); inflation risk (savings lose buying power); liquidity risk (cannot access money quickly); fraud and scam risk; insurance risk. *Quick check:* Which risk affects cash sitting in a bank account over decades?

**3. Diversification.** **Diversification** spreads risk by holding different assets and across different conditions. It does not eliminate risk; it reduces concentration. *Quick check:* Why is "all in one stock" risky?

**4. Worked example: a young investor.** A 17-year-old earns from a casual job. Goals: emergency fund $1,000; medium-term savings $5,000 for a car; longer-term investment for adulthood. Plan: high-interest savings for the emergency fund; a low-cost diversified index fund for longer-term goals; consider superannuation salary-sacrifice once working full-time. Insurance: think about health, contents and (eventually) income protection. *Follow the thinking:* identify goals → match products → review yearly.

**5. Insurance basics.** **Insurance** spreads risk across many policy-holders. Common types: health, contents, car, life, income protection. Premiums depend on risk profile. Avoid both under-insurance and over-insurance. *Quick check:* What is the difference between "insured" and "guaranteed"?

**6. Scams and bad products.** Scams exploit urgency, fear and trust. Bad products charge high fees for low value. Check ASIC's Moneysmart and the Financial Ombudsman lists; verify licensing through ASIC's professional register. *Quick check:* Why is verifying licensing the first step?

**7. Legal and ethical implications.** Legal: disclose conflicts of interest; provide accurate product information; meet the ACL and financial-services law. Ethical: do not push products beyond a client's need; avoid greenwashing; respect community contexts. *Quick check:* Why is "legal but unethical" a real category in finance?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** ASIC Moneysmart, Consumer Affairs Victoria and Financial Counsellors of Australia are key resources. The Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) resolves disputes.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** The Indigenous Consumer Assistance Network (ICAN) supports culturally appropriate financial advice. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual financial-counselling services, Vietnamese, Mandarin and Arabic-language resources, and faith-based finance considerations (e.g., shariah-compliant finance) support diverse Melbourne communities.

## Watch out for these traps
- "High returns are normal." High returns over time usually mean high risk.
- "Insurance is a waste if I do not claim." It is risk-sharing; not claiming is good news.
- "I do not need to read product disclosure." That is where the real terms live.
- "Legal = ethical." Some products are legal but exploitative.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *"Guaranteed high returns".* No legitimate product can guarantee high returns; the claim is a scam signal.
2. *Cash and inflation.* Inflation risk; buying power falls over decades even if the nominal balance is stable.
3. *Why "all in one stock" is risky.* A single company can fail or fall; concentration multiplies the loss.
4. *Insured vs guaranteed.* Insurance pays under defined events; a guarantee promises a specific result.
5. *Why verify licensing first.* Many scams claim false licences; ASIC's professional register confirms genuine providers.
6. *"Legal but unethical".* Pushing high-fee products to clients who do not need them; complex products to non-experts.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *$1,000 inheritance.* $500 emergency fund (low risk); $300 to a diversified index fund (long-term medium risk); $200 short-term goal (low risk).
2. *Frankston financial-literacy week.* Scams and security; basic investing; insurance basics.
3. *Junior financial-counsellor first meeting.* Listen; map current income and expenses; agree one immediate action and one longer plan.
4. *Young people and volatile assets debate.* For: learning and small stakes. Against: addictive features and losses. Both sides agree on limits and education.
5. *Vietnamese-Australian household.* Track remittance as a fixed expense; build an emergency fund; choose insurance that fits family obligations.
