## Core content

**1. What super is.** **Superannuation (super)** is a long-term savings system designed to fund retirement. Money is held in a super fund and invested. You can usually access it at retirement age (currently 60+ for most). *Quick check:* Why is super "forced" saving?

**2. Who contributes.** Employers pay **Superannuation Guarantee (SG)** contributions: a set percentage of ordinary-time earnings (12% from 1 July 2025). Employees can add **voluntary contributions** (after-tax) or salary-sacrifice (before-tax). The government adds co-contributions for low-income earners. *Quick check:* Who is the largest single contributor for most Australian workers?

**3. How money grows: compound returns.** Super is invested in shares, bonds, property and other assets. Returns are reinvested. Over decades, **compound growth** turns small contributions into large balances. *Quick check:* Why do "small contributions in your teens" matter more than the same contributions in your 50s?

**4. Worked example: a 17-year-old Australian worker.** Casual job: $300/week for 20 hours. Employer pays 12% super: $36/week → ~$1,870/year. Invested in a low-cost diversified fund, assuming long-term real returns, this grows substantially by age 65. Choices: choose a low-fee fund; consolidate accounts; check insurance arrangements; track via myGov. *Follow the thinking:* current contribution → expected growth → impact of fees → action.

**5. Fees and consolidation.** Multiple super accounts mean multiple fees and possibly duplicate insurance. **Consolidating** into one account reduces costs. Low-fee funds (e.g., default MySuper options) preserve more of your returns. *Quick check:* What is one consequence of multiple super accounts over a working life?

**6. Why super matters to Australian living standards.** Super reduces reliance on the age pension; provides capital for Australian businesses and infrastructure; supports retirement living. Total super assets in Australia exceed $3 trillion. *Quick check:* Why does super reduce future-Commonwealth budget pressure?

**7. Limits and ethics.** Super is not perfect: lower-paid and part-time workers (often women, carers) end up with less; balances depend on employment continuity. Ethics: investment choices can support sustainable or harmful activities; many funds now offer ethical options. *Quick check:* Why is "everyone benefits equally from super" an oversimplification?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Major Victorian super funds include Aware Super (operating in Victoria) and many industry funds. ASIC Moneysmart and the ATO have specific super guidance for Victorians and all Australians.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander workers often face super gaps due to interrupted employment. Programs like the Indigenous Superannuation Working Group and ICAN provide culturally appropriate support. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant workers may have super in multiple countries; some can transfer balances under tax-treaty arrangements. Multilingual ATO and ASIC resources support diverse communities.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Super is for old people, not me." Early years matter most due to compounding.
- "All super funds are the same." Fees, returns and options vary.
- "Super is locked away forever." It is accessible at preservation age, usually 60.
- "Salary sacrifice always saves tax." For some incomes yes; check your bracket and limits.

1. *Why super is "forced" saving.* The SG requires employers to contribute on top of wages; workers cannot opt out of the system.
