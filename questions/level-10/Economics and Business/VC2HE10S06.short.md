# VC2HE10S06 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Evaluate sources of data and information to determine authenticity and validity.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Authenticity asks "real?"; validity asks "measures what it claims?". Both matter for trust.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **evaluate sources** for **authenticity** and **validity**.
- You should be able to grade sources before using them.

## Core content

**Authenticity** is whether the source is really what it claims to be (real author, organisation, date). **Validity** is whether it measures what it claims to measure. Five criteria: authority, currency, method, independence, verifiability. Methodology targets validity most directly. AI-generated content can be polished but may lack authenticity or validity; verify claims through real sources.

**Worked example.** Assessing "Australians can't afford rent". Source A: real-estate-agent blog (authentic but biased and small sample). Source B: ABS rental-data release (authentic and valid for general trends). Source C: peer-reviewed paper on housing affordability (authentic and valid for analysis). Source D: anonymous social-media post with chart (fails authenticity). Use B and C as primary; A and D with caveats.

**Cultural context.** Authentic First Nations voices come through published, community-approved channels (NITV, IndigenousX, AIATSIS, First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria). Multilingual community media provide authentic community voices; validity checks still apply. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Authenticity: who really wrote this?
- Validity: does it measure what it claims?
- Document your evaluation briefly.
- Limits exist; name them.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Real-looking = authentic." Fakes can look real.
- "Authentic = valid." A real survey can still be poorly designed.
- "Anonymous = unbiased." Anonymity removes accountability.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *URL and authenticity.* Domain spelling and https; impersonation often shows up in URL.
- *Authentic but invalid.* A genuine survey with tiny sample or leading questions.
- *Criterion targeting validity.* Methodology.
- *Why is "looks professional" not enough?* Design is cheap; method and provenance are what matter.
- *Short summary of evaluating the rent claim.* ABS rental data and a peer-reviewed paper carry most weight; real-estate-blog and anonymous post are limited by bias and authenticity issues respectively.
