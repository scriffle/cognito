# VC2HE10S04 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Draw logical conclusions based on data and information from verified sources.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Conclusions follow from evidence. Logic bridges data and claim. Honest analysis names limits.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **draw logical conclusions** from **verified sources**.
- You should be able to make conclusions that earn belief.

## Core content

A **conclusion** is the claim your evidence and reasoning support. **Inference** draws the most plausible explanation; **assumptions** are checked before the chain is trusted. Verified sources (authority, currency, method, independence, verifiability) are the base. Honest conclusions name **alternative explanations** and **limits**, and the evidence that would change the view.

**Worked example.** Youth wage growth. Data: ABS wage-price index for 15–24 age group, 2019–2024; ABS CPI for the same period. Trend: wages grew slower than CPI in 2022–23. Conclusion: real youth wages fell over part of the period, with sector and sample limits. Reasoning: nominal − inflation = real change. Alternatives: industry mix change, hours worked.

**Cultural context.** Conclusions about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander outcomes should respect community context and avoid deficit framing. Community-level conclusions should engage with first-hand voices from multilingual community media. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Conclusion = supported claim.
- Logic bridges data and claim.
- Name limits and alternatives.
- "What would change my view" earns trust.

## Watch out for these traps
- "The data prove." Data support; conclusions need reasoning.
- "Naming limits weakens conclusions." It strengthens trust.
- "Alternative explanations are distractions." They are part of honest reasoning.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Assumption in "wages rose, workers better off".* Assumes wages outpaced inflation and that distribution did not worsen.
- *Why "I saw it on a forum" is poor.* Unverified authorship, method and currency.
- *Why "and that is why X" is too quick.* It implies causation without checking mechanism.
- *Why does "what would change my view" earn trust?* Shows openness to evidence.
- *Short summary of the youth-wages conclusion.* Real wages fell for part of 2022–23 in some sectors; conclusion holds with stated limits and named alternative explanations.
