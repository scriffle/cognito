## Core content

**1. What Australia trades.** Australia is a major exporter of iron ore, coal, natural gas, agricultural products (wheat, beef, wine), education and tourism, plus services. Imports include manufactured goods, vehicles, refined oil and pharmaceuticals. *Quick check:* Why are services like education counted as exports?

**2. Who Australia trades with.** China is Australia's largest trade partner, followed by Japan, the United States, South Korea and India. The pattern has shifted over decades from the UK and Europe to Asia. *Quick check:* Why has the shift toward Asia been so significant?

**3. Trade agreements.** Australia is part of multilateral agreements (WTO) and regional ones (RCEP, CPTPP, ASEAN-Australia–New Zealand FTA). Bilateral agreements (with China, Japan, South Korea, India, Indonesia, UK) shape access and rules. *Quick check:* What is one benefit of a trade agreement to Australian exporters?

**4. Worked example: China and Australian iron ore.** China imports large quantities of Australian iron ore for steel-making. Benefits: high export revenue, jobs in Pilbara region, government tax revenue. Costs: concentration risk (vulnerability to one buyer), exposure to demand swings, environmental impacts, debate about value-add in Australia. *Follow the thinking:* identify partner → identify good → list benefits and costs → discuss alternative paths.

**5. Costs of globalisation.** Job displacement in import-affected sectors; supply-chain vulnerability (COVID-19 showed this); cultural and environmental impacts; inequality between regions and groups. Honest analysis names these. *Quick check:* What is one Australian example of supply-chain vulnerability?

**6. Benefits of globalisation.** Cheaper consumer goods; access to capital and technology; export-driven jobs; cultural exchange; competition that drives innovation. Reform debates focus on managing transition. *Quick check:* What is one benefit of globalisation visible in your daily life?

**7. Changing patterns.** Trade patterns shift with development, technology, demographics, conflict and policy. Australia's shift to Asia is a generational change. Climate transition will reshape resource exports. *Quick check:* Why might lithium become a growing Australian export?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Victoria's exports include education (a major contributor), agriculture and dairy, manufactured goods and services. The Port of Melbourne is Australia's largest container port.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander businesses participate in international trade through agriculture, tourism, art and other sectors. Supply Nation lists Aboriginal exporters. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant communities in Melbourne maintain trade and personal links with home countries (India, Vietnam, China, Lebanon, Italy, Greece) shaping diaspora-driven business and cultural trade.

## Stretch yourself

## Watch out for these traps
- "Free trade benefits everyone." Aggregate benefits do not guarantee individual gains.
- "Concentration is always bad." Specialisation has benefits; diversification has costs.
- "Imports cost local jobs." Some, but they also lower costs and enable other jobs.
- "Globalisation is over." Patterns shift; reach continues.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Education as export.* International students bring revenue to Australian institutions and households, counted as a service export.
2. *Shift toward Asia.* Faster Asian growth and proximity made Asia the major source of demand for Australia's exports.
3. *Trade-agreement benefit.* Lower tariffs and clearer rules; predictable access.
4. *Supply-chain vulnerability.* COVID-19 disruption in semiconductors; pharmaceutical-supply concerns; floods affecting freight.
5. *Globalisation in daily life.* Wide product choice; lower prices on many goods; cultural exchange through food, music and study.
6. *Lithium as export.* Battery demand for the energy transition drives growth.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Imported clothing.* Cotton grown overseas; manufactured offshore; shipped to Australia and sold locally. Impact: lower retail prices; reduced local manufacturing employment.
2. *Greater Geelong export sectors.* Higher education; advanced manufacturing; agricultural and food products.
3. *India brief.* Growing middle class; diaspora ties; agricultural and education opportunities; political and regulatory complexity.
4. *Onshoring manufacturing.* For: resilience and jobs. Against: higher costs; viability of scale. Both sides agree targeted onshoring matters.
5. *Vietnam exports.* Benefits: language and cultural ties; growing market; community-based networks. Risks: regulatory shifts; logistics; currency.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Export: Victorian dairy.* Supply chain: farms → processors → exporters → overseas customers. Benefits: scale, brand reputation. Costs: market dependence, environmental impact, climate risk. Change: rising Asian middle-class demand and climate-adaptation in production. Sources cited.
