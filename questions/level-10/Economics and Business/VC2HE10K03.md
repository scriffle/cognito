## Core content

**1. Indicators tell us something specific.** No single number captures "the economy". **GDP** measures output. **CPI** measures consumer prices. The **unemployment rate** measures the share of the labour force without work seeking work. The **wage-price index** tracks wages. Each answers a different question. *Quick check:* Why is "the economy is good" too vague?

**2. GDP and growth.** **GDP** is the value of goods and services produced in a country in a period. Growth in real GDP usually signals economic expansion; falls signal slowdown. Per-capita GDP adjusts for population. *Quick check:* Why might GDP rise but per-person GDP fall?

**3. CPI and inflation.** **CPI** is a weighted basket of consumer prices. **Inflation** is the change in CPI over time. The RBA targets 2–3% inflation on average. Low and stable inflation supports planning. *Quick check:* Why does the inflation target matter for borrowers and savers?

**4. Unemployment and underemployment.** **Unemployment** counts those without work seeking work. **Underemployment** counts those working but wanting more hours. Together they show labour-market slack. *Quick check:* Why is unemployment alone misleading?

**5. Worked example: choosing a career path with indicators in mind.** A Year 10 student considers: GDP-growth trends; sector employment forecasts (Jobs and Skills Australia); CPI for cost-of-living planning; mortgage-rate expectations for housing decisions. Indicators help, but personal context matters. *Follow the thinking:* gather indicators → understand each → consider context → decide with limits stated.

**6. Influence on decisions.** Households (spending, saving), businesses (hiring, investing), governments (policy), the RBA (rate setting) all watch indicators. Different decisions weigh different indicators. *Quick check:* Which indicator does a small-business owner watch most closely?

**7. Limits.** Indicators are revised. Some lag (employment); some lead (consumer confidence). They miss unpaid work, environmental impact and distribution. Honest analysis names limits. *Quick check:* What is one unpaid activity GDP misses?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** ABS publishes Victorian indicators monthly (labour force, retail trade, CPI). The Reserve Bank of Australia and Department of Treasury and Finance interpret them in regular updates.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Closing the Gap targets include economic-participation indicators alongside health and education. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual ABS communication supports informed decision-making across Melbourne's diverse communities; community language council reports interpret indicators in cultural context.

## Memorable takeaways
- No single number is the economy.
- GDP, CPI, unemployment together tell more.
- Indicators are snapshots, with limits.
- Decisions weigh indicators differently.
- Honest analysis names what is missed.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Higher GDP = better life." Often, but distribution and environment matter.
- "Low unemployment = full employment." Underemployment may still be high.
- "If inflation is 3%, my costs rose 3%." Personal experience can differ markedly.
- "Indicators are precise." They are estimates with revisions.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *"The economy is good" too vague.* It does not say what is good and for whom; specific indicators clarify.
2. *GDP up, per-person GDP down.* Population grew faster than GDP; average output per person fell.
3. *Inflation target and borrowers/savers.* Low and stable inflation supports planning, mortgage rates and real returns on savings.
4. *Why unemployment alone misleads.* It misses underemployment and discouraged workers.
5. *Indicator a small-business owner watches.* Consumer confidence; sector demand; mortgage rates (which affect customer spending).
6. *Unpaid activity GDP misses.* Caring at home; volunteering; informal community work.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Inflation falling.* Borrowing costs may ease; price pressures softer; potential RBA rate cut; mixed effect on savings returns.
2. *Wyndham unemployment response.* Targeted training; small-business support grants; partnerships with employers.
3. *One-page snapshot.* GDP growth; unemployment; CPI; one regional indicator (e.g., labour-force participation).
4. *GDP vs wellbeing index debate.* For: capture wider dimensions. Against: harder to measure consistently. Both sides agree on need to look beyond a single number.
5. *Vietnamese-Australian employment tracking.* ABS labour-force data by birthplace; community-organisation surveys; participation rates by gender within community.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Indicators: CPI, unemployment, retail trade.* CPI: current value and 12-month change; influences interest-rate decisions. Unemployment: current rate and 12-month change; influences fiscal policy. Retail trade: current month change; influences consumer confidence assessment. Limits: revisions, lags, scope.
