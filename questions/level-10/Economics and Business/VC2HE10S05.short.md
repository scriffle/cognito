# VC2HE10S05 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Develop and evaluate a response to an economic and business issue, using cost-benefit analysis or criteria.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Response = specific plan. CBA quantifies; criteria broadens. Stakeholders see different impacts.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop and evaluate a response** using **cost-benefit analysis or criteria**.
- You should be able to design and judge real responses.

## Core content

A **response** is a specific plan with objective, target group, actions, resources, timeline and measures. **Cost-benefit analysis (CBA)** lists costs and benefits across time and stakeholders. **Multi-criteria analysis** scores options on impact, feasibility, equity, sustainability and risk. Stakeholders see different costs and benefits; consultation matters. Evaluate against measures set at design.

**Worked example.** A small-business support package in Greater Geelong. Two options: (A) one-off cash grant; (B) mentoring plus grant. CBA: A has higher immediate cost; B has lower one-off cost but ongoing administration. Multi-criteria: B scores better on impact, equity and sustainability; A scores higher on feasibility. Recommendation: B, with monitoring and a sunset clause.

**Cultural context.** Responses affecting Aboriginal communities should consider cultural and community impacts; Closing the Gap targets and the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria provide context. Multilingual community organisations help evaluate community-specific responses. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Response = specific plan.
- CBA quantifies; criteria broadens.
- Measure at design, evaluate after.
- Honest analysis names limits.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Raise awareness" rarely enough.
- "If most benefit, it is justified." Distribution still matters.
- "Unintended effects can be ignored." They often dominate.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Non-money costs/benefits in CBA.* Wellbeing, time, equity and environment shape real outcomes.
- *When does multi-criteria beat CBA?* When values matter more than dollars.
- *Easily forgotten stakeholder.* Frontline workers; affected community members not in formal channels.
- *Why are measures-at-design key?* Allow honest evaluation; reduce post-hoc rationalisation.
- *Short summary of the small-business support package.* Mentoring plus grant scores better on impact, equity and sustainability; recommend with monitoring and sunset clause.
