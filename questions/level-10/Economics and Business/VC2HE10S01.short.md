# VC2HE10S01 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Develop and modify questions suitable for investigation of contemporary economic, business, work or financial issues.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Topic ≠ question. Five criteria sharpen. Modification is part of the work.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop and modify questions** for economic investigations.
- You should be able to adjust your question as your understanding deepens.

## Core content

A topic is general; a question is specific. Refining narrows scope by adding place, time, actor and measure. Five criteria: specific, investigable, open, neutral, operationalisable. Modify when sources do not fit, variables cannot be operationalised, or bias-checks reveal loaded language. Keep a "question log" with each version and reason for change.

Stakeholder considerations apply: community priorities can shape the question, especially when research affects specific communities.

**Worked example.** Modifying a cost-of-living question. Initial: Is cost of living unfair? Refined v1: Has cost of living risen in Greater Geelong since 2019? Refined v2: How has the cost-of-living index for Greater Geelong households compared with national-average wage growth between 2019 and 2024, based on ABS data? Each step adds specificity and operationalises variables.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS ethical guidelines apply to research with First Nations communities; the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria publishes its own materials. Multilingual community media and ethnic community councils provide voices missing from mainstream sources. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Topic ≠ question.
- Five criteria sharpen.
- Modification is part of the work.
- Community priorities matter.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Modifying = failing." Modifying is responding to evidence.
- "Specific = narrow." Specific means clear; still substantive.
- "If sources exist, the question is fine." Check fit.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *"Globalisation" as topic.* Names a phenomenon but no specific place, time, sector or measure.
- *Most overlooked criterion.* Operationalisability.
- *Why does a question log help?* Tracks thinking and supports honest revision.
- *Why is constant switching counter-productive?* Each switch costs time and breaks momentum.
- *Short summary of the cost-of-living refinement.* Move from a value claim to a measurable comparison between local cost-of-living and wage data over a defined period.
