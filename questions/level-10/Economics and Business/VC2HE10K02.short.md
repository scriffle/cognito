# VC2HE10K02 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The ways government and the Reserve Bank of Australia intervene in the economy to improve economic performance and living standards.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Many tools, not just two. Living standards beyond GDP. Trade-offs are real.

## What you are learning
- You are learning how the **government** and the **RBA** intervene to improve **economic performance and living standards**.
- You should be able to see intervention as a toolkit with trade-offs.

## Core content

Reasons to intervene: market failure (externalities, public goods), inequality, stability and supporting living standards. Government tools: fiscal policy (tax and spending), regulation, public services (health, education), transfer payments, ownership of utilities, social insurance (Medicare, NDIS). RBA tools: cash rate, bank reserves, emergency liquidity. The RBA is independent on monetary policy.

**Living standards** include income but also housing, health, education, environment and social connection. GDP measures output; the OECD Better Life Index, ABS Measures of Australia's Progress and HILDA Survey provide richer measures.

**Worked example.** Managing high inflation in 2022–24. RBA raised the cash rate. Government delivered targeted cost-of-living relief, energy-bill assistance, and competition policy. Effects varied across mortgagors, renters and savers. Living standards measured across multiple dimensions.

**Cultural context.** Closing the Gap, the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria, IBA financing and Supply Nation procurement targets are part of intervention frameworks. Settlement services and multilingual public-health information support inclusive intervention. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Many tools, not just two.
- Living standards beyond GDP.
- Trade-offs are real.
- Independent RBA matters.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Government should stay out." Markets fail in many ways.
- "GDP captures wellbeing." It captures one dimension.
- "RBA controls the economy." It controls one major lever.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is clean air not market-led?* Markets struggle to price what everyone shares.
- *Medicare as a tool.* Social insurance; pooled funding for universal health access.
- *Why does RBA independence matter?* Reduces political pressure on rate-setting; supports stable policy.
- *GDP up, living standards down.* Possible if growth is concentrated, inequality rises, or environmental harm grows.
- *Short summary of inflation response 2022–24.* RBA rate rises plus government targeted relief; effects varied; living standards across multiple dimensions, not headline inflation alone.
