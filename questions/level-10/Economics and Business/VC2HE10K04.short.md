# VC2HE10K04 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The changing patterns of Australia's international trade and costs and benefits of globalisation.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Trade patterns shift. Globalisation has benefits and costs. Australia is regional with global links.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about the **changing patterns** of Australia's **international trade** and the **costs and benefits of globalisation**.
- You should be able to see Australia in a global map.

## Core content

Australia exports iron ore, coal, natural gas, agricultural products, education and tourism. It imports manufactured goods, vehicles, refined oil and pharmaceuticals. Top partners: China, Japan, the United States, South Korea, India. Trade has shifted from UK/Europe to Asia over decades.

Australia is part of multilateral (WTO) and regional agreements (RCEP, CPTPP, ASEAN-Australia–New Zealand FTA) plus bilateral deals. **Globalisation** benefits: cheaper goods, capital, technology, export-driven jobs, cultural exchange. Costs: job displacement, supply-chain vulnerability, environmental impacts, regional inequality.

**Worked example.** China and Australian iron ore. Benefits: high export revenue, jobs, tax revenue. Costs: concentration risk, exposure to demand swings, environmental impact, debate about value-add in Australia.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander businesses participate in international trade through agriculture, tourism, art. Supply Nation lists Aboriginal exporters. Migrant communities maintain trade and personal links with home countries shaping diaspora-driven business. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Trade patterns shift; current focus is Asia.
- Globalisation has benefits and costs.
- Concentration risk matters.
- Agreements shape rules.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Free trade benefits everyone." Aggregate benefits do not guarantee individual gains.
- "Imports cost local jobs." Some, but they also lower costs and enable other jobs.
- "Globalisation is over." Patterns shift; reach continues.
