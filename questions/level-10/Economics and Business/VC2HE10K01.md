## Core content

**1. The circular flow.** The **circular flow model** shows how money and goods move between households (who supply labour and buy goods) and firms (who produce goods and pay wages). Government, banks and the rest of the world expand the picture. *Quick check:* Why is "the economy is just shops" too narrow?

**2. Five sectors.** Households, firms, government (taxes and spending), the financial sector (savings and lending), and the foreign sector (exports and imports). Each adds inflows and outflows. *Quick check:* What is one example of a flow from households to government?

**3. Fiscal policy.** **Fiscal policy** is government decisions on taxation and spending. Expansionary fiscal policy increases spending or cuts taxes to boost demand. Contractionary fiscal policy does the reverse. The federal Treasurer leads, with the Department of Treasury. *Quick check:* What is one fiscal policy you have seen in the news this year?

**4. Monetary policy.** **Monetary policy** is the Reserve Bank of Australia's decisions on interest rates and money-supply tools. Lower rates encourage spending and investment; higher rates cool demand and inflation. The RBA Board meets regularly to set the cash rate. *Quick check:* Why does a higher cash rate slow inflation?

**5. Worked example: a Treasurer responds to a slowing economy.** Suppose employment is weakening and growth is slowing. Fiscal response: extra infrastructure spending, targeted tax cuts. Monetary response: the RBA may cut the cash rate. Each tool flows through the system: households spend more; firms hire; tax receipts and inflation respond. Trade-offs include debt levels and inflation risk. *Follow the thinking:* identify the problem → choose tools → trace the flow → name trade-offs.

**6. Coordination and limits.** Fiscal and monetary policy are run by different bodies. Coordination matters but is not always perfect. Each tool has limits: fiscal policy is slower; monetary policy affects different households differently. *Quick check:* Why is "the government can fix everything" not accurate?

**7. Australian institutional setting.** The Treasurer, the Department of the Treasury and the RBA Board are the key players. The Productivity Commission, ACCC and AEMO play supporting roles. The Federal Budget (May each year) is a major policy event. *Quick check:* What is one Australian institution that supports economic decision-making?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Victorian Department of Treasury and Finance releases state-level budget and economic-update papers. State fiscal policy includes payroll tax, stamp duty and infrastructure investment.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander economic-policy engagement includes the Closing the Gap targets, IBA, Supply Nation and the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant communities respond to economic policy daily through housing, work and remittance decisions. Multilingual ABS, ATO and RBA resources support understanding.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Fiscal and monetary are the same." They are run by different bodies and use different tools.
- "Higher interest rates hurt everyone equally." Effects vary across mortgagors, savers, renters and businesses.
- "Stimulus always works." Effects depend on timing, design and context.
- "Government and RBA always agree." Coordination is real but not automatic.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *"Economy is just shops" too narrow.* It misses households' labour, firms' investment, government, banks and overseas trade.
2. *Flow from households to government.* Income tax (PAYG); GST on purchases; council rates from property owners.
3. *Fiscal policy in the news.* Tax changes, infrastructure spending, cost-of-living relief measures.
4. *Why higher cash rate slows inflation.* Higher borrowing costs reduce spending and demand, which can ease price pressures over time.
5. *Why "government can fix everything" is inaccurate.* Tools have limits, lags and unintended effects; private decisions and global factors also matter.
6. *Supporting institution.* Productivity Commission; ACCC; AEMO; ABS.

**Try these scenarios**

2. *Hume state/federal policy.* Housing supply; transport infrastructure; tax and family payments.
3. *Junior economist circular flow.* Wages from firms to households; spending from households to firms; tax flows to government.
4. *Inequality through fiscal policy.* For: tax-transfer system is a powerful lever. Against: efficiency trade-offs. Both sides agree on the need to target effectively.
5. *Vietnamese-Australian small business and monetary policy.* Higher rates raise borrowing costs; affect customer demand; affect exchange rates and supplier costs.
