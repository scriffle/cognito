## Core content

**1. What HRM is.** **Human resource management** is the work of recruiting, training, paying, supporting and developing the people in a business. It runs from the first job ad to retirement. *Quick check:* Why is HR more than payroll?

**2. Recruitment and selection.** Clear job descriptions; transparent selection; bias-aware processes; reference checks; onboarding. Bad hiring is expensive; good hiring compounds. *Quick check:* What is one cost of a poor hire?

**3. Training and development.** Induction, on-the-job training, courses, mentoring, leadership development. Investment in skills boosts performance and retention. *Quick check:* Why do skilled workers often value learning over a small pay rise?

**4. Worked example: a Melbourne café chain.** Strategy: serve consistent quality at six locations. HR practices: structured hiring, two-day onboarding, weekly skill cards, monthly team meetings, profit-share at year-end, paid time for training. Result: lower turnover, higher customer satisfaction, repeat business. Measures: turnover rate, customer reviews, internal-promotion rate. *Follow the thinking:* identify the people need → design practices → measure outcomes → adjust.

**5. Motivation models.** **Maslow's hierarchy** (basic needs to self-actualisation); **Herzberg** (hygiene vs motivators: pay must be fair but does not by itself motivate; growth and recognition do); **Self-determination** (autonomy, competence, relatedness). Combine theories with the specific context. *Quick check:* Why does fair pay matter even if it does not by itself motivate?

**6. Retention and culture.** Retention reduces hiring costs and builds capability. Culture (how people treat each other and the work) shapes retention as much as pay. Diverse and inclusive cultures perform better in many studies. *Quick check:* What is one signal of a strong workplace culture?

**7. HR and competitive advantage.** Businesses with engaged, skilled, supported people often outperform. HR is strategic, not just administrative. Modern HR also covers wellbeing, mental-health support and flexible work. *Quick check:* Why is "HR is just paperwork" out of date?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Fair Work Ombudsman, WorkSafe Victoria and the Victorian Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission shape HR practice. Victorian universities and TAFEs train HR professionals.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Reconciliation Action Plans (RAPs), Aboriginal-employment strategies and culturally responsive HR practices support First Nations workforce participation. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Diverse workforce strategies, multilingual support, faith-aware leave policies and culturally inclusive on-boarding help businesses across Melbourne attract and retain talent.

## Watch out for these traps
- "HR is admin only." Modern HR is strategic.
- "Pay alone solves morale." It does not.
- "Everyone is motivated by the same thing." No; context matters.
- "Diversity is decoration." Inclusive culture drives performance.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *HR more than payroll.* It covers hiring, onboarding, training, performance, wellbeing, exit and culture.
2. *Cost of a poor hire.* Onboarding cost; lost productivity; team morale; turnover and replacement cost.
3. *Why skilled workers value learning.* Growth supports current role and long-term career; pay rises are felt once.
4. *Why fair pay matters.* It removes a major source of dissatisfaction; without it, other motivators do not land.
5. *Strong-culture signal.* Low turnover; honest feedback; pride in the work; visible inclusion.
6. *Why "HR is paperwork" is out of date.* HR shapes strategy, capability and reputation; data-driven HR is central to many modern businesses.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *First part-time job.* Clear job description; structured onboarding; transparent rosters and pay.
2. *Hume workforce strategy.* Diversity targets; wellbeing programmes; career-pathway pilots.
3. *New-team onboarding.* Schedule; named buddies; clear early-week tasks and feedback windows.
4. *Individual-only bonuses debate.* For: rewards effort. Against: erodes teamwork; can encourage gaming metrics. Both sides agree on transparency.
5. *Sudanese-Australian inclusive HR.* Cultural-leave provisions; bilingual training; mentors from the community; faith-aware spaces and breaks.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Organisation: a Victorian community-health service.* Practices: paid study leave; structured supervision; flexible-work options. Motivation: meaning, growth, autonomy. Outcome: lower turnover and award-winning service quality. Sources cited.
