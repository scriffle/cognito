## Core content

**1. Competitive advantage.** **Competitive advantage** is the reason customers choose one business over another. Sources include lower cost, better quality, faster service, unique features, brand trust, distribution and culture. Sustaining advantage is harder than creating it. *Quick check:* Why is "we're the only one" usually fragile?

**2. Strategy vs tactic.** **Strategy** is the long-term plan (who we serve, how we win). **Tactic** is the specific move (a promotion, a hire, a price change). Strategies guide tactics; tactics test strategies. *Quick check:* Why is "we should advertise more" a tactic, not a strategy?

**3. Entrepreneurship inside competitive advantage.** Entrepreneurs notice unmet needs and design new ways to meet them. Many advantages start as small experiments and grow. *Quick check:* What is one cheap experiment a small business could run to test an idea?

**4. Marketing.** **Marketing** is the work of understanding customers and bringing them a product they want. The classic "4Ps": product, price, place, promotion. Modern frameworks add purpose and people. *Quick check:* What is one "place" decision a Melbourne café could make?

**5. Worked example: a Melbourne specialty-tea brand.** Strategy: serve health-conscious customers with premium loose-leaf tea sourced ethically. Tactics: bright packaging, partnerships with cafés, social-media campaigns, in-store demos at Queen Victoria Market. PR: media stories on ethical sourcing; community events. Marketing measures: brand-awareness surveys, repeat-purchase rate, NPS. *Follow the thinking:* identify customer → match strategy → choose tactics → measure → adjust.

**6. Public relations.** **Public relations (PR)** manages a business's reputation with customers, media, regulators and community. PR is about earned attention; marketing is about paid and owned attention. The lines blur. *Quick check:* Why do crises test PR more than marketing?

**7. Limits and ethics.** Misleading marketing breaks the ACL. PR cannot rescue a bad product permanently. Ethics shapes long-term advantage: greenwashing and dishonest claims hurt eventually. *Quick check:* What is one ethical line marketing should not cross?

## Watch out for these traps
- "More advertising fixes it." If the strategy is wrong, more spending magnifies the wrong message.
- "Lowest price always wins." Not if quality, trust or service matter to the customer.
- "PR is spin." Good PR is honest reputation work.
- "Greenwashing is harmless." It misleads customers and is increasingly regulated.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why "we're the only one" is fragile.* Competition often catches up; sustainable advantage needs reinforcement (brand, supply, scale, culture).
2. *Why "advertise more" is tactic.* It does not name customer, position or measure; it is a single move within a plan.
3. *Cheap experiment.* Pre-sell a small batch; run a one-week pop-up; test a single-channel ad with a clear measure.
4. *Place decision for a café.* Location near foot traffic; presence on a delivery platform; partnership with a co-working space.
5. *Why crises test PR.* Reputation is built over time and tested in stress; quick honest action protects it.
6. *Ethical line marketing should not cross.* Misleading claims; targeting vulnerable consumers; making unverified health claims.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Café comparison.* Coffee quality; staff friendliness; speed; brand identity; loyalty programme.
2. *Wyndham support programme.* Digital marketing; export readiness; supplier-network access.
3. *Product-launch plan.* Customer research; positioning; channel mix; clear measures (sales, awareness, retention).
4. *Greenwashing debate.* For: imperfect messaging may still drive better behaviour. Against: misleading customers undermines genuine sustainability and trust. Both sides agree on transparency rules.
5. *Vietnamese-Australian bakery tactics.* Vietnamese-language social-media campaign; community-event sponsorship; loyalty cards aimed at families.
