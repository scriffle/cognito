# VC2HE10S03 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Evaluate and use data and information to address economic, business, work or financial issues through recognition of trends and cause-and-effect relationships.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Direction, magnitude, timeframe, uncertainty. Correlation hints; cause needs mechanism. Confounders mislead.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **evaluate and use data** to address issues through **trends and cause-and-effect**.
- You should be able to do real analysis, not just describe charts.

## Core content

Describe trends with direction, magnitude, timeframe and uncertainty. **Correlation** is two variables moving together. **Causation** requires a mechanism plus evidence that change in one drives change in the other. A **confounder** is a third variable that affects both, creating apparent correlation. Strong analysis names alternatives and limits; cites sources by name and date.

**Worked example.** House prices and interest rates in Australian capital cities, 2010–2024. Correlation often negative (lower rates, higher prices). Causation: lower borrowing costs increase borrowing power; investor sentiment also matters. Confounders: government policy, supply, demographics, foreign capital. Conclusion: rates influence prices, but they are not the only driver.

**Cultural context.** Closing the Gap targets monitor trends in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander outcomes; analyses should respect community context. Multilingual research bodies provide community-level trend data complementing ABS findings. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Direction, magnitude, timeframe, uncertainty.
- Correlation hints; cause needs mechanism.
- Confounders mislead the unwary.
- Cite sources by name and date.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Correlation = cause." Not without mechanism.
- "If it worked once, it works always." Replication and context matter.
- "Recent change = trend." Short-term moves are often noise.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Strong negative correlation, rates and spending.* When rates rise, spending often falls.
- *Ice-cream and drownings confounder.* Hot weather drives both.
- *Why does naming alternatives strengthen recommendations?* It shows you have weighed other explanations.
- *Why does "the data prove" overclaim?* Data support; claims need reasoning and limits.
- *Short summary of house prices and rates.* Rates influence house prices through borrowing costs and sentiment; other variables (policy, supply, demographics) also matter.
