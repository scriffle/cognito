## Core content

**1. What unions do.** **Trade unions** are member organisations that bargain on pay, conditions and safety, support members in disputes, and advocate for workforce-wide changes. The Australian Council of Trade Unions (ACTU) is the peak body. *Quick check:* What is one practical thing a union does for a member?

**2. What employer groups do.** **Employer groups** (e.g., Australian Industry Group, Business Council of Australia, Council of Small Business Organisations Australia) advocate for businesses, advise members on compliance, and bargain on industry-wide settings. *Quick check:* Why do small businesses join employer groups?

**3. Awards and agreements.** An **award** is a minimum-conditions safety net set by the Fair Work Commission for an industry or occupation. An **enterprise agreement** is a workplace-specific agreement between employer and employees (often with union representation) that must improve on the award and pass the better-off-overall test (BOOT). *Quick check:* What does the BOOT test ensure?

**4. Worked example: a hospitality enterprise agreement in Melbourne.** Twenty workers and their employer negotiate. Union: United Workers Union. Employer-side: an HR adviser, possibly with employer-group help. They negotiate wage rises, shift allowances, training and rostering rules. Vote: majority of employees must approve. Lodged with the Fair Work Commission. *Follow the thinking:* identify parties → set claims → negotiate → vote → register.

**5. Disputes and resolution.** Most disputes are resolved through grievance procedures, mediation and the Fair Work Commission. Industrial action (e.g., strikes) is regulated; "protected" action requires specific steps. *Quick check:* Why is "protected" action a key term?

**6. Workplace safety and culture.** Unions and employer groups both contribute to safer workplaces: training, reporting culture, WorkSafe compliance, after-incident reviews. Best workplaces invest before incidents. *Quick check:* What is one signal of a safety-first culture?

**7. Wider role.** Unions advocate beyond their workplaces (e.g., wage policy, paid parental leave campaigns). Employer groups influence policy and skills training. *Quick check:* Why do both groups talk to government, not just each other?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Victorian Trades Hall Council coordinates unions in Victoria. The Victorian Chamber of Commerce and Industry supports employers. Fair Work Commission Victoria-based hearings handle local disputes.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander workers participate in unions and are supported by First Nations workforce-development programs. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual workplace materials and union support help migrant workers in sectors like aged care, construction and hospitality. Wage-theft cases in Melbourne have led to enforcement and reform.

## Memorable takeaways
- Unions represent workers; employer groups represent businesses.
- Awards are floors; enterprise agreements improve on them.
- BOOT protects employees.
- Disputes have structured pathways.
- Both shape safety and policy.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Unions and employers are enemies." Often partners as well as negotiators.
- "All disputes mean strikes." Most are resolved through agreement and mediation.
- "Awards are optional." They are legally binding minimum conditions.
- "Migrants do not use unions." Many do, and union support has been central to wage-theft reform.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Practical union role for member.* Help with underpayment claim; safety advice; representation in a dispute.
2. *Why small businesses join employer groups.* Compliance advice, training, voice in policy.
3. *BOOT.* The agreement must leave employees better off overall than the award.
4. *Why "protected" action.* It is industrial action that meets legal rules and so cannot result in penalties for taking part.
5. *Safety-first culture.* Near-miss reporting; staff trained; visible leadership; quick action after incidents.
6. *Why both talk to government.* Workplace settings, taxes and skills funding are set by government; advocacy shapes outcomes.
