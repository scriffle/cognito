# VC2HE10S07 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Explain and present arguments about economics and business concepts and issues using subject-specific terminology, with reference to sources.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Four-part argument. Use precise terms. Audience and format together.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **explain and present arguments** using **subject terminology** and **source references**.
- You should be able to communicate as economists do.

## Core content

A solid argument has a **claim** (specific and testable), **evidence** (named sources, dated), **reasoning** (the mechanism) and a **counter-argument** (fairly stated). Use precise terms (scarcity, opportunity cost, GDP, CPI, monetary policy, fiscal policy, superannuation) and define for non-experts. Cite by author, year and access.

Choose format and tone for the audience: parliamentary submission for policymakers; op-ed for the public; explainer video for peers; report for assessors. Charts need labels and citations.

**Worked example.** Raising the minimum wage. Claim: a targeted rise above CPI would improve real living standards for low-income workers without significant unemployment effects. Evidence: Fair Work Commission annual review; ABS data; peer-reviewed studies. Reasoning: low-income workers have higher marginal propensity to consume; small rises align with productivity growth. Counter: small-business employment may shrink in low-margin sectors. Response: targeted rise with transition support.

**Cultural context.** Cite First Nations authors directly (Marcia Langton, Megan Davis, Bruce Pascoe). NITV and IndigenousX add primary voices. SBS Radio broadcasts in over 60 languages. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Four-part argument.
- Use precise terms.
- Cite by author, year, source.
- Integrity beats persuasion.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Sounding neutral" means no view. You can still argue strongly with neutral framing.
- "Counter-arguments weaken you." Engaging strengthens you.
- "Long quotes prove the point." Use sparingly; reasoning is yours.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why engage counter-arguments?* It shows careful thought and strengthens credibility.
- *Why does precise vocabulary build credibility?* It signals careful thinking and lets readers follow.
- *Why is "everyone knows" not a citation?* It hides the source.
- *Year 8 vs Treasury audience.* Peers: accessibility. Treasury: precise terms, full citations, technical analysis.
- *Short summary of the minimum-wage argument.* A targeted rise above CPI supports real wages for low-income workers; concerns about small-business employment addressed through transition support; backed by FWC and ABS data.
