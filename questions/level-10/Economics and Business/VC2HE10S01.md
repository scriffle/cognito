# VC2HE10S01 — Economics and Business

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HE10S01. Develop and modify questions suitable for investigation of contemporary economic, business, work or financial issues.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Economics and Business (Investigating).
- **Big ideas:**
  - A strong question makes investigation possible.
  - Modifying as you go is part of research.
  - Questions name place, time, actor and measure.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Critical and Creative Thinking; Numeracy; Literacy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop and modify questions** suitable for **investigation of contemporary economic, business, work or financial issues**.
- You are learning to adjust your question as your understanding deepens.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **investigable question**, **scope** and **modification** in your own words.
- Refine a broad topic through three iterations.
- Identify when to modify a question and when to keep it.
- Justify the final form with explicit criteria.

## Core content

**1. From topic to question.** A topic is general; a question is specific. Refining narrows scope by adding place, time, actor and measure. *Quick check:* Why is "globalisation" still a topic, not a question?

**2. Five criteria for a strong question.** Specific, investigable (sources exist and are accessible), open (multiple perspectives), neutral (avoids loaded wording), operationalisable (you can define the measure). *Quick check:* Which criterion is most often overlooked?

**3. Iterative modification.** Real research modifies questions as you learn. Modification is not failure; it is part of good practice. Keep notes on each version and why you changed. *Quick check:* Why does keeping a "question log" help?

**4. Worked example: modifying a question on cost of living.** Initial: Is cost of living unfair? Refined v1: Has cost of living risen in Greater Geelong since 2019? Refined v2: How has the cost-of-living index for Greater Geelong households compared with national-average wage growth between 2019 and 2024, based on ABS data? Each step adds specificity and operationalises the variables. *Follow the thinking:* draft → refine → check criteria → modify if needed.

**5. When to modify.** Modify when: sources do not match the question; variables cannot be operationalised; the question turns out to be answered already; bias-checks reveal loaded language; the time available is too short. *Quick check:* Why is "I have run out of time" sometimes a legitimate reason to modify?

**6. When to keep.** Keep if: the question is interesting and investigable; you have at least three sources; the measure is clear. Stick with it through challenges; do not abandon at the first hurdle. *Quick check:* Why is constantly switching questions usually counter-productive?

**7. Stakeholder considerations.** Investigations affecting specific communities should follow ethical guidance (e.g., AIATSIS for First Nations work). Community priorities can shape the question itself. *Quick check:* Why does community priority sometimes outrank academic interest?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** ABS, RBA, Productivity Commission and the Victorian Auditor-General publish accessible primary sources for student investigation. Parliament of Victoria research papers provide local angles.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** AIATSIS ethical guidelines apply to research with First Nations communities; the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria publishes its own materials. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual community media, ethnic community councils and SBS provide voices and data missing from mainstream sources.

## Stretch yourself
Pick one Victorian or Australian economic issue. Develop a question, modify it twice based on feedback or sources, and justify the final version. 250 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Modifying = failing." Modifying is responding to evidence.
- "Specific = narrow." Specific means clear about scope; can still be substantive.
- "Indigenous topics are off limits." They are valid with appropriate ethics.
- "If sources exist, the question is fine." Check whether they actually fit your question.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *"Globalisation" as topic.* It names a phenomenon but no specific place, time, sector or measure.
2. *Most overlooked criterion.* Operationalisability: students often pick variables they cannot measure.
3. *Why a question log helps.* Tracks your thinking, supports honesty about changes and helps future research.
4. *Why "ran out of time" is legitimate.* A narrower, completed question is more valuable than an abandoned big one.
5. *Why constant switching is counter-productive.* Each switch costs time and breaks momentum; refine within a question rather than abandoning.
6. *Why community priority can outrank academic interest.* Research should benefit those most affected, especially when working with First Nations communities.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Rent affordability.* Q1: Is rent affordable in Melbourne? Q2: Has rent affordability in Melbourne's outer suburbs changed since 2019? Q3: How has the ratio of median rent to median household income in three Melbourne outer suburbs changed between 2019 and 2024, based on REIV and ABS data?
2. *Casey local employment.* Has youth employment in Casey changed since 2019? Which sectors employ the most local young people? How has casualisation affected youth wages in Casey?
3. *Modifying after one week.* Sources do not fit; variable cannot be operationalised; question already answered by reputable studies.
4. *Sensitive financial-issue research.* Reframe to focus on systemic factors; ensure consent and anonymisation; partner with community organisations.
5. *Wadawurrung economic-development research.* Step 1: contact Wadawurrung Traditional Owners Aboriginal Corporation. Step 2: co-design the question. Step 3: ensure outputs benefit the community first.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Issue: Victorian rental affordability.* Q1: Are rents too high? Q2: Has rent risen in Melbourne since 2019? Q3 final: How has the gap between median rent and median household income in three Melbourne outer suburbs widened or narrowed between 2019 and 2024, based on REIV and ABS data, and compared with the same gap in Melbourne inner suburbs? Modifications: added specificity, sources, time window, and comparison. Sources cited.
