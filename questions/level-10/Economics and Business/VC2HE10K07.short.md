# VC2HE10K07 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The role of trade unions and employer groups in a workplace.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Unions and employer groups represent different sides. Awards are floors; enterprise agreements improve on them. Disputes have pathways.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **role of trade unions and employer groups** in a workplace.
- You should be able to see industrial relations as a working system.

## Core content

**Trade unions** bargain on pay, conditions and safety; support members in disputes; advocate workforce-wide. The ACTU is the peak body. **Employer groups** (AiG, BCA, COSBOA) advise members and bargain on industry-wide settings. An **award** is a minimum-conditions safety net set by the Fair Work Commission. An **enterprise agreement** is a workplace-specific agreement that must pass the **better-off-overall test (BOOT)**.

Most disputes are resolved through grievance procedures, mediation and the Fair Work Commission. "Protected" industrial action is regulated. Both groups also influence policy and skills training.

**Worked example.** A hospitality enterprise agreement in Melbourne. Twenty workers and their employer negotiate. Union: United Workers Union. They negotiate wages, shift allowances, training and rostering rules. Majority of employees must approve. Lodged with the Fair Work Commission.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander workers participate in unions and are supported by First Nations workforce-development programs. Multilingual workplace materials and union support help migrant workers in aged care, construction and hospitality. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Unions represent workers; employer groups represent businesses.
- Awards are floors; agreements improve on them.
- BOOT protects employees.
- Disputes have structured pathways.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Unions and employers are enemies." Often partners as well as negotiators.
- "All disputes mean strikes." Most are resolved through agreement.
- "Awards are optional." They are legally binding minimums.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Practical union role.* Help with underpayment claim; safety advice; dispute representation.
- *BOOT.* Agreement must leave employees better off overall than the award.
- *Why "protected" action.* Industrial action that meets legal rules and avoids penalties.
- *Why do both talk to government?* Workplace settings, taxes and skills funding are set by government.
- *Short summary of a hospitality enterprise agreement.* Negotiate wages, allowances, training and rostering; majority vote; FWC registration; agreement must beat the award overall.
