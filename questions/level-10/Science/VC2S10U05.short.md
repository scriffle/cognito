# VC2S10U05 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Evolution by natural selection — involving variation, isolation and adaptation — explains both past and present biodiversity, and all organisms are related to some degree.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Variation, selection, time. Isolation can drive new species. All life shares ancestry.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **evolution by natural selection** involves **variation**, **isolation** and **adaptation**, and explains **biodiversity**.
- You should be able to read evidence and connect mechanisms to outcomes.

## Core content

**Variation** within a population comes from mutation, sexual reproduction and migration. **Natural selection** is the process where individuals with traits suited to their environment leave more offspring; over generations those traits become more common. **Isolation** (geographic, behavioural, ecological) can lead populations to diverge and form new species. An **adaptation** is a trait shaped by selection that suits the organism to its environment; adaptations are usually not perfect, just better than alternatives.

Evidence for evolution comes from many lines: fossils, comparative anatomy, embryology, DNA sequences and biogeography matching plate tectonics. Adaptation works across generations through inherited variation, not within a single lifetime.

**Worked example.** Peppered moths shifted from light to dark forms as soot darkened tree trunks during the Industrial Revolution; light forms recovered after pollution controls. Australian rabbits initially died from RHDV calicivirus; survivors had genetic resistance and populations partially rebounded.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge of Country includes deep observation of species change, habitat shifts and biodiversity over thousands of years. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Variation, selection, time.
- Adaptations suit, not perfect.
- All life shares ancestry.
- Evidence comes from many lines.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Evolution is just a theory." No. In science, theory means well-tested explanation supported by evidence.
- "Individuals evolve." No. Populations evolve across generations.
- "Evolution always means progress." No. It means better fit to current conditions.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is a uniform population vulnerable?* Without variation, no raw material for adaptation if the environment changes.
- *Why is "survival of the fittest" not just strength?* Fitness is reproductive success, which depends on traits that suit the environment.
- *Why was Lamarck's "use and disuse" wrong?* Acquired traits are not encoded in DNA, so they are not passed on.
- *Why are independent evidence lines stronger?* Each can be questioned alone; together, they are hard to explain by anything else.
- *Short summary of mosquitoes and repellents.* Variation, selection and reproduction over generations make repellents less effective. The same mechanism explains antibiotic resistance.
