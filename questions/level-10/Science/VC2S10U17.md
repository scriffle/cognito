By the end of this you should be able to:

- Distinguish mass from weight.

## Core content

**1. Newton's first law: inertia.** A body at rest stays at rest, and a body in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted on by an unbalanced force. Inertia is the tendency to resist changes in motion. *Quick check:* Why do passengers lurch forward when a car brakes suddenly?

**2. Newton's second law: F = ma.** The acceleration of an object is the **net force** divided by its mass. Force in newtons, mass in kilograms, acceleration in metres per second squared. *Quick check:* If a 30 kg shopping trolley experiences a 60 N net force, what is its acceleration?

**3. Newton's third law: action-reaction.** For every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force on a different body. When you walk, you push the ground back; the ground pushes you forward. *Quick check:* What is the reaction pair when a rocket pushes hot gas out the back?

**4. Mass vs weight.** **Mass** is the amount of matter (kg). **Weight** is the gravitational force on that mass (N). On Earth, weight = mass × g (about 9.8 N/kg). *Quick check:* Would your mass change on the Moon? What about your weight?

**5. Worked example: braking a car.** Mass: 1500 kg. Initial speed: 60 km/h ≈ 16.7 m/s. Stopping in 3 s. Acceleration = (0 − 16.7) / 3 ≈ −5.6 m/s². Force: F = ma = 1500 × −5.6 = −8400 N (negative means braking direction). *Follow the thinking:* identify mass → calculate acceleration → apply F = ma.

**6. Real situations are messy.** Friction, air resistance and uneven loads all add up. A simple model captures the main forces; a more detailed model adds smaller ones. *Quick check:* Why do school physics problems often ignore air resistance for short events?

**7. Forces and safety.** Seatbelts, crumple zones and helmets all manage the force experienced during sudden deceleration by extending the time over which speed changes. *Quick check:* Why does a longer stopping time reduce force?

## Watch out for these traps
- "If something is moving, there must be a net force." No. Constant velocity needs zero net force.
- "Mass and weight are the same." No. Mass is constant; weight depends on gravity.
- "Action and reaction cancel out." No. They act on different bodies, so neither cancels.
- "Heavier means stronger force always." Weight is a force, but acceleration depends on net force ÷ mass.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Sudden brake passenger lurch.* Inertia. Passengers continue forward at the previous speed until restrained by seatbelts or the seat in front.
2. *60 N on 30 kg trolley.* a = F / m = 60 / 30 = 2 m/s².
3. *Rocket reaction pair.* Rocket pushes gas down and out; gas pushes rocket up. Equal and opposite forces on different bodies.
4. *Mass vs weight on the Moon.* Mass stays the same. Weight is about one-sixth of Earth weight because lunar gravity is weaker.
5. *Why ignore air resistance for short events.* The effect is small for slow speeds and short distances; including it complicates the calculation without changing the answer much.
6. *Why longer stopping time reduces force.* Same change in momentum spread over longer time means lower average force (F = Δp/Δt).

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Braking bus.* Inertia keeps your body moving forward; seatbelts or grab rails apply the unbalanced force needed to decelerate you. Friction between feet and floor also helps.
2. *Yarra school zones.* Speed limits reduce kinetic energy; speed humps lower vehicle speed before pedestrian crossings; pedestrian-only refuge islands shorten exposure to vehicle paths.
3. *Crumple zone.* Increase deformation distance to extend stopping time; channel forces away from passenger compartment; absorb energy through controlled buckling of materials.
4. *Phone-case drop.* Issues: damage and broken-glass risk. Alternative: use a controlled drop tower with foam catch and small heights, supervised conditions, recorded forces.
5. *Wurundjeri throwing tools.* Discuss with Elder permission and credit. Explain that the tool acts as a lever, multiplying the throwing speed and giving the projectile more energy. Use only published, community-approved material.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Sport: AFL.* Moments: kicking the ball (impact force), tackling (impulse and friction), marking (deceleration in air). Calculation example: a 0.45 kg AFL ball accelerated from 0 to 25 m/s in 0.05 s during a kick. a = 500 m/s²; F = 0.45 × 500 = 225 N. Discuss player technique that maximises this safely.
