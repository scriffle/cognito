# VC2S10U10 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Carbon cycles through Earth's systems via processes like photosynthesis, respiration, combustion and volcanic activity, and human actions affect this cycle.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Carbon moves through reservoirs. Natural processes balance over long timescales. Modern imbalance comes from us.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **carbon cycles** through Earth's systems via **photosynthesis**, **respiration**, **combustion** and **volcanic activity**, and that **human actions** affect this cycle.
- You should be able to trace carbon between reservoirs and explain modern shifts.

## Core content

A **carbon reservoir** is a place carbon is stored: atmosphere, oceans, rocks, soils, biosphere, fossil fuels. A **flux** is the movement between them. **Photosynthesis** moves carbon from CO₂ into organic molecules; **respiration** does the reverse. **Combustion** and decomposition return CO₂ to the atmosphere. **Volcanic activity** releases CO₂ from deep Earth; weathering of silicate rocks pulls it down over millions of years. Human emissions from fossil fuels, land clearing, cement and agriculture have grown to about 100 times annual volcanic output.

**Worked example.** A coal-fired power station in the Latrobe Valley. Reservoir: brown-coal seams (long-term storage). Flux: combustion releases CO₂ to the atmosphere. The atmosphere passes some to the ocean (acidifying it) and some to the biosphere. Net result: rising atmospheric CO₂ because combustion adds carbon faster than oceans and biosphere can absorb.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cool-burning practices in northern Australia and parts of Victoria reduce wildfire intensity and carbon release; carbon-credit schemes recognise this work. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Carbon moves through reservoirs.
- Photosynthesis and respiration balance over short time.
- Combustion and clearing add carbon fast.
- Modern imbalance comes from us.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Carbon dioxide is just pollution." No. It is part of a natural cycle; speed and scale are the issue.
- "Trees solve climate change." They help but cannot fully offset fossil-fuel emissions.
- "Volcanoes cause more CO₂ than humans." No. Human emissions are about 100× larger annually.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why does atmospheric CO₂ wiggle yearly?* Northern-hemisphere photosynthesis pulls it down in summer; respiration and decay push it up in winter.
- *Why do fires spike CO₂ for decades?* Combustion releases stored carbon quickly; regrowth absorbs it slowly.
- *Why is volcanism alone insufficient to explain modern rise?* Modern CO₂ rise tracks fossil-fuel use, which dwarfs volcanic output.
- *Why is "cycle" framing better than "emissions"?* Cycle highlights flows in and out; emissions alone hide reservoirs and interactions.
- *Short summary of the Latrobe Valley case.* Combustion of coal moves long-stored carbon to the atmosphere faster than oceans and biosphere can absorb, raising atmospheric CO₂.
