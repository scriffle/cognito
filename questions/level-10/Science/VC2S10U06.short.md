# VC2S10U06 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The atomic model evolved as electrons, protons and neutrons were discovered, and natural radioactive decay transforms unstable atoms into stable ones.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Models grow with evidence. Protons define the element; neutrons make isotopes. Unstable atoms decay to stable ones.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that the **atomic model evolved** as **electrons**, **protons** and **neutrons** were **discovered**, and that **radioactive decay** transforms unstable atoms.
- You should be able to track how a model improves with evidence.

## Core content

Dalton's atoms were indivisible spheres. Thomson discovered the **electron**, leading to the "plum-pudding" model. Rutherford's gold-foil experiment revealed a small, dense **nucleus**. Bohr added energy levels; modern quantum mechanics replaced orbits with probability clouds. Atoms contain **protons** (positive), **neutrons** (neutral) and **electrons** (negative). The number of protons defines the element. **Isotopes** of the same element differ in neutron count.

Radioactive decay types: **alpha** releases a helium nucleus (stopped by paper); **beta** releases an electron (stopped by aluminium); **gamma** releases high-energy electromagnetic radiation (needs lead or thick concrete). Each decay produces a new nucleus. **Half-life** is the time for half the unstable atoms to decay.

**Worked example.** Carbon-14 is unstable. It decays by beta emission with a half-life of about 5,730 years. Measuring carbon-14 left in old organic material lets scientists estimate age. Carbon dating has been used at Madjedbebe to confirm Aboriginal occupation of the Australian continent for around 65,000 years.

**Cultural context.** Carbon-dating studies of cultural sites are conducted under partnership agreements with Traditional Owners, recognising cultural ownership of findings. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Models grow with evidence.
- Protons define the element. Neutrons make isotopes.
- Alpha, beta, gamma differ in particle, mass and penetration.
- Half-life is the clock for dating.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Atoms are tiny solar systems." No. Modern models use probability clouds.
- "All radiation is dangerous." No. Background radiation is everywhere; doses matter.
- "Carbon dating works on anything." No. Only organic material up to about 50,000 years.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Experiment showing electrons.* Cathode-ray tube experiments by Thomson.
- *What did bouncing alphas tell us?* Atoms have a tiny dense positive nucleus; most of the atom is empty space.
- *Carbon-12 vs carbon-14.* Same number of protons (6), different number of neutrons (6 vs 8).
- *Decay raising atomic number.* Beta-minus decay: a neutron becomes a proton.
- *Short summary of carbon dating.* Carbon-14 decays at a known rate. Measuring how much remains in organic material lets scientists estimate age, used in Australian archaeology under Traditional Owner agreements.
