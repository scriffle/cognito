# VC2S10I02 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Plan valid, reproducible investigations that control for sources of error and bias, manage risks, address ethical issues and follow cultural protocols on Country.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Validity asks "the right thing". Reproducibility asks "the same way". Ethics and protocols are method, not paperwork.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **plan** valid, reproducible investigations that control **error** and **bias**, **manage risks**, address **ethics** and follow **cultural protocols** on Country.
- You should be able to write a method another team could repeat.

## Core content

**Validity** is whether the study measures what it claims. **Reproducibility** is whether others can repeat it and get similar results. Random errors arise from small variations and shrink with repetition; systematic errors come from miscalibration and shift the whole dataset. **Bias** is non-random influence: selection (who is sampled), observer (how recorded), confirmation (what is sought). Double-blinding reduces several bias types.

Risk assessments cover physical, chemical, biological and psychological hazards. Ethics asks who could be harmed, who consents, and how data are stored. Cultural **protocols** on Country include consent from Traditional Owner corporations, briefings and limits on photographing or sampling significant sites.

**Worked example.** A community air-quality study in Sunshine. Sensors at five sites for two weeks. Validity: do they measure the pollutant claimed? Reproducibility: same calibration, time and protocol across sites. Errors: sensor drift, wind variation. Bias: site selection should not exclude high-traffic streets. Ethics: anonymise photos near homes. Protocol: contact the Bunurong Land Council if any sites are on culturally significant land.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS and Victorian Traditional Owner corporations publish clear consent and protocol frameworks. Joint-management research with Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Wadawurrung, Bunurong and Gunaikurnai peoples in Victoria includes cultural protocols at every step. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Validity asks "the right thing". Reproducibility asks "the same way".
- Bias hides in choices.
- Repeats handle random. Calibration handles systematic.
- Community-led beats consulted.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If we are careful, bias goes away." No. Bias must be named, designed against and checked.
- "Risk assessments are routine paperwork." No. Updated risk thinking changes methods.
- "Cultural protocols slow down science." No. They make science usable, accountable and trustworthy.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Precise but invalid problem.* The data are repeatable but do not measure what is claimed, so the conclusion is wrong even though the numbers look tight.
- *Why does double-blinding reduce bias?* Neither participant nor observer knows the assignment, so expectations cannot bias responses or recordings.
- *Why is informed consent more than a signature?* It requires understanding, voluntary agreement and the right to withdraw.
- *Why is community-led research more than consultation?* Consultation can be cosmetic. Community leadership shapes the question, methods and reporting.
- *Short summary of the Sunshine air-quality plan.* Calibrated sensors at stratified sites, identical protocol and timing, named errors and biases, anonymised photographic ethics, and protocol contact with the relevant Traditional Owner corporation before fieldwork.
