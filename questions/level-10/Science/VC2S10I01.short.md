# VC2S10I01 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Formulate investigable questions, predictions and hypotheses that guide investigations aimed at testing and developing explanatory models.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Good questions test or develop models. Predictions follow from mechanisms. Hypotheses name the mechanism.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **formulate** investigable questions, predictions and hypotheses for studies that test or refine **explanatory models**.
- You should be able to design questions that probe a model, not just describe an outcome.

## Core content

An **explanatory model** describes how and why a phenomenon happens. A **prediction** states what should be observed if the model is right. A **hypothesis** at Year 10 names a mechanism: "If light intensity increases, then photosynthesis rate will increase, because more photons drive more electron-transport reactions, until enzymes become saturating." Strong scientists ask, "if my prediction holds, what else could explain the data?" and design controls accordingly. A hypothesis must be **falsifiable**.

**Worked example.** Osmosis in potato cores. Question: "How does the concentration of salt solution affect the change in mass of potato cores after 24 hours?" Hypothesis: "If salt concentration outside is higher than inside, then water flows out, because water moves down its concentration gradient through cell membranes, leading to net mass loss." Variables: concentration (independent), mass change (dependent), time, variety, surface area (controlled).

**Cultural context.** Traditional Owner corporations run scientific monitoring programs (water quality, fire ecology, bird counts) that combine investigable questions with long-term observation. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- A question worth asking tests a model.
- "If… then… because…" with a mechanism.
- List alternative explanations early.
- Falsifiable or it is not science.

## Watch out for these traps
- "More general questions are stronger." No. Specific, narrow questions are more testable.
- "A prediction is the same as a hypothesis." No. The hypothesis adds a mechanism.
- "Falsifiable means easy to disprove." No. It means data could in principle show it wrong.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Three parts of a Year 10 hypothesis.* Independent variable change, dependent variable change, mechanism.
- *Why is "I want to discover what happens" not a plan?* It names no model, prediction or method.
- *One alternative explanation for treatment success.* Placebo effect, observer bias, or selection differences.
- *Why is the "unknowable force" not scientific?* It cannot be falsified by any observation.
- *Short summary of the osmosis example.* Salt concentration changes water movement across membranes, predicted to shift mass from gain to loss as concentration rises, with a stated mechanism (water moves down its concentration gradient).
