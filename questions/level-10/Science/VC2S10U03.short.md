# VC2S10U03 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens like bacteria, viruses and fungi, while non-infectious diseases have other causes, and various measures can control disease transmission.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Infectious has a pathogen; non-infectious does not. Break the route, slow the disease. Public health is biology + behaviour + policy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **infectious diseases** are caused by **pathogens** while **non-infectious** diseases have other causes, and that **various measures** control transmission.
- You should be able to map a disease across cause, transmission and control.

## Core content

A **pathogen** is a disease-causing organism. Bacteria are single-celled (some beneficial, some harmful). Viruses need host cells to replicate. Fungi cause infections from skin to invasive disease. Antibiotics target bacterial structures that viruses lack.

**Transmission** routes include airborne, droplet, fomite, water, food, vector and bodily fluids. Hand-washing interrupts fomite spread. Vaccines train the immune system to recognise pathogens and reduce community transmission. **Non-infectious diseases** arise from genetics, environment, behaviour and aging. Many are influenced by multiple factors.

**Worked example.** Managing COVID-19 in Victoria. Pathogen: SARS-CoV-2. Transmission: airborne and droplet. Controls: vaccination, masks, ventilation, testing, isolation and public-health communication. Trade-offs: economic and social costs; disproportionate effects on multilingual and low-income communities.

**Cultural context.** The Victorian Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisation provides culturally appropriate care for both infectious and non-infectious diseases. Closing-the-Gap targets address chronic-disease disparities. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Infectious has a pathogen; non-infectious does not.
- Break the route, slow the disease.
- Vaccines train, not infect.
- Public health is biology + behaviour + policy.

## Watch out for these traps
- "All germs are bad." No. Many bacteria support digestion, immunity and skin health.
- "Antibiotics fix everything." No. Only bacteria; overuse drives resistance.
- "Non-infectious diseases are entirely lifestyle." No. Genetics, environment and aging also matter.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why do antibiotics work on bacteria but not viruses?* They target bacterial structures (cell walls, ribosomes) that viruses lack.
- *What route does hand-washing interrupt?* Fomite and direct-contact routes.
- *Why does the flu vaccine change yearly?* Influenza mutates; each year's formulation targets the circulating strains.
- *Why is "lifestyle disease" a misleading label?* It implies blame and ignores genetics, environment and access to healthy food and exercise.
- *Short summary of COVID-19 controls.* Combine vaccination, masks, ventilation, testing and isolation; tailor messaging for different communities; adapt as evidence updates.
