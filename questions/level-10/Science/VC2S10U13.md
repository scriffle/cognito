## Core content

**1. Scales of the universe.** A planet orbits a star (solar system). Stars cluster in **galaxies**, which group in clusters and superclusters. The observable universe is about 93 billion light-years across. *Quick check:* Why is "light-year" a measure of distance, not time?

**2. Stars and their lives.** Stars form from collapsing gas clouds, fuse hydrogen into helium and other elements, and end as white dwarfs, neutron stars or black holes depending on mass. Heavy elements in our bodies were forged in earlier-generation stars. *Quick check:* Why is the saying "we are made of stardust" literally true?

**3. Black holes.** A **black hole** is a region where gravity is so strong that not even light escapes. Stellar-mass black holes form from massive star collapse; supermassive black holes sit in galactic centres. Direct images (event horizon) confirm their existence. *Quick check:* Why does black-hole evidence rely on indirect signals as well as direct imaging?

**4. The Big Bang model.** The **Big Bang theory** says the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago in a very hot, dense state and has been expanding and cooling ever since. It is not an explosion in space; it is the expansion of space itself. *Quick check:* Why is "where did the Big Bang happen?" the wrong question?

**5. Evidence for the Big Bang.** Hubble's observation of galactic **redshift** shows distant galaxies moving away. The **cosmic microwave background** (CMB) is faint radiation left from the early universe, predicted before it was found. Light-element abundances and large-scale structure also fit predictions. *Quick check:* Why is independent evidence stronger than any single observation?

**6. Worked example: a thought experiment with redshift.** Imagine bread dough with raisins baking. As the dough rises, every raisin moves away from every other raisin. There is no centre. Distant raisins recede faster. Galaxies in our universe behave similarly. Hubble's law: speed = H₀ × distance. *Follow the thinking:* analogy → measurement (redshift) → relationship (Hubble's law) → infer expansion.

**7. Open questions.** What are dark matter and dark energy? How did the first galaxies form? What happens at a singularity? The model explains a great deal but leaves these for current research. *Quick check:* Why do open questions strengthen rather than weaken the model?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Swinburne University's Centre for Astrophysics and Supercomputing runs cosmological simulations and public outreach. Mt Burnett Observatory in the Dandenong Ranges runs public viewing nights.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have observed and recorded sky structures for thousands of years. Yolŋu and Boorong knowledge of the Milky Way and dark constellations align with concepts in modern astronomy. Researchers like Duane Hamacher have published peer-reviewed work on this. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Astronomers from Indian, Chinese and European-Australian backgrounds at Swinburne, Macquarie and the Australian Astronomical Observatory contribute to international cosmology research.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Big Bang was an explosion in space." No. Space itself expanded.
- "We can see the whole universe." No. We can only see the observable part.
- "Stars are unchanging." No. They evolve over millions to billions of years.
- "Aboriginal sky knowledge is myth." No. It is observation-based and increasingly aligned with peer-reviewed research.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Light-year as distance.* It is the distance light travels in one year, used because cosmic distances are too large for kilometres.
2. *Why "stardust" is literal.* Heavy elements (carbon, oxygen, iron) were forged in stars and supernovae; our bodies contain those atoms.
3. *Why indirect black-hole signals matter.* Light cannot escape, so we observe effects on nearby gas, stars and gravitational waves to infer their presence.
4. *Why "where did the Big Bang happen?" is wrong.* The Big Bang happened everywhere. Space itself was very hot and dense, then expanded.
5. *Why independent evidence is stronger.* Each line could be wrong on its own. Together they make alternative explanations very unlikely.
6. *Why open questions strengthen the model.* They show science is still updating, while existing evidence holds firm.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Friend on expansion.* Imagine raisins in baking dough. Every raisin moves away from every other; the dough is not expanding into anything within the analogy. Distant raisins recede faster.
2. *Manningham public-science panel.* A talk on the CMB; a hands-on exhibit on redshift; a panel on Aboriginal astronomy presented with community-approved material.
3. *Cosmology intern.* Initial conditions, gravity, gas physics, and dark-matter behaviour are needed to simulate galaxy formation realistically.
4. *Public funding for astronomy.* Issues: opportunity cost (other priorities) and equity (who benefits from research). Society benefits: technologies (digital cameras, GPS, communications) and educational inspiration.
5. *Wurundjeri sky knowledge.* Discuss with permission and credit. Pair Wurundjeri seasonal indicators with Western seasonal definitions where appropriate. Use only published, community-approved material.
