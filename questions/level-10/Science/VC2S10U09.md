## Core content

**1. Synthesis reactions.** **Synthesis** combines two or more reactants into one product: A + B → AB. Examples: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O; rusting (4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃). *Quick check:* Why is photosynthesis a synthesis reaction at the system level?

**2. Decomposition reactions.** **Decomposition** breaks one reactant into two or more products: AB → A + B. Examples: hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen; calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide on heating. *Quick check:* What pattern do "decomposes on heating" reactions share?

**3. Displacement reactions.** **Displacement** swaps elements: A + BC → AC + B. Single displacement: zinc + copper sulfate → zinc sulfate + copper. Double displacement: AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃. *Quick check:* Why does a more reactive metal displace a less reactive one?

**4. Exothermic vs endothermic.** **Exothermic** reactions release energy (combustion, neutralisation); **endothermic** reactions absorb energy (photosynthesis, dissolving ammonium nitrate). Heat changes can be measured with a thermometer. *Quick check:* Why does an instant cold pack feel cold?

**5. Worked example: zinc and hydrochloric acid.** Reaction: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂. Type: single displacement; also exothermic. Rate factors: more concentrated HCl → faster reaction; warmer temperature → faster; smaller zinc pieces (more surface area) → faster. *Follow the thinking:* identify reaction type → predict products → consider energy change → list rate factors.

**6. Collision theory.** Reaction rate depends on the frequency and energy of collisions. Higher temperature, higher concentration, smaller particle size and catalysts all increase effective collisions or lower the energy barrier. *Quick check:* Why does a catalyst lower the energy barrier without being consumed?

**7. Real-world rate management.** Refrigerators slow food spoilage. Catalytic converters speed up exhaust-gas reactions. Industries optimise temperature and pressure for yield and safety. *Quick check:* Why is too high a temperature sometimes bad for industrial yield?

## Watch out for these traps
- "Faster reactions are always better." No. Industrial yields often peak at moderate rates.
- "All exothermic reactions are dangerous." No. Many are safe at small scales.
- "Catalysts get used up." No. They are regenerated each cycle.
- "Concentration only matters for solutions." No. Pressure (for gases) plays a similar role.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Photosynthesis as synthesis.* CO₂ and H₂O combine to form glucose and O₂; small molecules build into a larger one.
2. *"Decomposes on heating" pattern.* Heat provides the energy needed to break bonds; many decomposition reactions are endothermic.
3. *Why a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one.* The more reactive metal forms more stable compounds, taking the partner away from the less reactive metal.
4. *Why instant cold packs feel cold.* The dissolution reaction (often ammonium nitrate in water) is endothermic, absorbing heat from the surroundings.
5. *Why catalysts lower the energy barrier.* They provide an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy, but are regenerated, not consumed.
6. *Why too-high temperature can hurt industrial yield.* Some products decompose at high temperatures or shift equilibria backwards.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Rust on a gate.* Synthesis (iron + oxygen + water → iron oxide hydrates), exothermic. Slow it with paint, galvanising, or oil to block oxygen and water.
2. *Manningham composting.* Aerobic decomposition (exothermic), supports microbes; manage moisture and turning to maintain reaction rate; avoid high pile temperatures that kill helpful microbes.
3. *Wine fermentation.* Temperature controls yeast activity; sugar concentration controls alcohol potential; oxygen exposure affects flavour. Trade-off: cooler fermentation keeps flavour delicate but slows the reaction.
4. *Strong-acid demos.* Issues: chemical burns and inhalation hazards. Alternative: dilute acids in small quantities under controlled fume-hood conditions, or use vinegar-based demonstrations.
5. *Wadawurrung roasting.* Discuss with permission and credit. Frame the chemistry: heat drives off water (endothermic), and Maillard reactions develop flavour (exothermic). Use only published, community-approved material.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Industry: Victorian dairy.* Reaction: lactose fermentation by bacteria, lactose → lactic acid + gases. Type: decomposition with biological catalysis. Exothermic. Rate factors: temperature, lactose concentration, bacteria population. Safety: pH monitoring; environmental note: refrigeration energy use.
