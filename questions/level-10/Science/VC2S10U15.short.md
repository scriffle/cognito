# VC2S10U15 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The Law of Conservation of Energy means energy in a system is never lost — only transferred or transformed — and we can measure how efficiently this happens.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Energy never disappears. Some always becomes thermal. Efficiency = useful out / total in.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **energy is conserved**, only **transferred** or **transformed**, and that we can measure **efficiency**.
- You should be able to track energy and judge efficiency.

## Core content

The total energy in a closed system stays constant. **Transfer** moves energy without changing form (heat from pan to hand). **Transformation** changes form (chemical to thermal in a candle). **Efficiency** = useful energy out ÷ total energy in. Modern LEDs are about 30% efficient; incandescent bulbs about 5%. Real systems lose energy to heat, sound, friction or air drag.

A **Sankey diagram** shows energy flows as arrows whose width matches the energy, making losses visible.

**Worked example.** A hydroelectric dam. Water held high has gravitational potential energy. Falling, it gains kinetic energy. Turbines convert kinetic to mechanical to electrical energy. Some energy lost to turbulence and heat. Hydroelectric efficiency is high (often 80–90%) compared with combustion engines (25–40%).

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander practices of cool burning, food preparation and shelter design demonstrate energy-efficient management of Country. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Energy never disappears.
- Some always becomes thermal.
- Efficiency = useful out / total in.
- Sankey makes losses visible.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Energy is created by power stations." No. They transform stored energy.
- "Efficiency over 100% is real." Only because heat pumps move heat, not create it.
- "Renewable means efficient." Not directly; some have lower efficiency but lower fuel cost and emissions.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Kettle efficiency: 90 kJ from 100 kJ.* 90%.
- *Why do brakes get hot?* Kinetic energy converts to thermal energy through friction in the brake pads.
- *Why is sunlight on bricks both transfer and transformation?* Light is transferred from Sun to brick, then transformed into thermal energy as the brick absorbs it.
- *Why does a heat pump appear over 100% efficient?* It moves thermal energy from one place to another; it does not create energy.
- *Short summary of a hydroelectric dam.* Gravitational potential to kinetic to mechanical to electrical, with small losses to turbulence and heat, typically 80–90% efficient.
