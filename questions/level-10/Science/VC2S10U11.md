## Core content

**1. The greenhouse effect.** Earth absorbs sunlight, then radiates heat as infrared. **Greenhouse gases** (water vapour, CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide) absorb some of that heat and re-emit it, warming the lower atmosphere. Without this effect, Earth would be far too cold for life. *Quick check:* Why is the natural greenhouse effect not the problem?

**2. The enhanced greenhouse effect.** Human emissions raise CO₂ and other greenhouse-gas concentrations, increasing **radiative forcing** (the energy imbalance Earth must adjust to). The world warms over time as the system reaches a new balance. *Quick check:* Why does adding CO₂ today still warm the climate decades later?

**3. Sources of emissions.** In Australia, the largest sources are electricity generation, transport, agriculture (especially livestock methane), industry and land-use change. Imported and exported emissions also matter. *Quick check:* Why is "Australia is only 1% of global emissions, so it does not matter" misleading?

**4. Worked example: a Melbourne household's emissions.** Major household sources: electricity (depends on grid mix), gas heating and cooking, petrol vehicles, food (red meat especially), waste, and flights. Mitigation options: switch electricity to renewables, electrify heating and cooking, drive less or switch to EVs, reduce red-meat consumption, reduce flying. Combined actions cut emissions much more than any one alone. *Follow the thinking:* identify sources → estimate sizes → list options → combine actions.

**5. Beyond households.** Industries, governments and international agreements all matter. Policy levers include carbon pricing, renewable mandates, vehicle standards and land-use rules. Behaviour change works best when policy supports it. *Quick check:* Why are individual actions important even though they cannot solve the problem alone?

**6. Adaptation as well as mitigation.** Some change is now locked in. Adaptation means preparing for higher temperatures, sea-level rise, fire risk and water stress. Cities like Melbourne are adapting through urban cooling, drainage upgrades and emergency planning. *Quick check:* Why is "mitigation alone" not enough at this stage?

**7. Equity in action.** Climate impacts hit hardest in low-income and culturally-diverse communities. Mitigation policy can be equitable or regressive depending on design. *Quick check:* Why do carbon-pricing policies often pair with rebates?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Victorian Energy Upgrades program, the Climate Change Authority and DEECA publish regular emissions reports and policy frameworks. Solar Victoria runs household incentives.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cool-burning practices reduce wildfire intensity and emissions; these are recognised in carbon-credit schemes. Joint-management agreements between Victorian Traditional Owner corporations and the state include climate-resilience planning. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual climate communication across Melbourne supports culturally-appropriate adoption of efficiency upgrades. Researchers from Indian, Iranian and Vietnamese-Australian backgrounds lead Australian work on solar materials, batteries and climate models.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Climate has always changed, so this is natural." No. Modern change is faster and tracks human emissions, not natural cycles.
- "It is too late to act." No. Every action reduces eventual warming.
- "Only governments can act." No. Individuals, communities and businesses all matter.
- "Cool burning is just symbolic." No. It demonstrably reduces wildfire emissions.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why is the natural greenhouse effect not the problem?* It keeps Earth warm enough for life. The issue is the additional warming from human-added gases.
2. *Why does today's CO₂ warm the climate decades later?* The system takes time to reach a new balance, and CO₂ stays in the atmosphere for a long time.
3. *"Australia 1% of emissions" misleading.* Per-capita emissions are among the world's highest, exports add to global emissions, and collective action requires every country to cut.
4. *Why individual actions matter.* They lower demand, build social norms and shift markets, even though they cannot solve the problem alone.
5. *Why mitigation alone is not enough.* Some warming is locked in by past emissions; adaptation is needed for current and near-term impacts.
6. *Why pair carbon pricing with rebates.* To prevent low-income households from bearing a disproportionate cost.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Household priorities.* Switch electricity to renewables (cuts the largest household emission source quickly). Electrify heating with a heat pump (replaces fossil-gas use with efficient electricity, lowering emissions further as the grid greens).
2. *Whitehorse climate plan.* Mitigation: building-energy upgrades, public-EV charging, urban tree planting. Adaptation: stormwater capacity upgrades for flash flooding.
3. *Renewable-energy proposal.* Lifecycle emissions, grid integration and reliability, environmental and community impacts on or near the site.
4. *Carbon-pricing debate.* Equity issues: low-income energy burden and rural transport reliance. Safeguard: targeted rebates and support for transitional industries.
5. *Wadawurrung cool burning.* Mitigation: reduces fuel loads and wildfire emissions. Cultural revitalisation: passes knowledge across generations and reinforces connection to Country. Discuss with permission and credit.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Sector: Victorian electricity.* Emissions footprint: largest single source historically, dropping as renewables grow. Mitigation: large-scale solar plus battery storage (high impact, high upfront cost); demand response and efficiency programs (lower cost, immediate impact). Equity: ensure access to energy upgrades for renters and low-income households. Anchor: DEECA.
