# VC2S10I07 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Construct evidence-based arguments that support conclusions or evaluate claims, while considering ethical issues and cultural protocols for using secondary data.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Claim, evidence, reasoning. Anticipate counter-evidence. Open data is not a free pass.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **construct evidence-based arguments** and **consider ethics** when using **secondary data**.
- You should be able to write a clear, fair argument that acknowledges limits.

## Core content

A **claim** is specific and testable. **Evidence** is data that fit the claim. **Reasoning** connects them through a mechanism. Year 10 arguments also acknowledge counter-evidence and name limits. **Secondary data** comes from others (BoM, ABS, EPA Victoria); always check who, when, how, peer review and version, and cite by date.

Reuse of data still requires consent and care. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander data carries additional cultural protocols, guided by the CARE principles (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics).

**Worked example.** Are heatwaves becoming more frequent in Melbourne? Claim: yes, increasing public-health risk. Evidence: BoM long-term temperature data; Department of Health hospital admissions; peer-reviewed analyses. Reasoning: rising baseline shifts the distribution toward extreme days. Counter-evidence: year-to-year variability is large. Conclusion: trend is robust over multi-decade analysis, with stated uncertainty. Ethics: aggregated hospital data; sources cited by date.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS and the Indigenous Knowledge Institute publish CARE-aligned guidelines for Indigenous data governance. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Claim, evidence, reasoning.
- Anticipate counter-evidence.
- Cite source and access date.
- Open data is not a free pass.

## Watch out for these traps
- "More evidence is always better." No. Relevant, well-cited evidence beats a long list.
- "If a graph is in the news, it is reliable." No. Always check source, date and method.
- "Open data has no ethics." No. Re-identification, stereotyping and misuse remain risks.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why does "the data show…" need "because…"?* Reasoning explains the mechanism that links data to claim.
- *Stronger version of "exercise helps mental health".* "Australian school-student studies show small to moderate reductions in depressive symptoms among adolescents meeting daily physical-activity guidelines."
- *Why does citation date matter?* Conditions and methods change. Old data may not represent current realities.
- *Why does open data still need ethics?* Open access does not waive consent or protect against re-identification.
- *Short summary of the heatwave argument.* Multi-decade BoM data and Department of Health admissions support the claim. Counter-evidence is year-to-year variability. Conclusion holds with stated uncertainty.
