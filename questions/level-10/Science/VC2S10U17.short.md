# VC2S10U17 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Newton's laws of motion can be used to quantitatively analyse how force, mass and acceleration are related.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Inertia keeps things moving or still. F = ma is the workhorse. Forces come in pairs.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to use **Newton's laws of motion** to **quantitatively analyse** force, mass and acceleration.
- You should be able to apply F = ma to real situations.

## Core content

**First law (inertia):** an object stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by a net force. **Second law:** F = ma. Force in newtons, mass in kilograms, acceleration in metres per second squared. **Third law:** every action has an equal and opposite reaction on another body.

**Mass** is the amount of matter (kg). **Weight** is the gravitational force on that mass (N). On Earth, weight = mass × 9.8.

**Worked example.** Braking a 1500 kg car from 60 km/h (16.7 m/s) to rest in 3 s. Acceleration = (0 − 16.7)/3 ≈ −5.6 m/s². Force = ma = 1500 × −5.6 = −8400 N. Seatbelts and crumple zones extend stopping time, lowering peak forces.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander throwing technologies (spears, woomeras, boomerangs) reflect deep understanding of force, mass and acceleration. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Inertia keeps things moving or still.
- F = ma is the workhorse.
- Forces come in pairs.
- Mass is matter; weight is force.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If something moves, there must be a net force." No. Constant velocity needs zero net force.
- "Mass and weight are the same." No. Mass is constant; weight depends on gravity.
- "Action and reaction cancel out." No. They act on different bodies.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *60 N on 30 kg trolley.* a = 60/30 = 2 m/s².
- *Rocket reaction pair.* Rocket pushes gas down; gas pushes rocket up.
- *Mass vs weight on the Moon.* Mass stays the same; weight is about one-sixth of Earth weight.
- *Why does longer stopping time reduce force?* Same change in momentum spread over longer time gives lower average force.
- *Short summary of the braking car.* 1500 kg decelerating from 16.7 m/s in 3 s gives a ≈ −5.6 m/s² and F ≈ −8400 N; safety features extend stopping time to lower peak forces on occupants.
