# VC2S10U14 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Wave and particle models describe how energy transfers through different media, and waves have measurable properties like amplitude, wavelength, frequency and speed.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Waves and particles both move energy. v = f × λ. Light is both wave and particle.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **wave** and **particle** models describe **energy transfer**, and that waves have **amplitude**, **wavelength**, **frequency** and **speed**.
- You should be able to use wave equations and pick the right model.

## Core content

A **transverse wave** has oscillations perpendicular to direction of travel (light, water surface). A **longitudinal wave** has oscillations along the direction (sound). **Amplitude** is oscillation size, **wavelength (λ)** is the distance between crests, **frequency (f)** is oscillations per second (hertz), **wave speed (v)** is distance per second. They link as v = f × λ.

Light shows wave behaviour (interference, diffraction) and particle behaviour (photoelectric effect): wave-particle duality. Sound travels faster in solids than liquids than gases, and not at all in vacuum. Reflection bounces, refraction bends, diffraction spreads.

**Worked example.** A guitar string. Vibration depends on length, tension and mass. The string disturbs air, creating a longitudinal sound wave. Pitch reflects frequency; loudness reflects amplitude; timbre reflects the mix of frequencies. v = f × λ applies to both string and air waves.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander instruments (didjeridu, clapsticks, songlines) all work through wave physics. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Transverse perpendicular; longitudinal parallel.
- v = f × λ.
- Light is both wave and particle.
- Medium matters for sound.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Sound is a particle." No. Sound is a wave; particles in the medium oscillate.
- "Light needs a medium." No. Light travels through vacuum.
- "Higher frequency means faster." Not always.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Wave through vacuum.* Electromagnetic, like light or radio.
- *Frequency doubles, speed same.* Wavelength halves.
- *Heard around a corner but not seen.* Diffraction is greater for longer wavelengths; sound waves are much longer than visible light.
- *Why is sound slower in air than in water?* Particles are further apart in gases, so vibrations transmit more slowly.
- *Short summary of the guitar string.* Vibrating string disturbs air to create a longitudinal sound wave; frequency sets pitch and amplitude sets loudness; v = f × λ applies in both string and air.
