# VC2S10U04 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** DNA, chromosomes, genes and alleles work together in genetic inheritance, and Mendelian principles can predict trait ratios in offspring.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** DNA, chromosomes, genes, alleles. Genotype is code; phenotype is appearance. Punnett squares show ratios.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **DNA**, **chromosomes**, **genes** and **alleles** work together in **inheritance**, and that **Mendelian principles** can predict **offspring ratios**.
- You should be able to use a Punnett square confidently.

## Core content

**DNA** stores genetic information using four bases (A, T, G, C). A **gene** is a stretch of DNA that codes for a protein or trait. **Chromosomes** are packaged DNA. An **allele** is a version of a gene. **Homozygous** means two copies of the same allele; **heterozygous** means two different alleles. A **dominant** allele's effect appears with one copy; a **recessive** allele needs two copies. **Genotype** is the genetic combination; **phenotype** is the observable trait.

**Worked example.** Cross of two heterozygous parents (Aa × Aa). Punnett square gives genotype ratio 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa, phenotype ratio 3 dominant : 1 recessive.

Many traits involve multiple genes (height, skin tone), are influenced by environment, or show incomplete or codominance (ABO blood group). Sex-linked recessive traits often appear more in XY individuals, who have only one X.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal-led genomic research, including the National Centre for Indigenous Genomics, develops protocols that respect community ownership and benefit. The CARE principles guide data use. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- DNA, chromosomes, genes, alleles.
- Genotype is code. Phenotype is appearance.
- Punnett squares show ratios.
- Most traits are more complex than one gene.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Genes determine everything." No. Environment and behaviour matter.
- "Each trait is one gene." No. Many traits involve many genes.
- "DNA is destiny." No. Most outcomes reflect gene–environment interaction.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Aa × Aa genotypes.* AA, Aa, Aa, aa.
- *Why AA and Aa look the same.* The dominant allele's effect appears with one copy.
- *Genotype of someone showing a recessive trait.* Homozygous recessive (aa).
- *Why sex-linked recessive traits appear more in XY individuals.* They have only one X, so a single recessive allele is not masked.
- *Short summary of the heterozygous cross.* Punnett square produces 1 : 2 : 1 genotype and 3 : 1 phenotype, giving a 75% chance of the dominant phenotype.
