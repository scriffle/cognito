# VC2S10I04 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Organise, process and summarise data using tables, graphs, descriptive statistics, models, symbols, formulas and mathematical relationships.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Process data before you interpret it. Pick the summary that helps the reader. Simple models beat over-fitting.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **organise**, **process** and **summarise** data using tables, graphs, **descriptive statistics**, models and formulas.
- You should be able to choose the summary that supports the claim.

## Core content

A tidy table has one observation per row, one variable per column, and units in headers. The **mean** is the average. The **median** is the middle value. The **range** is max minus min. The **standard deviation** shows spread around the mean.

Bar charts compare groups. Line graphs show change over a continuous variable like time. Scatter plots show relationships. Box plots show distribution and outliers. Simple relationships like y = mx + c summarise linear data; the gradient m is the rate of change. Spreadsheets need version control: save iterations before major edits.

**Worked example.** Solar panel output across two weeks. Tidy table of day vs kWh. Line graph (continuous time). Compute mean and standard deviation. Add a moving average to smooth daily variability. Fit a linear model: output = a × sunshine_hours + b.

**Cultural context.** Seasonal calendars created by Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung and other Victorian peoples are data summaries: many years of observation summarised into visual seasons and indicators. The Bureau of Meteorology has co-published Indigenous Weather Knowledge calendars. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- One row, one observation. Units in headers.
- Mean, median, range, standard deviation.
- Bar for groups. Line for time. Scatter for relations.
- Simple models beat over-fitted curves.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Mean is always the best summary." No. Skewed data are better described with median and range.
- "A trendline proves causation." No. It summarises shape, not cause.
- "Spreadsheets cannot be wrong." No. Formula errors cause major public data mistakes.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *When is median better than mean?* When data are skewed or contain outliers.
- *Chart for skew and outliers in one picture.* Box plot.
- *What does the gradient m mean in y = mx + c?* The rate of change of y with respect to x.
- *When does a curve fit better than a line?* When the relationship is non-linear, like saturation, growth or decay.
- *Short summary of the solar panel example.* Tidy daily data, summarise with mean and standard deviation, plot a line graph with moving average, and fit output against sunshine hours as a linear model.
