# VC2S10I06 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Evaluate the validity and reproducibility of investigation methods and conclusions by identifying assumptions, biases, errors and areas of uncertainty.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Evaluate as you go. Validity, reproducibility, bias and uncertainty each ask different questions. Naming limits strengthens the conclusion.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **evaluate validity** and **reproducibility** by identifying **assumptions**, **biases**, **errors** and **uncertainty**.
- You should be able to grade your own and others' work with structured questions.

## Core content

**Validity** asks whether the study measures what it claims. **Reproducibility** asks whether the same approach produces similar results. **Bias** is non-random influence (selection, observer, confirmation). **Confounding** is when a third variable explains an apparent relationship. Random errors fall with repetition; systematic errors survive unless calibration or design changes.

Quantified uncertainty (sample size, measurement precision, generalisability) supports trust. "Suggests" beats "proves".

**Worked example.** Evaluating a "phones and grades" survey. Validity: self-report is imperfect; grades may not capture learning. Reproducibility: another class could repeat with the same survey, but answers may shift with context. Bias: respondents who feel strongly may answer more. Confounding: sleep, socio-economic status, study habits. Follow-up: use phone-app logs and standardised tests in a larger sample with consent.

**Cultural context.** Country Needs People and the Indigenous Knowledge Institute publish program evaluations that list cultural protocols and limitations. Western evaluation can sit alongside community-led review. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Evaluate as you go.
- Validity, reproducibility, bias, error, uncertainty.
- Confounding hides in plain sight.
- "Suggests" beats "proves".

## Watch out for these traps
- "Evaluation is just self-criticism." No. It is what makes a finding trustworthy.
- "Bigger samples remove all bias." No. Bias is non-random.
- "Uncertainty undermines authority." No. Quantified uncertainty supports trust.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Validity threat in self-report.* Social desirability bias, where respondents answer to look good rather than honestly.
- *Why are internal repeats weaker than independent replication?* They cannot rule out lab-specific quirks.
- *Confounder for breakfast and grades.* Family routines: families ensuring breakfast may also ensure homework and sleep, which influence grades.
- *Error that survives repetition.* Systematic error: a miscalibrated sensor keeps producing biased results.
- *Short summary of evaluating the phones-grades survey.* Validity, reproducibility, bias and confounders all limit the conclusion. A larger, consented study with objective phone-use logs and standardised tests would strengthen the finding.
