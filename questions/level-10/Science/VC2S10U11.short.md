# VC2S10U11 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Global climate change results from interactions between greenhouse gas emissions and energy exchanges within Earth's systems, and mitigating it requires addressing multiple human activities.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Greenhouse gases trap heat. Today's emissions warm tomorrow. Mitigation needs many actions, not one.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **climate change** comes from **greenhouse gas emissions** and **energy exchanges**, and that **mitigation** requires **multiple actions**.
- You should be able to weigh actions and explain mechanisms.

## Core content

Earth absorbs sunlight and radiates infrared. **Greenhouse gases** absorb and re-emit some of that heat, warming the lower atmosphere. The natural effect makes life possible; the **enhanced** effect comes from human emissions, raising **radiative forcing**.

In Australia, the largest sources are electricity, transport, agriculture, industry and land-use change. **Mitigation** reduces emissions; **adaptation** prepares for changes already locked in. Policy levers include carbon pricing, renewable mandates, vehicle standards and land-use rules. Equity matters: climate impacts hit hardest in low-income and culturally-diverse communities.

**Worked example.** A Melbourne household. Major sources: electricity, gas heating and cooking, petrol vehicles, food, waste, flights. Mitigation: switch electricity to renewables, electrify heating and cooking, drive less or switch to EVs, reduce red-meat consumption, reduce flying. Combined actions cut emissions much more than any one alone.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cool-burning practices reduce wildfire intensity and emissions; these are recognised in carbon-credit schemes. Joint-management agreements between Victorian Traditional Owner corporations and the state include climate-resilience planning. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Greenhouse gases trap heat.
- Many sectors, many actions.
- Mitigation now, adaptation also.
- Equity shapes what works.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Climate has always changed, so this is natural." No. Modern change tracks human emissions.
- "It is too late to act." No. Every action reduces eventual warming.
- "Only governments can act." No. Individuals, communities and businesses all matter.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is the natural greenhouse effect not the problem?* It keeps Earth liveable. The issue is added warming from human emissions.
- *Why does today's CO₂ warm the climate decades later?* CO₂ stays in the atmosphere a long time, and the system takes years to reach a new balance.
- *Why is "Australia is only 1% of emissions" misleading?* Per-capita emissions are high, exports add to global emissions, and collective action requires every country to cut.
- *Why pair carbon pricing with rebates?* To prevent low-income households from bearing a disproportionate cost.
- *Short summary of household mitigation.* Switching electricity to renewables, electrifying heating and reducing high-emission travel and food choices, combined, cut household emissions far more than any single action.
