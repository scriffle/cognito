# VC2S10H02 — Science as a Human Endeavour

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2S10H02. Advances in technology enable advances in science, and scientific discoveries in turn drive new technologies and engineering.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Science (Science as a Human Endeavour).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Science and technology shape each other.
  - New tools open new questions.
  - New discoveries create new tools.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Critical and Creative Thinking; Digital Literacy; Sustainability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **advances in technology enable advances in science**, and that **scientific discoveries in turn drive new technologies and engineering**.
- You are learning to trace the back-and-forth between knowledge and tools.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **technology**, **engineering** and **research and development (R&D)** in your own words.
- Give one example where a new tool unlocked a new field.
- Give one example where a discovery led to new technology.
- Identify a current Victorian or Australian project that fits each pattern.

## Core content

**1. Tools open eyes.** Every leap in observation has been led by new tools. Galileo's telescope, the electron microscope, MRI scanners, gene sequencers and the JWST each opened previously unreachable scales. *Quick check:* Why did the discovery of cells follow the development of the microscope?

**2. Discoveries become products.** Once a phenomenon is understood, engineering can apply it. Quantum understanding became transistors. Genetic discoveries became genetically modified vaccines. Materials science became new battery types. *Quick check:* Why is "basic research" sometimes the foundation of products that appear decades later?

**3. R&D as a feedback loop.** **Research and development** combines exploration of new ideas (research) with practical application (development). Most modern industries depend on the loop. *Quick check:* Why does breaking the loop, by funding only development, slow long-term progress?

**4. Worked example: the synchrotron at Clayton.** The Australian Synchrotron uses an accelerator to generate intense beams of light used to study materials, biology and chemistry. The technology came out of high-energy physics; today it supports research from cancer drugs to airline alloys. The discoveries here, in turn, lead to better medicines, materials and industrial processes. *Follow the thinking:* technology enables → research generates discoveries → discoveries enable new technology.

**5. Speed of feedback.** Some loops close fast (mRNA vaccine platforms during COVID-19); others span decades (laser physics to barcode scanners and fibre-optic internet). Both shapes of progress exist, side by side. *Quick check:* Why is patience sometimes part of science policy?

**6. Limits and risks.** Tools can record more than they should. Sequencing data raises privacy questions; surveillance technology raises civil-liberties questions. Engineering choices include who controls the tool. *Quick check:* Why is it not enough to ask "can we" without asking "should we"?

**7. Australian capacity.** Australia leads in some fields (medical research, agricultural science, astronomy) and follows in others. Funding decisions shape where the loop strengthens. *Quick check:* What is one Australian research strength that has produced both knowledge and exports?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Australian Synchrotron, the Walter and Eliza Hall Institute, CSIRO Clayton and the Monash Institute of Pharmaceutical Sciences all sit close together. Their cluster is Victoria's research backbone.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander engineering traditions, including aquaculture (the Budj Bim eel traps on Gunditjmara Country), fire mosaics and seasonal calendars, integrate observation, experiment and refinement. The Indigenous Knowledge Institute publishes peer-reviewed work on these. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Researchers from Indian, Iranian and Anglo-Australian backgrounds at RMIT, Swinburne and Melbourne lead R&D in batteries, biotech and aerospace. Their work links Australian universities to global supply chains.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You upload a photo to a free image-recognition app. Trace the science and technologies that made the app possible.
2. **Civic.** Your council in Knox plans to invest in a tech precinct. List three R&D outcomes that would justify the public spend over a 10-year horizon.
3. **Vocational.** A junior research engineer at a Bendigo battery start-up presents progress. Outline three ways their work depends on prior physics and chemistry research.
4. **Ethical.** A class debates an Australian-led generative-AI project. Identify two ethical risks and one safeguard the project should adopt.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Gunditjmara Traditional Owners and the Heritage Council of Victoria collaborate on documenting Budj Bim. Outline how the research and engineering knowledge could be shared respectfully.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Basic research is wasted money." No. Most modern technologies trace back to curiosity-driven work.
- "Tools just measure; they do not shape thinking." No. Tools determine what questions can be asked at all.
- "Innovation is a single person's idea." No. Most modern innovation is collective and incremental.
- "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander engineering is folklore, not science." No. Sites like Budj Bim demonstrate sustained engineering and refinement.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why cells were discovered after microscopes.* The structures are too small to see with the unaided eye. Magnification opened a new scale that earlier observers could not access.
2. *Why basic research underpins later products.* Curiosity-driven discoveries identify general principles. Engineering then turns those principles into products, often years or decades later.
3. *Why funding only development slows progress.* Without basic research, the pipeline of new ideas thins. Long-term innovation depends on the upstream half of the loop.
4. *Why patience is policy.* Loops sometimes take decades to close. Cutting funding for slow loops leaves a country with fewer breakthroughs in the long run.
5. *"Can we" vs "should we".* Capability does not justify use. Ethical, social and environmental questions belong alongside technical ones.
6. *Australian research strength with exports.* Medical research has produced treatments and devices that are licensed worldwide; agricultural science has produced drought-tolerant crops.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Image-recognition app.* Maths and statistics for machine-learning models, neural-network research from the 1980s onward, GPU hardware for fast training, cloud infrastructure, smartphone cameras, all building on physics and computer science.
2. *Knox tech precinct.* Patents and licences from joint research; trained workforce; local manufacturing tied to research; partnerships with universities and TAFEs.
3. *Bendigo battery start-up.* Solid-state physics for materials, chemistry for electrolytes, electrochemistry for cell design. Each draws on basic research.
4. *Generative-AI project.* Risks: bias and misinformation; privacy and consent for training data. Safeguard: published model cards, dataset documentation, and a misuse-response plan.
5. *Budj Bim documentation.* Co-authored under partnership with Gunditjmara Traditional Owners. Use only published, community-approved material. Translate engineering insights into outputs that benefit the community first.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Invention: the cochlear implant (Australian-led).* Science enabling: physiology of hearing, electronics, materials science. Tools created: bionic ear externals and improved testing equipment. Timeline: 1970s research, commercialisation in the 1980s, ongoing improvement. Sources cited with date.
