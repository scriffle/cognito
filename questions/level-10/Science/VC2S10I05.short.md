# VC2S10I05 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Analyse and compare processed data to identify and explain qualitative and quantitative patterns, trends, relationships and anomalies.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Numbers and shapes together. Compare like with like. Investigate anomalies before deleting.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **analyse** and **compare** processed data to **identify and explain** patterns, trends, relationships and anomalies.
- You should be able to connect what you see to the model behind the study.

## Core content

Describe trends with direction, magnitude, timeframe and uncertainty: "Annual mean temperature rose by about 1.2°C from 1960 to 2020, with year-to-year variability of about ±0.5°C." **Correlation** is when two variables move together; causation needs a mechanism and other evidence. Anomalies can be measurement errors, recording errors, real outliers or signals of an event. Investigate before deleting.

Be cautious about overclaiming. A small effect or low sample size is fragile. Multiple comparisons inflate false positives. Use confidence intervals and pre-registered analyses where possible.

**Worked example.** Compare canopy cover and surface temperature in Hampton vs Sunshine, 2015–2024. Quantitative trend: Sunshine warmed faster. Qualitative observation: more dark surfaces in Sunshine. Relationship: canopy correlates with cooler surface temperatures. Anomaly: 2022 cool summer reduced the gap. Hypothesised cause: shade and evapotranspiration. Follow-up: control for density and elevation.

**Cultural context.** Long-running observations by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge holders capture qualitative and quantitative changes in Country, such as seasonal indicators and species abundance. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Quantitative and qualitative together.
- Direction, magnitude, timeframe, uncertainty.
- Compare like with like.
- Investigate, then explain anomalies.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Two things moving together must be linked." No. Coincidence is common.
- "Anomalies are mistakes." No. Investigate first.
- "Aboriginal ecological observations are anecdotal." No. They are long-running, systematic, increasingly peer-reviewed.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Proper trend statement vs "it got hotter".* The proper version states direction, magnitude, timeframe and uncertainty, making it testable.
- *Why a 10-year vs 100-year comparison misleads.* Different averaging periods include different climate patterns, inflating or hiding effects.
- *A spurious correlation.* Per-capita cheese consumption and bedsheet-tangling deaths.
- *Why do multiple comparisons inflate false positives?* Running many tests means some look significant by chance alone.
- *Short summary of the Hampton vs Sunshine analysis.* Canopy correlates with cooler surface temperatures, Sunshine warmed faster over the decade, and the 2022 anomaly reflects a cooler summer rather than a change in the relationship.
