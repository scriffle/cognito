# VC2S10U13 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The universe contains galaxies, stars, solar systems and black holes, and the Big Bang theory — supported by evidence — models the origin and evolution of the universe.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Planets, stars, galaxies, clusters, universe. Big Bang is space expanding, not exploding. Multiple evidence lines support the model.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that the **universe** contains **galaxies**, **stars**, **solar systems** and **black holes**, and that the **Big Bang theory** is supported by **evidence**.
- You should be able to read evidence and explain the model.

## Core content

A planet orbits a star (solar system). Stars cluster in **galaxies**, then clusters and superclusters. The observable universe is about 93 billion light-years across. Stars form heavy elements through fusion, ending as white dwarfs, neutron stars or **black holes** depending on mass.

The **Big Bang theory** says the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago in a hot, dense state and has been expanding ever since. It is not an explosion in space; space itself expanded. Evidence: galactic **redshift** (Hubble's law), the **cosmic microwave background** (CMB), light-element abundances and large-scale structure.

**Worked example.** A dough analogy with raisins. As the dough rises, every raisin moves away from every other raisin. There is no centre. Distant raisins recede faster. Galaxies behave similarly. Hubble's law: speed = H₀ × distance.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have observed and recorded sky structures for thousands of years. Yolŋu and Boorong knowledge of the Milky Way and dark constellations align with concepts in modern astronomy. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Planets, stars, galaxies, clusters, universe.
- Stars made the heavy elements.
- Big Bang is space expanding.
- Evidence: redshift, CMB, light elements.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Big Bang was an explosion in space." No. Space itself expanded.
- "We can see the whole universe." No. We can only see the observable part.
- "Aboriginal sky knowledge is myth." No. It is observation-based and increasingly peer-reviewed where shared.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Light-year as distance.* The distance light travels in one year, used for cosmic distances.
- *Why is "stardust" literal?* Heavy elements were forged in stars and supernovae; our bodies contain those atoms.
- *Why is "where did the Big Bang happen?" wrong?* It happened everywhere. Space itself was hot and dense, then expanded.
- *Why are independent evidence lines stronger?* Each could be questioned alone; together they are hard to explain by anything else.
- *Short summary of the dough analogy.* Every raisin moves away from every other raisin as the dough rises; distant raisins recede faster, mirroring galactic redshift in an expanding universe.
