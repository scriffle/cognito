# VC2S10H01 — Science as a Human Endeavour (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Scientific knowledge is validated and refined through methods such as replication, peer review, publication and building consensus, and it remains open to challenge.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Knowledge is built in public. Replication beats reputation. Always open to revision.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that scientific knowledge is **validated** and **refined** through **replication**, **peer review**, **publication** and **consensus**, and remains **open to challenge**.
- You should be able to judge a claim using these checks.

## Core content

**Replication** is doing a study again, often by independent teams, to see if the result holds. The "replication crisis" of the 2010s revealed how often single results do not survive. **Peer review** is critical reading by other experts before publication; it is imperfect but improves drafts. Publication in peer-reviewed journals lets others build on or challenge a finding. **Scientific consensus** emerges when independent lines of evidence point the same way, summarised by bodies like the IPCC.

Even consensus knowledge is open to revision. Real expertise sounds humble about uncertainty.

**Worked example.** Marshall and Warren proposed a bacterial cause of stomach ulcers in the early 1980s. Replication, larger trials and broader peer review followed. Consensus shifted, treatment changed, and they won the 2005 Nobel Prize. The same processes rejected cold fusion in 1989.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge systems have their own validation through community elders, intergenerational testing and consensus. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Knowledge is built in public.
- Replication beats reputation.
- Consensus is many lines, not many voices.
- Always open to revision.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Peer review proves a study is correct." No. It increases the chance, not the certainty.
- "Consensus is just opinion." No. It emerges from independent lines of evidence.
- "Aboriginal knowledge has no validation." No. It uses different processes including elders, community testing, and intergenerational consensus.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is "trust me" not a scientific argument?* Science depends on transparent methods, open data, replication and peer review.
- *Why is independent replication stronger than the same lab repeating itself?* It rules out lab-specific equipment, samples or biases.
- *Why is "settled science" sometimes misleading?* It implies finality. Even strong findings can be refined by new evidence.
- *Trade-off of pre-prints during COVID-19.* Faster sharing helped urgent decisions but raised the risk of acting on unverified data.
- *Short summary of the H. pylori story.* Initial claim, independent replication, peer review and consensus shift; treatment moved to antibiotics; the same public processes rejected cold fusion claims.
