# VC2S10U07 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The periodic table organises elements by atomic structure, revealing patterns in properties such as reactivity, metallic character and atomic size across rows and groups.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Position predicts properties. Group = same valence electrons. Noble gases are happy alone.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that the **periodic table** organises elements by **atomic structure**, revealing **patterns** in **reactivity**, **metallic character** and **atomic size**.
- You should be able to predict element behaviour from position.

## Core content

A **period** is a row; a **group** is a column. Elements in the same group share **valence electrons** and chemical behaviour. Metals dominate the left and centre; non-metals sit on the right; metalloids run a stair-step boundary. **Group 1 alkali metals** become more reactive going down (electrons easier to lose). **Group 17 halogens** become less reactive going down. Atomic size decreases across a period and increases down a group. **Group 18 noble gases** are largely unreactive because their outer shells are full.

**Worked example.** Magnesium (Mg) is in Group 2, Period 3. Two valence electrons, easily lost, forming Mg²⁺. Reacts with oxygen to form MgO; with water (slowly) to form Mg(OH)₂. Burns with a bright white flame. Position predicts behaviour.

Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic mass and predicted gaps for unknown elements. Confirmed predictions made the framework convincing. The modern table arranges by atomic number.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge of metals and minerals includes use of ochres (iron oxides) and mineral-rich locations. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Position predicts properties.
- Group = same valence electrons.
- Reactivity trends depend on electron arrangement.
- Noble gases are happy alone.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Reactivity always increases down a group." Only for some (alkali metals); for halogens it decreases.
- "Noble gases never react." Mostly true, but some heavier noble gases form rare compounds.
- "Bigger atom means heavier." Not always. Mass and size depend on different factors.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why are alkali metals stored under oil?* They react vigorously with water and oxygen.
- *Why is argon used in welding?* It shields hot metal from oxygen and nitrogen, preventing oxidation.
- *Magnesium vs calcium size.* Calcium is larger; it has one more electron shell.
- *Why was predicting unknown elements a strong test?* Confirmed predictions show the framework matches nature.
- *Short summary of magnesium chemistry.* Group 2 metal, two valence electrons, forms Mg²⁺, reacts with oxygen and slowly with water, burns brightly.
