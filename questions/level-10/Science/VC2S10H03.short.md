# VC2S10H03 — Science as a Human Endeavour (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Using science to address real-world sustainability issues can produce diverse outcomes, and the extent to which society adopts scientific solutions depends on their projected impacts.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Science offers options. Adoption depends on impacts. Trade-offs need to be open.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that science applied to **sustainability** produces **diverse outcomes**, and that **adoption** depends on **projected impacts**.
- You should be able to compare two solutions and recommend one with caveats.

## Core content

**Sustainability** is meeting present needs without preventing future generations from meeting theirs. **Impact assessment** estimates effects on environment, economy, society, health and equity. Adoption depends on cost, trust, community engagement and politics. The **precautionary principle** supports action where harm could be severe and irreversible, even with imperfect data.

Even effective solutions stall if people, businesses or governments do not act. A solution that is best on average may still harm specific communities. Naming assumptions matters as much as naming numbers.

**Worked example.** Closing brown-coal plants in the Latrobe Valley involves climate science, engineering, economics, social policy and Indigenous policy. Diverse projected outcomes mean different paths are debated: faster transition with more support, slower transition with cost protection, or rapid renewables and battery build-out. Each path balances trade-offs differently.

**Cultural context.** Victorian Traditional Owner corporations co-author land and water management plans that combine scientific monitoring with long-running cultural knowledge of Country. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Science provides options.
- Impacts decide adoption.
- Trade-offs need to be open.
- Vulnerable communities need protection.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Science decides which solution wins." No. Science narrows the options. Society chooses.
- "If a project is good for the climate, all impacts must be good." No. Most projects produce trade-offs.
- "Aboriginal consent is just process." No. It is essential and often legally required.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is "the science" not "the answer"?* Science maps the territory and offers options. Choosing depends on values, costs and politics.
- *Why do assumptions matter as much as numbers?* The same data can lead to different projections under different assumptions. Naming them lets others test the result.
- *One design feature that protects vulnerable communities.* Targeted rebates, training programs or distributed ownership shares.
- *Why environmental laws name uncertainty.* It allows action under partial information when delay would cause severe or irreversible harm.
- *Short summary of the Latrobe Valley decision.* Several scientifically valid paths exist. Choosing among them weighs emissions, cost, jobs, community impact and Country, with trade-offs that should be made openly.
