# VC2S10U10 — Science Understanding

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2S10U10. Carbon cycles through Earth's systems via processes like photosynthesis, respiration, combustion and volcanic activity, and human actions affect this cycle.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Science (Earth & Space Sciences).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Carbon moves between living things, atmosphere, oceans and rocks.
  - Natural processes balance the cycle over long timescales.
  - Human actions are pushing carbon out of long-term storage faster than nature returns it.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Sustainability; Critical and Creative Thinking; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **carbon cycles** through Earth's systems via processes like **photosynthesis**, **respiration**, **combustion** and **volcanic activity**, and that **human actions** affect this cycle.
- You are learning to trace carbon between reservoirs and explain modern shifts.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **carbon reservoir**, **flux**, **photosynthesis** and **respiration** in your own words.
- Identify five major carbon reservoirs.
- Describe two natural and two human-driven fluxes.
- Explain why the modern atmosphere is gaining carbon faster than nature can remove it.

## Core content

**1. Reservoirs and fluxes.** A **carbon reservoir** is a place carbon is stored: atmosphere, oceans, rocks, soils, biosphere, fossil fuels. A **flux** is the movement between them. Most reservoirs hold carbon for very different times. *Quick check:* Why is a coral reef both a reservoir and a flux site?

**2. Photosynthesis and respiration.** **Photosynthesis** moves carbon from CO₂ (atmosphere) into organic molecules (biosphere) in plants and algae. **Respiration** does the reverse, returning CO₂ to the atmosphere. Together they balance over short timescales. *Quick check:* Why does atmospheric CO₂ rise and fall slightly each year in northern hemisphere data?

**3. Combustion and decomposition.** **Combustion** burns organic material, releasing CO₂. Decomposition does it more slowly through microbes. Wildfires accelerate both. *Quick check:* Why does a forest fire produce a sudden CO₂ spike that can take decades to reabsorb?

**4. Slow geological fluxes.** **Volcanic activity** releases CO₂ from deep Earth. Weathering of silicate rocks pulls CO₂ into oceans and eventually into limestone. These fluxes balance over millions of years. *Quick check:* Why does volcanic emission alone not explain modern atmospheric CO₂ rise?

**5. Worked example: a coal-fired power station in the Latrobe Valley.** Reservoir: brown-coal seams (long-term carbon storage from ancient plants). Flux: combustion releases CO₂ to the atmosphere. The atmosphere passes some to the ocean (acidifying it) and some to the biosphere (plant uptake). Cycle imbalance: combustion adds carbon faster than oceans and biosphere can absorb. Net result: rising atmospheric CO₂. *Follow the thinking:* identify reservoirs → trace flux → check rates → explain imbalance.

**6. Human-driven changes.** Burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas), land clearing, cement production and intensive agriculture all add carbon. Reforestation, soil restoration and ocean protection slow the rise. *Quick check:* Why is land clearing both a one-time and an ongoing flux?

**7. Implications.** Rising atmospheric CO₂ amplifies the greenhouse effect, warming the climate. Oceans absorb some, becoming more acidic. Both shifts have ecological and human consequences. *Quick check:* Why is "carbon cycle" the right framing rather than "carbon emissions"?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Victorian Government's Emissions Reduction Target Act and the Climate Change Authority publish inventories that track reservoirs and fluxes for the state. Coastal mangroves and forest regeneration projects across Victoria store carbon.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cool-burning practices in northern Australia and parts of Victoria reduce wildfire intensity and carbon release; carbon-credit schemes recognise this work. The Indigenous Knowledge Institute publishes peer-reviewed work on these traditions. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant farming communities across Victoria, including Italian-Australian, Vietnamese-Australian and Sudanese-Australian growers, manage soil-carbon practices: composting, cover crops and agroforestry. These build long-term carbon storage in soils.

## Memorable takeaways
- Carbon moves through reservoirs.
- Photosynthesis and respiration balance over short time.
- Combustion and clearing add carbon fast.
- Slow rocks balance over millions of years.
- Modern imbalance comes from us.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Carbon dioxide is just pollution." No. CO₂ is part of a natural cycle. The problem is the speed and scale of change.
- "Trees solve climate change." They help, but cannot fully offset fossil-fuel emissions.
- "Volcanoes cause more CO₂ than humans." No. Human emissions are about 100 times larger annually.
- "Aboriginal land management is just history." No. It is a current part of carbon-cycle policy.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Coral reef as both reservoir and flux site.* Coral skeletons store carbon in carbonate; the reef also exchanges carbon with seawater through photosynthesis, respiration and dissolution.
2. *Northern-hemisphere annual CO₂ wiggle.* Photosynthesis dominates in summer (CO₂ falls), respiration and decay dominate in winter (CO₂ rises).
3. *Fire-driven CO₂ spike.* Combustion releases stored carbon quickly; regrowth absorbs it slowly, so the imbalance lasts decades.
4. *Volcanism alone insufficient.* Volcanic emissions are far smaller than human emissions; modern CO₂ rise tracks closely with fossil-fuel use.
5. *Land clearing as one-time and ongoing flux.* Clearing releases stored carbon (one-time pulse), but the lost forest cannot keep absorbing, so the deficit continues.
6. *"Cycle" vs "emissions" framing.* Cycle highlights flows in and out; emissions alone hide where the carbon goes and how reservoirs interact.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Goulburn apple.* Atmospheric CO₂ → photosynthesis in apple-tree leaves → glucose → fruit → your digestion → respiration releases CO₂ back to atmosphere; some carbon enters bones, soils and decomposers.
2. *Cardinia tree-planting.* Trees absorb CO₂ via photosynthesis, store it in wood and soil, and cool local microclimates. Limit: trees alone cannot offset fossil-fuel emissions, and forests can release carbon if disturbed.
3. *Coal-mine extension review.* Reservoir size; expected combustion fluxes; downstream effects on ocean acidification.
4. *Carbon-credit debate.* Issues: equity (who benefits and who pays) and integrity (whether credits represent real, additional and lasting reductions).
5. *Wadawurrung savanna burning.* Discuss with permission and credit. Frame the science: cool burning reduces fuel loads, which lowers wildfire intensity and net carbon release. Use only published, community-approved material.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Issue: Victorian peatland degradation in alpine areas.* Reservoirs: peat carbon stored over millennia. Fluxes: drying and burning release CO₂ rapidly. Human actions: drainage, hooved animals, fire frequency. Solution: peatland rehydration, fencing and integrated fire management. Anchor: Parks Victoria.
