# VC2S10U08 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Chemical reactions involve the rearrangement of atoms and are governed by the Law of Conservation of Mass, and they can be represented by balanced equations.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Atoms rearrange, never disappear. Mass before equals mass after. Balanced equations capture the bookkeeping.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that reactions **rearrange atoms** and obey the **Law of Conservation of Mass**, and that they can be shown as **balanced equations**.
- You should be able to read, write and balance simple equations.

## Core content

A **chemical reaction** breaks bonds and forms new ones. Atoms are not created or destroyed; they swap partners. Lavoisier showed mass is conserved using sealed-vessel experiments. A **balanced equation** has equal numbers of each kind of atom on both sides; coefficients are adjusted to balance. **Reactants** are on the left, **products** on the right, with state symbols (s, l, g, aq).

Common reaction types: combustion (hydrocarbon + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O); acid + base → salt + water; metal + acid → salt + hydrogen; decomposition (AB → A + B).

**Worked example.** Combustion of methane: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. Atom count: 1 C and 4 H on each side; 4 O on each side. Mass before equals mass after. Balanced equations let chemists calculate quantities for industry, lab and medicine.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander processing of foods, ochres, fibres and medicines involves chemical reactions. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Atoms rearrange, never disappear.
- Mass before equals mass after.
- Coefficients balance equations.
- Real chemistry depends on balanced bookkeeping.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Mass disappears in fire." No. Gases produced have mass.
- "Coefficients can be any number." No. Use the smallest whole-number ratios.
- "State symbols are decoration." No. They describe phase, which affects reactions and observations.

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *(aq) meaning.* Dissolved in water (aqueous solution).
- *NaOH + HCl products.* NaCl + H₂O.
- *Why does burning paper appear to destroy mass?* The CO₂ and water vapour escape into air; in a closed system mass is conserved.
