# VC2M10A02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Simplify algebraic products and quotients using exponent laws.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Exponent laws are shortcuts for multiplying and dividing repeated factors. They work for numerical and algebraic bases and extend to zero and negative exponents.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **simplify algebraic products and quotients** by applying the **exponent (index) laws**.
- You should handle expressions with multiple variables and combined operations.

## Core content

Exponents mean repeated multiplication: x⁴ = x × x × x × x. The laws:

- **Multiplication:** aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ (add exponents). Example: 3x² × 4x³ = 12x⁵.
- **Division:** aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (subtract exponents). Example: 20b⁶ ÷ 5b² = 4b⁴.
- **Power of a power:** (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ (multiply exponents). Example: (x³)⁴ = x¹².
- **Power of a product:** (2x³)² = 4x⁶ (apply the power to coefficient and variable).
- **Zero exponent:** a⁰ = 1. **Negative exponent:** a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ.

**Strategy for complex expressions:** (1) expand powers of products; (2) multiply numerator terms (add exponents per variable); (3) divide by denominator (subtract exponents); (4) simplify coefficients.

**Worked example.** (6a³b²)(2a²b⁴) ÷ (3a⁴b³) = 12a⁵b⁶ ÷ 3a⁴b³ = 4ab³.

**Victorian context.** Scientists at CSIRO and the Australian Synchrotron rely on exponent laws for scientific notation. Exponential models date First Nations sites like Madjedbebe (65,000+ years via carbon-dating algebra).

## Memorable takeaways
- Multiply: add exponents. Divide: subtract. Power of power: multiply.
- a⁰ = 1. a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ.
- Apply powers to coefficients too: (3x²)³ = 27x⁶.
- Coefficients multiply/divide separately from variables.

## Watch out for these traps
- Adding exponents for power of a power — you should multiply them.
- Forgetting to apply the power to the coefficient: (3x²)³ = 27x⁶, not 3x⁶.
- Thinking a⁰ = 0 — it equals 1 (from aⁿ ÷ aⁿ = 1).
