# VC2M10A08 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M10A08. Solve linear inequalities and graph their solutions on a number line.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics (Algebra).
- **Big ideas:**
  - An inequality states that one expression is greater than, less than, or not equal to another.
  - Solving works like equations — except multiplying or dividing by a negative flips the sign.
  - The solution is a range of values, shown on a number line with open or closed circles.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **solve linear inequalities** and **graph their solutions on a number line**.
- You are learning that an inequality has infinitely many solutions, represented as a region on a number line.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Use the symbols <, >, ≤, ≥ and interpret their meaning.
- Solve one-step, two-step and multi-step linear inequalities.
- Reverse the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.
- Graph the solution set on a number line using open (○) and closed (●) circles.

## Core content

**1. Inequality symbols.** < means "less than." > means "greater than." ≤ means "less than or equal to." ≥ means "greater than or equal to." These define relationships between expressions rather than exact values. *Quick check:* Write in words: x ≥ 5.

**2. Solving linear inequalities.** Treat them like equations — use inverse operations on both sides. 3x + 4 > 13 → 3x > 9 → x > 3. The solution means "any number greater than 3." *Quick check:* Solve 2x − 7 ≤ 11.

**3. The critical rule: multiplying or dividing by a negative.** When you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, you must **reverse the inequality sign**. Why? If 2 < 5, then multiplying by −1 gives −2 > −5 (the order flips on the number line). Example: −3x > 12 → divide by −3 and flip → x < −4. *Quick check:* Solve −2x + 6 ≥ 14.

**4. Multi-step inequalities.** Combine techniques: expand brackets, collect like terms, solve. 4(x − 2) < 3x + 5 → 4x − 8 < 3x + 5 → x < 13. *Quick check:* Solve 5 − 2(x + 1) > 7.

**5. Graphing on a number line.** Use an **open circle** (○) for < or > (the endpoint is NOT included). Use a **closed circle** (●) for ≤ or ≥ (the endpoint IS included). Shade the region in the direction of the solutions. x > 3: open circle at 3, shade right. x ≤ −1: closed circle at −1, shade left. *Quick check:* Graph x < 5 on a number line.

**6. Worked example: budgeting with an inequality.**

A student has $80 to spend at a music festival. Entry costs $25 and food items cost $5.50 each. How many food items can she buy?

*Follow the thinking:*
- Let n = number of food items.
- Total cost ≤ budget: 25 + 5.5n ≤ 80.
- Subtract 25: 5.5n ≤ 55.
- Divide by 5.5: n ≤ 10.
- Since n must be a whole number: n can be 0, 1, 2, ..., 10.
- Graph: closed circle at 10, shade left to 0 (whole numbers only in context).
- Interpretation: she can buy at most 10 food items.

**7. Compound inequalities.** Sometimes you have two bounds: 2 ≤ x < 7 means x is at least 2 and less than 7. Graph: closed circle at 2, open circle at 7, shade between. Solve in pieces or work from the middle. *Quick check:* Solve −1 < 2x + 3 ≤ 9.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** VicRoads posts speed limits as inequalities: 0 < speed ≤ 60 km/h in suburban zones. WorkSafe Victoria sets maximum noise exposure levels (dB ≤ 85) and maximum lifting loads — all real-world inequalities. Melbourne Water sets minimum environmental-flow requirements for rivers (flow ≥ threshold), protecting ecosystems.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Environmental-flow thresholds for rivers on Country involve inequalities — the Yorta Yorta Nation's water-rights negotiations for the Murray–Goulburn system set minimum flow levels below which ecological and cultural values are harmed. Fire-management planning uses inequalities for safe burn conditions (wind speed < threshold, humidity ≥ minimum). Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Financial literacy across communities uses inequalities: "Can I afford this?" translates to expenses ≤ income. Dietary guidelines (sugar < 25 g/day, fibre ≥ 30 g/day) are inequality statements that apply universally.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You are saving for a $450 guitar. You have $120 and save $35 per week. Write and solve an inequality to find after how many weeks you will have enough.
2. **Civic.** The City of Stonnington limits building height to at most 12 metres in a heritage overlay zone. A developer proposes a building whose height is modelled by h = 2.5s + 1.5, where s = number of storeys. Write and solve the inequality to find the maximum number of storeys.
3. **Vocational.** A delivery driver must keep the total load under 3 500 kg. The truck weighs 2 200 kg. Each pallet weighs 180 kg. Write and solve an inequality for the maximum number of pallets.
4. **Ethical.** A "free trial" streaming service says "cancel within 14 days or be charged $19.99/month." Write this as a compound inequality on the number line (days ≤ 14 = free; days > 14 = charged). Discuss whether this is clearly communicated to consumers.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A recipe says baking temperature must be between 160 °C and 180 °C inclusive. Write this as a compound inequality. Convert both bounds to Fahrenheit using F = 9C/5 + 32 and write the equivalent Fahrenheit inequality.

## Memorable takeaways
- Inequalities solve like equations — except flip the sign when multiplying/dividing by a negative.
- Solution is a range of values, not a single number.
- Open circle (○) for strict < or >. Closed circle (●) for ≤ or ≥.
- Graph on a number line: shade the direction of valid solutions.
- In context, check whether the answer must be a whole number.

## Stretch yourself
A music-festival organiser must set ticket prices. Fixed costs are $15 000. Variable costs are $8 per attendee. Revenue is $p × n (price × number of attendees). Market research shows that n = 2 000 − 40p (higher prices mean fewer attendees). (a) Write an inequality for profit ≥ 0 (revenue − costs ≥ 0). (b) Substitute n = 2 000 − 40p and expand. (c) Solve the resulting inequality for p (this will be quadratic — find the critical values and interpret the valid price range). (d) Graph the valid price range on a number line. (e) Discuss practical constraints (price must be positive, attendance must be positive). Show all working. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I forgot to flip the sign when dividing by a negative." −3x > 12 → x < −4, not x > −4. This is the most common error.
- "I used a closed circle for a strict inequality." x > 3 means 3 is NOT included — use an open circle.
- "I solved for x = 3 and stopped." An inequality has infinitely many solutions (a range), not just one value.
- "I ignored context." If x represents the number of items, it must be a non-negative whole number — not all values in the algebraic solution may be valid.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *x ≥ 5 in words.* "x is greater than or equal to 5" (or "x is at least 5").
2. *Solve 2x − 7 ≤ 11.* 2x ≤ 18 → x ≤ 9.
3. *Solve −2x + 6 ≥ 14.* −2x ≥ 8 → divide by −2 and flip → x ≤ −4.
4. *Solve 5 − 2(x+1) > 7.* 5 − 2x − 2 > 7 → 3 − 2x > 7 → −2x > 4 → x < −2.
5. *Graph x < 5.* Open circle at 5, shade left.
6. *Solve −1 < 2x + 3 ≤ 9.* Subtract 3: −4 < 2x ≤ 6. Divide by 2: −2 < x ≤ 3.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Guitar savings.* 120 + 35w ≥ 450. 35w ≥ 330. w ≥ 9.43. Since w must be whole: w ≥ 10. After 10 weeks.
2. *Building height.* 2.5s + 1.5 ≤ 12. 2.5s ≤ 10.5. s ≤ 4.2. Maximum 4 storeys (whole number).
3. *Delivery driver.* 2200 + 180n ≤ 3500. 180n ≤ 1300. n ≤ 7.22. Maximum 7 pallets.
4. *Free trial.* Compound inequality: days ∈ (0, 14] means free; days > 14 means charged. Discussion: the boundary is clear mathematically but many consumers miss the deadline due to unclear reminders.
5. *Baking temperature.* 160 ≤ C ≤ 180. In Fahrenheit: F = 9(160)/5 + 32 = 320 and F = 9(180)/5 + 32 = 356. So 320 ≤ F ≤ 356.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Revenue = pn = p(2000 − 40p) = 2000p − 40p². Costs = 15000 + 8(2000 − 40p) = 15000 + 16000 − 320p = 31000 − 320p. Profit ≥ 0: 2000p − 40p² − 31000 + 320p ≥ 0 → −40p² + 2320p − 31000 ≥ 0 → 40p² − 2320p + 31000 ≤ 0. Divide by 40: p² − 58p + 775 ≤ 0. Factorise or use quadratic formula: p = (58 ± √(3364 − 3100))/2 = (58 ± √264)/2 ≈ (58 ± 16.25)/2. Critical values: p ≈ 20.87 and p ≈ 37.13. Valid range: 20.87 ≤ p ≤ 37.13. Practical: price between $21 and $37 inclusive. Graph: closed circles at ~21 and ~37, shading between.
