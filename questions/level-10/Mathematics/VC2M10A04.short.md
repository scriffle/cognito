# VC2M10A04 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Expand binomial products and factorise monic quadratic expressions using a variety of strategies.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** A binomial product (x + p)(x + q) expands to x² + (p+q)x + pq. Factorising reverses this: find two numbers that add to b and multiply to c. Special patterns include perfect squares and difference of squares.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **expand binomial products** and **factorise monic quadratics** (x² + bx + c) using a variety of strategies.
- You should move flexibly between expanded and factorised forms.

## Core content

**Expanding:** Multiply each term in the first bracket by each term in the second. (x + 3)(x + 5) = x² + 5x + 3x + 15 = x² + 8x + 15.

**Special products:** Perfect square: (x + a)² = x² + 2ax + a². Difference of squares: (x + a)(x − a) = x² − a².

**Factorising monic quadratics (x² + bx + c):** Find p and q where p + q = b and p × q = c. Then x² + bx + c = (x + p)(x + q). Example: x² + 7x + 12 — numbers 3 and 4 (3 + 4 = 7, 3 × 4 = 12). Answer: (x + 3)(x + 4).

**When c is negative:** numbers have different signs. x² + 2x − 15: numbers 5 and −3. Answer: (x + 5)(x − 3).

**Difference of squares:** x² − 25 = (x + 5)(x − 5).

**Worked example.** Garden area = x² + 10x + 21. Numbers: 3 + 7 = 10, 3 × 7 = 21. Dimensions: (x + 3) m × (x + 7) m. Check by expanding ✓.

**Victorian context.** Quadratics model projectile motion (MCG cricket), structural arches (Southern Cross Station), and profit functions. Al-Khwārizmī and Brahmagupta developed foundational quadratic methods centuries before modern European algebra.

## Memorable takeaways
- (x + p)(x + q) = x² + (p + q)x + pq.
- To factorise: find p + q = b and p × q = c.
- Perfect square: (x ± a)² = x² ± 2ax + a².
- Difference of squares: x² − a² = (x + a)(x − a).
- Always check by expanding.

## Watch out for these traps
- Sign errors when c is negative — check both sum and product.
- x² + a² does NOT factorise as (x + a)(x − a) — that gives x² − a².
- Forgetting to check by expanding — it catches errors instantly.
