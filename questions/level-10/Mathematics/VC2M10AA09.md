# VC2M10AA09 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M10AA09. Solve linear and non-linear simultaneous equations using graphing or systematic methods.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics (Algebra).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Simultaneous equations require finding values that satisfy two or more equations at the same time.
  - Non-linear simultaneous equations involve at least one equation that is not linear — such as a parabola intersecting a line.
  - The number of solutions depends on the geometric relationship: a line may intersect a parabola at 0, 1, or 2 points.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **solve simultaneous equations** where at least one equation is non-linear, using both algebraic and graphical methods.
- You are learning to determine the number of solutions from the discriminant and to verify graphical results algebraically.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Solve a system of one linear and one quadratic equation by substitution.
- Solve a system involving a circle and a line.
- Determine the number of intersection points using the discriminant.
- Classify a line as a secant, tangent or non-intersecting relative to a curve.
- Set up and solve real-world problems leading to simultaneous equations.

## Core content

**1. Linear-quadratic systems by substitution.** To solve y = x² and y = 2x + 3 simultaneously, substitute: x² = 2x + 3, so x² − 2x − 3 = 0, giving (x − 3)(x + 1) = 0. Solutions: x = 3, y = 9 and x = −1, y = 1. The points of intersection are (3, 9) and (−1, 1). *Quick check:* Solve y = x² and y = x + 6 simultaneously.

**2. Circle and line systems.** For x² + y² = 25 and y = x + 1: substitute y = x + 1 into the circle equation. x² + (x + 1)² = 25 → x² + x² + 2x + 1 = 25 → 2x² + 2x − 24 = 0 → x² + x − 12 = 0 → (x + 4)(x − 3) = 0. Solutions: (3, 4) and (−4, −3). *Quick check:* Find the intersection points of x² + y² = 10 and y = x + 2.

**3. Using the discriminant to determine the number of solutions.** After substitution, the resulting equation is typically quadratic. The discriminant Δ determines the number of intersections: Δ > 0 → two intersection points (secant); Δ = 0 → one intersection point (tangent); Δ < 0 → no intersection (line misses the curve). *Quick check:* Determine whether y = x + 5 is a tangent to, secant of, or does not intersect y = x².

**4. Graphical interpretation.** Graphing both equations reveals the intersection points visually. A line crossing a parabola at two points is a secant. A line touching the parabola at exactly one point is a tangent. Graphical methods provide insight into the number and approximate location of solutions, while algebraic methods give exact coordinates. *Quick check:* A graph shows a line intersecting a circle at one point. What does this mean algebraically?

**5. Setting up real-world problems.** Many problems translate naturally into simultaneous equations. "A rectangular area of 60 m² has perimeter 34 m": let length = l and width = w. Then lw = 60 and 2l + 2w = 34, so l + w = 17. Substituting: l(17 − l) = 60 → l² − 17l + 60 = 0 → (l − 12)(l − 5) = 0. Dimensions: 12 m × 5 m. *Quick check:* Two numbers have sum 15 and product 56. Find the numbers.

**6. Worked example — determining tangency.**
Determine the value of k for which y = 2x + k is a tangent to y = x².

*Follow the thinking:*
- Substitute: x² = 2x + k → x² − 2x − k = 0.
- For tangency, Δ = 0: b² − 4ac = 4 − 4(1)(−k) = 4 + 4k = 0.
- Solve: k = −1.
- Verify: y = 2x − 1 and y = x² → x² − 2x + 1 = 0 → (x − 1)² = 0. Tangent at (1, 1) ✓.

**7. Systems with no solution.** Not every system has a solution. For y = x² + 3 and y = x + 1: x² + 3 = x + 1 → x² − x + 2 = 0. Δ = 1 − 8 = −7 < 0. The line lies entirely below the parabola — no intersection. *Quick check:* Show that y = x² + 4 and y = 2x do not intersect.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Simultaneous equations model equilibrium problems in physics and economics. Victorian engineers use these techniques to determine where structures intersect or where forces balance.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Finding points of intersection parallels the navigational skills of First Nations peoples, who determined meeting points of paths and waterways across complex terrain.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** The systematic solution of simultaneous equations has roots in Chinese mathematics — the "Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art" (circa 200 BCE) included methods for solving systems of linear equations.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A ball follows the path y = −x² + 6x (height in metres) and a net is stretched along the line y = x + 4. Find where the ball hits the net.
2. **Civic.** A town's boundary is circular: x² + y² = 100 (km). A proposed road follows y = 2x − 5. Determine whether the road enters the town and find the entry/exit points.
3. **Vocational.** An architect designs a parabolic arch y = −0.5x² + 8 and a support beam along y = 2. Find where the beam meets the arch.
4. **Ethical.** A student solves y = x² and y = 4x − 5 and gets x = (4 ± √(−4))/2. The student concludes "no intersection." Is this correct? Explain using a graph.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Research the "Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art." Then solve the system y = x² − 2x + 1 and y = 3x − 5 simultaneously.

## Memorable takeaways
- Substitute the linear equation into the non-linear one to create a single equation in one variable.
- The discriminant of the resulting quadratic determines: two points (Δ > 0), tangent (Δ = 0), or no intersection (Δ < 0).
- Always verify solutions in both original equations.
- Real-world problems often translate into simultaneous equation systems.
- Graphical methods give insight; algebraic methods give precision.

## Stretch yourself
A farmer has 100 m of fencing to enclose a rectangular paddock beside a straight river (the river side needs no fencing). The paddock must contain exactly 1200 m². (a) Set up two equations relating length l and width w. (b) Solve simultaneously to find the dimensions. (c) If the farmer instead requires the paddock to contain 1300 m², determine the number of solutions and interpret. (d) Find the maximum area the farmer can enclose with 100 m of fencing and the dimensions that achieve it. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Substituting the wrong equation.** Always substitute the simpler (usually linear) equation into the more complex one.
- **Losing solutions.** Check both solutions in both equations — do not reject a valid solution prematurely.
- **Forgetting the y-coordinate.** After finding x, substitute back to find y. A solution is a point (x, y), not just an x-value.
- **Misinterpreting tangency.** Tangent means Δ = 0 (one repeated root), not "the line appears close to the curve."

5. *Two numbers, sum 15, product 56.* x + y = 15, xy = 56. x(15 − x) = 56 → x² − 15x + 56 = 0 → (x − 7)(x − 8) = 0. Numbers: 7 and 8.
6. *Tangency condition.* k = −1. Tangent at (1, 1).
