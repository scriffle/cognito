# VC2M10A01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Factorise algebraic expressions by taking out a common algebraic factor.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Factorising rewrites an expression as a product — the reverse of expanding. The first step is always to find the highest common factor (HCF) of all terms: numerical part × lowest power of shared variables.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **factorise algebraic expressions** by identifying and extracting a **common algebraic factor**.
- You should be able to find the HCF, write the factorised form, and verify by expanding.

## Core content

**Factorising** rewrites a sum as a product. It reverses expanding: 6x² + 15x = 3x(2x + 5).

**Finding the HCF:** Take the largest number dividing all coefficients, and the lowest power of each shared variable. For 15a²b − 10ab²: numerical HCF = 5, variable HCF = ab. Result: 5ab(3a − 2b).

**Constant terms limit the variable HCF.** In 4x² + 8x + 12, the 12 has no x, so HCF = 4 (not 4x). Result: 4(x² + 2x + 3).

**More than two terms:** same method. 4x³ − 6x² + 10x → HCF = 2x → 2x(2x² − 3x + 5).

**Bracketed common factors:** x(a + b) + 3(a + b) = (a + b)(x + 3). The shared bracket comes out.

**Worked example.** Perimeter P = 6x² + 12x + 18. HCF = 6 (constant term blocks x). P = 6(x² + 2x + 3). Check: 6 × x² + 6 × 2x + 6 × 3 = 6x² + 12x + 18 ✓.

**Always check by expanding** — if you recover the original, your factorisation is correct.

**Victorian context.** Engineers at AMSI simplify formulas by factorising before computing. The word "algebra" comes from al-Khwārizmī's 9th-century Arabic text — systematic simplification has multicultural roots.

## Memorable takeaways
- HCF = highest numerical factor × lowest variable powers shared across all terms.
- A constant term means x cannot be part of the HCF.
- Always check by expanding back.
- Factorising is step one before solving equations or simplifying fractions.

## Watch out for these traps
- Not finding the *highest* common factor — 2(6x + 9) is incomplete; the full factorisation is 6(2x + 3).
- Including a variable when a constant term has none.
- Stopping when a further factor remains inside the bracket (e.g. 3(2x² + 3x) → 3x(2x + 3)).

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *HCF of 8 and 20.* 4.
- *HCF of x⁴ and x².* x².
- *Factorise 8m³n + 12m²n².* 4m²n(2m + 3n).
- *Factorise 3y³ + 9y² − 6y.* 3y(y² + 3y − 2).
- *Factorise 2m(x − 4) + 5(x − 4).* (x − 4)(2m + 5).
