# VC2M10A07 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M10A07. Solve problems involving linear equations, including those derived from formulas.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics (Algebra).
- **Big ideas:**
  - A linear equation has the unknown raised to the first power — no squares, no roots.
  - Solving means isolating the unknown using inverse operations on both sides.
  - Many real-world formulas become linear equations once you substitute known values.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **solve problems involving linear equations**, including equations that arise from substituting into formulas.
- You are learning to set up, solve and interpret equations in context.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Solve one-step, two-step and multi-step linear equations.
- Solve equations with the unknown on both sides.
- Solve equations involving brackets and fractions.
- Derive a linear equation from a formula or word problem, solve it, and interpret the answer.

## Core content

**1. What is a linear equation?** A **linear equation** contains an unknown raised to the first power: 3x + 7 = 22 is linear; x² + 1 = 10 is not. The graph of a linear equation is a straight line. *Quick check:* Is 5(x − 2) = 3x + 4 a linear equation?

**2. Solving one- and two-step equations.** Use inverse operations. 2x + 5 = 17 → subtract 5 → 2x = 12 → divide by 2 → x = 6. Always perform the same operation on both sides to keep the equation balanced. *Quick check:* Solve 4x − 9 = 15.

**3. Equations with the unknown on both sides.** Collect like terms on one side. 5x + 3 = 2x + 18 → subtract 2x → 3x + 3 = 18 → subtract 3 → 3x = 15 → x = 5. *Quick check:* Solve 7x − 4 = 3x + 20.

**4. Equations with brackets.** Expand first, then solve. 3(2x − 1) = 4(x + 5) → 6x − 3 = 4x + 20 → 2x = 23 → x = 11.5. *Quick check:* Solve 2(3x + 4) = 5(x − 1).

**5. Equations with fractions.** Multiply every term by the lowest common denominator (LCD) to clear fractions. (x + 3)/4 = (2x − 1)/6. LCD = 12: 3(x + 3) = 2(2x − 1) → 3x + 9 = 4x − 2 → 11 = x. *Quick check:* Solve x/3 + 5 = 2x/3 − 1.

**6. Worked example: equation from a formula.**

A plumber charges a $90 call-out fee plus $65 per hour. The formula is C = 90 + 65h. A customer receives a bill for $350. How many hours did the plumber work?

*Follow the thinking:*
- Substitute C = 350: 350 = 90 + 65h.
- Subtract 90: 260 = 65h.
- Divide by 65: h = 4.
- Interpretation: the plumber worked 4 hours.
- Check: 90 + 65(4) = 90 + 260 = 350. ✓

**7. Setting up equations from word problems.** Define the unknown (let x = ...). Translate the words into algebra. Solve. Interpret the answer in context. Check it makes sense. This is where the power of linear equations meets the real world. *Quick check:* "Three times a number, decreased by 7, equals 29." Write and solve the equation.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** VicRoads uses linear cost formulas (fixed cost + rate × distance) for road-pricing models. The Victorian taxi-fare formula (flagfall + rate × km + rate × waiting time) creates linear equations customers can check. Energy companies (e.g. AGL, Origin) bill using C = supply charge + usage rate × kWh — a linear formula Victorian households encounter every quarter.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal community organisations managing grants and service contracts solve linear budget equations: total funding = fixed costs + per-unit costs × number served. The Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations allocates project funding using formulas that land managers rearrange and solve to determine staffing levels or service delivery targets. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Small-business owners across Melbourne's multicultural communities solve linear equations daily — calculating break-even points (revenue = costs), pricing goods (markup formulas) and managing wages (hourly rate × hours + superannuation).

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A streaming service charges $14.99/month plus $2.50 per premium rental. Your monthly budget is $30. Set up and solve a linear equation to find the maximum number of rentals you can afford.
2. **Civic.** The City of Hume estimates the cost of resurfacing a road as C = 12 000 + 85L, where L is the length in metres. The budget is $46 000. What maximum road length can be resurfaced?
3. **Vocational.** An apprentice electrician earns $22 per hour plus a $15 daily travel allowance. She earned $213 in one day. Set up and solve for the number of hours worked.
4. **Ethical.** A "buy now, pay later" service charges no interest but applies a $10 fee plus $5 per missed payment. A student's total fees reach $45. How many payments did they miss? Discuss whether this fee structure is transparent.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Two catering companies quote for a school event. Company A: $500 + $12 per person. Company B: $200 + $18 per person. Set up and solve an equation to find the number of people at which both quotes are equal. Which is cheaper for 60 guests?

## Memorable takeaways
- Linear = unknown to the first power only.
- Solve by inverse operations on both sides — keep it balanced.
- Unknown on both sides: collect to one side first.
- Brackets: expand first. Fractions: multiply by LCD first.
- Always interpret your answer in context and check it.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I subtracted from one side but not the other." Every operation must be done to both sides of the equation.
- "I expanded brackets incorrectly." 3(2x − 1) = 6x − 3, not 6x − 1. Multiply every term inside the bracket.
- "I cleared fractions but missed a term." When multiplying by the LCD, every term in the equation must be multiplied, including constants.
- "My answer is negative — that can't be right." Negative solutions are mathematically valid. Whether they make sense depends on context (you can't work negative hours, but temperature can be negative).

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Is 5(x − 2) = 3x + 4 linear?* Yes — after expanding (5x − 10 = 3x + 4), the highest power of x is 1.
2. *Solve 4x − 9 = 15.* 4x = 24 → x = 6.

6. *"Three times a number decreased by 7 equals 29."* 3x − 7 = 29 → 3x = 36 → x = 12.
