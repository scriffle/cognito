# VC2M9A02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Simplify algebraic expressions, apply the distributive law to expand algebraic expressions including binomial products, and factorise monic quadratic expressions.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Simplify by collecting like terms. Distributive law expands brackets: a(b+c) = ab + ac. Binomial products: (x+a)(x+b) = x² + (a+b)x + ab. Factorising reverses expansion — find two numbers multiplying to c, adding to b.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **simplify, expand and factorise** algebraic expressions including binomial products and monic quadratics.
- You should see expanding and factorising as reverse operations.

## Core content

**Simplify.** Collect like terms. 3x + 5x = 8x. 2x² + 3x − x² + 5x = x² + 8x.

**Distributive law.** a(b+c) = ab + ac. Examples: 3(x+4) = 3x + 12. −2(3x−5) = −6x + 10.

**Binomial products.** (x+3)(x+5) = x² + 5x + 3x + 15 = x² + 8x + 15. General: (x+a)(x+b) = x² + (a+b)x + ab.

**Special products.** Perfect square: (x+a)² = x² + 2ax + a². Difference of squares: (x+a)(x−a) = x² − a².

**Common factors.** 6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3). Always look for HCF first.

**Factorising monic quadratics.** x² + 7x + 12: need numbers multiplying to 12, adding to 7. That's 3 and 4. So (x+3)(x+4). Check by expanding: x² + 7x + 12 ✓.

**Negatives.** x² + 2x − 15: multiply to −15, add to +2 → +5 and −3. (x+5)(x−3).

**Victorian context.** Algebraic manipulation is foundational for VCE Methods and Specialist Maths. Engineers expand/factorise in design equations. Pattern recognition in algebra parallels reading Country — identifying underlying structure within complexity. Al-Khwarizmi's 9th-century Baghdad algebra developed systematic expansion and factorisation.

## Memorable takeaways
- Distributive: a(b+c) = ab + ac.
- Binomial: (x+a)(x+b) = x² + (a+b)x + ab.
- Perfect square: (x+a)² = x² + 2ax + a² (never forget middle term).
- Factorise: find two numbers — multiply to c, add to b.
- Always check by expanding back.

## Watch out for these traps
- (x+3)² ≠ x² + 9. It's x² + 6x + 9. Don't forget the middle term.
- −2(x−3) = −2x + 6, not −2x − 6 (negative × negative = positive).
- 3(x+4) = 3x + 12, not 3x + 4 (multiply ALL terms).
- Always expand to check your factorisation.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Simplify 4a + 3b − 2a + 5b.* 2a + 8b.
- *Expand (x+4)(x−2).* x² + 2x − 8.
- *(x−4)².* x² − 8x + 16.
- *Factorise 12xy − 8x.* 4x(3y − 2).
- *Factorise x² − x − 6.* Multiply to −6, add to −1: −3 and +2. (x−3)(x+2).
- *Al-Khwarizmi: x² + 10x − 39.* (x+13)(x−3) = 0. x = 3.
