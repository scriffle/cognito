# VC2M9M03 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M9M03. Solve spatial problems, applying angle properties, scale, similarity, ratio, Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry in right-angled triangles.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics (Measurement).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Spatial problems integrate multiple mathematical concepts — you need to recognise which tool(s) apply.
  - Pythagoras' theorem (a² + b² = c²) finds unknown sides in right-angled triangles.
  - Trigonometry (sin, cos, tan) connects angles and sides in right-angled triangles.
  - Scale and similarity link diagrams to real-world measurements through constant ratios.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Design and Technologies.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **solve spatial problems** by selecting and applying appropriate mathematical tools: angle properties, scale, similarity, Pythagoras and trigonometry.
- You are learning to combine these tools when a problem requires multiple steps.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Apply Pythagoras' theorem to find unknown sides.
- Use trigonometric ratios (sin, cos, tan) to find unknown angles or sides.
- Use scale factors to calculate real distances from diagrams.
- Apply similarity ratios to find unknown measurements.
- Combine these tools in multi-step spatial problems.

## Core content

**1. Pythagoras' theorem.** In a right-angled triangle: c² = a² + b² (where c is the hypotenuse). To find a shorter side: a² = c² − b². Example: sides 5 and 12 → hypotenuse = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13. *Quick check:* A ladder reaches 8 m up a wall and stands 6 m from the base. How long is the ladder?

**2. Trigonometric ratios.** sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse. cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse. tan θ = opposite/adjacent. Mnemonic: SOH CAH TOA. Use to find: a side (when you know an angle and one side) or an angle (when you know two sides). *Quick check:* In a right triangle, the angle is 35° and the hypotenuse is 10 cm. Find the opposite side.

**3. Finding unknown angles.** Use inverse trig: θ = sin⁻¹(opp/hyp), θ = cos⁻¹(adj/hyp), θ = tan⁻¹(opp/adj). Example: opposite = 4, adjacent = 7. θ = tan⁻¹(4/7) ≈ 29.7°. *Quick check:* A ramp rises 1.5 m over a horizontal distance of 8 m. Find the angle of elevation.

**4. Scale and ratio.** A scale of 1:500 means 1 cm on the diagram = 500 cm (5 m) in reality. To find real distance: measure on diagram × scale factor. Scale factor = real length ÷ diagram length. *Quick check:* On a 1:200 plan, a room measures 4 cm × 3 cm. What are the real dimensions?

**5. Similarity.** Similar shapes have the same angles and proportional sides. If two triangles are similar with scale factor k, then all corresponding sides are in ratio k:1. Areas scale by k², volumes by k³. *Quick check:* Two similar triangles have corresponding sides of 6 cm and 9 cm. What is the scale factor? If the smaller has area 20 cm², what is the larger's area?

**6. Worked example — multi-step spatial problem.**
A surveyor stands 50 m from a building and measures the angle of elevation to the top as 32°. Their eye level is 1.6 m above the ground. Find the building's height.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Draw a right triangle: horizontal = 50 m, angle = 32°, vertical = h (above eye level).
- Use tan: tan 32° = h/50.
- h = 50 × tan 32° = 50 × 0.6249 = 31.24 m.
- Total building height = 31.24 + 1.6 = 32.84 m ≈ 32.8 m.
- Check: angle seems reasonable for this height-to-distance ratio.

**7. Combining tools.** Many spatial problems require multiple steps: find a side using Pythagoras, then use that side in a trig calculation; or use similarity to find a length, then apply Pythagoras. Strategy: (1) draw a diagram, (2) identify right triangles, (3) list known and unknown values, (4) choose the appropriate tool for each step. *Quick check:* A ship sails 12 km east then 5 km north. How far is it from its starting point? In what direction (angle from east)?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Surveyors mapping Victorian properties use trigonometry and Pythagoras daily. The Bolte Bridge pylons required angle and distance calculations. Architecture firms (Bates Smart, Denton Corker Marshall) use scale drawings that students can interpret. Melbourne's hilly terrain (from the Dandenong Ranges to the flat western plains) creates real problems where slope calculations matter — road gradients, drainage design, accessibility ramps.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Navigation across Country uses angle estimation, distance judgement and landmark triangulation — core spatial skills. Traditional knowledge of star positions for navigation involves understanding angles of elevation. The precise geometry of fish traps and stone structures demonstrates applied spatial reasoning without formal notation. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Architecture from every culture uses proportion and scale — from Islamic geometric patterns to East Asian timber framing ratios. Students from diverse backgrounds bring spatial reasoning from family trades (construction, tailoring, cooking) and cultural practices (art, craft, navigation).

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A kite string is 80 m long. The kite is directly above a point 60 m from where you stand. How high is the kite? (Assume the string is straight.) What angle does the string make with the ground?
2. **Civic.** A council needs a wheelchair ramp with maximum gradient 1:14 (rises 1 m for every 14 m horizontal). If the entrance is 0.7 m above ground, what is the minimum ramp length along the slope? Use Pythagoras.
3. **Vocational.** A roof has a pitch angle of 22° and spans 10 m across (5 m from centre to edge). Find the height of the peak above the wall top. What length of roofing material is needed per metre of roof (the slant length)?
4. **Ethical.** A student uses tan⁻¹ to find an angle and gets 127°. Is this possible in a right-angled triangle? What went wrong? (Hint: the two sides may have been swapped in the ratio.)
5. **Cross-cultural.** Eratosthenes (c. 240 BCE, Egypt) calculated Earth's circumference using shadows and angles. If two cities 800 km apart have a shadow angle difference of 7.2°, estimate Earth's circumference using the proportion: 7.2/360 = 800/C.

## Memorable takeaways
- Pythagoras: c² = a² + b² (right triangles only).
- SOH CAH TOA: sin = O/H, cos = A/H, tan = O/A.
- Inverse trig (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) finds angles from sides.
- Scale: diagram measurement × scale factor = real measurement.
- Similarity: corresponding sides in same ratio; areas scale by k².

## Stretch yourself
From point A on level ground, the angle of elevation to the top of a cliff is 28°. After walking 100 m directly toward the cliff to point B, the angle of elevation is 40°. Find the height of the cliff and the distance from B to the base. (Hint: set up two right triangles sharing the same height. Let the base-to-cliff distance from B = d. Then from A it's d + 100. Use tan for both triangles and solve simultaneously.) Then: a ship at sea observes a lighthouse at 15° elevation. After sailing 500 m toward it, the elevation is 25°. Find the lighthouse height above sea level. State any assumptions. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Using Pythagoras on non-right triangles.** c² = a² + b² ONLY works for right-angled triangles.
- **Mixing up opposite and adjacent.** Always label from the specific angle you're using — opposite and adjacent change depending on which angle you're working with.
- **Calculator in wrong mode.** Ensure your calculator is in degrees (DEG), not radians (RAD), for these problems.
- **Forgetting to add/subtract.** In the building height example, don't forget to add the eye height. In cliff problems, read carefully about what height is measured from.
- **Not drawing a diagram.** Always sketch the situation — it helps identify which tools to use and prevents errors.

A note on certainty. Pythagoras' theorem and the trigonometric ratios are mathematical certainties — proven relationships that hold exactly for all right-angled triangles. When applied to real-world measurements, the accuracy of the result depends on the accuracy of the input measurements (angles and distances), but the mathematical relationships themselves are exact and have been proven for over 2500 years.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**
1. *Ladder.* c² = 8² + 6² = 100. c = 10 m.
2. *Opposite with angle 35°, hyp 10.* opp = 10 × sin 35° = 10 × 0.5736 = 5.74 cm.
3. *Ramp angle.* tan θ = 1.5/8 = 0.1875. θ = tan⁻¹(0.1875) ≈ 10.6°.
4. *Scale 1:200.* Real: 4 × 200 = 800 cm = 8 m by 3 × 200 = 600 cm = 6 m.
5. *Similarity.* Scale factor = 9/6 = 1.5. Area scales by 1.5² = 2.25. Larger area = 20 × 2.25 = 45 cm².
6. *Building height.* 32.8 m (worked through in example).
7. *Ship.* Distance = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 km. Angle from east = tan⁻¹(5/12) ≈ 22.6° north of east.

**Try these scenarios**
1. *Kite.* Height = √(80² − 60²) = √(6400 − 3600) = √2800 ≈ 52.9 m. Angle = cos⁻¹(60/80) = cos⁻¹(0.75) ≈ 41.4°.
2. *Wheelchair ramp.* Horizontal = 14 × 0.7 = 9.8 m. Ramp length = √(9.8² + 0.7²) = √(96.04 + 0.49) = √96.53 ≈ 9.83 m.
3. *Roof.* Height = 5 × tan 22° = 5 × 0.4040 = 2.02 m. Slant = 5/cos 22° = 5/0.9272 = 5.39 m per side.
4. *127° error.* Not possible — in a right triangle, the other angles are between 0° and 90°. The student likely put adjacent/opposite instead of opposite/adjacent (or hyp in wrong place). Check which sides are being used.
5. *Eratosthenes.* 7.2/360 = 800/C → C = 800 × 360/7.2 = 40,000 km. Remarkably accurate — Earth's actual circumference ≈ 40,075 km.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Let h = cliff height, d = distance from B to base. From B: tan 40° = h/d → h = d × tan 40°. From A: tan 28° = h/(d + 100) → h = (d + 100) × tan 28°. Set equal: d × tan 40° = (d + 100) × tan 28°. d(0.8391) = (d + 100)(0.5317). 0.8391d = 0.5317d + 53.17. 0.3074d = 53.17. d = 173.0 m. h = 173 × tan 40° = 145.2 m. Check: from A, tan 28° = 145.2/273 = 0.532 ✓. Lighthouse problem: same method. Let h = height, d = distance from closer point. tan 25° = h/d and tan 15° = h/(d + 500). h = d × tan 25° = (d + 500) × tan 15°. d(0.4663) = (d + 500)(0.2679). 0.1984d = 133.97. d = 675.3 m. h = 675.3 × tan 25° = 314.9 m. Assumptions: flat sea surface, observer at sea level, cliff/lighthouse vertical.
