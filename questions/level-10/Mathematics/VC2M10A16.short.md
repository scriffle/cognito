# VC2M10A16 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve equations graphically or using systematic numerical guess-check-and-refine with digital tools, with consideration of whether all solutions have been found.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Not all equations can be solved algebraically — graphical and numerical methods give approximate solutions. Solutions are found where graphs intersect or cross the x-axis. Systematic guess-check-refine narrows intervals to any accuracy. Always check completeness (have you found ALL solutions?).

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **solve equations graphically and numerically** when algebraic methods are difficult or unavailable.
- You should use digital tools systematically and consider whether all solutions have been found.

## Core content

**Graphical — intersection method.** To solve f(x) = g(x): graph both, find intersection x-values. Example: x² = 2x + 3 → graph y = x² and y = 2x + 3. Intersections at x = −1 and x = 3.

**Graphical — x-intercept method.** Rearrange to f(x) = 0, graph y = f(x), find where it crosses the x-axis.

**Guess-check-refine (interval bisection).** Find where f(x) changes sign → solution lies between. Test midpoint, narrow the interval, repeat. Example: x³ = 20. x = 2: 8 (small). x = 3: 27 (big). x = 2.7: 19.68 (small). x = 2.8: 21.95 (big). x = 2.71: 19.9 (small). Solution ≈ 2.71.

**Digital tools.** Desmos/GeoGebra: graph and read intersections. Spreadsheets: calculate f(x) for many x-values, spot sign changes. Zoom in for accuracy.

**Worked example.** x³ − 3x + 1 = 0. Graph shows THREE x-intercepts (cubic). Read: x ≈ −1.88, 0.35, 1.53. Refine each by interval bisection. The graph tells you how many solutions to look for.

**Completeness.** Always: (1) graph over a wide domain first, (2) count intersections/intercepts, (3) find each one, (4) check end behaviour for solutions outside your window.

**Victorian context.** Melbourne Metro Tunnel engineers solved geological intersection equations numerically. Bureau of Meteorology uses iterative methods for weather prediction. Financial analysts use trial-and-improvement for internal rates of return. Iterative refinement mirrors traditional practices of progressive improvement in crafting and land management.

## Memorable takeaways
- Graphical: solutions are intersection points or x-intercepts.
- Numerical: sign change means a solution exists in that interval.
- Guess-check-refine: test midpoint, keep the half with the sign change, repeat.
- The graph tells you HOW MANY solutions exist.
- Digital tools make these methods practical and accurate.

## Watch out for these traps
- Stopping at one solution when more exist — always check the graph for completeness.
- Viewing window too small — zoom out first to see all solutions.
- Confusing "close to zero" with "equals zero" — look for sign changes.
- Rounding too early — refine to the specified accuracy before rounding.
