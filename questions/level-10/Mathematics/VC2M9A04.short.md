# VC2M9A04 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Find the gradient of a line segment, the midpoint of the line interval and the distance between 2 distinct points on the Cartesian plane.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Gradient = rise/run between two points. Midpoint = average of coordinates. Distance uses Pythagoras on the coordinate plane.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **find the gradient, midpoint and distance** for a line segment defined by two points on the Cartesian plane.
- You should apply these tools to analyse line segments and solve coordinate geometry problems.

## Core content

**Gradient.** For points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂): m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). Positive → up-right. Negative → down-right. Zero → horizontal. Undefined → vertical. Parallel lines have equal gradients. Perpendicular lines: gradients multiply to −1.

**Midpoint.** M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2). Average the x's, average the y's. The midpoint always lies on the segment.

**Distance.** d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]. This is Pythagoras applied to the horizontal and vertical differences between the points.

**Worked example.** Two survey posts at (10, 25) and (46, 49), grid units = metres. Gradient: (49−25)/(46−10) = 24/36 = ⅔. Midpoint: (28, 37). Distance: √(36² + 24²) = √1872 ≈ 43.3 m.

**Connecting the tools.** Use gradient to check if lines are parallel or perpendicular. Use midpoint to find centres. Use distance to compare lengths. Together they classify shapes: a parallelogram has opposite sides with equal gradients and equal lengths.

**Special cases.** Horizontal segment: gradient = 0, distance = |x₂ − x₁|. Vertical segment: gradient undefined, distance = |y₂ − y₁|.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Land surveyors across Victoria use coordinate geometry for property boundaries. Melbourne's grid streets make midpoint and distance intuitive — finding the centre of a block or length of a boundary. First Nations land management at Budj Bim reflects applied understanding of terrain gradient for water flow across Country. Navigation apps used across Melbourne's communities calculate routes using these principles.

## Memorable takeaways
- Gradient: m = rise/run = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁).
- Midpoint: average the coordinates.
- Distance: d = √[(Δx)² + (Δy)²].
- Parallel = equal gradients. Perpendicular = gradients multiply to −1.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I subtracted in the wrong order." Be consistent: (y₂ − y₁) on top, (x₂ − x₁) on bottom. Swapping one but not both gives the wrong sign.
- "I forgot to square root." The distance formula gives d² — don't forget the final √.
- "I said undefined when it's zero." Zero = horizontal (valid). Undefined = vertical (x₁ = x₂, division by zero).

## Model responses
