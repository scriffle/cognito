# VC2M10P02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Describe the results of two- and three-step chance experiments, both with and without replacements, assign probabilities to outcomes and determine probabilities of events; investigate the concept of independence.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Multi-step experiments use tree diagrams — multiply along branches. With replacement: probabilities stay constant (independent). Without replacement: probabilities change (dependent). Independence means P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate probabilities for multi-step experiments** with and without replacement.
- You should distinguish independence from dependence and apply the multiplication rule correctly.

## Core content

**With replacement.** Item returned after each draw. Probabilities constant. Bag: 3 red, 2 blue. P(red, red) = 3/5 × 3/5 = 9/25. Events are independent.

**Without replacement.** Item not returned. Probabilities change. Same bag: P(red, red) = 3/5 × 2/4 = 6/20 = 3/10. Events are dependent.

**Three-step.** Extend the tree. Coin flipped 3 times: P(HHH) = 1/8. P(exactly 2 heads) = 3 × 1/8 = 3/8.

**Independence test.** P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)? If yes → independent. If no → dependent. Example: 60% pass maths, 50% pass English, 35% pass both. P(M) × P(E) = 0.30 ≠ 0.35. Not independent.

**Worked example — raffle.** 100 tickets, you hold 5, three prizes drawn without replacement. P(no prizes) = 95/100 × 94/99 × 93/98 ≈ 0.858. P(at least one) ≈ 14.2%.

**Complement rule.** P(at least one) = 1 − P(none). Often much easier than adding all the "at least one" paths.

**Victorian context.** Lottery draws and Melbourne Cup barrier draws operate without replacement. Traditional Aboriginal games embed sequential chance reasoning. Victorian gambling regulations require accurate odds display. Card games across all cultures involve without-replacement probability.

## Memorable takeaways
- With replacement → constant probabilities → independent.
- Without replacement → changing probabilities → dependent.
- Multiply along tree diagram branches.
- P(at least one) = 1 − P(none).
- Test independence: does P(A∩B) = P(A) × P(B)?

## Watch out for these traps
- Forgetting to adjust denominators without replacement (total decreases by 1).
- Assuming independence when items are removed.
- Confusing "or" (add) with "and" (multiply).
- Forgetting to count all paths (HHT, HTH, THH are three different paths for "exactly 2 heads").
