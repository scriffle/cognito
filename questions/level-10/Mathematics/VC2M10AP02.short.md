# VC2M10AP02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Evaluate research studies reported in the media by examining their planning and implementation.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Valid conclusions require sound design — sample size, sampling, controls and blinding. Correlation ≠ causation. Media reports often overstate findings.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **critically evaluate research studies** by examining design, confounders and reporting.
- You should be able to distinguish observational from experimental studies and identify when conclusions are overstated.

## Core content

**Sample size:** Small samples are unreliable. The sample must also be representative of the population.

**Sampling:** Random sampling minimises bias. Convenience sampling introduces selection bias.

**Control groups and blinding:** RCTs randomly assign treatment/control and ideally are double-blind — the gold standard for causation.

**Correlation ≠ causation:** Co-occurring variables may be explained by confounders. Ice cream and drowning correlate via temperature.

**Confounding variables:** A third factor influencing both variables, creating a spurious association.

**Observational vs RCT:** Observational identifies associations; RCTs establish causation. RCTs are not always ethical.

**Victorian context.** The Burnet Institute and Murdoch Children's Research Institute exemplify rigorous study design. Contemporary First Nations health research requires community partnership and Indigenous data sovereignty.

## Memorable takeaways
- Larger, representative samples give more reliable results.
- Random sampling and control groups are hallmarks of quality design.
- Correlation does not prove causation — always look for confounders.
- Media headlines are designed for impact, not accuracy.

## Watch out for these traps
- Assuming correlation means causation — the most common interpretation error.
- Ignoring sample size — dramatic results from 10 people are unreliable.
- Trusting self-reported data without considering placebo effects or recall bias.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Caffeine study limitations.* Small sample (20), non-representative (university students only).
- *Random vs convenience sampling.* Random gives equal chance, reducing bias.
