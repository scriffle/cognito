## Core content

**1. Exponential functions — review.** An exponential function has the form y = aˣ where a > 0, a ≠ 1. Key features: y-intercept at (0, 1), horizontal asymptote y = 0, always positive, and either always increasing (a > 1) or always decreasing (0 < a < 1). Growth is multiplicative — each unit increase in x multiplies y by a. *Quick check:* State the y-intercept and asymptote of y = 3ˣ.

**2. What is a logarithm?** The logarithm logₐ(b) answers the question: "To what power must the base a be raised to give b?" In symbols: logₐ(b) = c means aᶜ = b. For example, log₂(8) = 3 because 2³ = 8. The base must be positive and not equal to 1; the argument b must be positive. *Quick check:* Evaluate log₁₀(1000) and explain the reasoning.

**3. Converting between forms.** Every exponential statement has an equivalent logarithmic statement and vice versa. 5² = 25 is equivalent to log₅(25) = 2. Similarly, log₃(81) = 4 is equivalent to 3⁴ = 81. Fluent conversion is the foundation for solving exponential equations. *Quick check:* Write 10⁴ = 10000 in logarithmic form.

**4. Evaluating logarithms.** Some logarithms can be evaluated exactly: log₂(32) = 5 because 2⁵ = 32. Others require a calculator: log₁₀(500) ≈ 2.699. Special values: logₐ(1) = 0 for any valid base (since a⁰ = 1), and logₐ(a) = 1 (since a¹ = a). *Quick check:* Evaluate log₅(125) and log₇(1).

**5. The inverse relationship graphically.** The graph of y = logₐ(x) is the reflection of y = aˣ in the line y = x. Key features of y = logₐ(x): x-intercept at (1, 0), vertical asymptote x = 0, domain x > 0, and either always increasing (a > 1) or always decreasing (0 < a < 1). The point (a, 1) lies on every logarithmic curve with base a. *Quick check:* Sketch y = 2ˣ and y = log₂(x) on the same axes. What line of symmetry relates them?

**6. Worked example — solving an exponential equation using logarithms.**
Solve: 10ˣ = 500.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Convert to logarithmic form: x = log₁₀(500).
- Use a calculator: x ≈ 2.699.
- Interpretation: 10 must be raised to the power of approximately 2.699 to give 500.
- Verify: 10²·⁶⁹⁹ ≈ 500 ✓.

**7. Logarithmic scales.** Logarithmic scales compress large ranges into manageable numbers. The Richter scale: an increase of 1 unit represents a tenfold increase in amplitude. The pH scale: pH = −log₁₀[H⁺], so a pH of 3 has 10 times the hydrogen ion concentration of pH 4. The decibel scale: an increase of 10 dB represents a tenfold increase in sound intensity. *Quick check:* An earthquake of magnitude 7 on the Richter scale has how many times the amplitude of a magnitude 5 earthquake?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Scientists at the Australian Synchrotron and environmental researchers use logarithmic scales to analyse data spanning many orders of magnitude. The pH scale is routinely applied in water quality monitoring across Victorian waterways.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** The concept of orders of magnitude — distinguishing vastly different scales — resonates with the spatial reasoning of First Nations navigators who operated across scales from local waterhole distances to continental trade routes.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** The Scottish mathematician John Napier invented logarithms in 1614 to simplify astronomical calculations. The Indian mathematician Jyeṣṭhadeva (16th century) independently explored series expansions related to logarithmic functions.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A bank account grows according to A = 1000 × 1.05ⁿ, where n is years. How many years until the account reaches $2000? Set up and solve using logarithms.
2. **Civic.** A town's population is modelled by P = 5000 × 1.03ᵗ. The council needs to know when the population will exceed 10 000. Express this as a logarithmic equation and solve.
3. **Vocational.** A chemist measures a solution with [H⁺] = 0.001 mol/L. Calculate the pH. Then find [H⁺] for a solution with pH = 5.2.
4. **Ethical.** A news report states that an earthquake of magnitude 6 is "twice as powerful" as one of magnitude 5. Evaluate this claim using the Richter scale.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Research John Napier's invention of logarithms. Then solve: 1.05ⁿ = 3, giving the answer to two decimal places.

## Memorable takeaways
- logₐ(b) = c means aᶜ = b — the logarithm is the exponent.
- logₐ(1) = 0 and logₐ(a) = 1 for any valid base.
- Exponential and logarithmic graphs are reflections in y = x.
- Each unit on a logarithmic scale represents a tenfold (or other constant factor) change.
- Logarithms convert multiplication into addition, making them powerful simplification tools.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Confusing the base and the argument.** In log₂(8) = 3, the base is 2 and the argument is 8 — not the reverse.
- **Assuming log(a + b) = log(a) + log(b).** This is false. The log of a sum is not the sum of the logs.
- **Forgetting domain restrictions.** logₐ(x) is defined only for x > 0. The logarithm of zero or a negative number does not exist.
- **Confusing "times" with "plus" on logarithmic scales.** A Richter magnitude increase of 1 means 10 times the amplitude, not 10 plus.

A note on certainty. The inverse relationship between exponentials and logarithms is a mathematical identity — if y = aˣ, then x = logₐ(y), without exception (for a > 0, a ≠ 1, y > 0). Logarithmic scales are exact transformations, not approximations.

2. *Population.* 1.03ᵗ = 2 → t = log(2)/log(1.03) ≈ 23.45 years.
