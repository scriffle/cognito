# VC2M9P02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Calculate relative frequencies from given or collected data to estimate probabilities of events involving 'and', inclusive 'or' and exclusive 'or'.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Relative frequency = frequency/total, estimates probability. "And" = both occur. Inclusive "or" = at least one. Exclusive "or" = exactly one. Addition rule avoids double-counting.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate relative frequencies** and use them to estimate probabilities of compound events involving "and," inclusive "or" and exclusive "or."
- You should apply the addition rule and distinguish inclusive from exclusive "or."

## Core content

**Relative frequency.** Frequency of outcome / total trials. In 200 tosses, 108 heads → relative frequency = 0.54. Estimates probability; improves with more data.

**"And" (intersection).** Both events occur. Count outcomes where A and B both happen, divide by total. In 200 customers, 45 bought coffee AND cake: P = 45/200 = 0.225.

**Inclusive "or" (union).** A or B or both. P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). Subtract the overlap to avoid counting it twice. 40 white cars, 25 SUVs, 10 white SUVs out of 100: P(white or SUV) = 0.55.

**Exclusive "or."** A or B but NOT both. P(exclusive or) = P(A) + P(B) − 2×P(A and B). Only counts outcomes in exactly one category.

**Worked example.** Canteen: 250 students, 120 hot meal, 90 drink, 50 both. P(inclusive or) = 0.48 + 0.36 − 0.20 = 0.64. P(exclusive or) = 0.48 + 0.36 − 2(0.20) = 0.44. Neither: 1 − 0.64 = 0.36.

**Venn diagrams and two-way tables.** Venn: overlap = "and," combined area = "or." Two-way tables organise counts systematically. Both prevent counting errors.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Insurance companies use relative frequency to estimate combined risks. VicHealth reports use "or" probability for health behaviours. First Nations ecological knowledge estimates compound event probabilities through generations of observation — weather AND flowering patterns guide seasonal planning.

## Memorable takeaways
- Relative frequency estimates probability from data.
- P(A or B) inclusive = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B).
- P(A or B) exclusive = P(A) + P(B) − 2×P(A and B).
- Always subtract overlap to avoid double-counting.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I forgot to subtract the overlap." P(A or B) ≠ P(A) + P(B) unless events are mutually exclusive.
- "I confused inclusive and exclusive." Inclusive allows both. Exclusive means one or the other only.
- "I divided by the wrong total." Use the grand total of all observations for relative frequency.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Die rolled 150 times, 3 appears 28 times — relative frequency?* 28/150 ≈ 0.187.
- *40 white, 25 SUVs, 10 white SUVs out of 100 — P(white or SUV)?* 0.40 + 0.25 − 0.10 = 0.55.
- *18 sport, 15 music, 12 both out of 30 — P(exclusive or)?* 18/30 + 15/30 − 2(12/30) = 9/30 = 0.3.
- *500 households: 320 recycle, 180 compost, 120 both — P(neither)?* P(or) = 380/500 = 0.76. Neither = 0.24.
- *Canteen: 120 hot meal, 50 both — hot meal only?* 120 − 50 = 70 students.
