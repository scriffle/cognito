# VC2M10AA06 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Use polynomial understanding to sketch curves and describe their features from their equation.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Polynomial sketching combines roots, intercepts, turning points and end behaviour. The degree determines the maximum turning points (n − 1). The leading coefficient's sign determines end behaviour.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **sketch polynomial curves** by analysing equations for roots, intercepts, turning points and end behaviour.
- You should be able to connect factorised forms to graph features and produce labelled sketches.

## Core content

**End behaviour:** Odd degree + positive leading → bottom-left to top-right. Even degree + positive → both ends up. Negative leading coefficient reverses the pattern.

**x-intercepts from factors:** P(x) = (x − a)(x − b)(x − c) has intercepts at a, b, c. Single factors cross; repeated factors create tangent points.

**y-intercept:** Evaluate P(0). In factored form, multiply the constant parts of each factor.

**Turning points:** At most n − 1 for degree n. A cubic has at most 2; a quartic at most 3.

**Worked example.** P(x) = (x + 1)²(x − 2)(x − 4). Degree 4, positive leading. Intercepts: −1 (tangent), 2 (cross), 4 (cross). y-intercept: 8. Both ends up. At most 3 turning points.

**Victorian context.** Polynomial models appear in Victorian engineering (road curvature), economics (cost-revenue) and environmental science (population dynamics). Descartes' analytic geometry (17th century) unified algebra and geometry.

## Memorable takeaways
- End behaviour depends on degree (odd/even) and sign of leading coefficient.
- Degree n → at most n intercepts and n − 1 turning points.
- Repeated factors → tangent; single factors → crossing.
- Always find P(0) to anchor the sketch.

## Watch out for these traps
- Repeated factors change crossing behaviour — do not assume all intercepts are crossings.
- A cubic cannot have 3 turning points (maximum is 2).
- Ignoring the leading coefficient sign leads to inverted sketches.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *End behaviour of −2x⁴ + x² − 1.* Even degree, negative leading: both ends down.
- *Intercepts of x(x − 3)(x + 2).* x = 0, 3, −2; all crossings.
- *y-intercept of (x − 4)(x + 1)(x − 2).* (−4)(1)(−2) = 8.
- *Max turning points for degree 5.* 4.
- *x³ − 9x intercepts.* x(x − 3)(x + 3): intercepts at 0, ±3.
- *Can a cubic have 3 turning points?* No. Maximum is 2.
