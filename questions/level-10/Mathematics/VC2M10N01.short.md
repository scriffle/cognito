# VC2M10N01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Recognise the effect of using approximations of real numbers in repeated calculations and compare the results when using exact representations.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Irrational numbers have infinite non-repeating decimals. Rounding errors compound in repeated calculations. Exact forms (surds, fractions, π) preserve accuracy through multi-step work. Only round the final answer.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **recognise how rounding errors accumulate** and to compare approximate versus exact approaches.
- You should use exact forms in intermediate steps and make informed decisions about when approximation is acceptable.

## Core content

**Exact vs approximate.** √2 is exact; 1.414 is an approximation. π is exact; 3.14 is an approximation. 1/3 is exact; 0.333 is an approximation.

**Errors compound.** √2 × √2 = 2 (exact). 1.414 × 1.414 = 1.999396 (error 0.0006). After 10 multiplications: (√2)¹⁰ = 32 exactly, but 1.414¹⁰ ≈ 28.93 (error ≈ 3). Small errors grow dramatically.

**Repeated multiplication.** Each operation multiplies the error. Engineers and scientists keep exact forms during working and only round the final answer.

**When it matters.** A machinist cutting 200 pieces at √2 cm using 1.41: total error ≈ 0.84 cm. A bank compounding daily interest: rounding errors over a year produce measurable discrepancies on large accounts.

**When approximation is fine.** Single-step calculations, estimates, or contexts where high precision isn't needed (recipes, crowd sizes).

**Worked example.** Daily rate 5%/365. Exact factor: 1 + 1/7300. Rounded to 6 d.p.: 1.000137. After 365 days on $10,000: exact gives $10,512.67; rounded gives $10,512.47. Difference: $0.20. After 10 years: ≈ $2.00 error.

**Victorian context.** Melbourne's Skyrail required sub-millimetre precision over kilometres. The Australian Synchrotron demands extreme precision in beam calculations. Aboriginal land knowledge maintained exact boundary locations over millennia through oral tradition and physical markers. Zu Chongzhi (5th century China) calculated π to 7 decimal places — π ≈ 355/113.

## Memorable takeaways
- Exact forms carry zero rounding error.
- Rounding errors compound — small errors grow large over many steps.
- Keep exact forms through working; round only the final answer.
- Context determines acceptable precision.
- Subtraction of nearly-equal numbers magnifies relative error.

## Watch out for these traps
- Assuming small errors stay small — they compound with repetition.
- Rounding intermediate steps instead of keeping exact forms.
- Confusing precision (decimal places) with accuracy (closeness to truth).
- Trusting calculators are exact — they store finite digits internally.
