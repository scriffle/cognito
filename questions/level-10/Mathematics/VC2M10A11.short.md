# VC2M10A11 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Explore the connection between algebraic and graphical representations of relations such as simple quadratic, reciprocal, circle and exponential, using digital tools as appropriate.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Every algebraic relation has a graphical shape. Parabolas, hyperbolas, circles and exponentials each have distinctive features linked to their equations. Digital tools let you explore connections dynamically.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **connect algebraic and graphical representations** of quadratics, reciprocals, circles and exponentials.
- You should predict graph features from equations and identify equations from graphs.

## Core content

**Parabola** (y = ax²): U-shape, vertex at origin (or shifted to (h, k) in y = a(x−h)² + k). Opens up if a > 0, down if a < 0. Larger |a| = narrower.

**Hyperbola** (y = k/x): two branches in opposite quadrants. Asymptotes at x = 0 and y = 0. Never touches the axes. Shifts move the asymptotes.

**Circle** (x² + y² = r²): closed curve, all points distance r from centre. Centre (h, k) with (x−h)² + (y−k)² = r². Not a function — fails vertical line test.

**Exponential** (y = aˣ, a > 1): growth curve. y-intercept at (0, 1). Asymptote at y = 0. Doubles every 1/log₂(a) units. Decay: y = a⁻ˣ or 0 < a < 1.

**Transformations:** changing h shifts horizontally, k shifts vertically, a stretches/reflects. y = (x−3)² shifts RIGHT 3 (opposite sign).

**Worked example.** Desmos: y = x² − 4 (parabola, vertex (0,−4)), y = 2/x (hyperbola), x² + y² = 9 (circle, r = 3), y = 2ˣ (exponential through (0,1)).

**Victorian context.** Southern Cross Station (parabolic arches), MCG (circular), Melbourne population growth (exponential). The stone circle at Wurdi Youang on Wadawurrung Country demonstrates precise circular geometry.

## Memorable takeaways
- Parabola: U-shape, vertex, one axis of symmetry.
- Hyperbola: two branches, two asymptotes, never touches axes.
- Circle: closed, all points equidistant from centre, not a function.
- Exponential: rapid growth/decay, horizontal asymptote, always positive.
- Changing constants translates, stretches or reflects the graph.

## Watch out for these traps
- A circle is a relation, not a function (fails vertical line test).
- y = (x − 3)² shifts right, not left (opposite of the sign).
- Exponentials approach but never reach their asymptote — don't draw them touching.

- *Vertex of y = (x−3)² + 2.* (3, 2), opens up.
- *Asymptotes of y = 1/x.* x = 0 and y = 0.
