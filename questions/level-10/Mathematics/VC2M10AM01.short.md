# VC2M10AM01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve problems involving the surface area and volume of pyramids, cones, spheres and composite objects.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Pyramids, cones and spheres have specific volume and SA formulas. Composite objects are decomposed into standard shapes. The cone:sphere:cylinder volume ratio is 1:2:3 (Archimedes).

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate the surface area and volume** of pyramids, cones, spheres and composite objects.
- You should be able to select formulas, decompose composites, and solve applied 3D problems.

## Core content

**Cone:** V = ⅓πr²h. SA = πr² + πrl (l = slant height = √(r² + h²)). A cone is ⅓ the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height.

**Sphere:** V = ⅔πr³. SA = 4πr². Maximum volume for given surface area.

**Pyramid:** V = ⅓Ah (A = base area). SA = base + sum of triangular faces.

**Composites:** Add volumes; for SA, exclude shared internal surfaces. Hemisphere on cylinder: V = πr²h + ⅔πr³.

**Archimedes' ratio:** Cone:sphere:cylinder (same r, h = 2r) = 1:2:3.

**Working backwards:** V = 288π for sphere → r³ = 216 → r = 6 cm.

**Victorian context.** Victorian engineers use these calculations for silos, tanks and pylons. Archimedes considered the 1:2:3 ratio his greatest discovery.

## Memorable takeaways
- Cone and pyramid volumes include the ⅓ factor.
- Cone SA uses slant height, not perpendicular height.
- Sphere: V = ⅔πr³, SA = 4πr².
- Composite SA: do not count shared internal surfaces.

## Watch out for these traps
- Using height instead of slant height for cone SA.
- Forgetting the ⅓ factor (triples the answer).
- Double-counting shared surfaces in composites.
