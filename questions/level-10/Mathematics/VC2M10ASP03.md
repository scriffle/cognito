# VC2M10ASP03 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M10ASP03. Define sin(x), cos(x) and tan(x) using the unit circle and graph these trigonometric functions.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics (Space).
- **Big ideas:**
  - The unit circle (radius 1, centred at origin) defines sin(θ) as the y-coordinate and cos(θ) as the x-coordinate of the point at angle θ.
  - tan(θ) = sin(θ)/cos(θ), undefined when cos(θ) = 0.
  - Trigonometric functions are periodic — sin and cos have period 360°, tan has period 180°.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **define trigonometric functions using the unit circle** and to **graph y = sin(x), y = cos(x) and y = tan(x)**.
- You are learning to identify amplitude, period, key features, and the signs of trig functions in each quadrant.

By the end of this you should be able to:

- State exact values for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° and their extensions.
- Sketch graphs of sin, cos and tan, labelling key features.
- Identify the signs of trig functions in each quadrant (ASTC rule).
- Describe transformations of trig graphs (amplitude, period changes).

## Core content

**1. The unit circle.** A circle of radius 1 centred at the origin. A point P on the circle at angle θ (measured anticlockwise from the positive x-axis) has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ). This extends trig ratios beyond right-angled triangles to all angles 0°–360°. *Quick check:* Using the unit circle, what are the coordinates of the point at θ = 90°?

**2. Exact values.** sin(30°) = ½, cos(30°) = √3/2. sin(45°) = cos(45°) = √2/2. sin(60°) = √3/2, cos(60°) = ½. sin(0°) = 0, cos(0°) = 1. sin(90°) = 1, cos(90°) = 0. tan(θ) = sin(θ)/cos(θ). *Quick check:* Find the exact value of tan(60°).

**3. ASTC — signs in each quadrant.** All trig functions positive in Q1. Sin positive in Q2. Tan positive in Q3. Cos positive in Q4. Memory aid: All Stations To Central. This determines signs for angles beyond 90°: sin(150°) = sin(30°) = ½; cos(150°) = −cos(30°) = −√3/2. *Quick check:* Find sin(210°) and cos(300°).

**4. Graph of y = sin(x).** Starts at (0, 0), reaches maximum 1 at 90°, returns to 0 at 180°, minimum −1 at 270°, returns to 0 at 360°. Amplitude = 1, period = 360°. The curve is smooth and wavelike. *Quick check:* At what angles does sin(x) = 0 in one full cycle?

**5. Graph of y = cos(x).** Starts at (0, 1), decreases to 0 at 90°, minimum −1 at 180°, returns to 0 at 270°, maximum 1 at 360°. Same shape as sin but shifted 90° left: cos(x) = sin(x + 90°). *Quick check:* Describe the phase relationship between sin(x) and cos(x).

**6. Worked example — graph of y = tan(x).**
tan(x) = sin(x)/cos(x). Undefined at x = 90° and x = 270° (where cos = 0), creating vertical asymptotes.

*Key features:*
- Period = 180° (not 360°).
- Passes through origin and every 180° thereafter.
- Between asymptotes, increases from −∞ to +∞.
- tan(45°) = 1, tan(0°) = 0.

**7. Transformations.** y = a·sin(bx): amplitude = |a|, period = 360°/b. For y = 2sin(3x): amplitude 2, period 120°. The graph oscillates between −2 and 2, completing one full cycle every 120°. *Quick check:* State the amplitude and period of y = 3cos(2x).

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Trigonometric functions model waves, oscillations and circular motion — central to Victorian industries in communications, acoustics and electrical engineering. VCE Mathematical Methods begins with unit circle trigonometry.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Periodic natural phenomena — tides, seasons, lunar cycles — have been tracked by First Nations peoples for millennia. The mathematical formalisation of periodicity through trig functions connects to this deep observational tradition.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Indian mathematician Aryabhata (5th century CE) produced the first known sine table. The unit circle approach was developed in the European mathematical tradition, building on ancient Greek and Islamic foundations.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A Ferris wheel has radius 10 m and centre 12 m above ground. Height h(t) = 12 + 10sin(θ). Find the height at θ = 30° and θ = 90°.
2. **Civic.** Daylight hours in Melbourne follow approximately y = 12 + 2.5sin(30(t − 3)°), where t is months after January. Find the maximum daylight and when it occurs.
3. **Vocational.** An electrician models AC voltage as V(t) = 340sin(t°). State the amplitude and interpret it.
4. **Ethical.** A student claims sin(180°) = 1 because "sine keeps increasing." Use the unit circle to correct this.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Research Aryabhata's sine table. Then find exact values: sin(120°), cos(225°), tan(315°).

## Memorable takeaways
- Unit circle: cos θ = x-coordinate, sin θ = y-coordinate of point at angle θ.
- ASTC: All, Sin, Tan, Cos — positive in quadrants 1, 2, 3, 4 respectively.
- sin and cos: amplitude 1, period 360°. tan: no amplitude, period 180°.
- For y = a·sin(bx): amplitude |a|, period 360°/b.
- Exact values at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° must be memorised.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Forgetting quadrant signs.** sin(210°) is NEGATIVE (Q3: only tan positive).
- **Confusing period of tan with sin/cos.** tan has period 180°, not 360°.
- **Wrong exact values.** sin(60°) = √3/2 ≈ 0.866, NOT ½. sin(30°) = ½.
- **Assuming tan is defined everywhere.** tan is undefined at 90°, 270°, etc.
