# VC2M10AA10 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Experiment with functions and relations using digital tools, making conjectures and verifying them.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Digital tools enable dynamic exploration of functions. The experimental-conjecture-verification cycle mirrors authentic mathematical research. Technology builds intuition that complements algebraic understanding.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **use digital tools** to explore functions, form conjectures, and verify them algebraically.
- You should be able to distinguish between observing a pattern and proving it.

## Core content

**Parameter exploration:** Graph y = a(x − h)² + k with sliders. a controls width/direction, h shifts horizontally, k vertically.

**Families of functions:** y = x² + k: all have the same shape, different vertical position. Varying one parameter reveals its role.

**Forming conjectures:** Graph y = x², x⁴, x⁶ → conjecture: all even-power functions pass through (0,0), (1,1), (−1,1) and open upward. Verified by substitution.

**Investigating intersections:** y = x² + k and y = 4x − 3: Δ = 4 − 4k. Two intersections when k < 1, tangent at k = 1, none when k > 1.

**Inverse functions:** y = 2ˣ reflected in y = x gives y = log₂(x). Domain and range swap.

**Derivatives graphically:** At turning points of f(x), the derivative f'(x) = 0. Where f is increasing, f' is positive.

**Victorian context.** VCE Mathematics requires CAS proficiency. Ramanujan often conjectured results from patterns before proofs were found — the conjecture-verification cycle is authentic mathematical practice.

## Memorable takeaways
- Technology shows what happens; algebra explains why.
- A conjecture is a pattern observed; verification requires algebraic proof.
- Families of functions share structure — varying one parameter reveals its role.
- Inverse functions are reflections in y = x.

## Watch out for these traps
- Confusing observation with proof — three examples do not constitute a proof.
- Over-generalising from limited data.
- Misreading graphs due to zoom level or window settings.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Increasing h in y = (x − h)².* Shifts the parabola right.
- *Family y = mx + 1.* All pass through (0, 1); m controls gradient.
- *Odd-power conjecture.* All pass through (0,0), (1,1), (−1,−1); odd symmetry; rise bottom-left to top-right.
- *y = x² + k has no x-intercepts when…* k > 0.
- *Inverse of y = x³.* y = ∛x.
- *Sum of roots = −b/a.* Verified by expanding a(x − r)(x − s) and comparing coefficients.
