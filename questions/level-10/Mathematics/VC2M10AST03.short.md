# VC2M10AST03 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Investigate and describe bivariate numerical data, fit a straight line, interpret slope and intercept, and use the correlation coefficient.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Scatterplots reveal relationships between two variables. Line of best fit summarises the trend. Correlation coefficient r measures strength and direction (−1 to 1). Correlation ≠ causation.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **investigate bivariate data** using scatterplots, lines of best fit, and the correlation coefficient.
- You should be able to interpret slope and intercept in context and distinguish correlation from causation.

## Core content

**Scatterplot:** Plot (x, y) pairs. Explanatory variable on x-axis, response on y-axis.

**Association:** Positive (both increase), negative (one increases, other decreases), or none. Linear or non-linear. Strong or weak.

**Line of best fit:** ŷ = a + bx. Slope b = predicted change in y per unit change in x. Intercept a = predicted y when x = 0.

**Correlation coefficient r:** −1 ≤ r ≤ 1. |r| > 0.8 strong, 0.5–0.8 moderate, < 0.5 weak. Only measures linear association.

**Correlation ≠ causation:** Confounding variables may explain the relationship. Controlled experiments needed to establish causation.

**Victorian context.** Bureau of Meteorology uses regression for climate modelling. Karl Pearson (1896) developed the correlation coefficient; Galton pioneered regression analysis.

## Memorable takeaways
- Scatterplot reveals the relationship; line of best fit summarises it.
- Slope = change in y per unit change in x. Intercept = predicted y when x = 0.
- r: −1 to 1. Strong |r| > 0.8, moderate 0.5–0.8, weak < 0.5.
- Correlation does not imply causation — always consider confounders.

## Watch out for these traps
- Confusing correlation with causation.
- Extrapolating predictions beyond the data range.
- Ignoring non-linear patterns (r only measures linear association).

## Model responses

- *Study hours (x) vs test score (y).* Hours on x-axis (explanatory), score on y-axis (response).
- *Temperature and ice cream.* Positive association.
- *ŷ = 100 − 2x for car value.* Each year reduces value by $2000. New car predicted at $100K.
- *r = −0.85.* Strong negative linear relationship.
- *Screen time and headaches correlation.* Does not prove causation — confounders like stress may explain both.
- *Mobile phones and life expectancy.* Confounding variable: national wealth drives both.
