# VC2M10A12 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M10A12. Solve linear equations involving simple algebraic fractions.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics (Algebra).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Linear equations with algebraic fractions are solved by eliminating the denominators — multiply both sides by the LCD.
  - Once the fractions are cleared, the equation becomes a standard linear equation.
  - Always check your solution by substituting back and ensure the denominator is never zero.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **solve linear equations that contain algebraic fractions** with numerical or single-variable denominators.
- You are learning to clear fractions systematically and check solutions.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Identify the LCD of all fractions in an equation.
- Multiply both sides by the LCD to clear fractions.
- Solve the resulting linear equation.
- Check the solution and note any restrictions on the variable.

## Core content

**1. Why clear fractions?** Equations with fractions are harder to solve when the fractions remain. Multiplying every term by the **lowest common denominator (LCD)** eliminates all denominators at once, leaving a simpler equation with whole-number or whole-variable terms. *Quick check:* What is the LCD of fractions with denominators 3 and 5?

**2. Equations with numerical denominators.** x/3 + x/5 = 8. LCD = 15. Multiply every term by 15: 5x + 3x = 120 → 8x = 120 → x = 15. Check: 15/3 + 15/5 = 5 + 3 = 8 ✓. *Quick check:* Solve x/2 − x/6 = 4.

**3. Equations with constants and fractions.** (2x + 1)/4 = 3. Multiply both sides by 4: 2x + 1 = 12 → 2x = 11 → x = 5.5. When the fraction has a multi-term numerator, the denominator multiplies the entire numerator — use brackets to make this clear. *Quick check:* Solve (3x − 2)/5 = 4.

**4. Equations with fractions on both sides.** (x + 3)/2 = (2x − 1)/3. LCD = 6. Multiply both sides: 3(x + 3) = 2(2x − 1) → 3x + 9 = 4x − 2 → 9 + 2 = 4x − 3x → x = 11. Check: (11 + 3)/2 = 7; (22 − 1)/3 = 7 ✓. *Quick check:* Solve (x + 1)/4 = (x − 2)/3.

**5. Equations with variable denominators.** 5/x = 2. Multiply both sides by x: 5 = 2x → x = 2.5. **Restriction:** x ≠ 0 (cannot divide by zero). Always state restrictions when the variable is in the denominator. *Quick check:* Solve 12/x = 4. What is the restriction?

**6. Worked example — a work-rate problem.**
Two painters can finish a room together in t hours. Painter A alone takes 6 hours (rate = 1/6 per hour). Painter B alone takes 10 hours (rate = 1/10). Together: 1/6 + 1/10 = 1/t.

*Follow the thinking:*
- LCD of 6, 10 and t is 30t.
- Multiply every term by 30t: 30t/6 + 30t/10 = 30t/t → 5t + 3t = 30 → 8t = 30 → t = 30/8 = 3.75 hours.
- Check: 1/6 + 1/10 = 5/30 + 3/30 = 8/30 = 4/15. And 1/t = 1/3.75 = 4/15 ✓.
- Restrictions: t ≠ 0.

**7. Checking solutions and restrictions.** Always: (1) Substitute your solution into the original equation to verify. (2) State any values of x that would make a denominator zero — these are **excluded values**. If your solution equals an excluded value, the equation has no valid solution. *Quick check:* You solve 3/(x − 2) = 1 and get x = 5. Is this valid? What is the excluded value?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Engineers at Engineers Australia and CSIRO solve equations with fractional terms when modelling flow rates, electrical circuits (Ohm's Law rearrangements), and mixture problems. Financial planners solve fractional equations when calculating loan repayments and break-even points. AMSI resources connect algebraic fractions to real-world problem solving.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Resource-sharing problems — dividing water flows, harvest yields or funding between groups — often produce equations with fractional terms. Aboriginal corporations managing joint ventures and shared budgets encounter this reasoning in practical financial management. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Proportional reasoning with fractions is universal in family and business contexts: splitting costs, calculating ingredient ratios for large-batch cooking, or determining work-rate problems when multiple people contribute to a task.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You and a friend are saving for a $120 gift. You save x/4 dollars per week and your friend saves x/3 dollars per week. Together you save $14 per week. Solve for x.
2. **Civic.** A council's two recycling trucks each cover different fractions of a suburb per hour. Truck A covers 1/5 of the suburb per hour; Truck B covers 1/8. Write and solve an equation to find how long both trucks working together take to cover the whole suburb.
3. **Vocational.** A plumber mixes hot and cold water. Hot flows at 2/x litres per second; cold flows at 3/x litres per second. Together they fill a 10-litre bucket in 2 seconds. Set up and solve the equation.
4. **Ethical.** A student solves 6/(x − 3) = 2 and gets x = 6. They do not check. Verify their answer by substituting. Then solve 6/(x − 3) = 2 where the answer is x = 3. What goes wrong?
5. **Cross-cultural.** Ancient Egyptian "aha" problems (from the Rhind Papyrus, c. 1650 BCE) often involved equations like "a quantity plus its seventh equals 19." Write this as x + x/7 = 19 and solve.

## Memorable takeaways
- Clear fractions by multiplying every term by the LCD.
- Use brackets when multiplying a multi-term numerator.
- After clearing, solve as a standard linear equation.
- Always check by substituting back into the original.
- State restrictions: variable denominators cannot equal zero.

## Stretch yourself
A swimming pool has two inlet pipes and one drain. Pipe A fills the pool in x hours; Pipe B fills it in (x + 2) hours. The drain empties it in 12 hours. With both pipes open and the drain open, the pool fills in 4 hours. Set up the equation: 1/x + 1/(x + 2) − 1/12 = 1/4. Multiply through by the LCD to clear fractions. Solve the resulting equation (it will become quadratic — use factorisation or the quadratic formula). Check both solutions and determine which is physically valid (positive time). State all restrictions. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Multiplying only one side.** When you multiply by the LCD, you must multiply every term on both sides.
- **Forgetting brackets.** If the numerator has multiple terms, multiply the whole numerator: 3 × (2x + 1) = 6x + 3, not 6x + 1.
- **Missing restrictions.** If x is in a denominator, always state what x cannot be (e.g. x ≠ 0, x ≠ 2).
- **Not checking the answer.** Especially with variable denominators, your algebraic solution might make a denominator zero — always verify.

**Quick checks (Core content)**
1. *LCD of 3 and 5.* 15.
2. *Solve x/2 − x/6 = 4.* LCD = 6. 3x − x = 24 → 2x = 24 → x = 12. Check: 12/2 − 12/6 = 6 − 2 = 4 ✓.
3. *Solve (3x − 2)/5 = 4.* 3x − 2 = 20 → 3x = 22 → x = 22/3. Check: (22 − 2)/5 = 20/5 = 4 ✓.
4. *Solve (x+1)/4 = (x−2)/3.* LCD = 12. 3(x+1) = 4(x−2) → 3x + 3 = 4x − 8 → x = 11. Check: 12/4 = 3; 9/3 = 3 ✓.
5. *Solve 12/x = 4.* x = 3. Restriction: x ≠ 0.
6. *Painters.* t = 3.75 hours (worked through in example).
7. *x = 5 for 3/(x−2) = 1.* Check: 3/(5−2) = 3/3 = 1 ✓. Valid. Excluded value: x ≠ 2.

**Try these scenarios**
1. *Saving.* x/4 + x/3 = 14. LCD = 12. 3x + 4x = 168 → 7x = 168 → x = 24. Check: 24/4 + 24/3 = 6 + 8 = 14 ✓.
2. *Recycling trucks.* 1/5 + 1/8 = 1/t. LCD = 40t. 8t + 5t = 40 → 13t = 40 → t = 40/13 ≈ 3.08 hours.
3. *Water mixing.* 2/x + 3/x = 10/2 → 5/x = 5 → x = 1. Flow rates: 2 L/s and 3 L/s = 5 L/s total. 10 L ÷ 5 L/s = 2 s ✓.
4. *Checking x = 6.* 6/(6−3) = 6/3 = 2 ✓. Now if solution were x = 3: 6/(3−3) = 6/0 — undefined. x = 3 is excluded; no valid solution in that case.
5. *Egyptian aha problem.* x + x/7 = 19. LCD = 7. 7x + x = 133 → 8x = 133 → x = 133/8 = 16.625. Check: 16.625 + 16.625/7 = 16.625 + 2.375 = 19 ✓.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** 1/x + 1/(x+2) − 1/12 = 1/4. LCD = 12x(x+2). Multiply through: 12(x+2) + 12x − x(x+2) = 3x(x+2). Expand: 12x + 24 + 12x − x² − 2x = 3x² + 6x. Simplify: 22x + 24 − x² = 3x² + 6x → 0 = 4x² − 16x − 24 → x² − 4x − 6 = 0 (dividing by 4 gives a different quadratic — let me recompute). Actually: 24x + 24 − x² − 2x = 3x² + 6x → 22x + 24 − x² = 3x² + 6x → 4x² − 16x − 24 = 0 → x² − 4x − 6 = 0. Quadratic formula: x = (4 ± √(16+24))/2 = (4 ± √40)/2 = 2 ± √10. x ≈ 5.16 or x ≈ −1.16. Only x ≈ 5.16 is valid (positive time). Restrictions: x ≠ 0, x ≠ −2. Pipe A fills in about 5.16 hours; Pipe B in about 7.16 hours.
