# VC2M10SP01 — Mathematics

## Snapshot

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **construct deductive proofs** about plane shapes and to **apply geometric theorems** to solve problems.
- You are learning the difference between inductive observation (noticing a pattern) and deductive proof (showing it must always hold).

By the end of this you should be able to:
- State and apply key theorems about angles, triangles and quadrilaterals.
- Construct short deductive proofs with clear logical steps and reasons.
- Use congruence and similarity criteria in proofs.
- Solve spatial problems by selecting and applying appropriate theorems.

## Core content

**1. What is deductive reasoning?** Deductive reasoning starts with known truths and uses logical steps to reach a conclusion that MUST follow. Each step cites a reason (a definition, axiom, or previously proven theorem). This differs from inductive reasoning (observing patterns in examples), which suggests but doesn't prove. *Quick check:* "I measured angles in 10 triangles and they all added to 180°" — is this a proof? Why or why not?

**2. Structure of a proof.** Given → To prove → Proof (chain of statements + reasons) → Therefore (conclusion). Each statement follows logically from previous statements plus a cited reason. Example structure:
- Given: ABC is an isosceles triangle with AB = AC.
- To prove: angle B = angle C.
- Proof: (uses congruent triangles formed by the altitude from A).
*Quick check:* What is the difference between "Given" and "To prove"?

**3. Key theorems for angles.** Angles on a straight line sum to 180°. Vertically opposite angles are equal. Angles in a triangle sum to 180°. Exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles. Alternate angles are equal (parallel lines). Co-interior angles sum to 180° (parallel lines). *Quick check:* Find the missing angle: a triangle has angles 50° and 70°. What is the third? What theorem did you use?

**4. Congruence criteria.** Two triangles are congruent (identical in shape and size) if they satisfy: SSS (three sides equal), SAS (two sides and included angle), AAS (two angles and a corresponding side), or RHS (right angle, hypotenuse, one other side). Congruence is used in proofs to show corresponding parts are equal. *Quick check:* Triangle PQR has PQ = 5, QR = 7, angle Q = 60°. Triangle XYZ has XY = 5, YZ = 7, angle Y = 60°. Are they congruent? Which criterion?

**5. Similarity criteria.** Two triangles are similar (same shape, different size) if: AAA (two angles equal — third follows), SSS ratio (all sides in proportion), or SAS ratio (two sides in proportion with included angle equal). Similar triangles have corresponding sides in the same ratio. *Quick check:* A triangle has angles 30°, 60°, 90°. Another has angles 60°, 90°, 30°. Are they similar?

**6. Worked example — proving a quadrilateral property.**
Prove: the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Given: ABCD is a parallelogram. Diagonals AC and BD intersect at M.
- To prove: AM = MC and BM = MD.
- Proof:
  - In triangles ABM and CDM:
  - AB = CD (opposite sides of parallelogram).
  - Angle ABM = angle CDM (alternate angles, AB ∥ CD).
  - Angle BAM = angle DCM (alternate angles, AB ∥ CD).
  - Therefore triangle ABM ≡ triangle CDM (AAS).
  - Therefore AM = CM and BM = DM (corresponding parts of congruent triangles).
- QED: diagonals bisect each other. ✓

**7. Applying theorems to solve problems.** Once a theorem is proven, use it as a tool. Example: find x in a parallelogram where one angle is (3x + 10)° and the adjacent angle is (2x + 20)°. By the theorem that co-interior angles in a parallelogram sum to 180°: 3x + 10 + 2x + 20 = 180 → 5x + 30 = 180 → x = 30. *Quick check:* A triangle has angles 2x°, 3x° and 4x°. Find x.

Engineers at the Melbourne Metro Tunnel project use deductive geometric reasoning to prove clearances are maintained at every point. ## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A picture frame is a parallelogram (not quite rectangular due to a manufacturing error). One angle measures 85°. Using theorems about parallelograms, find all four angles. Then prove: opposite angles in a parallelogram are equal.
2. **Civic.** A surveyor measures a triangular plot: two sides are 50 m and 80 m with an included angle of 60°. A second measurement gives the same two sides and same angle. Explain using congruence criteria why the triangle is uniquely determined.
3. **Vocational.** A carpenter needs to verify that a window frame is rectangular. They measure both diagonals and find them equal. Prove: if a parallelogram has equal diagonals, it is a rectangle.
4. **Ethical.** A student writes: "I checked 100 cases and it always works, so it's proven." Explain the difference between inductive evidence and deductive proof. Give an example where a pattern holds for many cases but eventually fails.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Euclid's first proposition (c. 300 BCE) proves that an equilateral triangle can be constructed on any given line segment. Research the proof (it uses two circles). Verify it by construction with compass and straightedge.

## Memorable takeaways
- Deductive proof: starts from known truths, each step has a reason, conclusion must follow.
- Proof structure: Given → To prove → Proof (with reasons) → QED.
- Congruence: SSS, SAS, AAS, RHS.
- Similarity: AAA, SSS (ratio), SAS (ratio).
- Once proven, a theorem becomes a tool for solving problems.

## Stretch yourself
Prove the following: In a circle, the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference (standing on the same arc). Start with the special case where one arm of the inscribed angle is a diameter (use isosceles triangles and the exterior angle theorem). Then extend to the general case by drawing a diameter from the vertex of the inscribed angle and splitting into two cases. State clearly what is given, what you are proving, and cite a reason for every step. Then apply this theorem: if the angle at the centre is 140°, what is the angle at the circumference? If an inscribed angle is 90°, what can you conclude about the arc? 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Assuming what you're trying to prove.** Never use the conclusion as one of your reasons — that's circular reasoning.
- **Citing the wrong congruence criterion.** SSA (two sides and a non-included angle) is NOT a valid criterion — it can produce two different triangles.
- **Forgetting reasons.** Every statement in a proof needs a reason (theorem, definition, or previous result).
- **Using examples as proof.** "It works for this triangle" proves nothing about all triangles. Proof must work for the general case.
- **Confusing congruence with similarity.** Congruent = same shape AND size. Similar = same shape, possibly different size.

A note on certainty. A deductive proof is the highest level of mathematical certainty — once proven, a theorem is true in all cases, not just the ones you've checked. This is what distinguishes mathematical proof from scientific evidence (which can always be overturned by new data). The theorems in this topic have been proven for over 2000 years and will remain true forever because they follow necessarily from the axioms of Euclidean geometry.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**
1. *Is measuring 10 triangles a proof?* No — it's inductive evidence. A proof must show the property holds for ALL triangles using logical deduction, not just the ones measured.
2. *Given vs To prove.* "Given" states the known facts/conditions. "To prove" states what must be shown to follow from those facts.
3. *Missing angle.* 180° − 50° − 70° = 60°. Theorem: angle sum of a triangle is 180°.
4. *SAS congruence.* Yes — PQ = XY, QR = YZ, angle Q = angle Y (included). Criterion: SAS.
5. *Similar triangles.* Yes — same three angles (30°, 60°, 90°). Criterion: AAA (equiangular).
6. *Parallelogram diagonals.* Proved by AAS congruence (worked through in example).
7. *Triangle angles.* 2x + 3x + 4x = 180 → 9x = 180 → x = 20. Angles: 40°, 60°, 80°.

**Try these scenarios**
1. *Frame angles.* Adjacent angles in a parallelogram are supplementary: 85° + 95° = 180°. Angles: 85°, 95°, 85°, 95°. Proof of opposite angles equal: adjacent angles sum to 180° (co-interior, parallel sides) → angle A + angle B = 180° and angle B + angle C = 180° → angle A = angle C.
2. *Surveyor.* Two sides (50, 80) and included angle (60°) determine the triangle uniquely by SAS. Any triangle with these measurements must be congruent to the first.
3. *Rectangle proof.* Given: ABCD is a parallelogram with AC = BD. In triangles ABC and BAD: AB = AB (common), AC = BD (given), BC = AD (opposite sides of parallelogram). By SSS, triangle ABC ≡ triangle BAD. Therefore angle ABC = angle BAD. But angle ABC + angle BAD = 180° (co-interior). So each = 90°. Therefore ABCD is a rectangle.
4. *Inductive vs deductive.* Example: "the sum of the first n odd numbers = n²" holds for n = 1, 2, 3, ..., 100 — but this doesn't prove it for all n (though it happens to be true and can be proven by induction). Better example: "n² + n + 41 is prime" works for n = 0 through 39 but fails at n = 40 (40² + 40 + 41 = 1681 = 41²).
5. *Euclid's Proposition 1.* Draw segment AB. Circle centre A radius AB. Circle centre B radius AB. Circles intersect at C. AC = AB (radii of first circle). BC = AB (radii of second circle). Therefore AC = BC = AB. Triangle ABC is equilateral.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Special case: inscribed angle BAC where BC is a diameter. Let O be the centre. OA = OB = radius → triangle OAB is isosceles → angle OAB = angle OBA = α. By exterior angle theorem: angle AOC (at centre) = α + α = 2α = 2 × angle at circumference. General case: draw diameter AD from A. Angle BAD = α, angle DAC = β. Central angles: angle BOD = 2α, angle DOC = 2β. Angle at centre BOC = 2α + 2β = 2(α + β) = 2 × angle BAC. QED. Application: angle at centre 140° → angle at circumference = 70°. If inscribed angle = 90°, then angle at centre = 180° — the arc is a semicircle, meaning the chord is a diameter. This is Thales' theorem: angle in a semicircle is 90°.
