# VC2M9A06 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Use mathematical modelling to solve applied problems involving change, including financial contexts involving simple interest; formulate problems, choosing to use either linear or quadratic functions; interpret solutions in terms of the context; evaluate the model and report methods and findings.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Linear models suit constant-rate change. Quadratic models suit changing-rate change. Simple interest (I = Prt) is linear. Good modelling includes assumptions and evaluation.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **use mathematical modelling** with linear and quadratic functions to solve applied problems, including simple interest.
- You should formulate, solve, interpret, evaluate and report — the full modelling cycle.

## Core content

**Choosing a model.** Is the rate of change constant (linear: y = mx + c) or changing (quadratic: y = ax² + bx + c)? Fixed salary growth, constant speed, simple interest → linear. Projectile motion, growing areas, braking distance → quadratic.

**Simple interest.** I = Prt. P = principal, r = rate as decimal, t = years. Total = P(1 + rt). This is linear in t. Example: $2 000 at 4% for 3 years: I = 2 000 × 0.04 × 3 = $240.

**Linear modelling.** Taxi: C = 4.50 + 2.10d. Predicts cost at any distance. To find when C = $50: solve 50 = 4.50 + 2.10d → d ≈ 21.7 km. State assumptions (fixed rate, no surge pricing).

**Quadratic modelling.** Ball: h = −5t² + 20t. Vertex at t = 2: max height 20 m. Lands at t = 4. Valid only for 0 ≤ t ≤ 4.

**Worked example.** Saving for a $3 000 trip in 2 years at 4.5% simple interest. P(1.09) = 3 000 → P = $2 752.29. Assumption: rate stays fixed. Limitation: banks typically use compound interest.

**The modelling cycle.** Formulate → solve → interpret → evaluate → report. State assumptions, check the answer makes sense in context, identify what the model misses.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Victorian banks offer simple and compound interest products — financial literacy matters for consumer decisions. VicRoads models braking distance as quadratic in speed. Myki pricing uses a linear zone model. First Nations sustainable management at Budj Bim involves modelling fish stock growth. Understanding interest helps families across Melbourne's diverse communities make informed borrowing decisions.

## Memorable takeaways
- Linear: constant rate of change. Quadratic: changing rate of change.
- Simple interest: I = Prt. Linear in time.
- Always interpret solutions in context and check they make sense.
- State assumptions. Evaluate whether the model fits reality. Report limitations.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I used the rate as a percentage." Convert: 4% = 0.04 in the formula.
- "I assumed linear when it's quadratic." If the rate of change is itself changing, a straight line won't fit.
- "I didn't state assumptions." Every model simplifies reality — name what you assumed.
