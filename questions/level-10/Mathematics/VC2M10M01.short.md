# VC2M10M01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve problems involving the surface area and volume of composite objects using appropriate units.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Composite objects combine standard solids. Volume: add/subtract components. Surface area: sum exposed faces, subtract hidden (joined) surfaces.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate the surface area and volume of composite objects** using appropriate units.
- You should decompose complex 3D shapes into standard components and handle shared surfaces.

## Core content

**Composite objects** join or subtract standard solids: prisms, cylinders, cones, spheres, hemispheres.

**Key formulas.** Cylinder: V = πr²h. Cone: V = ⅓πr²h. Sphere: V = ⁴⁄₃πr³. Hemisphere: V = ⅔πr³.

**Volume by addition.** Silo (cylinder r=3, h=8 + hemisphere r=3): V = 72π + 18π = 90π ≈ 282.7 m³.

**Volume by subtraction.** Block with a hole drilled through: total = block − cylinder.

**Surface area.** Add all exposed faces. Where shapes join, the shared surface is hidden — do not count it. Silo: lateral cylinder + hemisphere curve + base. The top circle of the cylinder is hidden (hemisphere sits there).

**Worked example.** Tank: cylinder (r=2.5, h=4) + hemispherical dome. SA = lateral (20π) + base (6.25π) + hemisphere curve (12.5π) = 38.75π ≈ 121.7 m². Hidden: top circle of cylinder.

**Units.** 1 m³ = 1 000 L. Volume → capacity. Surface area → material/paint costs.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Melbourne's water towers are composite (cylinder + dome). Federation Square's angular cladding required composite SA calculations. Coolamon and canoes carved by First Nations craftspeople combine curved and flat surfaces — composite-form engineering. Mosque domes (hemisphere + cylinder) demonstrate the same geometry.

## Memorable takeaways
- Decompose into standard solids.
- Volume: add (or subtract) components.
- Surface area: exposed faces only — subtract hidden joins.
- Always check: which faces are inside vs outside?

## Watch out for these traps
- "Included the hidden face." The join between shapes is internal — don't count it.
- "Forgot the base." Check if it's exposed or sits on the ground.
- "Used diameter instead of radius." Halve the diameter first.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Hemisphere r=6 volume?* ⅔π(216) = 144π ≈ 452.4 cm³.
- *Rocket (cylinder r=2,h=10 + cone r=2,h=3)?* 40π + 4π = 44π ≈ 138.2 units³.
- *Which face is hidden (cylinder+cone)?* Top circle of cylinder = base of cone.
- *Tank 5.4 m³ in litres?* 5 400 L.
- *Silo SA — what's hidden?* The circle where hemisphere meets cylinder top.
