## Core content

**1. Inverse proportion.** Two quantities are inversely proportional when their product is constant: y = k/x (as one increases, the other decreases). Example: speed and time for a fixed distance — if d = 300 km, then t = 300/v. Double the speed, halve the time. Graph: a hyperbola. *Quick check:* If 4 workers take 6 hours, how long do 8 workers take (assuming inverse proportion)?

**2. Simple interest.** Interest calculated on the original principal only: I = Prt, where P = principal, r = annual rate (as decimal), t = time in years. After t years, total amount = P + Prt = P(1 + rt). Growth is linear — the same amount each period. *Quick check:* $2000 at 5% simple interest for 3 years. Calculate the total.

**3. Compound interest — repeated simple interest.** Each period, interest is calculated on the new balance (principal + accumulated interest). Year 1: A = P(1 + r). Year 2: A = P(1 + r)(1 + r) = P(1 + r)². After n periods: **A = P(1 + r)ⁿ**. This is exponential growth — the graph curves upward. *Quick check:* $1000 at 8% p.a. compounded annually for 3 years. Calculate A.

**4. Growth and decay models.** Exponential growth: A = A₀ × bᵗ (b > 1). Exponential decay: A = A₀ × bᵗ (0 < b < 1). The base b represents the growth/decay factor. If something grows by 5% per period, b = 1.05. If it decays by 10%, b = 0.90. *Quick check:* A car worth $30,000 depreciates by 15% per year. Write the model and find its value after 4 years.

**5. Choosing the right model.** Linear: constant rate of change (adding the same amount each period). Quadratic: rate of change itself changes linearly (e.g. area, projectile motion). Exponential: constant percentage change (multiplying by the same factor each period). Look at the data pattern — constant differences suggest linear; constant ratios suggest exponential. *Quick check:* Population doubles every decade. Is this linear, quadratic or exponential?

**6. Worked example — comparing savings strategies.**
Aisha invests $5000 at 6% p.a. compounded annually. Ben invests $5000 at 6% simple interest. Compare after 10 years.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Aisha (compound): A = 5000(1.06)¹⁰ = 5000 × 1.7908... = $8954.24.
- Ben (simple): A = 5000(1 + 0.06 × 10) = 5000 × 1.6 = $8000.
- Difference: $954.24 in favour of compound interest.
- Aisha's growth is exponential (curve); Ben's is linear (straight line).
- Assumption: interest rate remains constant. In reality, rates fluctuate.

**7. The modelling cycle.** (1) Formulate: identify variables, choose model type, state assumptions. (2) Solve: use algebra to find unknowns. (3) Interpret: translate the mathematical answer back to the real context. (4) Evaluate: does the answer make sense? Are assumptions reasonable? (5) Report: communicate findings, limitations and possible improvements. *Quick check:* You model population growth as exponential. After 100 years your model predicts 50 billion people. What assumption might be wrong?

5. **Cross-cultural.** The concept of compound interest was discussed in ancient Mesopotamian mathematics (c. 2000 BCE) and in Indian mathematics by Brahmagupta (7th century). Calculate: $100 at 10% compounded annually vs. 10% simple interest after 20 years. How large is the difference?

## Memorable takeaways
- Inverse proportion: y = k/x (product is constant).
- Simple interest (linear): A = P(1 + rt).
- Compound interest (exponential): A = P(1 + r)ⁿ.
- Growth factor > 1 means growth; between 0 and 1 means decay.
- Linear = constant difference. Exponential = constant ratio.
- All models have assumptions — always evaluate and report.

## Stretch yourself
A family takes out a $400,000 home loan at 6% p.a. compounded monthly (monthly rate = 0.5%). They make monthly repayments of $M. After one month, the balance is 400000 × 1.005 − M. After two months: (400000 × 1.005 − M) × 1.005 − M. Show that after n months the balance follows B = 400000(1.005)ⁿ − M × [(1.005ⁿ − 1)/0.005]. If the loan is paid off in 30 years (360 months), set B = 0 and solve for M. Calculate the total amount paid (360 × M) and the total interest. Discuss assumptions (fixed rate, no extra payments). Compare with a 20-year loan — what happens to M and total interest? 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Confusing growth rate with growth factor.** 5% growth means factor = 1.05, not 0.05.
- **Using simple interest when compound is meant.** Read carefully — "compounded annually" means A = P(1+r)ⁿ, not A = P(1+rn).
- **Forgetting units.** If rate is annual but time is in months, convert: monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12.
- **Extrapolating too far.** Exponential models break down eventually — populations hit limits, investments change rates. Always state assumptions.
- **Assuming inverse proportion without checking.** Not all decreasing relationships are inversely proportional — verify that the product is constant.

A note on certainty. The compound interest formula A = P(1 + r)ⁿ is mathematically exact given its assumptions (fixed rate, regular compounding). Real financial products have variable rates, fees, and different compounding frequencies. The formula gives a precise prediction under stated conditions; the modelling cycle requires you to identify when those conditions do or do not hold in practice.
