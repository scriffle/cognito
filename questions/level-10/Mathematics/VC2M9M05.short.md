# VC2M9M05 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving direct proportion, rates, ratio and scale, including financial contexts; formulate problems and interpret solutions in terms of the situation; evaluate the model and report methods and findings.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Direct proportion: y = kx. Rates compare quantities with different units. Ratios compare same-unit quantities. Scale links models to reality. Models need assumptions and evaluation.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **use mathematical modelling** with proportion, rates, ratio and scale to solve practical and financial problems.
- You should formulate, solve, interpret, evaluate and report — including stating assumptions.

## Core content

**Direct proportion and rates.** y = kx. The constant k is often a rate: $/hour, km/h, $/kg. Fuel at $1.85/L: cost = 1.85 × litres.

**Ratio.** Compares same-kind quantities. Concrete 1:2:4 (cement:sand:gravel), total 2.1 m³: parts = 7, each = 0.3 m³. Cement 0.3, sand 0.6, gravel 1.2.

**Scale.** 1:25 000 means 1 cm = 250 m. Areas scale as n², volumes as n³.

**Financial — unit pricing.** Compare by cost per unit. 750 g for $4.20 = $5.60/kg. 1.2 kg for $6.00 = $5.00/kg. Bigger pack wins.

**Currency exchange.** AUD 1 = USD 0.66. Convert $500 AUD: 500 × 0.66 = USD 330. Assumptions: no fees, rate constant.

**Worked example.** BBQ for 320 people, ratio 5:3:2 (sausages:burgers:veg). Parts = 10. Sausages: 160, burgers: 96, veg: 64. Cost at $1.20, $2.50, $1.80 each: $192 + $240 + $115.20 = $547.20. Add 10–15% buffer for waste.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Supermarkets display unit pricing (legal requirement). VicRoads uses scale drawings. Melbourne housing reports $/m². First Nations trade networks involved proportional exchange ratios. Currency exchange is daily reality for families sending remittances. Queen Victoria Market traders work with rates ($/kg) constantly.

## Memorable takeaways
- Direct proportion: y = kx. Rate k connects the quantities.
- Ratio: total parts = sum of ratio numbers. Divide accordingly.
- Scale: 1:n means ×n for real dimensions. Area ×n², volume ×n³.
- Unit pricing finds the best buy. Always state assumptions.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I used the wrong rate direction." Speed = distance/time. For time, divide distance by speed.
- "I forgot unit conversion." Ensure cm/m/km are compatible before calculating.
- "I divided ratio wrong." Total parts = SUM of all ratio numbers, not the largest.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Water at 12 L/min for 45 min?* 540 litres.
- *Share $450 in ratio 2:3:4?* Parts = 9. Shares: $100, $150, $200.
- *Map 1:50 000, distance 3.8 cm?* 3.8 × 50 000 = 190 000 cm = 1.9 km.
- *OJ: 2L for $5.40 or 3L for $7.50?* $2.70/L vs $2.50/L. 3L is better value.
- *AUD 1 200 to EUR at 0.58?* €696.
