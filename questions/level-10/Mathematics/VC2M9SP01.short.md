# VC2M9SP01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Recognise the constancy of the sine, cosine and tangent ratios for a given angle in right-angled triangles using properties of similarity.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** All right triangles with the same angle are similar (AA). Similar triangles have constant side ratios. This constancy IS sin, cos and tan — they depend only on the angle, not triangle size.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **recognise why trig ratios are constant** for any given angle — because all right-angled triangles with that angle are similar.
- You should connect similarity to the definitions of sin, cos and tan.

## Core content

**Similar triangles, same ratios.** All right triangles with the same acute angle share angles (AA criterion) → similar → side ratios are identical. A 30-60-90 triangle of any size has the same opp/hyp ratio.

**The constancy.** Draw right triangles with a 35° angle — hypotenuse 5, 10, 20. In all cases, opp/hyp ≈ 0.574. The size doesn't matter. Only the angle determines the ratio.

**Defining sin, cos, tan.** Because ratios are constant: sin θ = opp/hyp, cos θ = adj/hyp, tan θ = opp/adj. These are properties of the angle, not any particular triangle. A calculator stores one value for sin 35° that works universally.

**Verification.** Draw three 45° right triangles of different sizes. Measure opp/hyp — all give ≈ 0.707 (= sin 45°). Differences are measurement error, not mathematical variation.

**Why this matters.** We can use a small angle measurement to find lengths in huge triangles (buildings, cliffs, distances). The small diagram is similar to the real triangle, so the ratio transfers exactly.

**Worked example.** Two 37° right triangles, hypotenuse 8 and 20 (scale factor 2.5). opp/hyp = 4.81/8 = 12.04/20 = 0.601 in both. Constant because similar.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Melbourne surveyors rely on this: the instrument triangle is similar to the building triangle. First Nations toolmakers scale boomerang designs while maintaining angle relationships. Navigational traditions across all seafaring cultures discovered angle-to-side constancy independently — geometry is universal.

## Memorable takeaways
- Same angle + right angle → similar triangles (AA).
- Similar → constant side ratios → sin, cos, tan work at any scale.
- Trig ratios depend ONLY on the angle, never on triangle size.
- This is why trigonometry enables indirect measurement.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Trig ratios depend on size." They don't — similarity guarantees constancy for a given angle.
- "I got different ratios when measuring." Measurement error, not mathematical failure. The constancy is exact.
- "I confused corresponding sides." Match sides by position relative to the equal angle, not by length.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Two right triangles both with 40° — similar?* Yes. Both have 90° + 40° → third angle 50°. Same angles → similar.
- *Opp in triangle B (hyp 25) if opp in triangle A (hyp 10) = 7.66?* Scale × 2.5 → 19.15.
- *sin 28° = 0.469 means what?* opp/hyp = 0.469 in ANY right triangle with a 28° angle.
- *cos 45° for three different-sized 45° triangles?* All give ≈ 0.707.
- *sin 60° vs sin 30° — which larger?* sin 60° ≈ 0.866 > sin 30° = 0.5. Steeper angle → larger opposite relative to hypotenuse.
