## Core content

**1. Angle at the centre theorem.** The angle subtended at the centre of a circle by an arc is twice the angle subtended at the circumference by the same arc. If the central angle is 2α, the inscribed angle is α. This holds for any point on the major arc. *Quick check:* An inscribed angle subtends the same arc as a central angle of 120°. Find the inscribed angle.

**2. Angles in the same segment.** All inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equal. If two chords from the same arc meet the circumference at different points, the angles they make are identical. This follows directly from the centre-angle theorem. *Quick check:* Two inscribed angles subtend the same arc. One measures 35°. What is the other?

**3. Thales' theorem.** An angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle (90°). This is a special case of the centre-angle theorem: the diameter subtends a central angle of 180°, so the inscribed angle is 90°. *Quick check:* A triangle is inscribed in a circle with one side being a diameter. What type of triangle is it?

**4. Tangent-radius perpendicularity.** A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency. If the tangent meets the circle at P and the centre is O, then angle OPT = 90° (where T is any point on the tangent other than P). *Quick check:* A tangent touches a circle of radius 5 cm at point P. The tangent is 12 cm long to an external point T. Find OT.

**5. Alternate segment theorem.** The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the inscribed angle in the alternate segment. If a tangent at A and chord AB make an angle θ, then the inscribed angle in the opposite segment also equals θ. *Quick check:* A tangent at point A makes a 40° angle with chord AB. Find the inscribed angle in the alternate segment.

**6. Worked example — cyclic quadrilateral.**
Prove that opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

*Proof:*
- Let ABCD be a cyclic quadrilateral inscribed in a circle with centre O.
- Arc BCD subtends angle BAD at the circumference. The reflex angle BOD at the centre = 2 × angle BAD.
- Arc BAD subtends angle BCD at the circumference. The reflex angle corresponding = 2 × angle BCD.
- The two central angles (reflex and non-reflex) sum to 360°.
- So 2 × angle BAD + 2 × angle BCD = 360°, giving angle BAD + angle BCD = 180°.
- Similarly, angle ABC + angle ADC = 180°.

**7. Multi-step problems.** Many problems require combining two or more circle theorems. A typical approach: identify which theorem applies at each step, mark known angles, and work through logically. *Quick check:* In a circle, a tangent at A and a chord AB subtend 50° at A. If B and C are on the circle and angle ACB is in the alternate segment, find angle ACB.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Circle geometry underpins engineering design (gears, cams, bearings) and architecture (arched structures, dome geometry) across Victorian industry.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Circular forms are central to First Nations art, ceremony and spatial organisation. The geometric properties of circles are embedded in the design of ceremonial grounds and stone arrangements found across Australia.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Circle theorems were systematically developed by Euclid (3rd century BCE) and Apollonius of Perga. Thales' theorem (6th century BCE) is among the earliest known geometric proofs.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A circular clock face has centre O. Two marks at 12 and 4 subtend what angle at the centre? What angle would they subtend at a point on the circumference between 6 and 8?
2. **Civic.** A circular roundabout has radius 15 m. A tangent lane meets the circle at point P. A car on the tangent is 20 m from the centre. Find the distance from the car to the tangent point.
3. **Vocational.** An engineer designs a gear with a chord connecting two teeth 8 cm apart. The radius is 5 cm. Find the perpendicular distance from the centre to the chord.
4. **Ethical.** A student claims that opposite angles in every quadrilateral are supplementary. Is this true? Correct the statement.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Research Thales' theorem and its historical significance. Then prove that the angle in a semicircle is 90° using the centre-angle theorem.

## Memorable takeaways
- Centre angle = 2 × inscribed angle (same arc).
- Angles in the same segment are equal.
- Angle in a semicircle = 90° (Thales).
- Tangent ⊥ radius at the point of contact.
- Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°.

## Stretch yourself
In a circle with centre O, tangent TA touches at A. Chord AB subtends angle ATB = 50° at the tangent point. (a) Find the inscribed angle ACB in the alternate segment. (b) Find the central angle AOB. (c) If OA = 10 cm, find the length of chord AB. (d) Prove that if two tangents are drawn from an external point, they are equal in length. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Confusing central and inscribed angles.** The central angle is at the centre; the inscribed angle is at the circumference. The central is always twice the inscribed.
- **Applying cyclic quadrilateral theorem to non-cyclic shapes.** The supplementary property holds only when all four vertices lie on a circle.
- **Forgetting that tangent-radius is 90°.** The perpendicularity is exact, not approximate.
- **Mixing up alternate segment angles.** The angle between the tangent and chord equals the inscribed angle in the OPPOSITE segment, not the same one.

## Try drawing this
Draw a circle with a tangent at point A. Draw a chord AB. Mark the angle between the tangent and chord (α). Then mark the inscribed angle in the alternate segment (also α). This illustrates the alternate segment theorem.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**
1. *Inscribed angle from 120° central.* 120°/2 = 60°.
2. *Same segment angles.* Both are 35°.
3. *Triangle in semicircle.* Right-angled triangle (angle opposite the diameter = 90°).
4. *OT distance.* By Pythagoras: OT = √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13 cm.
5. *Alternate segment angle.* 40°.
6. *Cyclic quadrilateral proof.* Opposite angles sum to 180° — proven via centre-angle theorem.
7. *Multi-step problem.* Alternate segment: angle ACB = 50°.

**Try these scenarios**
1. *Clock.* 12 to 4 spans 4 hours = 4 × 30° = 120° at centre. Inscribed angle between 6 and 8: 60°.
2. *Roundabout.* Distance = √(20² − 15²) = √175 = 5√7 ≈ 13.2 m.
3. *Gear chord.* Half-chord = 4 cm. Perpendicular distance = √(5² − 4²) = √9 = 3 cm.
4. *Student claim.* Only cyclic quadrilaterals (all vertices on a circle) have supplementary opposite angles. General quadrilaterals do not.
5. *Thales' proof.* Diameter subtends 180° at centre. Inscribed angle = 180°/2 = 90° ✓.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** (a) Alternate segment: ACB = 50°. (b) Centre angle = 2 × 50° = 100°. (c) Triangle OAB is isosceles (OA = OB = 10). Angle AOB = 100°, so base angles = 40° each. AB = 2 × 10 × sin(50°) ≈ 15.32 cm. (d) Let tangents TA and TB touch at A and B. OA ⊥ TA, OB ⊥ TB. Triangles OAT and OBT share OT, OA = OB (radii), angles OAT = OBT = 90°. By RHS congruence, TA = TB.
