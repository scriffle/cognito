# VC2M9M02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve problems involving very small and very large measurements, timescales and intervals expressed in scientific notation.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Scientific notation: a × 10ⁿ (1 ≤ a < 10). Multiply → multiply a-values, add exponents. Divide → divide a-values, subtract exponents. Orders of magnitude measure relative scale. Essential for science, engineering and understanding our world.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **work with scientific notation** for very large and small measurements.
- You should convert, calculate, compare magnitudes, and solve problems in scientific contexts.

## Core content

**Form.** a × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ a < 10. Large: 5,400,000 = 5.4 × 10⁶. Small: 0.00032 = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴.

**Multiply.** (3 × 10⁴) × (2 × 10⁵) = 6 × 10⁹. Multiply a-values, add exponents.

**Divide.** (8 × 10⁷) ÷ (4 × 10³) = 2 × 10⁴. Divide a-values, subtract exponents.

**Add/subtract.** Match exponents first. 3.2 × 10⁵ + 4.1 × 10⁴ = 3.2 × 10⁵ + 0.41 × 10⁵ = 3.61 × 10⁵.

**Orders of magnitude.** Each power of 10 is one order. Bacterium (10⁻⁶) to Earth (10⁷) = 13 orders apart.

**Worked example.** Light speed: 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Sun distance: 1.5 × 10¹¹ m. Time = 1.5×10¹¹ ÷ 3×10⁸ = 5×10² = 500 seconds ≈ 8.3 minutes.

**SI prefixes.** nano = 10⁻⁹, micro = 10⁻⁶, milli = 10⁻³, kilo = 10³, mega = 10⁶, giga = 10⁹.

**Victorian context.** Australian Synchrotron works at 10⁻¹⁰ m. CSIRO telescopes detect signals from 10²⁵ m. Deep time — 65,000 years (6.5 × 10⁴) of continuous First Nations culture — requires large-number literacy. COVID virus: 10⁻⁷ m (measured at Doherty Institute).

## Memorable takeaways
- a × 10ⁿ: a between 1 and 10, n is integer.
- Multiply: multiply a-values, add exponents.
- Divide: divide a-values, subtract exponents.
- Add/subtract: match exponents first.
- Adjust if result not in form (e.g. 15 × 10³ → 1.5 × 10⁴).

## Watch out for these traps
- a must be 1 ≤ a < 10. Adjust after operations (15 × 10³ → 1.5 × 10⁴).
- Negative exponent = small number, not negative number.
- Can't add/subtract without matching exponents first.
- Positive exponent = large, negative = small (not the sign of the number).
