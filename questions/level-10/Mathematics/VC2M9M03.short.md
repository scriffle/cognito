# VC2M9M03 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve spatial problems, applying angle properties, scale, similarity, ratio, Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry in right-angled triangles.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Spatial problems integrate multiple tools. Pythagoras (c² = a² + b²) finds sides. Trig (SOH CAH TOA) connects angles and sides. Scale links diagrams to reality. Similarity means proportional sides. Combine tools for multi-step problems.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **solve spatial problems** by selecting and combining Pythagoras, trigonometry, scale and similarity.
- You should draw diagrams, identify right triangles, and choose appropriate tools.

## Core content

**Pythagoras.** c² = a² + b² (right triangles only). Example: 5 and 12 → c = 13. To find shorter side: a = √(c² − b²).

**Trigonometry.** sin θ = O/H, cos θ = A/H, tan θ = O/A. Find sides: rearrange. Find angles: use inverse (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹).

**Scale.** 1:500 means 1 cm = 5 m. Real = diagram × scale factor.

**Similarity.** Same angles → proportional sides (ratio k). Areas scale by k², volumes by k³.

**Worked example — building height.** 50 m from building, elevation angle 32°, eye height 1.6 m. h = 50 × tan 32° = 31.2 m. Total = 31.2 + 1.6 = 32.8 m.

**Combining tools.** Ship sails 12 km east, 5 km north. Distance = √(144 + 25) = 13 km. Direction = tan⁻¹(5/12) ≈ 22.6° north of east.

**Victorian context.** Surveyors mapping Victorian land use trig daily. Bolte Bridge pylons, road gradients in Dandenong Ranges, accessibility ramps. Navigation across Country uses angle estimation and triangulation. Eratosthenes calculated Earth's circumference (40,000 km) using shadows and angles.

## Memorable takeaways
- Pythagoras: c² = a² + b² (right triangles only).
- SOH CAH TOA: sin = O/H, cos = A/H, tan = O/A.
- Inverse trig finds angles from sides.
- Scale: real = diagram × factor.
- Always draw a diagram first.

## Watch out for these traps
- Pythagoras only works for RIGHT-angled triangles.
- Opposite/adjacent depend on which angle you use — always label from your angle.
- Calculator must be in degrees (DEG) mode, not radians.
- Always draw a diagram — prevents mixing up sides and angles.
