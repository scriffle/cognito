# VC2M9P01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** List all outcomes for two-step chance experiments both with and without replacement, using lists, tree diagrams, tables or arrays; assign probabilities to outcomes and events.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** With replacement: probabilities unchanged (independent). Without: they change (dependent). Tree diagrams list all outcomes systematically. Multiply along branches, add across paths.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **list all outcomes** for two-step experiments and **assign probabilities**, distinguishing between with and without replacement.
- You should use tree diagrams, tables and systematic lists to find every outcome.

## Core content

**Two-step experiments.** Two stages in sequence. Sample space = all combined outcomes. Coin flip + die roll = 2 × 6 = 12 outcomes.

**With replacement.** Item goes back. Probabilities stay the same. Draws are independent. Bag of 4 green, 6 yellow: P(GG) = 4/10 × 4/10 = 16/100.

**Without replacement.** Item stays out. Total decreases, composition changes. Draws are dependent. Same bag: P(GG) = 4/10 × 3/9 = 12/90 = 2/15.

**Tree diagrams.** Branches show each step. Write probabilities on branches (must sum to 1 at each node). Multiply along a path for that outcome's probability. Add across favourable paths for an event.

**Worked example.** Raffle: 10 tickets, 4 prizes, draw 2 without replacement. P(both prizes) = 4/10 × 3/9 = 12/90 = 2/15. P(exactly one) = 4/10 × 6/9 + 6/10 × 4/9 = 48/90 = 8/15. P(none) = 6/10 × 5/9 = 30/90 = 1/3. Check: 2/15 + 8/15 + 5/15 = 1 ✓.

**Tables and arrays.** For independent steps, a grid shows all combinations. Rows = step 1, columns = step 2. Works best for small, independent experiments.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Tattslotto draws balls without replacement — dependent probability determines actual odds. Melbourne Cup barrier draws use without-replacement selection. Card games across cultures (rummy, poker, mahjong) all involve without-replacement probability. Community raffles at cultural festivals use the same structure.

## Memorable takeaways
- With replacement: independent, same probabilities each step.
- Without replacement: dependent, update fractions (smaller denominator).
- Tree: multiply along paths (AND), add across paths (OR).
- All outcome probabilities must sum to 1.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I didn't update the denominator." Without replacement: total drops by 1 after each draw.
- "I multiplied when I should add." Multiply along ONE path. Add across MULTIPLE favourable paths.
- "I forgot order matters." Red-then-blue ≠ blue-then-red. Both count for "one of each."

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Coin + die: how many outcomes?* 2 × 6 = 12.
- *P(GG) with replacement, 4G and 6Y?* 4/10 × 4/10 = 4/25.
- *P(GG) without replacement?* 4/10 × 3/9 = 2/15.
- *Entrée (2 options) × main (3 options)?* 6 combinations.
- *P(both hearts) from deck, no replacement?* 13/52 × 12/51 = 1/17.
- *Socks: 6 black, 4 white, P(matching pair)?* P(BB) + P(WW) = 30/90 + 12/90 = 42/90 = 7/15.
