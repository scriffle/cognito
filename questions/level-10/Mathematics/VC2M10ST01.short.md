# VC2M10ST01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Compare data distributions for continuous numerical variables using quartiles and interquartile range and appropriate data displays including boxplots, histograms and dot plots; discuss shapes in terms of centre, spread, shape and outliers.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Distributions are described by centre (median), spread (IQR), shape (symmetric/skewed) and outliers. Boxplots display the five-number summary. IQR = Q3 − Q1 measures the middle 50% and resists outliers. Parallel boxplots enable visual comparison.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **compare data distributions** using quartiles, IQR, and displays (boxplots, histograms, dot plots).
- You should describe distributions using centre, spread, shape and outliers in context.

## Core content

**Five-number summary.** Min, Q1, median, Q3, max. Q1 = median of lower half; Q3 = median of upper half.

**IQR.** Q3 − Q1. Measures spread of middle 50%. Not affected by outliers. Small IQR = data clustered.

**Boxplots.** Box from Q1 to Q3, median marked inside, whiskers to min/max (or last non-outlier). Outlier rule: below Q1 − 1.5×IQR or above Q3 + 1.5×IQR.

**Shape.** Symmetric (mirror image), positively skewed (tail right), negatively skewed (tail left).

**Worked example.** Class A: median 62, IQR 15. Class B: median 63, IQR 10. Similar centres, but A is more variable. B performs more consistently.

**Comparing.** Use parallel boxplots. Compare: which has higher median? Greater spread (IQR)? More/less symmetric? Outliers? Always interpret in context.

**Victorian context.** ABS uses boxplots to compare Victorian income distributions. VCE results analysed via five-number summaries. Melbourne Water compares rainfall distributions across decades. Data literacy empowers Aboriginal communities to interpret and challenge research about themselves.

## Memorable takeaways
- Five-number summary: min, Q1, median, Q3, max.
- IQR = Q3 − Q1 (resistant to outliers).
- Outlier: below Q1 − 1.5×IQR or above Q3 + 1.5×IQR.
- Describe: centre, spread, shape, outliers — always in context.
- Parallel boxplots for quick comparison.

## Watch out for these traps
- Q1 is a data value, not "25% of the data."
- The box contains exactly 50% of data — wider box means more spread, not more data.
- Always check shape before choosing mean vs median (use median for skewed data).
- Numbers without context are meaningless — always interpret in terms of what's measured.
