# VC2M10ASP01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Prove and apply angle relationships involving radii, diameters, chords and tangents of circles.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Centre angle = 2 × inscribed angle. Tangent ⊥ radius at contact. Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **prove and apply circle theorems** involving angles, chords, tangents and radii.
- You should be able to construct deductive proofs and solve multi-step circle geometry problems.

## Core content

**Centre-angle theorem:** Central angle = 2 × inscribed angle (same arc). 120° central → 60° inscribed.

**Same segment:** All inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equal.

**Thales' theorem:** Angle in a semicircle = 90°.

**Tangent-radius:** Tangent ⊥ radius at the point of tangency.

**Alternate segment:** Angle between tangent and chord = inscribed angle in the opposite segment.

**Cyclic quadrilateral:** Opposite angles are supplementary (sum to 180°).

**Victorian context.** Circle theorems underpin gear design, arched structures and dome geometry. Euclid systematised these results in the 3rd century BCE; Thales' theorem dates to the 6th century BCE.

## Memorable takeaways
- Centre angle = 2 × inscribed angle.
- Angle in a semicircle = 90°.
- Tangent ⊥ radius at contact point.
- Cyclic quadrilateral: opposite angles sum to 180°.

## Watch out for these traps
- Confusing central and inscribed angles.
- Applying cyclic quadrilateral rules to non-cyclic shapes.
- Alternate segment: the angle equals the inscribed angle in the OPPOSITE segment.
