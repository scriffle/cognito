# VC2M10M02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Interpret and use logarithmic scales in applied contexts involving small and large quantities and change.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Log scales compress huge ranges into manageable numbers. Each step is ×10, not +10. Richter, decibel and pH scales are real-world examples.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **interpret and use logarithmic scales** for quantities spanning many orders of magnitude.
- You should calculate actual ratios from log-scale differences.

## Core content

**Why log scales?** Some quantities span millions or billions. On a linear scale, small values vanish. A log scale spaces powers of 10 equally: 1, 10, 100, 1 000 are evenly spaced.

**Logarithm (intuitive).** log₁₀(x) = "10 to what power gives x?" log₁₀(1 000) = 3. Each +1 on the log means ×10 in reality.

**Richter scale.** +1 magnitude = 10× ground shaking, ~31.6× energy. Magnitude 7 vs 4: shaking 10³ = 1 000×; energy ≈ 31 623×.

**Decibels.** +10 dB = 10× intensity. Conversation (60 dB) to concert (110 dB) = 10⁵ = 100 000×.

**pH scale.** Each −1 unit = 10× more acidic. pH 3 is 100× more acidic than pH 5.

**Worked example.** 2021 Melbourne quake (5.9) vs 2004 Boxing Day (9.1). Difference = 3.2. Shaking = 10^3.2 ≈ 1 585×. Energy = 10^4.8 ≈ 63 096×.

**Log-scale graphs.** Equal spacing = equal ratios. A straight line on a log-linear graph = exponential growth. Small visual gaps can represent enormous real differences.

**Victorian and cultural context.** The 2021 Melbourne earthquake brought log scales into public conversation. EPA Victoria measures noise in decibels. Melbourne Water monitors river pH. Aboriginal ranger programs use pH measurement for water-quality monitoring on Country.

## Memorable takeaways
- Each log step is ×10, not +10.
- Richter: +1 = 10× shaking. dB: +10 = 10× intensity. pH: −1 = 10× acidity.
- Small log-scale differences hide enormous real-world ratios.
- Log scales reveal patterns (exponential, power laws) that linear scales cannot.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Magnitude 6 is twice magnitude 3." It is 1 000× the shaking — log scales are multiplicative.
- "+10 dB = 10% louder." It is 10× the intensity.
- "pH 3 is three times more acidic than pH 1." pH 3 is 100× LESS acidic than pH 1.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *log₁₀(10 000)?* 4.
- *Magnitude 5 vs 3 — shaking ratio?* 10² = 100×.
- *90 dB vs 30 dB — intensity ratio?* 10⁶ = 1 000 000×.
- *pH 2 vs pH 5 — acidity ratio?* 10³ = 1 000×.
- *Two points 2 units apart on log graph?* Actual ratio = 100.
