# VC2M10M03 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve practical problems by applying Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry to right-angled triangles, including problems involving direction and angles of elevation and depression.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Pythagoras' theorem (a² + b² = c²) finds unknown sides. Trigonometric ratios (sin, cos, tan) connect angles to sides. Elevation/depression are measured from the horizontal.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **apply Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry** to solve practical problems in right-angled triangles, including direction, elevation and depression.
- You should choose the right tool (Pythagoras or trig ratio) for each problem.

## Core content

**Pythagoras' theorem.** In a right-angled triangle: a² + b² = c² (c = hypotenuse). Use when you know two sides and need the third. To find a shorter side, rearrange: a² = c² − b².

**Trigonometric ratios (SOH CAH TOA).** For angle θ (not the right angle): sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent. Label sides relative to the angle you are using.

**Finding an unknown angle.** Use the inverse function. If tan θ = 8/6, then θ = tan⁻¹(1.333) ≈ 53.1°. Always check your calculator is in degree mode.

**Angles of elevation and depression.** Elevation is measured upward from the horizontal to a line of sight. Depression is measured downward from the horizontal. Both form right triangles with horizontal and vertical components.

**Worked example.** Standing 200 m from Eureka Tower, angle of elevation to top = 56°. tan 56° = h/200. h = 200 × tan 56° = 200 × 1.4826 ≈ 296.5 m. Check: actual height is 297 m. ✓

**Direction and bearings.** Bearings are measured clockwise from north. Navigation problems split journeys into right-triangle components (north–south and east–west), then use Pythagoras for total distance and tan for bearing angle.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Surveyors across Victoria use trigonometry to measure heights of buildings and terrain without climbing them. The Eureka Tower, Rialto Tower and Arts Centre Spire are all verifiable by angle-of-elevation problems. Traditional First Nations navigation uses star angles, landmark elevation and horizon observations — applied trigonometry embedded in cultural practice. The Wurdi Youang stone arrangement (Wadawurrung Country) aligns to solstice positions requiring elevation-angle calculations.

## Memorable takeaways
- Pythagoras: a² + b² = c² — for sides when you know two sides.
- SOH CAH TOA — for connecting angles to sides.
- Elevation: up from horizontal. Depression: down from horizontal.
- Bearings: clockwise from north. Split into right-triangle components.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I used the wrong sides for the ratio." Label opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse relative to the angle you are working with.
- "I mixed up elevation and depression." Both measured from the horizontal — elevation looks up, depression looks down.
- "My calculator was in radians." Ensure degree mode when working with degrees.

## Model responses

- *Hypotenuse, legs 5 and 12?* c = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13 cm.
- *Ladder: 8 m up wall, 6 m from base?* c = √(64 + 36) = √100 = 10 m.
- *Opposite side, angle 35°, hyp 20?* opp = 20 sin 35° = 20 × 0.5736 ≈ 11.5 cm.
- *Ramp: rises 3 m over 12 m horizontal?* θ = tan⁻¹(3/12) = tan⁻¹(0.25) ≈ 14.0°.
- *Building height: 50 m away, 40° elevation?* h = 50 tan 40° ≈ 42.0 m.
- *Boat: 8 km east, 6 km north?* Distance = √(64+36) = 10 km. Bearing = tan⁻¹(8/6) ≈ 053°.
