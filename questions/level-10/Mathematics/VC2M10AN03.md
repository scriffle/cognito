# VC2M10AN03 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M10AN03. Define logarithms, apply log laws and investigate logarithmic scales.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**
  - A logarithm logₐ(b) = c means aᶜ = b — it answers "what power of a gives b?"
  - Log laws mirror exponent laws: log(ab) = log(a) + log(b), log(a/b) = log(a) − log(b), log(aⁿ) = n·log(a).
  - Logarithmic scales (pH, Richter, decibels) compress vast ranges into manageable numbers.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking.

## Core content

**1. Definition.** logₐ(b) = c means aᶜ = b, where a > 0, a ≠ 1, b > 0. The base a and argument b must be positive. log₁₀(1000) = 3 because 10³ = 1000. log₂(16) = 4 because 2⁴ = 16. logₐ(1) = 0 for any valid base. logₐ(a) = 1. *Quick check:* Evaluate log₃(81).

**2. The product law.** logₐ(mn) = logₐ(m) + logₐ(n). Proof outline: let logₐ(m) = p and logₐ(n) = q. Then m = aᵖ and n = aᵠ. mn = aᵖ⁺ᵠ. So logₐ(mn) = p + q. Example: log₁₀(20) = log₁₀(4 × 5) = log₁₀(4) + log₁₀(5). *Quick check:* Express log₁₀(50) in terms of log₁₀(2) and log₁₀(5).

**3. The quotient law.** logₐ(m/n) = logₐ(m) − logₐ(n). This follows from the product law applied to m = (m/n) × n. Example: log₁₀(5) = log₁₀(10/2) = log₁₀(10) − log₁₀(2) = 1 − log₁₀(2). *Quick check:* Simplify log₁₀(200) − log₁₀(2).

**4. The power law.** logₐ(mⁿ) = n·logₐ(m). This "brings the exponent down" as a multiplier. Example: log₁₀(8) = log₁₀(2³) = 3·log₁₀(2). This law is essential for solving exponential equations. *Quick check:* Express log₂(32) using the power law.

**5. Solving exponential equations.** For 10ˣ = 250: take log₁₀ of both sides: x = log₁₀(250) ≈ 2.398. For 2ˣ = 100: x = log₁₀(100)/log₁₀(2) = 2/0.301 ≈ 6.644 (using the change-of-base formula). *Quick check:* Solve 5ˣ = 200 to two decimal places.

**6. Worked example — combining log laws.**
Simplify: 2·log₁₀(3) + log₁₀(4) − log₁₀(6).

*Follow the thinking:*
- 2·log₁₀(3) = log₁₀(3²) = log₁₀(9) [power law].
- log₁₀(9) + log₁₀(4) = log₁₀(36) [product law].
- log₁₀(36) − log₁₀(6) = log₁₀(36/6) = log₁₀(6) [quotient law].

**7. Logarithmic scales.** pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]: a pH of 3 has 10 times the H⁺ concentration of pH 4. Richter scale: magnitude increase of 1 = 10× amplitude. Decibels: +10 dB = 10× sound intensity. These scales make it practical to compare quantities spanning many orders of magnitude. *Quick check:* A solution has [H⁺] = 10⁻⁵ mol/L. Find its pH.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Logarithmic analysis is used in environmental science (water pH monitoring), seismology, and acoustics across Victorian research institutions and industries.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** The ability to reason about vast differences in scale — from the micro to the macro — resonates with First Nations peoples' understanding of ecological systems, which operate across spatial scales from individual plants to continent-wide fire management.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** John Napier (Scotland, 1614) and Henry Briggs (England) developed logarithm tables that revolutionised computation. Logarithmic ideas also appear in Indian mathematical traditions, particularly in the works of the Kerala school.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A concert measures 110 dB and a whisper measures 30 dB. How many times more intense is the concert than the whisper? (Each 10 dB = 10× intensity.)
2. **Civic.** An environmental agency tests water: [H⁺] = 3.16 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L. Find the pH and determine if the water is acidic (pH < 7).
3. **Vocational.** An engineer solves 1.06ⁿ = 2 to find how many years for an investment to double at 6% annual growth. Express in terms of logarithms and evaluate.
4. **Ethical.** A news report claims an earthquake of magnitude 8 is "three times worse" than magnitude 7. Evaluate using the Richter scale.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Research Napier's invention of logarithms. Then simplify: log₁₀(125) + log₁₀(8) using log laws.

## Memorable takeaways
- logₐ(b) = c means aᶜ = b — the logarithm is the exponent.
- Product: log(ab) = log(a) + log(b). Quotient: log(a/b) = log(a) − log(b). Power: log(aⁿ) = n·log(a).
- logₐ(1) = 0, logₐ(a) = 1 — these are universal.
- Logarithmic scales convert multiplicative relationships to additive ones.
- To solve aˣ = b: x = log(b)/log(a).

## Watch out for these traps
- **log(a + b) ≠ log(a) + log(b).** There is no log law for sums. The product law applies to log(a × b).
- **log(a) − log(b) ≠ log(a − b).** The quotient law gives log(a/b), not log(a − b).
- **Forgetting domain restrictions.** log(x) is undefined for x ≤ 0. Always check the argument is positive.
- **Misusing the power law.** log(a²) = 2·log(a), but (log a)² ≠ 2·log(a).
