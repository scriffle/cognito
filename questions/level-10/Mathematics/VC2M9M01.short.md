# VC2M9M01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve problems involving the volume and surface area of right prisms, cylinders and composite objects using appropriate units.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Volume = cross-sectional area × height. Surface area = sum of all face areas. Composite objects: break into parts, add (or subtract for holes). Units matter: 1 cm³ = 1 mL, 1000 L = 1 m³.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate volumes and surface areas** of prisms, cylinders and composite objects.
- You should select appropriate formulas, handle composites, and convert units.

## Core content

**Prisms.** V = A_cross-section × h. Rectangular: V = lwh, SA = 2(lw + lh + wh). Triangular: V = ½bh × length.

**Cylinders.** V = πr²h. SA = 2πr² + 2πrh. Curved surface unwraps to rectangle: 2πr × h.

**Unit conversions.** 1 cm³ = 1 mL. 1000 cm³ = 1 L. 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ = 1000 L.

**Composite objects.** Break into recognisable shapes. Calculate each part. Add volumes. For SA: subtract shared/internal faces.

**Worked example.** Shed: rectangular base (4×3×2 = 24 m³) + triangular roof (½×4×1×3 = 6 m³) = 30 m³ total.

**Objects with holes.** Subtract the void: block volume − cylinder hole volume.

**Victorian context.** Metro Tunnel excavation volumes, Skyrail cylindrical pylons, council swimming pool chemical dosing (volume-dependent). Water management on Country requires capacity calculations. Paint coverage, tank sizing, packaging — all use these formulas.

## Memorable takeaways
- Volume = cross-section × height (prisms and cylinders).
- Cylinder: V = πr²h. SA = 2πr² + 2πrh.
- Composite: break into parts, add/subtract.
- 1 cm³ = 1 mL. 1 L = 1000 cm³. 1 m³ = 1000 L.
- Always state units. Convert before calculating if dimensions are mixed.

## Watch out for these traps
- Using diameter instead of radius in πr²h.
- Missing faces in surface area — draw a net to check.
- Mixing units (cm and m in the same calculation).
- For composites: internal/shared faces aren't on the surface — subtract them from SA.
