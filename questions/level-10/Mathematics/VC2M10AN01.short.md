# VC2M10AN01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Define rational and irrational numbers, and perform operations with surds and fractional exponents.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Rational = p/q; irrational = non-terminating, non-repeating. Surds give exact representations. Fractional exponents unify roots and powers: x^(1/n) = ⁿ√x.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **classify numbers as rational or irrational**, **simplify surds**, and **work with fractional exponents**.
- You should be able to add, multiply, and rationalise surds, and convert between surd and exponent notation.

## Core content

**Rational vs irrational:** ¾, 0.333… are rational. √2, π are irrational (non-terminating, non-repeating decimals).

**Simplifying surds:** Extract largest perfect square factor. √50 = 5√2. √72 = 6√2.

**Adding surds:** Only like surds combine. 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2. √12 + √27 = 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3.

**Multiplying:** √a × √b = √(ab). √8 × √2 = √16 = 4.

**Rationalising:** 1/√3 = √3/3. Multiply top and bottom by the surd.

**Fractional exponents:** x^(m/n) = (ⁿ√x)^m. 8^(2/3) = (∛8)² = 4. 27^(2/3) = 9.

**Victorian context.** Exact surd expressions are standard in VCE Mathematics. The Pythagorean school discovered √2 is irrational. Indian mathematicians Aryabhata and Brahmagupta advanced work with irrational quantities.

## Memorable takeaways
- Only like surds can be added: √2 + √3 ≠ √5.
- Simplify by extracting the largest perfect square factor.
- x^(m/n) = (ⁿ√x)^m — fractional exponents follow the same laws as integer exponents.
- Rationalise denominators to standard form.

## Watch out for these traps
- Adding unlike surds: √2 + √3 ≠ √5.
- Incomplete simplification: √72 = 6√2, not 3√8.
- Wrong exponent addition: x^(1/2) × x^(1/3) = x^(5/6), not x^(1/6).
