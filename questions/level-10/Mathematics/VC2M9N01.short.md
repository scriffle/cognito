# VC2M9N01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Recognise that the real number system includes the rational numbers and the irrational numbers, and solve problems involving real numbers with and without using digital tools.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Real numbers = rational + irrational. Rational: a/b form, terminates or repeats. Irrational: non-terminating, non-repeating (√2, π). Surds are irrational roots.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **recognise and work within the real number system** — distinguishing rational from irrational numbers and solving problems with both.
- You should classify numbers, simplify surds, and perform operations with real numbers.

## Core content

**The real number system.** Every point on the number line is a real number — either rational or irrational. No gaps.

**Rational numbers.** Can be written as a/b (integers, b ≠ 0). Decimal terminates (0.75 = 3/4) or repeats (0.333... = 1/3). All integers, fractions and percentages are rational.

**Irrational numbers.** Decimal is non-terminating AND non-repeating. Cannot be any fraction. Examples: √2, √3, √5, π. Not all roots are irrational — √4 = 2 (rational), √9 = 3 (rational).

**Surds.** Irrational roots. Simplify by finding perfect square factors: √12 = √(4×3) = 2√3. √50 = 5√2. Operations: like surds add (3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2); unlike surds don't combine. Multiply: √a × √b = √(ab).

**Worked example.** Square with side 5 cm: diagonal = √(25+25) = √50 = 5√2 ≈ 7.07 cm. The exact answer 5√2 is irrational.

**Estimation.** Locate surds between consecutive integers using nearby perfect squares. √7 is between √4 = 2 and √9 = 3 (≈ 2.65).

**Victorian and cultural context.** Builders across Victoria encounter √2 every time they cut a square tile diagonally. The MCG's circular dimensions involve π. GPS coordinates use irrational numbers. First Nations circular structures (fish traps, stone arrangements) implicitly involve π and surds in their geometry. The golden ratio φ = (1+√5)/2 appears in design traditions across many cultures.

## Memorable takeaways
- Real = rational + irrational. Complete number line, no gaps.
- Rational: fraction form, decimal terminates or repeats.
- Irrational: no fraction form, decimal neither terminates nor repeats.
- Simplify surds: find perfect square factors. √12 = 2√3.
- Like surds combine. Unlike surds don't.

## Watch out for these traps
- "√4 is irrational." No — √4 = 2 (integer, rational). Only roots of non-perfect-squares are irrational.
- "22/7 is irrational." No — 22/7 is a fraction (rational). It approximates π but isn't equal to it.
- "0.333... is irrational." No — repeating decimals ARE rational. 0.333... = 1/3.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *0.272727... as a fraction?* 27/99 = 3/11.
- *Classify √7, √25, π, 22/7?* Irrational, rational (=5), irrational, rational (fraction).
