# VC2M10AM02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Explore how the average rate of change approaches a limiting value as intervals become smaller.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Average rate of change = secant gradient = (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a). As the interval shrinks to zero, this approaches the tangent gradient — the instantaneous rate of change (derivative).

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate average rates of change** and understand how they approach an **instantaneous rate** as intervals shrink.
- You should be able to compute rates over shrinking intervals and identify the limiting value.

## Core content

**Average rate of change:** (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a). For f(x) = x² from 1 to 3: (9 − 1)/2 = 4.

**Secant line:** The straight line through (a, f(a)) and (b, f(b)). Its gradient equals the average rate.

**Shrinking intervals:** For f(x) = x² at x = 2, average rate from 2 to 2+h: h=1→5, h=0.1→4.1, h=0.01→4.01. Approaches 4.

**General formula:** ((x+h)² − x²)/h = 2x + h → 2x as h → 0. Instantaneous rate of x² at x = a is 2a.

**Real-world example:** Position s(t) = t² + 3t. Instantaneous speed at t = 2: s'(2) = 2(2) + 3 = 7 m/s.

**Local linearity:** Zooming in on a smooth curve near a point, it appears indistinguishable from the tangent line.

**Victorian context.** This concept underpins VCE Mathematical Methods. Newton and Leibniz developed calculus in the 17th century, building on earlier work by the Kerala school of Indian mathematics.

## Memorable takeaways
- Average rate = secant gradient; instantaneous rate = tangent gradient.
- The limit as h → 0 converts average to instantaneous.
- For f(x) = x², the instantaneous rate at x = a is 2a.
- Numerical tables suggest the limit; algebra proves it.

## Watch out for these traps
- Confusing average rate over an interval with the rate at a specific point.
- Using Δx/Δf instead of Δf/Δx.
- Forgetting units (e.g. metres per second, not just a number).
