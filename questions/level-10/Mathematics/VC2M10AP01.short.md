# VC2M10AP01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Counting principles and factorial notation for efficient counting in probability and combinatorics.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Multiplication principle: m × n for sequential choices. n! counts arrangements. Permutations count ordered arrangements; combinations count unordered selections.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **apply counting principles and factorial notation** to determine arrangements and selections.
- You should be able to distinguish permutations from combinations and use them in probability.

## Core content

**Multiplication principle:** 5 shirts × 3 pants = 15 outfits. Extends to any number of stages.

**Factorial:** n! = n × (n−1) × … × 1. 5! = 120. 0! = 1. Counts arrangements of n objects.

**Permutations (order matters):** P(n,r) = n!/(n−r)!. Rank 3 from 10: P(10,3) = 720.

**Combinations (order irrelevant):** C(n,r) = n!/[r!(n−r)!]. Choose 3 from 10: C(10,3) = 120.

**Key question:** Does order matter? Committee → combination. Ranked positions → permutation.

**Probability example:** Committee of 3 from 5 women + 4 men. P(≥2 women) = 50/84 = 25/42.

**Victorian context.** Pascal's triangle encoding C(n,r) was studied by Yang Hui (China, 13th century), Pingala (India, 2nd century BCE), and al-Karajī (Persia, 10th century) before Pascal.

## Memorable takeaways
- Multiplication principle: m × n for sequential independent choices.
- n! counts total arrangements of n distinct objects.
- Permutations = ordered; combinations = unordered. C = P/r!.
- Ask "does order matter?" to choose the right formula.

## Watch out for these traps
- Using permutations when combinations are needed (dividing by r! is the fix).
- Forgetting that 0! = 1 by definition.
- Confusing "with repetition" (nʳ) and "without repetition" (P(n,r)).
