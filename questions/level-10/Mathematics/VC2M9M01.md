# VC2M9M01 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M9M01. Solve problems involving the volume and surface area of right prisms, cylinders and composite objects using appropriate units.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics (Measurement).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Volume measures the space inside a 3D object; surface area measures the total area of all its faces.
  - Volume of a prism or cylinder = area of cross-section × height (or length).
  - Composite objects are made from combinations of simpler shapes — calculate by adding or subtracting component volumes/areas.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Design and Technologies.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate volumes and surface areas** of prisms, cylinders and composite objects.
- You are learning to select appropriate formulas and units for 3D measurement problems.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Calculate the volume and surface area of rectangular and triangular prisms.
- Calculate the volume and surface area of cylinders.
- Break composite objects into components and calculate their total volume and surface area.
- Use appropriate units (cm³, m³, litres) and convert between them.

## Core content

**1. Volume of right prisms.** Volume = cross-sectional area × length (or height). Rectangular prism: V = lwh. Triangular prism: V = (1/2 × base × height of triangle) × length. Any right prism: V = A_cross-section × h. Units: cm³, m³. *Quick check:* Find the volume of a rectangular prism with l = 5 cm, w = 3 cm, h = 8 cm.

**2. Surface area of prisms.** SA = sum of areas of all faces. Rectangular prism: SA = 2(lw + lh + wh). Triangular prism: SA = 2 × triangle area + 3 rectangular faces. Systematically list every face. *Quick check:* Find the surface area of a cube with side 4 cm.

**3. Volume of cylinders.** V = πr²h (area of circular cross-section × height). Example: r = 3 cm, h = 10 cm → V = π(9)(10) = 90π ≈ 282.7 cm³. *Quick check:* A cylinder has diameter 8 cm and height 12 cm. Find its volume.

**4. Surface area of cylinders.** SA = 2πr² + 2πrh (two circular ends + curved surface). The curved surface "unwraps" into a rectangle with dimensions 2πr × h. Example: r = 3, h = 10 → SA = 2π(9) + 2π(3)(10) = 18π + 60π = 78π ≈ 245.0 cm². *Quick check:* A can has radius 4 cm and height 11 cm. Find the area of the label (curved surface only).

**5. Unit conversions.** 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ = 1,000 L. 1 cm³ = 1 mL. 1 L = 1000 cm³. To convert: multiply each dimension's conversion factor. 2 m × 3 m × 1.5 m = 9 m³ = 9000 L. *Quick check:* A fish tank is 60 cm × 30 cm × 40 cm. Find its volume in cm³ and in litres.

**6. Worked example — composite object.**
A storage shed has a rectangular base (4 m × 3 m × 2 m high walls) topped by a triangular prism roof (height of triangle = 1 m, base = 4 m, length = 3 m).

*Follow the thinking:*
- Volume of rectangular section: 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 m³.
- Volume of triangular roof section: (1/2 × 4 × 1) × 3 = 6 m³.
- Total volume: 24 + 6 = 30 m³.
- Surface area: Base = 4 × 3 = 12 m². Two long walls = 2(3 × 2) = 12 m². Two end walls (rectangle + triangle) = 2[(4 × 2) + (1/2 × 4 × 1)] = 2(8 + 2) = 20 m². Roof = 2 × (slant height × 3). Slant = √(2² + 1²) = √5 ≈ 2.24 m. Roof area ≈ 2(2.24 × 3) = 13.4 m².
- Total SA ≈ 12 + 12 + 20 + 13.4 = 57.4 m² (excluding the base if it's a floor).

**7. Problem-solving strategies.** For composite objects: (1) sketch and label all dimensions, (2) break into recognisable shapes, (3) calculate each component's V or SA separately, (4) add (or subtract if a shape is cut away), (5) check units. For "objects with holes," subtract the void from the solid. *Quick check:* A rectangular block (10 × 8 × 5 cm) has a cylindrical hole drilled through it (radius 2 cm, through the full 5 cm). How would you find the remaining volume?

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A water tank is a cylinder with diameter 2 m and height 1.5 m. Calculate its volume in m³ and convert to litres. If water costs $2.50 per 1000 L, how much to fill it?
2. **Civic.** A council builds a triangular prism-shaped planter box: cross-section is a right triangle with legs 40 cm and 30 cm, and length 2 m. Calculate the volume of soil needed (in litres).
3. **Vocational.** A painter needs to coat the outside of a cylindrical water tank (radius 1.5 m, height 2.5 m) including the top but not the bottom. Calculate the surface area to be painted. If one litre covers 10 m², how much paint is needed?
4. **Ethical.** A company advertises a "50% bigger" cereal box. The original is 20 × 8 × 30 cm. The new box is 20 × 8 × 35 cm. Is it actually 50% bigger by volume? Calculate and discuss truth in advertising.
5. **Cross-cultural.** The Great Pyramid of Giza is approximately a square-based pyramid: base 230 m, height 146 m. V = (1/3) × base area × height. Calculate its volume. How many Olympic swimming pools (50 × 25 × 2 m) would fit inside?

## Memorable takeaways
- Volume of prism/cylinder = cross-sectional area × height.
- Surface area = sum of ALL face areas (don't miss any!).
- Cylinder: V = πr²h. SA = 2πr² + 2πrh.
- Composite: break into parts, calculate each, add (or subtract for holes).
- 1 cm³ = 1 mL. 1000 cm³ = 1 L. 1 m³ = 1000 L.

## Stretch yourself
Design a composite water storage tank made from a cylinder with a hemispherical top and a cone-shaped base. Given total height = 3 m (hemisphere radius = 0.8 m, cone height = 0.5 m, cylinder height = remaining), calculate: (1) the volume of each component and total capacity in litres; (2) the total external surface area (for coating); (3) if the tank costs $45 per m² of material, what is the total material cost? Then redesign: if you keep the same total volume but change to a pure cylinder (no hemisphere or cone), what dimensions minimise the surface area? (Research: the optimal cylinder has h = 2r.) Compare costs. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Using diameter instead of radius.** πr²h uses radius, not diameter. If given diameter, divide by 2 first.
- **Missing faces in surface area.** Draw a net to ensure you count every face. Don't forget the ends of a prism.
- **Mixing units.** If some dimensions are in cm and others in m, convert everything to the same unit before calculating.
- **Forgetting that composite SA ≠ sum of individual SAs.** Where objects join, those faces are internal (not on the surface). Subtract the shared/hidden areas.
- **Not including units in answers.** Volume in cm³ or m³; surface area in cm² or m².

A note on certainty. The formulas for volume and surface area of prisms and cylinders are mathematically exact — they are derived from the definitions of area and the principle that volume = cross-section × height for uniform objects. When applied to real objects, measurement uncertainty in the dimensions affects the final answer, but the mathematics itself is precise.
