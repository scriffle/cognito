# VC2M9A01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Apply the exponent laws to numerical expressions with integer exponents and the zero exponent, and extend to variables.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Exponent laws simplify powers with the same base. Multiply → add exponents. Divide → subtract. Power of power → multiply. Zero exponent = 1. Negative exponent = reciprocal. Laws extend from numbers to algebraic expressions.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **apply exponent laws** to numerical and algebraic expressions with integer exponents.
- You should simplify using multiplication, division, power-of-power, zero and negative exponent rules.

## Core content

**Multiplication law.** aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ. Same base → add exponents. 2³ × 2⁴ = 2⁷. x⁵ × x³ = x⁸.

**Division law.** aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ. Same base → subtract. 3⁷ ÷ 3⁴ = 3³.

**Power of power.** (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ. Multiply exponents. (2³)⁴ = 2¹².

**Power of product.** (ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ. Apply power to all factors. (3x)² = 9x².

**Zero exponent.** a⁰ = 1 (for a ≠ 0). Proof: aⁿ ÷ aⁿ = a⁰ = 1.

**Negative exponent.** a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ. Flip to denominator. 2⁻³ = 1/8. x⁻² = 1/x².

**Worked example.** (2x³y²)⁴ ÷ (4x⁵y) = 16x¹²y⁸ ÷ 4x⁵y = 4x⁷y⁷.

**Victorian context.** CSIRO uses scientific notation (powers of 10) for atomic to astronomical scales. Australian Synchrotron measures in extreme powers. Indian mathematics named numbers up to 10⁵³. Computer science relies on powers of 2.

## Memorable takeaways
- Multiply: add exponents (aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ).
- Divide: subtract exponents (aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ).
- Power of power: multiply exponents ((aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ).
- a⁰ = 1 (not zero). a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ (not negative).
- Bases must be the same to apply multiplication/division laws.

## Watch out for these traps
- Can't add exponents with different bases (2³ × 3² ≠ 6⁵).
- (x³)⁴ = x¹² (multiply), but x³ × x⁴ = x⁷ (add) — don't confuse.
- a⁰ = 1, not 0. Negative exponent = reciprocal, not negative number.
- (2xy)³ = 8x³y³, not 2x³y³ — apply power to ALL factors.

- *10⁸ ÷ 10³.* 10⁵.
