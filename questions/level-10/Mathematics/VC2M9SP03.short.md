# VC2M9SP03 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Design, test and refine algorithms involving a sequence of steps and decisions based on geometric constructions and theorems; discuss and evaluate refinements.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** An algorithm is a precise step-by-step procedure. Geometric constructions can be expressed as algorithms with decision points. Every step should be justified by a theorem. Test by having others follow; refine for clarity and efficiency.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **design, test and refine algorithms** for geometric constructions.
- You should include decision points, justify steps with theorems, and evaluate for correctness, clarity and efficiency.

## Core content

**Geometric algorithms.** Step-by-step instructions producing a construction. Must be precise, unambiguous, correctly ordered. Example: perpendicular bisector in 4 clear steps.

**Decision points.** If/then conditions: "If arcs don't intersect, THEN increase compass width." Handle edge cases and errors.

**Linking to theorems.** Each step works because of a geometric theorem. Bisector works because "points equidistant from endpoints lie on the perpendicular bisector."

**Testing.** Give to someone unfamiliar. Can they follow exactly? Reveals ambiguity, missing steps, wrong order.

**Refining.** Improve clarity, add missing steps, remove redundancy, handle edge cases.

**Worked example — SSS triangle.** (1) Check triangle inequality. (2) Draw base. (3) Arc from left end (radius b). (4) Arc from right end (radius c). (5) If no intersection → impossible. (6) Connect intersection to endpoints. (7) Verify.

**Evaluation criteria.** Correctness, completeness, clarity, efficiency, justification.

**Victorian context.** CAD software runs geometric algorithms for architecture. CNC machines follow algorithmic cuts. Traditional construction (baskets, shelters, fish traps) are algorithms refined over millennia. Islamic geometric patterns follow precise algorithmic procedures. VCE Algorithmics teaches these principles.

## Memorable takeaways
- Algorithm = precise, step-by-step, always works.
- Include decision points (if/then) for edge cases.
- Justify every step with a theorem.
- Test with someone else → reveals ambiguity.
- Evaluate: correct? complete? clear? efficient? justified?

## Watch out for these traps
- Assuming the reader knows what you know — test with a novice.
- Missing decision points for when things go wrong.
- Steps out of order (each depends on previous ones).
- Writing the algorithm without justifying WHY each step works.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Compass > half segment: why?* Arcs must overlap to intersect. Too small = no intersection.
- *"Draw a circle" — ambiguous.* Missing: centre and radius.
- *Angle bisector justification.* Intersection equidistant from both rays → lies on bisector (equidistance theorem).
- *Hexagon algorithm.* Step compass around circle at radius width. Works because central angle = 60° (equilateral triangles).
- *8 steps (clear) vs 5 steps (efficient).* Better depends on audience: novice needs clarity; expert prefers efficiency.
