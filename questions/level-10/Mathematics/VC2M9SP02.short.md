# VC2M9SP02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Apply the enlargement transformation to shapes and objects using dynamic geometry software as appropriate; identify and explain, using language of similarity, ratio and scale, aspects that remain the same and those that change.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Enlargement multiplies distances from a centre by scale factor k. Angles stay the same. Lengths × k, area × k². Image is similar to original.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **apply enlargement transformations** and identify what stays the same (angles, shape) and what changes (lengths × k, area × k²).
- You should use similarity language and dynamic geometry software to explore enlargements.

## Core content

**What is enlargement?** Every point moves to k times its distance from the centre of enlargement. k > 1: bigger. 0 < k < 1: smaller. k < 0: inverted through centre. Image is similar to original.

**What stays the same.** Angles, shape, proportions between sides. The image looks identical except for size.

**What changes.** Lengths × k. Perimeter × k. Area × k². If k = 3: lengths triple, area × 9.

**Finding centre and scale factor.** Join corresponding points — lines meet at the centre. k = image distance from centre / original distance.

**Worked example.** Logo 8 cm × 5 cm enlarged for 2 m banner. k = 200/8 = 25. New height: 5 × 25 = 125 cm. New area: 40 × 625 = 25 000 cm². Angles unchanged.

**Scale factors < 1 and negative.** k = 0.5: half-size reduction. k = −1: same size, opposite side of centre (180° rotation). k = −2: double size, inverted.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Melbourne architects scale plans from A3 to construction scale. The tram network map is a reduction of actual routes. 3D printing scales digital models to physical objects. First Nations artists scale designs from small artworks to large murals while preserving proportional relationships. Fashion designers scale patterns between sizes — each is an enlargement of the template.

## Memorable takeaways
- Enlargement: same shape, different size. From a centre, ×k.
- Stays same: angles, shape, proportions.
- Changes: lengths ×k, perimeter ×k, area ×k².
- k > 1 bigger, 0 < k < 1 smaller, k < 0 inverted.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Area scales by k." No — area scales by k². Double lengths → quadruple area.
- "Angles change." Never. Enlargement preserves all angles (similarity transformation).
- "I applied k to area directly." If k = 3, area = 20 → new area = 20 × 9 = 180, NOT 60.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Vertex 4 cm from O, scale factor 3 — new distance?* 4 × 3 = 12 cm.
- *Area 20 cm², enlarged ×4 — new area?* 20 × 16 = 320 cm².
- *Segment 5 cm → 12.5 cm — scale factor?* 12.5/5 = 2.5.
- *Factor −1 — what happens?* Same size, opposite side of centre (180° rotation).
- *Factor 0.5 from centroid — area?* 0.5² = 0.25 = quarter of original.
