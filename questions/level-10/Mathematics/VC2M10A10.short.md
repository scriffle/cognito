# VC2M10A10 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve problems involving gradients of parallel and perpendicular lines.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Parallel lines have equal gradients (m₁ = m₂). Perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal gradients (m₁ × m₂ = −1). These relationships let you determine line properties and construct new lines.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **use gradient relationships** to solve problems involving **parallel and perpendicular lines**.
- You should find equations of lines parallel or perpendicular to a given line through a specified point.

## Core content

**Gradient:** m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). Positive = uphill left-to-right; negative = downhill.

**Parallel:** m₁ = m₂. Same direction, never meet. y = 3x + 2 and y = 3x − 5 are parallel.

**Perpendicular:** m₁ × m₂ = −1. If m = 2, perpendicular m = −1/2. If m = −3/4, perpendicular m = 4/3.

**Finding equations:** Use point-gradient form y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). For parallel: use the same gradient. For perpendicular: use the negative reciprocal.

**Worked example.** Path y = (3/4)x + 2 crossed at right angles at (4, 5). Perpendicular m = −4/3. Equation: y − 5 = −4/3(x − 4) → y = −4x/3 + 31/3. Check: (3/4)(−4/3) = −1 ✓.

**Special case:** Vertical (undefined gradient) is perpendicular to horizontal (gradient 0).

**Victorian context.** Melbourne's Hoddle Grid has parallel streets crossed by perpendicular streets. Surveyors use perpendicular offsets for property mapping. Engineers on the Melbourne Metro Tunnel calculated perpendicular cross-sections.

## Memorable takeaways
- Parallel: m₁ = m₂.
- Perpendicular: m₁ × m₂ = −1 (negative reciprocals).
- Point-gradient form: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
- Vertical ⊥ horizontal is a special case.

## Watch out for these traps
- Forgetting the negative — the reciprocal of 3 is 1/3, but the *negative* reciprocal is −1/3.
- Using the wrong point when writing the new equation.
- The product rule doesn't apply to vertical/horizontal — handle as a special case.
