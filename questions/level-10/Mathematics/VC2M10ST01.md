## Core content

**1. The five-number summary.** For ordered data: **minimum**, **Q1** (lower quartile — median of lower half), **median** (Q2 — middle value), **Q3** (upper quartile — median of upper half), **maximum**. These five values summarise the distribution's position and spread. *Quick check:* Find the five-number summary for: 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 14, 16, 20.

**2. Interquartile range (IQR).** IQR = Q3 − Q1. It measures the spread of the middle 50% of data. Unlike the range (max − min), the IQR is not affected by outliers. A small IQR means data is clustered; large IQR means data is spread out. *Quick check:* Data has Q1 = 12 and Q3 = 28. What is the IQR? What percentage of data lies between Q1 and Q3?

**3. Boxplots.** A **boxplot** displays the five-number summary graphically: a box from Q1 to Q3 (with median marked inside), whiskers extending to min and max (or to the last non-outlier value). Outliers plotted as individual dots beyond the whiskers. Outlier rule: any value below Q1 − 1.5×IQR or above Q3 + 1.5×IQR. *Quick check:* IQR = 10, Q1 = 20, Q3 = 30. What are the outlier boundaries?

**4. Histograms and dot plots.** Histograms group continuous data into intervals (bins) and display frequencies as bars. They show the shape of the distribution clearly. Dot plots show individual data points — useful for smaller data sets. Both reveal clusters, gaps and outliers that boxplots may hide. *Quick check:* A histogram has most of its bars on the left with a tail stretching right. What shape is this distribution?

**5. Describing distributions — centre, spread, shape, outliers.** Centre: median (or mean). Spread: IQR (or range). Shape: symmetric (mirror image), positively skewed (tail right), negatively skewed (tail left). Outliers: unusually far from the bulk. Always discuss in context: "The median house price is $750,000 with an IQR of $200,000" — not just numbers without meaning. *Quick check:* Describe this distribution: exam scores with median 72, IQR 15, range 60, symmetric shape.

**6. Worked example — comparing two data sets.**
Two classes sit the same test. Class A: 45, 52, 55, 58, 60, 62, 65, 68, 70, 75, 92. Class B: 50, 55, 58, 60, 61, 63, 64, 66, 68, 70, 72.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Class A: min=45, Q1=55, median=62, Q3=70, max=92. IQR=15.
- Class B: min=50, Q1=58, median=63, Q3=68, max=72. IQR=10.
- Outlier check A: 55 − 1.5(15) = 32.5; 70 + 1.5(15) = 92.5. No outliers (92 < 92.5).
- Comparison: similar medians (62 vs 63), but A has greater spread (IQR 15 vs 10) and wider range (47 vs 22). A has more variability in performance. B is more consistently clustered around the median.
- Context: Class B performed more consistently; Class A had some very high and very low performers.

**7. Parallel boxplots for comparison.** Drawing boxplots on the same scale (parallel) makes comparison of centre, spread and outliers immediate and visual. Look for: which has higher median? Which is more spread? Which is more symmetric? Are there outliers in one but not the other? *Quick check:* Two boxplots show median salaries. Company A's box is narrow and high; Company B's box is wide and low. What does this tell you?

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** Record the daily screen time (in minutes) for two weeks. Calculate the five-number summary and IQR. Draw a boxplot. Is your screen time symmetric or skewed? Are there any outlier days?
2. **Civic.** A council compares water usage (kL/quarter) for 50 houses in two suburbs. Draw parallel boxplots and compare. Which suburb uses more water? Which is more variable? What factors might explain differences?
3. **Vocational.** A factory measures the weight of 40 products. If Q1 = 498g and Q3 = 502g (target 500g), calculate the IQR. Products outside 495g–505g are rejected. How does the IQR relate to quality control?
4. **Ethical.** A newspaper headline says "Average income in this suburb is $120,000." The median is $85,000. Explain how skewness causes the mean and median to differ. Which measure better represents the "typical" resident? Discuss how choice of statistic can mislead.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Compare life expectancy distributions for 5 different countries using boxplots (find data from the World Bank). Which countries have the highest median? The most variation? What might explain outliers?

## Memorable takeaways
- Five-number summary: min, Q1, median, Q3, max.
- IQR = Q3 − Q1 (middle 50%, resistant to outliers).
- Outlier rule: below Q1 − 1.5×IQR or above Q3 + 1.5×IQR.
- Describe distributions: centre, spread, shape, outliers — always in context.
- Parallel boxplots enable quick visual comparison.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Confusing Q1 and Q3 with "25% of the data."** Q1 is the value below which 25% of data lies — it's a data value, not a percentage.
- **Including the median when finding Q1 and Q3.** For odd-numbered data sets, exclude the median when finding quartiles of each half.
- **Reading boxplots as "the box contains most data."** The box contains exactly 50% (the middle half). Wider boxes mean MORE spread, not more data.
- **Ignoring context.** "Median = 72" means nothing without units and context. Always interpret in terms of what the data measures.
- **Assuming symmetric.** Many real data sets are skewed. Always check the histogram or boxplot shape before choosing mean vs median.

A note on certainty. The five-number summary and IQR are exact calculations from the data — there is no uncertainty in their computation. What requires judgment is the interpretation: whether a difference between two distributions is meaningful depends on context, sample size, and the purpose of the comparison. Statistical displays reveal patterns; human judgment interprets their significance.
