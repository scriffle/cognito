# VC2M10AN01 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M10AN01. Define rational and irrational numbers, and perform operations with surds and fractional exponents.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10 Advanced, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Rational numbers can be expressed as p/q (integers, p ≠ 0); irrational numbers cannot — their decimals neither terminate nor repeat.
  - Surds (e.g. √2, √3) are exact representations of irrational numbers, preferable to decimal approximations in precise mathematical work.
  - Fractional exponents unify root and power notation: x^(1/n) = ⁿ√x.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **distinguish rational from irrational numbers**, to **simplify surds**, and to **convert between surd and fractional exponent notation**.
- You are learning to perform arithmetic with surds and fractional exponents fluently.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Classify numbers as rational or irrational with justification.
- Simplify surds by extracting perfect square factors.
- Add, subtract, multiply and divide surds.
- Rationalise the denominator of a fraction containing a surd.
- Convert between surd notation and fractional exponent notation.

## Core content

**1. Rational and irrational numbers.** A rational number can be written as a fraction p/q where p and q are integers, q ≠ 0. Examples: ¾, −2, 0.333… = ⅓. An irrational number cannot be expressed as such a fraction — its decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-repeating. Examples: √2 ≈ 1.41421356…, π, e. *Quick check:* Classify each as rational or irrational: √9, √7, 0.125, π.

**2. Simplifying surds.** To simplify √n, find the largest perfect square factor. √50 = √(25 × 2) = 5√2. The surd is in simplest form when the number under the root has no perfect square factors other than 1. √12 = √(4 × 3) = 2√3. √75 = √(25 × 3) = 5√3. *Quick check:* Simplify √72.

**3. Adding and subtracting surds.** Only like surds (same number under the root) can be combined. 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2. Unlike surds cannot be combined: 3√2 + 5√3 stays as written. Simplification may reveal like surds: √12 + √27 = 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3. *Quick check:* Simplify √18 + √50.

**4. Multiplying and dividing surds.** √a × √b = √(ab). √a ÷ √b = √(a/b). For example: √3 × √5 = √15. √6 × √6 = √36 = 6. 2√3 × 4√5 = 8√15. *Quick check:* Simplify √8 × √2.

**5. Rationalising the denominator.** To eliminate a surd from the denominator, multiply numerator and denominator by that surd. 1/√3 = (1 × √3)/(√3 × √3) = √3/3. For 5/√2 = 5√2/2. The purpose is to express the result in a standard form. *Quick check:* Rationalise the denominator of 4/√5.

**6. Worked example — combining surd operations.**
Simplify: 3√12 − √48 + √27.

*Follow the thinking:*
- 3√12 = 3 × 2√3 = 6√3.
- √48 = √(16 × 3) = 4√3.
- √27 = √(9 × 3) = 3√3.
- Combined: 6√3 − 4√3 + 3√3 = 5√3.

**7. Fractional exponents.** x^(1/2) = √x, x^(1/3) = ∛x, x^(m/n) = (ⁿ√x)^m = ⁿ√(x^m). For example: 8^(2/3) = (∛8)² = 2² = 4. This notation unifies roots and powers under the exponent laws: x^a × x^b = x^(a+b), (x^a)^b = x^(ab). *Quick check:* Evaluate 27^(2/3) and 16^(3/4).

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Exact surd expressions are standard in VCE Mathematics, where decimal approximations lose precision. Engineers and scientists use fractional exponents in formulas for wave behaviour, signal decay and material stress.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** The concept of exact versus approximate value resonates with the precision required in First Nations navigation — where exact knowledge of star positions and seasonal timing is critical, not approximate estimates.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** The discovery that √2 is irrational is attributed to the Pythagorean school (5th century BCE) and was considered revolutionary. Indian mathematicians Aryabhata and Brahmagupta worked extensively with irrational quantities and square roots.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A square courtyard has area 72 m². Express the side length as a simplified surd and as a decimal approximation.
2. **Civic.** A council plots a diagonal path across a square park of side 50 m. Express the path length exactly using surds.
3. **Vocational.** A carpenter needs the diagonal of a rectangular board 3 m by 4 m. Then find the diagonal of one 5 m by 12 m. Express both exactly.
4. **Ethical.** A student writes √2 + √3 = √5. Evaluate this claim numerically and explain the error.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Research the Pythagorean discovery of irrational numbers. Then simplify completely: √200 − √50 + √32.

## Memorable takeaways
- Rational = expressible as p/q; irrational = non-terminating, non-repeating decimal.
- Simplify surds by extracting the largest perfect square factor.
- Only like surds can be added or subtracted.
- x^(m/n) = (ⁿ√x)^m — fractional exponents unify roots and powers.
- Rationalise denominators by multiplying by the surd.

## Stretch yourself
Prove that √2 is irrational using proof by contradiction. Then: (a) simplify (3√2 + √8)², (b) rationalise 6/(√3 − 1) by multiplying by the conjugate (√3 + 1)/(√3 + 1), (c) express 32^(3/5) × 8^(2/3) as a single power of 2, (d) determine whether √2 × √3 × √6 is rational or irrational, with justification. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Adding unlike surds.** √2 + √3 ≠ √5. Surds can only be added if the number under the root is the same.
- **Incomplete simplification.** √72 = √(36 × 2) = 6√2, not √(9 × 8) = 3√8 (which can be simplified further).
- **Wrong exponent rule.** x^(1/2) × x^(1/3) = x^(5/6), not x^(1/6). Add the exponents.
- **Forgetting to rationalise.** Leaving √3 in the denominator is not considered simplified form.

## Try drawing this

**Try these scenarios**
1. *Square courtyard.* Side = √72 = 6√2 ≈ 8.485 m.
2. *Diagonal path.* d = √(50² + 50²) = √5000 = 50√2 ≈ 70.71 m.
3. *Carpenter diagonals.* √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5 m (exact). √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13 m (exact).

5. *Simplification.* √200 − √50 + √32 = 10√2 − 5√2 + 4√2 = 9√2.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Proof: assume √2 = p/q in lowest terms. Then 2 = p²/q², so p² = 2q². Thus p² is even, so p is even — write p = 2k. Then 4k² = 2q², so q² = 2k², meaning q is also even. But p and q both even contradicts "lowest terms." Therefore √2 is irrational. (a) (3√2 + 2√2)² = (5√2)² = 50. (b) 6(√3 + 1)/((√3)² − 1²) = 6(√3 + 1)/2 = 3(√3 + 1) = 3√3 + 3. (c) 32 = 2⁵, so 32^(3/5) = 2³ = 8. 8 = 2³, so 8^(2/3) = 2² = 4. Product = 8 × 4 = 32 = 2⁵. (d) √2 × √3 × √6 = √(2 × 3 × 6) = √36 = 6. Rational.
