# VC2M10A05 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M10A05. Substitute values into formulas to determine an unknown and rearrange formulas to solve for a particular term.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics (Algebra).
- **Big ideas:**
  - A formula is a rule connecting variables — substituting known values lets you find the unknown.
  - Rearranging (changing the subject) uses inverse operations in reverse order.
  - The same formula can be written with any variable as the subject.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **substitute values into formulas** to determine an unknown, and to **rearrange formulas** to solve for a particular term.
- You are learning that algebraic manipulation is a tool for unlocking information locked inside a relationship.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Substitute known values into a formula and evaluate the unknown.
- Rearrange a formula to change the subject, including formulas with squares, square roots and fractions.
- Check your rearrangement by substituting values back in.
- Apply these skills to formulas from science, finance and measurement.

## Core content

**1. Substitution.** Given a formula like v = u + at, if u = 12, a = 3 and t = 5, substitute: v = 12 + 3(5) = 27. Replace every variable with its value, then evaluate. *Quick check:* If A = πr² and r = 4, find A (leave in terms of π or evaluate to 2 d.p.).

**2. Solving for the unknown after substitution.** Sometimes the unknown is not the subject. If v = u + at, v = 30, u = 10, a = 4, find t: 30 = 10 + 4t → 20 = 4t → t = 5. Substituting first, then solving, works when you have numbers for everything except one variable. *Quick check:* If P = 2l + 2w, P = 56 and l = 18, find w.

**3. Rearranging (changing the subject).** To make a different variable the subject, use inverse operations in reverse order. From v = u + at, make t the subject: subtract u → v − u = at; divide by a → t = (v − u)/a. *Quick check:* Rearrange A = ½bh to make h the subject.

**4. Rearranging with squares and square roots.** From E = ½mv², make v the subject: multiply both sides by 2 → 2E = mv²; divide by m → 2E/m = v²; take the square root → v = √(2E/m). From c = √(a² + b²), make a the subject: square both sides → c² = a² + b²; subtract b² → a² = c² − b²; square root → a = √(c² − b²). *Quick check:* Rearrange s = ut + ½at² to make a the subject.

**5. Rearranging with fractions.** From 1/f = 1/u + 1/v (lens formula), make v the subject: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u → v = 1/(1/f − 1/u). Or multiply through by fuv to clear fractions: uv = fv + fu → uv − fv = fu → v(u − f) = fu → v = fu/(u − f). *Quick check:* Rearrange the formula R = V/I to make I the subject.

**6. Worked example: speed, distance, time in a Victorian context.**

A V/Line train travels from Southern Cross to Ballarat (110 km). Using d = st:

*Follow the thinking:*
- Given d = 110 km, t = 1.5 hours. Find s.
- Rearrange: s = d/t = 110/1.5 ≈ 73.3 km/h.
- Check: 73.3 × 1.5 = 110 ✓.
- Now: if a different train averages 90 km/h, find t.
- Rearrange: t = d/s = 110/90 ≈ 1.22 hours ≈ 1 h 13 min.

**7. Checking your rearrangement.** Always verify by substituting values into both the original and rearranged formulas. If they give the same result, the rearrangement is correct. This is your error-detection tool. *Quick check:* You rearranged v² = u² + 2as to get s = (v² − u²)/(2a). How would you check this is correct?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Engineers at VicRoads use the formula v² = u² + 2as (kinematics) to calculate stopping distances for road safety design. Melbourne Water uses flow-rate formulas (Q = Av) rearranged to find pipe cross-sections. The Bureau of Meteorology applies temperature-conversion formulas (F = 9C/5 + 32) in climate reports. AMSI publishes formula-manipulation resources for Victorian teachers.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal land-management planning involves area and volume formulas rearranged to find dimensions — for example, calculating the width of a controlled-burn strip given a target area and fixed length. Water allocation formulas for environmental flows on Country (e.g. Yorta Yorta water-rights calculations along the Murray) require rearrangement to solve for flow rates or time. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Financial formulas (simple interest I = PRT, compound interest A = P(1 + r)ⁿ) are used by families across all cultural backgrounds for savings, loans and business decisions. Currency-conversion formulas are daily tools for multicultural communities with international connections.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** The formula for converting Celsius to Fahrenheit is F = 9C/5 + 32. Melbourne's average summer maximum is 26 °C. Convert to Fahrenheit. Then rearrange to make C the subject and convert 98.6 °F to Celsius.
2. **Civic.** A council engineer at the City of Greater Dandenong uses the formula A = πr² to design a circular roundabout with area 200 m². Rearrange to find r. Calculate the radius to 2 decimal places.
3. **Vocational.** An electrician uses the power formula P = V²/R. A heater draws P = 2 400 W from V = 240 V. Rearrange to find R. Then rearrange again to make V the subject.
4. **Ethical.** A loan company advertises using the simple-interest formula I = PRT but does not clearly explain what each variable means. Discuss why understanding formula rearrangement empowers consumers to check whether advertised rates match actual charges.
5. **Cross-cultural.** You are helping a family member compare mobile phone plans across two countries. Plan A charges in AUD using the formula C = 0.05m + 30 (where m = minutes). Rearrange to find how many minutes they can use for a budget of $55. Then substitute to verify.

## Memorable takeaways
- Substitute: replace variables with values, then evaluate.
- Rearrange: undo operations in reverse order (inverse operations).
- Squares → square root. Multiply → divide. Add → subtract.
- Always check by substituting values into both forms.
- The same formula serves many purposes depending on what you solve for.

## Stretch yourself
The compound-interest formula is A = P(1 + r)ⁿ, where A = final amount, P = principal, r = interest rate per period, and n = number of periods. (a) Substitute P = $5 000, r = 0.04, n = 3 to find A. (b) Rearrange to make P the subject. If A = $10 000, r = 0.06 and n = 5, find the initial investment needed. (c) Rearrange to make r the subject (this requires taking an nth root). If P = $2 000, A = $2 500 and n = 4, find r as a percentage. Show all working and check each answer by substituting back. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I substituted but forgot order of operations." Always follow BODMAS/BIDMAS after substituting. Evaluate powers before multiplication.
- "I rearranged but lost a negative sign." Track signs carefully, especially when subtracting terms from both sides.
- "I took the square root but forgot ±." In physical contexts, often only the positive root makes sense (length, speed), but mathematically v² = 25 gives v = ±5.
- "I rearranged correctly but didn't check." One wrong operation contaminates everything that follows. Substituting back catches errors.

A note on certainty. Algebraic rearrangement follows fixed logical rules — if the operations are applied correctly, the result is certain. The formulas themselves are models; their accuracy depends on the context and assumptions behind them.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *A = πr², r = 4.* A = 16π ≈ 50.27.
2. *P = 56, l = 18, find w.* 56 = 2(18) + 2w → 56 = 36 + 2w → 2w = 20 → w = 10.
3. *A = ½bh, make h the subject.* Multiply by 2: 2A = bh. Divide by b: h = 2A/b.
4. *s = ut + ½at², make a the subject.* Subtract ut: s − ut = ½at². Multiply by 2: 2(s − ut) = at². Divide by t²: a = 2(s − ut)/t².
5. *R = V/I, make I the subject.* I = V/R.
6. *Check s = (v² − u²)/(2a).* Substitute values (e.g. v = 10, u = 2, a = 3): s = (100 − 4)/6 = 16. Check in original: v² = u² + 2as → 100 = 4 + 2(3)(16) = 4 + 96 = 100. ✓

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Temperature.* F = 9(26)/5 + 32 = 46.8 + 32 = 78.8 °F. Rearranged: C = 5(F − 32)/9. C = 5(98.6 − 32)/9 = 5(66.6)/9 = 37 °C.
2. *Roundabout.* r = √(A/π) = √(200/π) = √63.66 ≈ 7.98 m.
3. *Electrician.* R = V²/P = 240²/2400 = 57600/2400 = 24 Ω. V = √(PR) = √(P × R).
4. *Consumer empowerment.* If you can rearrange I = PRT to find R = I/(PT), you can calculate the actual rate being charged and compare it to the advertised rate — protecting yourself from hidden costs.
5. *Mobile plan.* C = 0.05m + 30. Rearrange: m = (C − 30)/0.05. For $55: m = (55 − 30)/0.05 = 25/0.05 = 500 minutes. Check: 0.05(500) + 30 = 25 + 30 = 55. ✓

Check: 7472.58 × 1.06⁵ ≈ 10000 ✓. (c) r = (A/P)^(1/n) − 1 = (2500/2000)^(1/4) − 1 = 1.25^0.25 − 1 = 1.0574 − 1 = 0.0574 = 5.74%. Check: 2000 × 1.0574⁴ ≈ 2500 ✓.
