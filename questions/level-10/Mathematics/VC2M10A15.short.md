# VC2M10A15 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Use mathematical modelling to solve applied problems involving inverse proportion, growth and decay, including in financial contexts to establish the compound interest formula; formulate problems choosing linear, quadratic or exponential models; interpret solutions; evaluate and modify models.
- **Year / Area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Mathematical models translate real situations into equations. Compound interest is repeated simple interest (exponential growth). Choosing the right model depends on the pattern of change. All models have assumptions and limitations.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **use mathematical modelling** to solve problems involving inverse proportion, growth, decay and finance.
- You should choose appropriate models, interpret solutions in context, and evaluate assumptions.

## Core content

**Inverse proportion.** y = k/x. Product is constant. Example: 4 workers × 6 hours = 24 worker-hours; 8 workers take 3 hours. Graph is a hyperbola.

**Simple interest (linear).** A = P(1 + rt). Same amount added each period. $2000 at 5% for 3 years: A = 2000 × 1.15 = $2300.

**Compound interest (exponential).** A = P(1 + r)ⁿ. Interest on interest. $1000 at 8% for 3 years: A = 1000(1.08)³ = $1259.71.

**Growth and decay.** Growth factor > 1: increasing. Factor < 1: decreasing. 5% growth → factor 1.05. 15% depreciation → factor 0.85. Car at $30,000 depreciating 15%/year for 4 years: 30000(0.85)⁴ = $15,660.

**Choosing a model.** Constant differences → linear. Constant ratios → exponential. Rate of change changes linearly → quadratic.

**Worked example.** Aisha: $5000 at 6% compound for 10 years = 5000(1.06)¹⁰ = $8954. Ben: 6% simple = 5000(1.6) = $8000. Compound wins by $954.

**Modelling cycle.** Formulate → Solve → Interpret → Evaluate → Report. Always state assumptions and limitations.

**Victorian context.** RBA interest rates affect Victorian mortgages. Melbourne Water models dam levels with decay models during drought. Aboriginal land management reflects understanding of ecosystem recovery rates. Financial literacy programs address compound interest in community lending. ## Memorable takeaways
- Inverse proportion: y = k/x (product constant).
- Simple interest (linear): A = P(1 + rt).
- Compound interest (exponential): A = P(1 + r)ⁿ.
- Growth factor: 1 + rate. Decay factor: 1 − rate.
- Linear = constant difference. Exponential = constant ratio.
- All models have assumptions — evaluate and report.

## Watch out for these traps
- Confusing rate with factor: 5% growth means multiply by 1.05, not 0.05.
- Using simple interest formula when compound is specified.
- Forgetting to convert units (annual rate with monthly time → divide by 12).
- Extrapolating exponential models too far (populations hit limits).
