## Core content

**1. Pythagoras' theorem.** In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c and shorter sides a and b: **a² + b² = c²**. Use it when you know two sides and need the third. *Quick check:* A right triangle has legs 5 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

**2. Finding a shorter side.** Rearrange: a² = c² − b². If the hypotenuse is 10 m and one leg is 6 m: a² = 100 − 36 = 64 → a = 8 m. *Quick check:* A ladder reaches 8 m up a wall. Its foot is 6 m from the wall. How long is the ladder?

**3. Trigonometric ratios.** For angle θ in a right-angled triangle (not the right angle):
- **sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse** (SOH)
- **cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse** (CAH)
- **tan θ = opposite / adjacent** (TOA)

Label sides relative to the angle you are using. *Quick check:* In a right triangle with angle 35°, hypotenuse 20 cm, find the opposite side.

**4. Finding an unknown angle.** Use the inverse function. If tan θ = 8/6, then θ = tan⁻¹(8/6) = tan⁻¹(1.333) ≈ 53.1°. Always check your calculator is in degree mode. *Quick check:* A ramp rises 3 m over a horizontal distance of 12 m. Find the angle of inclination.

**5. Angles of elevation and depression.** The **angle of elevation** is measured upward from the horizontal to a line of sight. The **angle of depression** is measured downward from the horizontal. Both form right triangles with the horizontal and vertical. They are alternate angles when between parallel horizontal lines. *Quick check:* You look up at a 40° angle to see the top of a building. You are 50 m from the base. How tall is the building?

**6. Worked example: height of the Eureka Tower from a distance.**

Standing 200 m from the base of the Eureka Tower, the angle of elevation to the top is 56°.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Draw the right triangle: horizontal = 200 m (adjacent), height = h (opposite), angle = 56°.
- Use tan: tan 56° = h / 200.
- h = 200 × tan 56° = 200 × 1.4826 ≈ 296.5 m.
- Check: Eureka Tower is 297 m tall. ✓ (Our answer is consistent with reality.)

**7. Direction and bearing problems.** Bearings are measured clockwise from north (e.g. N40°E or 040°). Navigation problems often require splitting a journey into right-triangle components (north–south and east–west). Then use Pythagoras for total distance and tan for the bearing angle. *Quick check:* A boat travels 8 km east then 6 km north. What is the straight-line distance from the start, and on what bearing?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Surveyors across Victoria use trigonometry to measure heights of buildings, trees and terrain features without climbing them. The Eureka Tower (297 m), Melbourne's Rialto Tower and the Arts Centre Spire all have heights verifiable by angle-of-elevation problems. VicRoads uses angles of inclination for road-grade calculations. The Port of Melbourne uses bearings and trigonometry for ship navigation in the channel.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Traditional navigation across First Nations uses star angles, landmark elevation and horizon observations — applied trigonometry embedded in cultural practice. The Wurdi Youang stone arrangement (Wadawurrung Country) has been studied for its alignment to solstice sunset positions, requiring angle-of-elevation calculations. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Trigonometry underpins satellite-dish alignment (elevation angles for different latitudes), a practical skill for families across Melbourne's multicultural communities connecting to international broadcasts.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A 5 m ladder leans against a wall making a 70° angle with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach? How far from the wall is the base?
2. **Civic.** A surveyor at the City of Knox stands 80 m from the base of a communication tower. Her angle of elevation to the top is 62°. Calculate the tower's height.
3. **Vocational.** A roof truss forms an isosceles triangle. The base (span) is 12 m and the roof rises 3.5 m at the centre. Find the angle the roof makes with the horizontal and the length of each sloping rafter.
4. **Ethical.** A drone operator measures the angle of depression from a drone at 120 m altitude to a building entrance as 35°. Calculate the horizontal distance. Discuss privacy concerns about using trigonometry for surveillance.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A ship leaves the Port of Melbourne heading on a bearing of 065° for 15 km. How far east and how far north of the starting point is it? Use trigonometry to find both components.

## Memorable takeaways
- Pythagoras: a² + b² = c² — for sides when you know two sides.
- SOH CAH TOA — for connecting angles to sides.
- Choose the ratio based on which sides you have and need.
- Elevation: up from horizontal. Depression: down from horizontal.
- Bearings: clockwise from north. Split into right-triangle components.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I used the wrong sides for the ratio." Always label opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse relative to the angle you are working with, not relative to the right angle.
- "I mixed up elevation and depression." Elevation looks UP from horizontal. Depression looks DOWN. Both are measured from the horizontal, not from vertical.
- "My calculator was in radians." Ensure degree mode when working with degrees. A common source of wrong answers.
- "I applied Pythagoras when I only have one side and an angle." You need trigonometry (sin, cos, tan) when you have an angle and one side. Pythagoras requires two sides.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Ladder.* Height = 5 sin 70° = 5 × 0.9397 ≈ 4.7 m. Base distance = 5 cos 70° = 5 × 0.3420 ≈ 1.7 m.
2. *Communication tower.* h = 80 tan 62° = 80 × 1.8807 ≈ 150.5 m.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Let d = distance Q to cliff base, h = height. From Q: tan 42° = h/d → h = d tan 42°. From P: tan 28° = h/(d + 100) → h = (d + 100)tan 28°. Equate: d tan 42° = (d + 100)tan 28°. d(0.9004) = (d + 100)(0.5317). 0.9004d = 0.5317d + 53.17. 0.3687d = 53.17. d ≈ 144.2 m. h = 144.2 × tan 42° ≈ 129.8 m. Total distance P to base = 144.2 + 100 = 244.2 m. Cliff face from P = √(244.2² + 129.8²) ≈ √(59 634 + 16 848) ≈ √76 482 ≈ 276.6 m.
