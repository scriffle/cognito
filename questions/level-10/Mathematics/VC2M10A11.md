## Core content

**1. Quadratic relations — parabolas.** The graph of y = x² is a **parabola** — a U-shaped curve symmetric about the y-axis, with vertex at the origin. y = (x − h)² + k shifts the vertex to (h, k). y = ax² stretches (|a| > 1) or compresses (|a| < 1) the curve; negative a flips it upside down. *Quick check:* Where is the vertex of y = (x − 3)² + 2? Does it open up or down?

**2. Reciprocal relations — hyperbolas.** The graph of y = 1/x is a **hyperbola** — two separate branches in opposite quadrants, never touching the x-axis or y-axis (these are **asymptotes**). y = k/x stretches vertically; y = 1/(x − h) + k shifts the asymptotes to x = h and y = k. As x → ∞, y → 0; as x → 0, y → ∞. *Quick check:* What are the asymptotes of y = 1/x?

**3. Circle relations.** The equation x² + y² = r² describes a **circle** centred at the origin with radius r. (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² has centre (h, k). A circle is not a function (it fails the vertical line test) — it is a **relation**. *Quick check:* What is the radius and centre of x² + y² = 25?

**4. Exponential relations.** The graph of y = aˣ (a > 1) is an **exponential growth** curve — it starts slowly, then rises steeply. y = (1/a)ˣ or y = a⁻ˣ gives **exponential decay**. Key features: y-intercept at (0, 1), horizontal asymptote at y = 0 (the curve approaches but never reaches 0), always positive. y = 2ˣ doubles for every unit increase in x. *Quick check:* What is the y-intercept of y = 3ˣ? Does the graph ever cross the x-axis?

**5. Connecting algebra to graph features.** Each relation type has a "signature":
- Parabola: one turning point, symmetric, degree 2.
- Hyperbola: two branches, two asymptotes, no turning point.
- Circle: closed curve, all points equidistant from centre.
- Exponential: one-directional growth or decay, horizontal asymptote.

Changing constants in the equation translates, stretches or reflects the graph. *Quick check:* How does the graph of y = 2ˣ + 3 differ from y = 2ˣ?

**6. Worked example — matching equations to graphs using Desmos.**
A student enters four equations into Desmos: y = x² − 4, y = 2/x, x² + y² = 9, y = 2ˣ.

*Follow the thinking:*
- y = x² − 4: parabola, vertex at (0, −4), opens up, x-intercepts at x = ±2.
- y = 2/x: hyperbola, branches in quadrants 1 and 3, asymptotes at x = 0 and y = 0.
- x² + y² = 9: circle, centre (0, 0), radius 3.
- y = 2ˣ: exponential growth, passes through (0, 1), asymptote y = 0, rises steeply to the right.
- Each graph has a distinctive shape that matches its algebraic form.

**7. Using digital tools.** Graphing software (Desmos, GeoGebra, CAS calculators) lets you: change parameters with sliders to see transformations in real time; identify intercepts, asymptotes and turning points; overlay multiple graphs to compare. Use digital tools to explore "what if" questions — what happens when you change a, h, k? *Quick check:* You use a slider for a in y = ax². What happens to the parabola as a increases from 1 to 5?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Parabolic arches appear in Melbourne's Southern Cross Station roof and the Bolte Bridge cables. The exponential function models population growth (Melbourne's historical growth curve), compound interest (Victorian banking), and radioactive decay (Australian Synchrotron research). Circular geometry is everywhere in Victorian design — the State Library dome, MCG, roundabouts. Hyperbolic relationships model inverse proportions in economics and physics.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Boomerang flight paths approximate parabolic curves; the circular stone arrangements at Wurdi Youang near Little River (Wadawurrung Country) demonstrate precise circular geometry. Exponential models are used in carbon-dating that establishes the deep antiquity of First Nations presence (65,000+ years). Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Students encounter these curves in diverse contexts: satellite dishes (parabolas), population models (exponentials), wheels and arches (circles), and inverse relationships in cooking or economics (hyperbolas). Every culture uses circular forms in art and architecture.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A ball is thrown upward. Its height follows h = −5t² + 20t + 1.5. Identify the type of curve, the direction it opens, and what the vertex represents physically.
2. **Civic.** A council designs a circular roundabout with equation x² + y² = 16 (units in metres). What is the radius? A house is at (5, 0). How far from the roundabout edge is the house?
3. **Vocational.** An electrician knows that resistance and current are inversely proportional (I = V/R). If V = 12, sketch the graph of I vs R and identify the asymptotes. What type of curve is this?
4. **Ethical.** A social media post claims "exponential growth means things double every day." Is this always true? Under what conditions does y = 2ˣ double daily? What about y = 3ˣ?
5. **Cross-cultural.** Use Desmos to graph y = x², y = (x−2)², y = (x+3)², and y = x² + 4 on the same screen. Describe how changing h and k in y = (x−h)² + k transforms the graph. Take a screenshot and annotate it.

## Memorable takeaways
- Parabola (y = ax²): U-shape, vertex, opens up (a > 0) or down (a < 0).
- Hyperbola (y = k/x): two branches, asymptotes at axes.
- Circle (x² + y² = r²): closed curve, radius r, centre at origin.
- Exponential (y = aˣ): growth (a > 1) or decay (0 < a < 1), asymptote at y = 0.
- Changing constants translates, stretches or reflects the graph.

## Stretch yourself
A biologist models a bacterial population with P = 100 × 2^(t/3), where t is time in hours. Sketch the graph (by hand or using digital tools). Identify the y-intercept, horizontal asymptote, and what the equation tells you about the doubling time. Calculate the population after 6 hours and after 12 hours. Then the bacteria are treated with an antibiotic and the population follows P = 6400 × (1/2)^(t/4) (t measured from the moment of treatment). Sketch this decay curve on the same axes. Find when the population drops below 100 (solve algebraically or graphically). Discuss why exponential models eventually become unrealistic and what factors might cause the real population to behave differently. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Assuming all curves are functions.** A circle (x² + y² = r²) is a relation, not a function — it fails the vertical line test.
- **Confusing transformations.** y = (x − 3)² shifts right (not left). The sign inside the bracket is the opposite of the direction.
- **Thinking exponentials are linear.** Early exponential growth looks slow and linear, but it accelerates dramatically. Do not confuse the early stages with a straight line.
- **Forgetting asymptotes.** Hyperbolas and exponentials approach but never reach their asymptotes. Do not draw the curve touching or crossing an asymptote (unless specifically transformed to do so).

A note on certainty. The algebraic-graphical connections described here are exact mathematical correspondences. Every point on the graph satisfies the equation, and every solution to the equation lies on the graph. Digital tools give visual insight; algebraic methods give exact values. Both together build deep understanding.

**Try these scenarios**
1. *Ball trajectory.* Parabola opening down (a = −5 < 0). The vertex represents the maximum height. Vertex at t = −20/(2×−5) = 2 seconds; h = −5(4) + 40 + 1.5 = 21.5 m.
2. *Roundabout.* Radius = 4 m. House at (5, 0): distance from centre = 5. Distance from edge = 5 − 4 = 1 m.
3. *Resistance-current.* I = 12/R is a hyperbola with asymptotes at R = 0 and I = 0. As R increases, I decreases inversely.
4. *Exponential doubling.* y = 2ˣ doubles for every unit increase in x (if x = days, it doubles daily). y = 3ˣ triples daily, not doubles. "Exponential" does not always mean doubling — it depends on the base.
5. *Desmos exploration.* y = (x−2)² shifts right 2. y = (x+3)² shifts left 3. y = x² + 4 shifts up 4. Changing h translates horizontally; changing k translates vertically.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** P = 100 × 2^(t/3). y-intercept: P(0) = 100. Doubling time: 3 hours (exponent reaches 1 when t = 3). P(6) = 100 × 2² = 400. P(12) = 100 × 2⁴ = 1600. Decay: P = 6400 × (1/2)^(t/4). Half-life: 4 hours. Below 100: 6400 × (1/2)^(t/4) < 100 → (1/2)^(t/4) < 1/64 = (1/2)⁶ → t/4 > 6 → t > 24 hours. Exponential models become unrealistic because populations face resource limits, and decay cannot go below zero. Real populations level off (logistic growth) or fluctuate.
