# VC2M9N01 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M9N01. Recognise that the real number system includes the rational numbers and the irrational numbers, and solve problems involving real numbers with and without using digital tools.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 9–10, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**
  - The real number system = rational numbers + irrational numbers.
  - Rational numbers can be expressed as a fraction a/b (b ≠ 0) and have terminating or repeating decimals.
  - Irrational numbers cannot be expressed as fractions and have non-terminating, non-repeating decimals.
  - Surds (like √2, √3, √5) are irrational. π is irrational.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **recognise and work within the real number system** — understanding the distinction between rational and irrational numbers, and solving problems that involve both.
- You are learning that numbers on the number line fall into either rational or irrational categories, and together they make up the real numbers.

By the end of this you should be able to:

- Solve problems involving real numbers.

## Core content

**1. The real number system.** Every point on the number line corresponds to a real number. The real numbers split into two groups: **rational** (can be written as a/b where a, b are integers, b ≠ 0) and **irrational** (cannot be written as any fraction). Together they make up ℝ, the complete number line with no gaps. *Quick check:* Is 0.75 rational or irrational? Justify.

**2. Rational numbers.** A number is rational if it can be expressed as a fraction of integers. Equivalent: its decimal either **terminates** (e.g. 0.375 = 3/8) or **repeats** (e.g. 0.333... = 1/3). All integers are rational (e.g. 5 = 5/1). All fractions are rational. All percentages are rational. *Quick check:* Write 0.272727... as a fraction.

**3. Irrational numbers.** A number is irrational if its decimal goes on forever without repeating. Key examples: √2 ≈ 1.41421356..., √3 ≈ 1.73205..., √5 ≈ 2.23607..., π ≈ 3.14159... These cannot be expressed as any fraction. **Not all square roots are irrational** — √4 = 2, √9 = 3, √16 = 4 are all rational (they simplify to integers). *Quick check:* Classify each as rational or irrational: √7, √25, π, 22/7.

**4. Surds.** A **surd** is an irrational root that cannot be simplified to a rational number. √2, √3, √5, ∛7 are surds. √4 is NOT a surd (it equals 2). **Simplifying surds:** look for perfect square factors. √12 = √(4 × 3) = 2√3. √50 = √(25 × 2) = 5√2. √72 = √(36 × 2) = 6√2. *Quick check:* Simplify √48.

**5. Operations with surds.** 
- **Adding/subtracting:** only like surds can combine. 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2. But 3√2 + 5√3 cannot be simplified.
- **Multiplying:** √a × √b = √(ab). 2√3 × 4√5 = 8√15.
- **Dividing:** √a ÷ √b = √(a/b). √12 ÷ √3 = √4 = 2.

*Quick check:* Simplify 2√3 + √12.

**6. Worked example: diagonal of a square.**

A square has side length 5 cm. Find the exact length of its diagonal.

*Follow the thinking:*
- By Pythagoras: d² = 5² + 5² = 25 + 25 = 50.
- d = √50.
- Simplify: √50 = √(25 × 2) = 5√2.
- Exact answer: 5√2 cm. Approximate: 5 × 1.414 ≈ 7.07 cm.
- Note: 5√2 is irrational — the diagonal cannot be expressed as a fraction of centimetres. This is an exact answer; 7.07 is an approximation.

**7. The number line and estimation.** Irrational numbers have specific positions on the number line even though we can't write them exactly as decimals. √2 ≈ 1.414 (between 1 and 2, closer to 1.4). √10 ≈ 3.162 (between 3 and 4). π ≈ 3.14159 (between 3 and 4, just past 3.1). To estimate: find the nearest perfect squares either side. √7: between √4 = 2 and √9 = 3, closer to 3 (since 7 is closer to 9 than to 4? No — 7 is closer to 4+3=7... actually √7 ≈ 2.646). *Quick check:* Between which two consecutive integers does √20 lie?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Irrational numbers appear constantly in Victorian construction and design. The diagonal of a 1 m × 1 m square tile is √2 ≈ 1.414 m — builders encounter this every time they cut diagonals. The MCG's circular dimensions involve π. GPS coordinates across Melbourne use irrational numbers. The length of fencing needed for circular parks involves πd.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** The precise geometry of First Nations circular and curved structures — from fish traps to stone arrangements — implicitly involves irrational proportions (π in circular forms, surds in diagonal measurements). The Wurdi Youang stone arrangement's geometry connects to these mathematical constants. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Engineering and architecture across cultures work with irrational numbers — the golden ratio (φ = (1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.618) appears in design traditions from ancient Greece to Islamic geometry. Circular motifs in art from many cultures involve π.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** Your square bedroom has area 12 m². What is the exact side length? Simplify this surd. What is the approximate length to the nearest centimetre? How much skirting board do you need (exact and approximate)?
2. **Civic.** A council designs a circular roundabout with radius 8 m. The circumference (painted edge) is 2πr. Express this exactly and approximately. If paint costs $12.50 per metre, find the exact cost expression and approximate cost.
3. **Vocational.** A carpenter needs the diagonal of a rectangular door frame 0.9 m × 2.1 m (to check it's square). Calculate the exact diagonal as a surd and simplify if possible. What approximate measurement should the carpenter get?
4. **Ethical.** A calculator displays √2 = 1.414213562. A student writes this as the "exact" answer. Explain why the displayed value is still an approximation, and discuss when approximate answers are acceptable versus when exact (surd) form is required.
5. **Cross-cultural.** The golden ratio φ = (1 + √5)/2 appears in art and architecture across many cultures. Calculate φ to 4 decimal places. Show that φ² = φ + 1 (substitute and simplify). Is φ rational or irrational? Justify.

## Memorable takeaways
- Real numbers = rational + irrational. Every point on the number line is one or the other.
- Rational: terminates or repeats as a decimal. Can be written as a/b.
- Irrational: non-terminating, non-repeating. Cannot be any fraction. √2, π.
- Simplify surds by finding perfect square factors: √12 = 2√3.
- Like surds add: 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2. Unlike surds don't combine.
- Exact answers (surds) are sometimes better than decimal approximations.

## Stretch yourself
(a) Prove that √2 is irrational using proof by contradiction. (Assume √2 = a/b in lowest terms. Square both sides. Show a must be even. Then show b must also be even — contradicting "lowest terms.") (b) The diagonal of a unit cube (1 × 1 × 1) goes from one corner to the opposite corner through the interior. Find its exact length. (Hint: first find the face diagonal, then use Pythagoras again.) (c) Show that the sum of a rational and an irrational number is always irrational. (d) Is √2 + √3 rational or irrational? Justify by squaring. (e) Between any two rational numbers, there is always an irrational number. Find an irrational number between 3.1 and 3.2. Show all working. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I said √4 is irrational." √4 = 2, which is an integer and therefore rational. Only square roots of non-perfect-squares are irrational.
- "I said 22/7 is irrational because it approximates π." 22/7 is a fraction — it is rational. It is close to π but not equal to π. π itself is irrational.
- "I added unlike surds." 3√2 + 5√3 ≠ 8√5. Unlike surds cannot be combined, just as unlike terms in algebra cannot.
- "I said 0.333... is irrational because it never terminates." Repeating decimals ARE rational. 0.333... = 1/3. Only non-repeating, non-terminating decimals are irrational.

A note on certainty. The classification of numbers as rational or irrational is established mathematics. The irrationality of √2 has been proven since ancient Greece (attributed to the Pythagoreans, c. 500 BCE). The irrationality of π was proven by Lambert in 1761. These are not approximations or opinions — they are mathematical facts.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *0.75 rational or irrational?* Rational — it terminates. Also: 0.75 = 3/4.
2. *0.272727... as a fraction.* Let x = 0.2727... 100x = 27.2727... 100x − x = 27. 99x = 27. x = 27/99 = 3/11.
3. *Classify.* √7: irrational (7 is not a perfect square). √25 = 5: rational. π: irrational. 22/7: rational (it's a fraction).
4. *Simplify √48.* √48 = √(16 × 3) = 4√3.
5. *Simplify 2√3 + √12.* √12 = 2√3. So 2√3 + 2√3 = 4√3.
6. *√20 lies between?* √16 = 4 and √25 = 5. So √20 is between 4 and 5 (≈ 4.47).

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Bedroom.* Side = √12 = 2√3 m ≈ 3.46 m (346 cm). Skirting = 4 × 2√3 = 8√3 ≈ 13.86 m.
2. *Roundabout.* Circumference = 2π(8) = 16π ≈ 50.27 m. Cost = 16π × 12.50 = 200π ≈ $628.32.
3. *Door diagonal.* d = √(0.81 + 4.41) = √5.22 ≈ 2.285 m. This doesn't simplify to a neat surd. Carpenter should measure approximately 2 285 mm.
4. *Calculator display.* The display shows only 10 digits of √2, but √2's decimal continues infinitely without repeating. The exact value has infinitely many digits. Approximations are acceptable for construction and practical measurement. Exact surd form is required in proofs, further calculations (to avoid rounding error compounding), and when the question asks for "exact" answers.
5. *Golden ratio.* φ = (1 + √5)/2 = (1 + 2.2360679...)/2 = 3.2360679.../2 = 1.6180. Check φ² = φ + 1: φ² = ((1+√5)/2)² = (1 + 2√5 + 5)/4 = (6 + 2√5)/4 = (3 + √5)/2. φ + 1 = (1+√5)/2 + 1 = (3+√5)/2. Equal ✓. φ is irrational because √5 is irrational, and a rational number plus/times an irrational is irrational.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** (a) Assume √2 = a/b (lowest terms). Then 2 = a²/b², so a² = 2b². This means a² is even, so a is even (a = 2k). Then (2k)² = 2b² → 4k² = 2b² → b² = 2k² → b is even. Both even contradicts lowest terms. Therefore √2 is irrational. (b) Face diagonal = √(1² + 1²) = √2. Space diagonal = √((√2)² + 1²) = √(2 + 1) = √3. (c) Assume rational r + irrational i = rational q. Then i = q − r = rational − rational = rational. Contradiction. So the sum must be irrational. (d) Suppose √2 + √3 = r (rational). Then (√2 + √3)² = r² → 2 + 2√6 + 3 = r² → 5 + 2√6 = r² → √6 = (r² − 5)/2 = rational. But √6 is irrational (6 is not a perfect square). Contradiction. So √2 + √3 is irrational. (e) Between 3.1 and 3.2: try π ≈ 3.14159... (irrational, lies in range). Or: √10 ≈ 3.162... (irrational, lies in range).
