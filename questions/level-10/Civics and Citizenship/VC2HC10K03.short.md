# VC2HC10K03 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The key features and values of Australia's system of government compared with at least one other democratic or non-democratic system of government in the Asia-Pacific region.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Compare, do not rank. Features sit on top of values. Asia-Pacific holds many models.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **key features and values** of Australia's system **compared with** another **Asia-Pacific** country.
- You should be able to compare honestly, not rank.

## Core content

Australia is a federal parliamentary democracy with two houses, constitutional monarchy, compulsory federal voting, independent judiciary and protected freedoms. Asia-Pacific neighbours offer many models: NZ (parliamentary, one chamber, MMP voting); Japan (constitutional monarchy, parliamentary); Indonesia (presidential democracy); Singapore (parliamentary with dominant-party rule); China (one-party state under the CCP).

Cooperation across systems happens through ASEAN dialogues, the Pacific Islands Forum, and bilateral agreements. Every system has trade-offs: Australia's compulsory voting raises turnout but compels participation; Singapore's efficiency comes with media limits.

**Worked example.** Australia and Indonesia. Both democracies with parliaments and elections. Australia separates head of state and government (Governor-General, PM); Indonesia merges them in a directly-elected President. Both are decentralised: Australia is federal; Indonesia divides power across provinces and regencies. Both protect freedoms with local variation.

**Cultural context.** First Nations governance models have their own structures: clan groups, councils, treaty negotiations. Many Victorians have lived experience of governance systems across Asia-Pacific countries. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Compare, do not rank.
- Features sit on top of values.
- Asia-Pacific holds many models.
- Every system has trade-offs.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Australian democracy is the best." Comparison reveals strengths and limits.
- "Non-democratic systems do not work." Many deliver economic results; trade-offs are with freedoms.
- "Compulsory voting equals democracy." It is one practice; many democracies use voluntary voting.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Constitutional monarchy vs monarchy.* In a constitutional monarchy, the monarch's powers are limited by a constitution.
- *Risk of ranking.* It assumes one system is universally best, ignoring context, history and trade-offs.
- *Why does cooperation across systems matter?* Shared challenges (climate, trade, security) need coordinated action.
- *Shared feature between Australia, NZ and Japan.* Parliamentary democracy with independent judiciary.
- *Short summary of Australia vs Indonesia.* Both elect representatives and protect freedoms; key differences are presidential vs parliamentary structure and federal vs decentralised arrangements.
