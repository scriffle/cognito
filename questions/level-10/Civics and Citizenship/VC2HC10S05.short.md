# VC2HC10S05 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Evaluate the power of Australian and international political actors with regard to contemporary issues, and these political actors' connections with institutions.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Hard, soft, structural. Power flows through institutions. Context shapes weight.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **evaluate the power** of **Australian and international political actors** on **contemporary issues** and their **connections with institutions**.
- You should be able to weigh power across actors and contexts.

## Core content

**Hard power** is direct authority (laws, force, sanctions). **Soft power** is influence through attraction (culture, diplomacy, expertise). **Structural power** shapes the rules and agenda within which others act. Australian actors include the PM, ministers, regulators, the RBA, major media owners and peak interest groups. International actors include foreign governments, UN agencies, regional bodies (ASEAN, Pacific Islands Forum), multinational corporations and transnational NGOs.

Power often flows through institutional connections: a minister through a department, a CEO through a corporation, a leader through a party. Accountability tools include disclosure, FOI, judicial review, parliamentary committees, a free press and elections.

**Worked example.** Climate action. Domestic actors: PM (hard), state Premiers (hard within states), the Clean Energy Council (soft), major energy companies (structural). International actors: UN climate bodies (soft + structural), the EU (regulatory rule-making), countries with large emissions (hard in negotiations).

**Cultural context.** The First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria exercises authority within its mandate; Traditional Owner corporations exercise power over land-management decisions. Multicultural advisory bodies, faith-based organisations and community leaders exercise soft power through advocacy and trust networks. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Hard, soft, structural.
- Power flows through institutions.
- Context shapes weight.
- Accountability runs alongside.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Power equals office." Not all power flows through formal positions.
- "Soft power is weak." It often shapes the agenda before formal decisions.
- "Multinational corporations are not political." Their decisions affect public life and policy.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Hardest form to see.* Structural, because it shapes the rules others act within.
- *Significant structural power.* RBA on financial system rules; major media owners on visibility of issues.
- *Why are multinationals political?* Their investment, hiring and lobbying shape policy.
- *Why does context shape power?* The same actor has different power across issues and forums.
- *Short summary of climate-action power map.* PM and ministers hold hard power; advocacy groups exercise soft; energy companies and UN bodies set the structural rules.
