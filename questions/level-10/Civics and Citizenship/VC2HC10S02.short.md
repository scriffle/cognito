# VC2HC10S02 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Evaluate information, data, perspectives and ideas from a range of sources on contemporary issues.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Evaluate, do not just collect. Provenance is the first check. Different sources answer different questions.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **evaluate** information, data, perspectives and ideas from a **range of sources**.
- You should be able to grade sources for use, not just collect them.

## Core content

**Provenance** is where a source comes from. **Methodology** is how it produced its information. A **conflict of interest** exists when the source has a stake in the result; disclosure beats secrecy. Five criteria: authority, currency, method, independence, verifiability.

Personal experience is essential when the claim is about lived experience or impact. Expert testimony is essential when the claim is about systems, mechanisms or large-scale patterns. Strong analysis uses both, attributed appropriately.

**Worked example.** Claims about youth crime. Source A: an opinion column citing a few cases (perspective and framing). Source B: an Australian Institute of Criminology report (systemic trends). Source C: a community-led survey of young people (lived experience). The right source depends on the specific claim you are evaluating.

**Cultural context.** Cite Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander authors directly (e.g., Marcia Langton, Megan Davis). AIATSIS and the Indigenous Knowledge Institute publish authoritative information. Multilingual community media (SBS Radio in over 60 languages) provides first-hand information. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Evaluate, do not just collect.
- Provenance is the first check.
- Method shapes what the source can support.
- Personal and expert sources answer different questions.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Government data is unbiased." Independent, but still framed.
- "Personal stories are anecdote." They are essential evidence on lived experience.
- "Newer is always better." Older primary sources still matter for some claims.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is "looked good" not provenance?* Visual polish is not authorship.
- *One poll methodology question.* Sample size and sampling method; question wording.
- *Why does disclosure beat secrecy?* Readers can weigh the source themselves.
- *Why is "experts know best" too simple?* Experts are reliable on mechanisms; lived experience is essential on impacts and meaning.
- *Short summary of youth-crime sources.* Use opinion for framing, Institute of Criminology for systemic data, and community-led survey for lived experience; match each to specific claims.
