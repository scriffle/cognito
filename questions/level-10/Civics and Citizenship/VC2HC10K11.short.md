# VC2HC10K11 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The influence of a range of media, including social media, in shaping identity and attitudes to diversity.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Media is a system. Representation shapes identity. Algorithms add a new layer.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **influence of a range of media**, including **social media**, on **identity** and **attitudes to diversity**.
- You should be able to read media as a system, not just content.

## Core content

**Media** includes public broadcasters (ABC, SBS), commercial outlets, independent and ethnic media, online news and social platforms. **Representation** is how groups are shown: who appears, in what roles, with what voice. Framing differs from facts. An **algorithm** decides what each user sees; it learns from behaviour and rewards engagement, sometimes creating "filter bubbles" and amplifying outrage.

Media literacy includes identifying sources, distinguishing news from opinion, checking citations, recognising paid content, and understanding how algorithms work.

**Worked example.** Social-media algorithms. User behaviour shapes algorithm; algorithm chooses content; content shapes user response; the loop reinforces patterns. Public bodies (ACMA), journalists and civil-society groups push for transparency and media-literacy education.

**Cultural context.** NITV, IndigenousX and NIRS provide First Nations voices in media. SBS Radio broadcasts in over 60 languages. Multilingual newspapers and community media reach audiences not served by mainstream outlets. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Media is a system, not a single thing.
- Representation shapes identity.
- Framing affects attitudes.
- Algorithms add a new layer.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If it has lots of likes, it must be true." Engagement is not accuracy.
- "Mainstream media is unbiased." All media has framing choices.
- "Algorithms are neutral." They embed design and business choices.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *How does business model affect editorial?* Ad-funded outlets chase engagement; subscriber-funded outlets serve loyal readers; public broadcasters serve a broad public.
- *Why does representation matter?* It validates identities and reduces stereotyping when done well.
- *Framing vs facts.* Facts are what happened; framing is how the story is shaped.
- *Why does social media amplify fast?* Algorithms reward novelty and engagement; sharing is one tap.
- *Short summary of the algorithm loop.* Behaviour → algorithmic choice → content shown → user response → reinforced pattern. Awareness can break the loop.
