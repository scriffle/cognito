# VC2HC10S01 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Develop and evaluate questions to investigate contemporary political, legal and civic issues.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Topic ≠ question. Five criteria sharpen. Refining is part of the work.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop and evaluate questions** for contemporary issues.
- You should be able to make sure your question is worth investigating before you start.

## Core content

Five criteria for a strong question: **specific** (clear about place, time, actors), **investigable** (sources exist), **open** (allows multiple perspectives), **neutral** (avoids loaded wording), **operationalisable** (you can define and measure the variable). **Bias-check**: re-read for assumed conclusions, privileged groups or omitted voices.

**Operationalisation** turns abstract terms into observable measures. "Civic engagement" could be measured by turnout, donations, volunteering hours or survey responses. Naming the measure lets others test it.

**Worked example.** Initial: Are Australians losing trust in media? Refined: How has trust in ABC News, as measured by the Reuters Institute Digital News Report, changed between 2015 and 2024 among Australians aged 18 to 24? The refined version scores well on all five criteria.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS publishes ethical research guidelines for working with First Nations communities. The First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria publishes its own materials. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Topic ≠ question.
- Five criteria sharpen.
- Operationalise the variable.
- Refining is part of the work.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Specific = narrow." Specific means clear about scope; it can still be substantive.
- "Neutral = no perspective." Neutral framing avoids loaded language.
- "Indigenous topics are too sensitive to research." They are valid with appropriate ethics and partnership.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *"Compulsory voting" as topic.* It lacks place, time, measure and a clear question.
- *Most overlooked criterion.* Operationalisability: students often pick variables they cannot measure.
- *Operational measure of trust.* Survey responses; turnout; complaint volumes.
- *Why "keep refining" beats "abandon".* You retain time invested and learn how to ask better.
- *Short summary of evaluating a media-trust question.* Draft → score against five criteria → refine with specific dataset and age group → re-score → use.
