# VC2HC10S04 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Evaluate how the principles of justice (fairness, equality and access) are achieved through legal institutions and processes.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Three principles guide justice. Achievement is uneven. Evaluation needs evidence.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **evaluate** how **fairness, equality and access** are achieved through legal institutions.
- You should be able to grade the system against named principles.

## Core content

**Fairness** is treating like cases alike and providing due process. **Equality** is applying the law without privileging some over others (formal vs substantive). **Access** is the practical ability to use the system: cost, language, distance, time, complexity.

Victoria Legal Aid means-tests eligibility. Community legal centres extend access. The Koori Court applies culturally responsive practices in the Magistrates' Court. Reforms include specialist family-violence courts, restorative-justice options, technology-enabled VCAT hearings and therapeutic jurisprudence in mental-health and drug courts.

**Worked example.** A Victorian tenancy dispute at VCAT. Fairness: clear procedures and right to be heard. Equality: rules apply to landlords and tenants in principle. Access: lower cost than higher courts; interpreters; online options. Gaps: short notice periods can disadvantage unrepresented tenants; legal-aid coverage varies.

**Cultural context.** The Victorian Aboriginal Legal Service, the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria, the Marram-Ngala Ganbu Koori Family Hearing Day in the Children's Court, and treaty negotiations all bring First Nations perspectives. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Three principles: fairness, equality, access.
- Formal and substantive both count.
- Access turns rights into reality.
- Reform targets specific gaps.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Same rules = fair." Same rules can fail substantive equality.
- "Lower cost = full access." Language and time also matter.
- "Statistics speak for themselves." They show patterns; evaluation interprets them.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Formal vs substantive equality.* Formal: same rules for all. Substantive: same access and outcomes in practice.
- *Why is means-testing essential and limiting?* It targets help to those most in need but excludes others who still cannot afford private representation.
- *Why is overrepresentation an evaluation question?* It signals systemic issues that justice institutions should address.
- *Limitation of technology-enabled hearings.* Digital-access and privacy barriers; complex cases may still need in-person.
- *Short summary of a VCAT tenancy evaluation.* Clear procedures (fairness) and equal rules (equality) need to meet practical access; remaining gaps include legal-aid coverage and notice periods.
