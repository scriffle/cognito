# VC2HC10K09 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Individual and group participation in, and contribution to, civic life and global citizenship.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Civic life is broader than elections. Local, national, global scales connect. Groups multiply voices.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about **individual and group participation** in **civic life** and **global citizenship**.
- You should be able to see your own role across scales.

## Core content

Civic life includes volunteering, sport, faith communities, school councils, environmental groups, advocacy and creative expression. **Local participation**: community gardens, school councils, sports clubs, council meetings. **National participation**: interest groups, inquiry submissions, petitions, voting, running for office, jury service. **Global citizenship** adds awareness of shared challenges (climate, pandemics, refugees) without replacing national citizenship.

Groups multiply voices: examples include the Country Women's Association, the Australian Conservation Foundation, Reconciliation Australia. Voluntary action complements, not replaces, government responsibility.

**Worked example.** A Year 10 student joins a local Landcare group, plants trees and records bird counts. They join a national climate group via school chapters and write to their federal MP. They also support a global campaign on ocean plastics. Local, national and global contributions reinforce each other.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander contribution to civic life is broad, including the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria, Reconciliation Australia and VACCHO. Migrant and faith communities run schools, hospitals and advocacy groups. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Civic life is broader than elections.
- Local, national, global scales connect.
- Groups multiply voices.
- Global citizenship adds, not replaces.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Volunteering can replace policy." Both are needed.
- "Global citizenship dilutes national identity." It complements rather than replaces.
- "Only adults make meaningful contributions." Year 10 students have shaped major issues.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Non-government civic contribution.* Volunteering at a community garden; running a sports club; mentoring.
- *Why does local matter?* It builds civic habits and trains skills used at larger scales.
- *What do groups add?* Sustained organisation, shared resources, collective bargaining and historical memory.
- *Is "global citizenship is unpatriotic" true?* No. Caring about shared challenges does not reject national community.
- *Short summary of multi-scale contribution.* A student plants trees locally, joins national advocacy, and supports a global campaign; each scale strengthens the others.
