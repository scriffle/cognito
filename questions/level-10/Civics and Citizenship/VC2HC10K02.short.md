# VC2HC10K02 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The processes through which government policy is shaped, developed and implemented, including the role of political parties, interest groups, the media and legislative processes.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Policy is broader than law. Many actors play. Implementation makes or breaks policy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **processes** through which **policy is shaped, developed and implemented**.
- You should be able to follow a policy from idea to outcome.

## Core content

**Policy** is a government's plan to address an issue, broader than law. The **policy cycle**: agenda-setting → development → decision → implementation → evaluation → revision. Political parties bring ideas onto the agenda and use party discipline in parliament. Interest groups (unions, business councils, NGOs) bring expertise and mobilise supporters. The media shapes the agenda through coverage choices. Implementation depends on regulations, staffing, IT systems and time.

**Worked example.** A federal climate-policy package. Agenda-setting (election platforms, IPCC reports, advocacy). Development (departments, advisory committees, party rooms). Decision (cabinet, both houses, Senate amendments). Implementation (regulations, funding, monitoring). Evaluation (Auditor-General, parliamentary committees, public reaction). Revision in the next cycle.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander policy in Victoria is shaped through the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria, Closing the Gap targets and Traditional Owner corporations. Multicultural advisory bodies shape settlement, education, health and language-services policy. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Policy is broader than law.
- The cycle has many stages.
- Implementation makes or breaks policy.
- Evaluation feeds the next cycle.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Once a bill passes, the policy is done." Implementation often takes years.
- "Media just reports." It also shapes which issues become urgent.
- "Lobbyists are unrestricted." There are registers, codes of conduct and disclosure rules.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Policy without new law.* Funding decisions, regulatory changes within existing law.
- *Why "cycle".* Stages loop back; revisions and evaluations feed into new policy work.
- *Why a lobbyist register?* Transparency about who is paid to influence government.
- *Implementation example.* The COVID-19 vaccine rollout depended on logistics, supply and communication, not just legislation.
- *Short summary of a climate-policy package.* It moves through agenda-setting, development, parliamentary decision and implementation; each stage involves different actors and changes the result.
