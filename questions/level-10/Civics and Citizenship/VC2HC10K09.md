# VC2HC10K09 — Civics and Citizenship

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HC10K09. Individual and group participation in, and contribution to, civic life and global citizenship.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship (Citizenship, diversity and identity).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Civic life happens at local, national and global scales.
  - Individuals and groups contribute in different ways.
  - Global citizenship complements national citizenship.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Personal and Social Capability; Ethical Capability; Intercultural Capability; Sustainability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about **individual and group participation** in, and **contribution to**, **civic life** and **global citizenship**.
- You are learning to see your own role across scales.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **civic life**, **global citizen** and **contribution** in your own words.
- Identify three forms of contribution at local, national and global scales.
- Describe one Australian example of group contribution to global citizenship.
- Justify why "citizen" can apply beyond legal status.

## Core content

**1. Civic life beyond voting.** Civic life includes everything that builds shared public life: volunteering, sport clubs, faith communities, school councils, environmental groups, online civic spaces, advocacy and creative expression. Participation is broader than electoral politics. *Quick check:* What is one civic contribution that does not involve government at all?

**2. Local participation.** Joining a community garden, working on a school council, volunteering at a sports club, donating, attending council meetings. Local action keeps neighbourhoods working and trains civic skills. *Quick check:* Why do local contributions matter even when they seem small?

**3. National participation.** Joining national interest groups, donating to causes, submitting to inquiries, signing parliamentary petitions, voting, running for office, paying tax, jury service. National contributions shape policy and laws. *Quick check:* Why is jury service part of civic contribution, not just a duty?

**4. Worked example: an Australian environmental campaign.** A Year 10 student joins a local Landcare group. They plant trees, record bird counts and attend council meetings. The same student joins a national climate group via school chapters, signs an online petition, and writes to their federal MP. They also support a global campaign on ocean plastics through donations and social-media engagement. Local, national and global contributions reinforce each other. *Follow the thinking:* find the issue → choose actions at each scale → combine sustained contributions.

**5. Group contributions.** Groups multiply individual voices. Examples include the Country Women's Association, the Australian Conservation Foundation, Reconciliation Australia, the Australian Council of Trade Unions, religious and ethnic associations and disability advocacy groups. *Quick check:* What does a group give that individuals alone cannot?

**6. Global citizenship.** A **global citizen** identifies with humanity beyond national borders. This does not replace national citizenship; it adds awareness of shared challenges (climate, pandemics, refugees) and responsibilities (consumer choices, ethical investment, international aid). *Quick check:* Why is "global citizenship is unpatriotic" a false binary?

**7. Limits and tensions.** Global citizenship can clash with national priorities (e.g., climate vs short-term economic concerns). Voluntary action is sometimes used as a substitute for government responsibility. Honest reflection asks where individual action stops and structural change starts. *Quick check:* Why does asking "where does action stop and policy start" matter?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Victoria has a strong tradition of community involvement: the Country Fire Authority (volunteer firefighters), neighbourhood houses, Surf Life Saving Victoria, Reconciliation Victoria, Sustainability Victoria community grants.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander contribution to civic life is broad: leadership in environmental protection, advocacy, the arts, sport, education, healthcare. The First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria, Reconciliation Australia and the Victorian Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisation are examples. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant and faith communities run schools, hospitals, welfare programs, sports clubs and advocacy groups across Victoria. Diaspora communities also contribute internationally, sending support to home countries.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Voting is enough." It is a foundation, not the whole.
- "Volunteering can replace policy." Both are needed.
- "Global citizenship dilutes national identity." It complements rather than replaces.
- "Only adults make meaningful contributions." Year 10 students have shaped major issues, from climate to LGBTIQ+ rights.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Non-government civic contribution.* Volunteering at a community garden; running a sports club; mentoring younger students.
2. *Why local matters.* It builds civic habits, helps neighbours and trains skills used at larger scales.
3. *Jury service.* It is a duty and a contribution: ordinary citizens deliberate on serious legal questions.
4. *What groups add.* Sustained organisation, shared resources, collective bargaining and historical memory.
5. *"Global citizenship is unpatriotic" false binary.* Caring about shared global challenges does not mean rejecting national community.
6. *Why ask where action stops and policy starts.* Voluntary action helps, but structural problems need structural solutions; mixing the two avoids blame and locates responsibility.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Three scales of contribution.* Local: volunteer at a school garden. National: join a youth-advisory group. Global: support an international NGO with skills and donations.
2. *Cardinia Year 10 civic-skills program.* Local council shadowing; community-issue project; debate club with cross-school links.
3. *Outreach project elements.* Multilingual materials; partnerships with Traditional Owner corporations; flexible scheduling for diverse work and faith commitments.
4. *Ethical buying debate.* For: shifts consumer pressure; raises standards. Against: privileges those who can afford it; risks performative action. Both sides can be addressed with broader policy.
5. *Pacific community climate advocacy.* They bring direct experience of climate impacts in their home countries; they connect Australian audiences to global stakes; they coordinate across networks that bridge Australia and the Pacific.
