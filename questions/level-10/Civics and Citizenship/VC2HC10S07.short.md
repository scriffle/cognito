# VC2HC10S07 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Participate in and evaluate the methods and strategies of civic participation with regard to contemporary issues, from local to global.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Strategy guides; tactics deliver. Write the theory of change. Multi-scale beats single-scale.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **participate in and evaluate** methods and strategies of **civic participation** across scales.
- You should be able to act and assess.

## Core content

A **strategy** is the overall plan: goal, audience, timeline. A **tactic** is the specific action: petition, rally, meeting. A **theory of change** explains how actions lead to the goal. Methods cross scales: local (council, school SRC), state (state MPs, peak groups), national (federal MPs, large NGOs), global (international NGOs, UN bodies).

Evaluate against reach, response, outcomes and sustainability. Adjust on the fly; sustain by sharing the load and rotating roles. Burnout is a common failure mode for student campaigns.

**Worked example.** A Year 10 school-food campaign in Victoria. Goal: healthier and more affordable food in secondary schools. Strategy: build school-level support, push state-level inquiry submission, coordinate with VicSRC. Tactics: peer survey, school-council meeting, parent newsletter, state MP visit, op-ed. Theory of change: aligned peer and parent voices plus formal submission may secure inclusion in the next student-wellbeing review.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander youth networks contribute through Reconciliation Australia, the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria and AIATSIS programs. Multicultural Youth Network activities support participation across languages. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Strategy guides; tactics deliver.
- Write the theory of change.
- Multi-scale beats single-scale.
- Sustain by sharing the load.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Single-tactic wins." Real campaigns combine tactics.
- "If we follow the plan, we will win." Reality changes; adjust.
- "Burnout is personal failure." It is a systemic risk to manage.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is "protest" a tactic, not a strategy?* It does not name the goal, audience or what else accompanies it.
- *Why does writing the theory of change help?* It forces you to be explicit and reveals missing assumptions.
- *Hardest criterion to measure quickly.* Outcomes; many take months or years to appear.
- *Habit against burnout.* Rotate roles; set a fixed weekly time; share the load.
- *Short summary of the school-food campaign.* Combine peer organising, parent engagement, MP contact and an inquiry submission; align tactics with a tested theory of change; evaluate and adjust.
