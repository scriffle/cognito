# VC2HC10K04 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The Australian Government's roles and responsibilities at a regional and global level, including diplomatic relations in the Asia-Pacific region.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Australia is a regional and global actor. Diplomacy is more than treaties. Domestic and international decisions are linked.

## What you are learning
- You are learning Australia's **roles and responsibilities** at **regional and global level**, including **diplomatic relations** in the **Asia-Pacific**.
- You should be able to see Australia in a wider map.

## Core content

**Diplomacy** is the work of representing a country abroad. **Bilateral** relations involve one country; **multilateral** relations involve many. DFAT leads diplomacy; Defence handles defence cooperation; the PM and Foreign Minister are the most visible figures. Tools include treaties, trade, aid, defence cooperation, sanctions and cultural exchange.

Australia engages in the region through ASEAN dialogues, the Pacific Islands Forum, the Quad and AUKUS. International treaties Australia signs (e.g., Paris Agreement) often need domestic legislation to bind individuals.

**Worked example.** Australia in the Pacific. Cooperation: climate adaptation, disaster relief, security, the Pacific Australia Labour Mobility scheme, humanitarian responses. Tensions: Australian climate policy, deep-sea mining, past detention agreements. The relationship is close and contested.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have their own regional connections, including Torres Strait communities in PNG and Pacific Indigenous networks. The UN Permanent Forum on Indigenous Issues is one international forum. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Australia is a regional and global actor.
- Bilateral and multilateral both matter.
- Aid, trade and security travel together.
- Domestic and international are linked.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Foreign policy is unconnected from daily life." Trade, climate, migration and education are all touched.
- "Aid is charity." It is also strategic and reciprocal.
- "Australia is only a small player." Its regional weight is significant, especially in the Pacific.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Climate as both national and international.* Emissions at home; treaties, shared seas, regional partnerships abroad.
- *Multilateral advantage.* Shared rules and burdens; small states get a louder voice.
- *Why are China relations complicated?* Major trading partner, strategic competitor, large diaspora, complex on rights and security.
- *Why does a treaty need domestic law?* To bind individuals in Australian courts.
- *Short summary of Pacific cooperation.* Climate adaptation, disaster relief, labour mobility and humanitarian response, with tensions around Australia's climate policy and past detention arrangements.
