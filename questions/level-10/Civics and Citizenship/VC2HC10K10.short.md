# VC2HC10K10 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The challenges to and ways of sustaining a resilient democracy and a cohesive society in Australia and/or in our region or globally.
- **Year / Area:** Year 10, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Resilience absorbs shocks. Cohesion is a shared "we". Reform is constant.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about **challenges** to and ways of **sustaining** a **resilient democracy** and **cohesive society**.
- You should be able to name threats clearly and plan responses honestly.

## Core content

A resilient democracy absorbs shocks without losing institutions, rights and trust. Common challenges: polarisation, misinformation, declining institutional trust, foreign interference, inequality, identity exclusion, climate stress, rapid tech change. **Democratic backsliding** is when democratic norms (elections, press freedom, courts, civil rights) weaken within an electoral framework.

**Social cohesion** is the sense that "we" includes everyone. The Scanlon Foundation tracks Australian cohesion year by year. Multilateral bodies (UN, OECD), regional groupings (ASEAN, Pacific Islands Forum), international election observation and human-rights monitoring build resilience across borders.

**Worked example.** A response to misinformation. Layer responses: media-literacy education in schools; transparent platform moderation; legal tools; strengthening public broadcasters; civic-science initiatives. None alone is enough.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have demonstrated democratic resilience over generations through advocacy and institutions like the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria. Multicultural advisory bodies and faith-based organisations strengthen cohesion daily. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Resilience absorbs shocks.
- Cohesion is shared "we".
- Many tools, no single fix.
- Reform is constant.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Resilience means no change." It means change without breaking core institutions.
- "Cohesion means unanimity." It means coexisting respectfully across disagreement.
- "All countries are backsliding." Not all; avoid broad-brush claims.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is "no change" not resilience?* Stagnant institutions lose legitimacy; adaptive reform is needed.
- *Why is "still has elections" not enough?* Elections can occur in systems with restricted media or judicial interference.
- *Why does regional cooperation help democratic resilience?* Shared standards and peer review strengthen each member.
- *Why is reform continual?* Societies change; institutions must adapt.
- *Short summary of a layered misinformation response.* Education, platform engagement, regulation and trusted public information together; no single fix.
