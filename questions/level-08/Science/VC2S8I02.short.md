# VC2S8I02 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Plan reproducible investigations by identifying variables, managing risks, considering ethical issues and following cultural protocols on Country.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Reproducibility makes a result a finding. Safety and ethics are part of the method. On Country, protocol comes before sampling.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **plan** reproducible investigations by identifying variables, managing risks and following protocols.
- You should be able to write a method another team could repeat.

## Core content

**Reproducibility** means another team, given your method, could get a similar result. Identify the independent, dependent and at least three controlled variables before you collect data. A simple **risk assessment** lists hazards, who is at risk and the control. Ethics asks who could be harmed and how you protect them. A **protocol** is a set of agreed rules for behaviour, especially when working on Country.

**Worked example.** On Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country at Merri Creek, your class plans to test pH and turbidity at three sites. Reproducible method: sample at the same time of day, with the same calibrated probes, at the same depth, in the same week. Risks: slippery banks, water quality, sun. Ethics: do not photograph other waterway users. Protocol: contact the Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation for guidance before sampling.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS publishes a Code of Ethics for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Research. Traditional Owner corporations across Victoria publish protocols for working on Country. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material, and on-Country fieldwork should be cleared with the relevant corporation.

## Memorable takeaways
- A method another team can repeat is a finding.
- Name variables before you start.
- Hazard, who, control. Three columns, every time.
- Protocol comes before sampling.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Risk assessments are paperwork." No. They prevent injuries and shape better methods.
- "Acknowledging Country is enough." No. Acknowledgement is one step. Permission and consultation follow.
- "If it worked once, it is reproducible." No. Reproducibility needs independent repeats.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *What is reproducibility?* Another team, following your method, could get a similar result.
- *Three columns of a risk assessment.* Hazard, who is at risk, and the control measure.
- *One ethical issue in a sleep survey.* Privacy. Use anonymous responses and let students opt out.
- *Why acknowledging Country is only the start.* It signals respect, but consent, consultation and ranger guidance are what actually protect Country and relationships.
- *Short summary of the Merri Creek plan.* Sample three sites at the same time of day with the same calibrated probes for one week, follow the risk assessment and ethics steps, and seek protocol guidance from the Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation before starting.
