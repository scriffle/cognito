## Core content

**1. The particle theory.** **Particle theory** says all matter is made of tiny particles, with spaces between them, and that particles attract one another. The model is simple, but it explains a lot. *Quick check:* Why do you smell a passing candle without touching it?

**2. Kinetic theory adds motion.** **Kinetic theory** says particles are always moving. They move faster when warmer and slower when cooler. Temperature is a measure of average particle speed. *Quick check:* What does it mean to say "warm" at the particle level?

**3. The three states.** A **solid** has particles in fixed positions vibrating in place, with strong attractions. A **liquid** has particles touching but moving past each other. A **gas** has particles spread far apart, moving fast and freely. *Quick check:* Which state can change shape but not volume?

**4. Worked example: ice to steam.** Start with ice at -5°C. Particles vibrate in place. Heat: particles vibrate harder until attractions are overcome. Ice melts at 0°C. The liquid has flowing particles. Continue heating: particles move faster, some escape the surface (evaporation). At 100°C, the rest break free and become steam. *Follow the thinking:* state → particle arrangement → movement → energy added → next state.

**5. Pressure as collisions.** Gas pressure comes from particles colliding with the walls of a container. More particles, smaller container, or higher temperature all mean more or harder collisions, so higher pressure. *Quick check:* Why does a closed bottle of gas warmed in the sun bulge?

**6. Diffusion explained.** **Diffusion** is the spread of particles from where they are concentrated to where they are not. It is faster in gases, slower in liquids, very slow in solids. *Quick check:* Why do you smell a perfume across a room within seconds?

**7. Limits of the model.** The particle picture explains many observations but simplifies. Real particles include atoms, molecules and ions of different masses and charges. Models are useful, not exact. *Quick check:* Name one thing the simple particle model does not show.

## Memorable takeaways
- All matter is particles in motion.
- Hot is fast. Cold is slow.
- Solids hold. Liquids flow. Gases fill.
- Pressure is collisions per second.
- Models simplify on purpose.

Pick three everyday processes (defogging a window, inflating a tyre, drying clothes). Explain each at the particle level with a labelled diagram, and predict how a temperature change of 10°C would alter the rate. 200 words or three small panels.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Particles are tiny but cool, immobile balls." No. They are constantly moving, vibrating or flying.
- "Cold has no energy." No. Particles still move at low temperatures, just slower.
- "Gas has no mass because it is invisible." No. Gas particles have mass, and a sealed container of gas measurably weighs more than vacuum.
- "Diffusion stops when particles meet." No. It continues until particles are evenly spread; even then they keep moving.

## Try drawing this
A "states triptych": solid, liquid, gas in three panels with the same number of dots. Show vibration arrows in the solid, longer flow arrows in the liquid, and long fast arrows scattered in the gas.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why you smell a passing candle.* Volatile particles leave the candle, mix with air and reach your nose, where they activate smell receptors.
2. *"Warm" at particle level.* Particles are moving faster on average than in cooler matter.
3. *State that changes shape but not volume.* Liquid.
4. *Why a sealed bottle bulges in the sun.* Heating speeds up the gas particles. They collide with the walls more often and harder, raising pressure.
5. *Why a perfume reaches across a room quickly.* Gas particles diffuse through air, carrying scent particles widely as they collide with air molecules.
6. *One thing the simple particle model misses.* Differences between atoms and molecules, charges, and forces beyond simple attraction.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Glass of water in the sun.* Surface particles gain energy and escape as vapour (evaporation). The water level slowly drops. The remaining water cools slightly because the most energetic particles leave.
2. *Heat pumps.* They move thermal energy from a cooler space to a warmer one by changing the state and pressure of a working fluid. Compressing gas raises particle collisions and temperature; releasing pressure lowers them.
3. *Yarra Valley fermentation.* Yeast metabolism speeds up with temperature, but too high and proteins denature. Particle motion drives reactions, so temperature controls how fast and how cleanly fermentation proceeds.
4. *Helium balloon release.* Issues: marine and wildlife harm from balloon waste, helium scarcity. Alternative: use a sealed glass apparatus to compare gas behaviour at different temperatures, or run a video demonstration.
5. *Wurundjeri ochre demonstration.* Heating drives off water and changes mineral structure, altering colour. Particle-level explanation: kinetic energy frees water particles and rearranges atoms in the ochre. Discuss with the Elder's permission, credit the demonstrator and use only published, community-approved material.
