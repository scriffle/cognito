# VC2S8I01 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Develop investigable questions, predictions and hypotheses that guide investigations and help identify patterns and test relationships.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** A good question makes the answer testable. A prediction points a direction. A hypothesis proposes a relationship and a reason.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop** investigable questions, predictions and hypotheses that **guide** investigations.
- You should be able to take a vague wondering and turn it into a testable plan with named variables.

## Core content

An **investigable question** is one your equipment, time and ethics can answer. A **variable** is anything that can change. The **independent variable** is what you change. The **dependent variable** is what you measure. **Controlled variables** are kept constant. A **prediction** states what you expect to see, ideally with a direction. A **hypothesis** is a testable statement linking variables and proposing a reason: "If… then… because…".

Pattern-seeking questions ask "what is happening" across a system. Relationship-testing questions ask "does X cause Y". Both are valid science.

**Worked example.** Wondering: "Is the school car park hotter than the lawn?" Investigable question: "How does ground surface affect midday temperature?" Variables: surface (independent), temperature (dependent), time and weather (controlled). Hypothesis: "If the surface is asphalt, then midday temperature will be highest, because dark surfaces absorb more solar radiation."

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Wadawurrung and Bunurong/Boon Wurrung knowledge of seasons rests on long observation that asks investigable questions about Country, such as when silver wattle flowers and what that signals. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- A wondering is curiosity. A question is testable curiosity.
- Independent changes. Dependent measures. Controlled stays still.
- "If… then… because…" beats "I think it will work".
- A peer reproduction test exposes weak wording.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Big questions are better." No. Useful questions are narrow enough to test in your time and conditions.
- "A prediction is the same as a hypothesis." No. A prediction states what you expect. A hypothesis adds a relationship and a reason.
- "Aboriginal knowledge is just stories, not inquiry." No. It rests on careful, long-term observation tested across generations.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *What makes a question investigable?* Your equipment, time and ethics can answer it within the conditions you have.
- *Three parts of a hypothesis.* The independent variable, the predicted change in the dependent variable, and a brief mechanism explaining why.
- *Prediction vs hypothesis.* A prediction states what you expect to see. A hypothesis links two variables and gives a reason.
- *Investigable version of "Why are some leaves bigger?"* "How does light availability affect average leaf area in saplings in the schoolyard?"
- *Short summary of the heat-island worked example.* Surface type is changed (asphalt, mulch, grass), temperature is measured, weather and time are held constant, and the hypothesis predicts asphalt is hottest because dark surfaces absorb more solar radiation.
