# VC2S8H01 — Science as a Human Endeavour (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Scientific knowledge, including models and theories, can change when new evidence comes to light.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Science is provisional. Evidence updates models. Refinement is common, replacement is rare.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that scientific knowledge **can change** when new evidence comes to light.
- You should be able to give one example where evidence changed an accepted idea, and explain why that is science working well.

## Core content

A **theory** is a well-tested explanation that ties many observations together. It is not a guess. A **model** is a simplified version of something complex, used to predict and explain. **Evidence** is repeatable data from observation or experiment.

Most updates are refinements. The germ theory of disease was extended, not thrown out, when viruses were identified. Sometimes a theory is replaced. The idea that continents sat still gave way to **plate tectonics** in the 1960s after seafloor mapping.

**Worked example.** Until the 1980s, doctors blamed stomach ulcers on stress. Australian researchers Barry Marshall and Robin Warren found *Helicobacter pylori* in ulcer tissue. Marshall tested the link by drinking a culture and developed gastritis. Repeated trials confirmed the cause. Treatment shifted to antibiotics. Old idea, anomaly, bold test, replication, new theory.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal astronomical knowledge held by peoples across Victoria, including Boorong knowledge from Wergaia Country, has been observed and refined across many generations. Long-running, evidence-based knowledge systems exist outside Western science and also update over time. Specific star knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved sources.

## Memorable takeaways
- Theories are best current explanations, not final truths.
- Evidence updates models. That is science working.
- One study is a clue. Many studies are a case.
- Uncertainty stated is honesty, not weakness.

## Watch out for these traps
- "A theory is just a guess." No. A scientific theory is well-tested and evidence-supported.
- "If scientists changed their mind, they were wrong before." No. They had the best explanation for the evidence then available.
- "New always replaces old." No. Most updates refine the existing model.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Is "theory" closer to a hunch or a best current explanation?* Best current explanation. It is well-tested and supported by many lines of evidence.
- *Why is one study not enough?* One result can be a fluke or a one-off. Repeated independent studies make coincidence unlikely.
- *Refinement or replacement when continents were shown to move?* Replacement. The fixed-continents idea was set aside for plate tectonics.
- *Why is stating uncertainty a strength?* It shows the model's limits honestly, so readers can judge how far to trust it.
- *Short answer on the ulcer story.* Old idea: stress caused ulcers. Anomaly: bacteria found in ulcer tissue. Test: Marshall infected himself and developed gastritis. Replication: confirmed by other studies. New theory: most ulcers are caused by *H. pylori* infection. Treatment shifted to antibiotics.
