# VC2S8U03 — Science Understanding

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2S8U03. The structure of cells, tissues and organs is directly related to their function, and organ systems keep organisms alive.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Science (Biological Sciences: Classification & Cells).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Life is organised in levels: cell, tissue, organ, system.
  - Each level shows structure-fits-function.
  - Systems work together to keep organisms alive.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Critical and Creative Thinking; Personal and Social Capability; Literacy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that the **structure** of cells, tissues and organs is directly related to their **function**, and that **organ systems** keep organisms alive.
- You are learning to trace one job, like getting oxygen to a muscle, across the levels of organisation.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **tissue**, **organ** and **organ system** in your own words.
- Place an example at each level of organisation.
- Describe how the structure of one organ matches its function.
- Explain how two organ systems cooperate to do one job.

## Core content

**1. Levels of organisation.** Cells form **tissues**. Tissues form **organs**. Organs form **organ systems**. Organ systems form an organism. Each level adds emergent capability. *Quick check:* Place "small intestine" at the right level.

**2. What is a tissue?** A **tissue** is a group of similar cells working together. Muscle tissue contracts. Epithelial tissue lines surfaces. Connective tissue supports and binds. Nervous tissue carries signals. *Quick check:* Which tissue covers the inside of the small intestine?

**3. What is an organ?** An **organ** combines tissues to do a larger job. The heart combines muscle, connective and nerve tissue to pump blood. The leaf combines tissues to photosynthesise and exchange gas. *Quick check:* What two tissues are essential to a beating heart?

**4. What is an organ system?** An **organ system** is a set of organs working together. The respiratory system, the circulatory system, the digestive system, the nervous system and the urinary system are core examples in humans. *Quick check:* Which organ system removes wastes from the blood?

**5. Worked example: a sprint to the bus.** You see the bus and start running. The eye and brain sense and decide. Skeletal muscles contract using stored energy. Mitochondria in muscle cells need oxygen. Lungs pull air in, the diaphragm and intercostal muscles work, oxygen crosses alveoli into blood. The heart pumps blood faster. Capillaries deliver oxygen to muscle cells. Carbon dioxide leaves by the same path in reverse. *Follow the thinking:* one task → multiple systems → coordinated structures → cellular finish.

**6. Structure fits function at every level.** Alveoli are thin and many, so gases diffuse fast across a large area. The small intestine has tiny villi, which look like fingers, to increase surface area for absorption. A leaf is thin and flat to capture light. *Quick check:* What feature does the alveolus share with the villus?

**7. Plants are organised too.** Plants have tissues such as xylem and phloem, organs such as leaves, stems and roots, and systems such as the shoot system and the root system. Roots take up water; xylem carries it to leaves; phloem distributes sugars made by photosynthesis. *Quick check:* Which two plant tissues form the transport system?

## Stretch yourself
Pick one organ system. Describe one disease that affects it, name the level of organisation where the problem starts, and explain how it disrupts the system's job. Cite a Victorian institute that researches the area. 200 words or one annotated diagram.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Organ systems work alone." No. Most jobs need two or more systems cooperating.
- "Bigger means better." No. Many tissues, like alveoli, work because they are small and many.
- "Plants do not have organ systems." No. They have shoot and root systems with specialised tissues.
- "Cells are simple bags." No. Each cell type has specialised structures and roles.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Small intestine level.* Organ.
2. *Tissue lining the small intestine.* Epithelial tissue.
3. *Two tissues in a beating heart.* Cardiac muscle and nervous tissue. Connective tissue is also essential.
4. *System removing wastes from the blood.* Urinary system.
5. *Alveolus and villus shared feature.* Both maximise surface area for exchange or absorption.
6. *Two plant tissues that form the transport system.* Xylem and phloem.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Eating a sandwich.* Mouth and salivary glands begin digestion (organ, system: digestive). Stomach uses muscle tissue and acid (organ). Small intestine uses villi to absorb (organ, system: digestive). Cells in body tissues use glucose (cell, tissue, system: cellular and circulatory).
2. *Melton walking trail.* Regular walking strengthens the cardiovascular and respiratory systems. Cellular reason: more efficient oxygen delivery and use, more mitochondria in muscle cells over time, lower resting heart rate.
3. *CPR.* Chest compression mimics the heart's pump, moving oxygenated blood to the brain. Without it, alveoli, capillaries and tissues do not exchange gases, and brain cells start to die within minutes.
4. *Sheep heart dissection ethics.* Source from a regulated supplier, treat respectfully, dispose properly, and provide opt-out for students with personal or cultural reasons. Pair the practical with diagrams or videos.
5. *Local tree structure.* The tree has tissues (xylem, phloem), organs (leaf, stem, root) and systems (shoot, root). Wadawurrung knowledge of which trees thrive on Country adds context, with permission and credit, on what species do well in local conditions.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *System: respiratory.* Disease: asthma. Level where it starts: tissue level (bronchial smooth muscle and epithelial cells become inflamed). Disruption: airway narrowing reduces airflow, lowering oxygen delivery to capillaries, which slows the cardiovascular system's job. Victorian anchor: Murdoch Children's Research Institute on childhood asthma.
