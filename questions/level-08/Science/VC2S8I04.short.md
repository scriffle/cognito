# VC2S8I04 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Organise and process data by constructing appropriate representations such as tables, graphs, keys and models.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Table first, then graph. Choose the chart that fits the data. Models simplify to predict.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **organise** and **process** data using tables, graphs, keys and models.
- You should be able to choose the right representation for the question and the data type.

## Core content

A tidy table has rows for trials, columns for variables, and headings with units. Use a **bar chart** for **categorical** data, a **line graph** for a **continuous** independent variable measured at intervals (like time), and a **scatter plot** for two continuous variables you want to relate. Always label both axes with name and unit, and choose a scale that does not mislead.

A **dichotomous key** is a series of yes-or-no questions on observable features that lead to an identification. A **model** is a simplified version of a system used to explain or predict.

**Worked example.** Plant growth across light levels. Independent: light intensity (lux). Dependent: average height (cm) after 14 days. Step 1: tidy table with means and ranges. Step 2: scatter plot with a line of best fit. Step 3: a simple model: "Height increases with light up to about 8000 lux, then plateaus."

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal seasonal calendars are themselves models. The Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung calendar describes seven seasons defined by indicators on Country, such as the flowering of silver wattle. The Bureau of Meteorology has published Indigenous Weather Knowledge calendars in partnership with communities. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Table first, then graph.
- Bar for categories. Line for time. Scatter for relationships.
- Label everything, units and all.
- Models simplify on purpose, not by accident.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Bar charts work for everything." No. They hide trends in continuous data.
- "Models are guesses." No. Models are deliberate simplifications used to predict and explain.
- "Outliers should be deleted." No. Investigate them. They may reveal a fault or a real signal.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Bar, line or scatter?* Bar for categorical groups, line for a continuous variable measured at set intervals, scatter for two continuous variables you want to relate.
- *Why a non-zero y-axis can mislead.* It exaggerates small differences by cropping the scale.
- *Mass added vs spring extension.* Scatter plot with a line of best fit. Both are continuous and you want the relationship.
- *Why is "looks like a typical leaf" a poor key step?* Not observable in a clear yes-or-no way. Keys need objective features.
- *Short summary of the plant-growth example.* Tidy the data into a table with means, plot a scatter graph of height against light intensity, fit a trend, and describe the relationship as a simple model that includes the plateau.
