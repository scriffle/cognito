# VC2S8U09 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Earth's resources can be renewable or non-renewable, and how we extract and use them involves both benefits and risks to sustainability.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Renewable replenishes within human time. Non-renewable does not. Every option has trade-offs.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that Earth's resources can be **renewable** or **non-renewable**, with **benefits** and **risks** to **sustainability**.
- You should be able to weigh those benefits and risks for a real Australian resource.

## Core content

A **renewable** resource is replenished naturally on a timescale similar to its use, like sunlight, wind and well-managed forests. A **non-renewable** resource forms over geological timescales, like coal, oil, gas and metals. **Sustainability** is meeting present needs without preventing future generations from meeting theirs. Renewables can be overused, and non-renewables can be stretched by recycling.

**Worked example.** Brown coal in the Latrobe Valley. Benefits: cheap electricity, jobs, baseload power. Risks: high carbon emissions, air-quality impacts, mine fires (Hazelwood 2014), site rehabilitation costs. As Victoria transitions to renewables, the trade-offs change shape rather than disappear.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have managed Country sustainably for tens of thousands of years through fire, water and harvest practices. Gunaikurnai, Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Wadawurrung and other Victorian peoples have published joint-management agreements for parks, water and forests. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Renewable: replenished within human time.
- Non-renewable: built over geological time.
- Every option has trade-offs.
- Recycling extends, it does not replace.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Renewable means clean." No. Solar panels and turbines need mining and recycling.
- "We can recycle everything." No. Some products mix materials in ways that block recycling.
- "Aboriginal land management is just history." No. It is current practice shaping modern policy.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is fresh water both renewable and limited?* Rainfall replenishes it, but overuse can outpace replenishment in a region.
- *Why is coal called fossilised sunlight?* It formed from ancient plants that captured solar energy through photosynthesis.
- *Renewable but unsustainable?* Yes. A forest is renewable, but cutting trees faster than they regrow is unsustainable.
- *Why is "renewable" not "no impact"?* Producing solar panels, wind turbines and batteries needs mined materials, energy and land. Impacts shrink but do not vanish.
- *Short summary of brown coal trade-offs.* Cheap power and jobs versus high emissions, air-quality risks and mine rehabilitation costs. The transition rebalances who pays the costs and how.
