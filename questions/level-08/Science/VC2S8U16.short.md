# VC2S8U16 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Household energy use can be measured through an energy audit and is affected by the appliances we choose, building design, season and climate.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Audit before you fix. Big appliances drive the bill. Climate sets priorities.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that household energy use can be **measured** through an **energy audit**, and that **appliances**, **building design**, **season** and **climate** all matter.
- You should be able to read a bill and identify changes that save money and emissions.

## Core content

An **energy audit** is a structured check of how energy enters and is used in a building. A **kilowatt-hour (kWh)** is one kilowatt of power for one hour. Bills show daily kWh, peak/off-peak rates, supply charges and any solar export.

In Melbourne homes, heating, cooling, hot water and large appliances usually use the most energy. Lighting and small electronics are smaller shares. **Insulation** in walls, ceilings and floors slows heat moving in or out. Window orientation, shading and double glazing reduce summer heat gain and winter heat loss.

**Worked example.** Audit at a Werribee home. Read 12 months of bills. List main appliances with rated power and weekly hours. Walk through and check insulation, draughts, hot-water settings. Rank improvements by saving and cost. Common winners: replace a gas heater with a reverse-cycle heat pump, seal draughts, switch to LED lighting, install rooftop solar.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander shelter design across Australia historically used materials, orientation and seasonal practice to manage temperature with minimal external energy. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Audit before you fix.
- Big appliances drive the bill.
- Insulation and shade beat tinkering with thermostats.
- Climate sets the priorities.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Switching off lights solves the bill." No. Heating, cooling and hot water are bigger.
- "Solar panels eliminate the need to be efficient." No. Efficiency reduces what you need to generate.
- "Old houses cannot be made efficient." No. Many low-cost retrofits work for older homes.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *kWh from 2 kW heater for 3 hours.* 6 kWh.
- *Heat pump vs halogen lamp on bills.* Heat pump uses more energy per hour. Lighting matters but is a smaller share.
- *Mornington vs Mildura priorities.* Mornington: heating and draught sealing. Mildura: cooling, shading and reflective roofing.
- *Why audit instead of guessing?* Guessing misses the largest users. Measurement directs effort to the biggest savings.
- *Short summary of the Werribee audit.* Read bills, list appliances and hours, check insulation and draughts, rank changes by saving and cost. Replacing the gas heater, sealing draughts, switching to LEDs and adding rooftop solar are common winners.
