# VC2S8I07 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Construct evidence-based arguments to support conclusions or evaluate claims, including considering ethical issues when using secondary data.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Claim, evidence, reasoning. Specific claims earn stronger arguments. Open data is not a free pass.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **construct** evidence-based arguments and **consider** ethics when using secondary data.
- You should be able to write a clear "claim, evidence, reasoning" response and cite your source.

## Core content

A **claim** is what you assert. **Evidence** is the data that support it. **Reasoning** is the logic, usually a mechanism, that links them. **Secondary data** is data collected by someone else, such as Bureau of Meteorology rainfall or EPA Victoria air-quality logs.

Strong claims are specific. Evidence has to fit the claim type: long-term claims need long-term data, relationship claims need paired measurements. Reasoning explains why the evidence supports the claim. Always cite sources, dates and methods.

**Worked example.** Claim: "2024 was warmer than the 1991–2020 average in Melbourne." Evidence: Bureau of Meteorology monthly mean temperatures for Melbourne airport for 2024 and the 1991–2020 baseline. Reasoning: comparing both means, and noting the difference is larger than year-to-year noise. Ethics: cite the source, quote dates, do not crop a graph to exaggerate.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS publishes CARE principles for Indigenous Data Governance: Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics. Indigenous datasets need community consent for re-use. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Claim, evidence, reasoning. Three legs, one stool.
- Match evidence to claim type.
- Cite sources, dates and methods.
- Open data is not a free pass.

## Watch out for these traps
- "More evidence is always better." No. Relevant, well-cited evidence beats a long list.
- "If a graph is in the news, it is reliable." No. Always check source, date and method.
- "Reasoning is just opinion." No. Reasoning explains the mechanism that links data to claim.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *What is missing from "Plants grew 12 cm. Therefore light helps growth"?* Reasoning. You need to explain why this growth supports the claim, including a comparison with a control.
- *More specific version of "exercise is good for you".* "Adolescents who do at least 60 minutes of moderate physical activity most days have lower rates of depressive symptoms in studies of Australian school students."
- *Why does the date of secondary data matter?* Conditions and methods change. Old data may not represent current conditions; definitions may have shifted.
- *Two ethical actions when using public data.* Cite the source with the access date and check that no individual could be re-identified.
- *Short summary of the CER for "2024 was warmer than the baseline".* Claim, BoM evidence with dates, reasoning that compares means to year-to-year variation, and a clear citation of the source.
