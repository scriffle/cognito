# VC2S8U14 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Balanced and unbalanced forces determine whether an object stays still, speeds up, slows down or changes direction, and force diagrams help us represent this.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Forces add as vectors. Balanced means no change. Unbalanced means acceleration.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **balanced** and **unbalanced** forces determine whether an object stays still, speeds up, slows down or changes direction.
- You should be able to draw and read a clear force diagram.

## Core content

A **force** has size and direction. **Net force** is the single force with the same effect as all forces combined. If forces are **balanced**, net force is zero and the object continues at constant velocity (which can be standing still). An **unbalanced** net force causes **acceleration**: speeding up, slowing down or changing direction. Bigger net force or smaller mass means larger acceleration (Newton's second law: a = F/m).

A **free-body diagram** shows all forces on a single object as arrows from a dot. Common forces are gravity (down), normal (up from a surface), friction (against motion), applied force, and tension.

**Worked example.** A 30 kg shopping trolley is pushed with 60 N. Friction acts back at 20 N. Net force = 60 − 20 = 40 N forward. The trolley accelerates forward. If the customer reduces the push to 20 N, friction balances it and the trolley continues at the same speed.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge of throwing and trapping technologies (boomerangs, woomeras, spears, fish traps) reflects deep practical understanding of force, balance and trajectory. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Forces have size and direction.
- Balanced means no change in motion.
- Unbalanced means acceleration.
- Constant velocity is balanced.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If something is moving, there must be a net force." No. Constant velocity needs zero net force.
- "Friction always slows things down." Often, but it can also be why wheels drive forward.
- "Heavier means stronger force." Weight is a force, but acceleration depends on net force ÷ mass.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Net force on a book on a table.* Zero. Gravity down balances normal force up.
- *Skydiver at terminal velocity.* Air resistance up equals weight down. Net force zero, so acceleration is zero, even at high speed.
- *Why a small car accelerates faster than a heavy truck with the same engine.* Same net force, smaller mass, larger acceleration (a = F/m).
- *Direction of friction on a moving object.* Opposite to the direction of motion.
- *Short summary of the trolley example.* 60 N push minus 20 N friction = 40 N net forward, so the trolley accelerates. Reducing push to 20 N balances friction, so velocity stays constant.
