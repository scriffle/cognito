# VC2S8U10 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Earth's tectonic plates move, creating geological features at plate boundaries, and evidence supports the theory of plate tectonics.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Plates move because the mantle convects. Boundaries make mountains, quakes and volcanoes. Many evidence lines support one theory.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that Earth's **tectonic plates** **move**, creating **geological features** at **plate boundaries**.
- You should be able to link a feature to a process at the boundary.

## Core content

The crust and uppermost mantle form the **lithosphere**, broken into **tectonic plates**. **Convection** in the mantle, driven by heat from radioactive decay and Earth's formation, slowly carries the plates. Three boundary types: at a **divergent** boundary, plates move apart and new crust forms (mid-ocean ridges); at a **convergent** boundary, plates collide and one may dive under (subduction zones, mountain ranges); at a **transform** boundary, plates slide past each other.

Evidence includes continental fit, matching fossils across oceans, magnetic stripes on the seafloor, GPS measurements of motion today, and the global distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes.

**Worked example.** The Pacific Ring of Fire. The Pacific Plate meets many other plates around its edge, with subduction zones producing volcanoes and earthquakes from New Zealand to Alaska. Australia sits inside the Indo-Australian Plate, far from those edges, so it experiences fewer big quakes today, but is moving north at about 7 cm per year.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander oral traditions across Australia preserve memories of major events such as sea-level rise after the last Ice Age and ancient volcanic eruptions. Researchers such as Patrick Nunn have collaborated with communities to document these accounts. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Plates move because the mantle convects.
- Three boundary types: apart, together, sliding.
- Many evidence lines, one theory.
- Australia is geologically calm today but has a long active history.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Continents float on the ocean." No. Plates are crust plus uppermost mantle.
- "Plate motion is fast." No. About 2–10 cm per year.
- "All earthquakes are at plate edges." No. Some occur in plate interiors, including in Australia.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why does the lithosphere break into plates while the asthenosphere does not?* The lithosphere is rigid and brittle. The asthenosphere is hot and ductile, so it deforms without breaking.
- *What is the heat source for mantle convection?* Radioactive decay in Earth's interior plus residual heat from formation.
- *Feature at a divergent boundary.* A mid-ocean ridge with new ocean crust forming.
- *Why are many independent evidence lines stronger than one?* Each can be questioned alone. Together they are hard to explain by anything other than plate tectonics.
- *Short summary of Mount Fuji and the Japan trench.* Convergent boundary with subduction. The Pacific Plate dives under the Eurasian Plate, creating the trench and feeding magma to the volcano above.
