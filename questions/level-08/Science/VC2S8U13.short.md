# VC2S8U13 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Simple machines such as levers, pulleys, wedges and screws change the direction or size of forces to make work easier.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Machines change force size or direction. No free lunch: less force, more distance.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **simple machines** change the direction or size of forces to make **work** easier.
- You should be able to identify the simple machine in everyday tools.

## Core content

A **force** is a push or pull. **Work** = Force × distance. A **lever** is a rigid bar that pivots at a fulcrum; longer levers reduce force but increase distance. A **pulley** is a wheel and rope; multiple pulleys reduce the force needed but you pull more rope. A **wedge** is two inclined planes back to back, used in axes, knives and chisels. A **screw** is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder; finer threads need less force but more turns.

**Mechanical advantage** is how much the machine multiplies your effort. A 2:1 advantage means half the effort but double the distance. Total work is conserved.

**Worked example.** Lifting a 60 kg engine block (about 600 N). A single fixed pulley still needs 600 N but lets you pull down. A 2-pulley block-and-tackle reduces the force to 300 N, but you must pull twice as much rope. Total work is unchanged.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander tool-making includes wedges (stone axes from Mount William on Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country), levers (digging sticks) and woven ropes. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Machines change force size or direction.
- No free lunch: less force, more distance.
- Lever, pulley, wedge, screw, ramp.
- The right tool turns hard into possible.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Machines create energy." No. They redistribute force and distance.
- "Pulleys give free lifting." No. You pull more rope to lift less effort.
- "Wedges only mean axes." No. Knives, doorstops, even teeth are wedges.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Work done pushing 50 N over 2 m.* 50 × 2 = 100 J.
- *Why a longer crowbar makes prying easier.* Greater lever arm length reduces the input force needed for the same output force.
- *Why a sharper knife is easier.* A longer, thinner wedge increases mechanical advantage at the cutting edge.
- *Why is "free force" impossible?* Energy is conserved. Less input force always means more input distance.
- *Short summary of the engine-block pulley example.* A 600 N load can be lifted with 300 N using a 2-pulley block-and-tackle, but you pull twice the rope length. The total work stays the same.
