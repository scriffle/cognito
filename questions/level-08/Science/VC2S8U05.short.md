# VC2S8U05 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The particle and kinetic theories explain how the arrangement and movement of particles determine a substance's properties and behaviour.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** All matter is particles in motion. Hot is fast, cold is slow. Solids hold, liquids flow, gases fill.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that the **particle** and **kinetic theories** explain how the **arrangement** and **movement** of particles determine a substance's **properties**.
- You should be able to explain a state change in particle terms.

## Core content

**Particle theory** says all matter is made of tiny particles, with spaces between them, that attract one another. **Kinetic theory** adds that particles are always moving, faster when warmer and slower when cooler. Temperature is a measure of average particle speed.

A **solid** has particles in fixed positions vibrating in place. A **liquid** has particles touching but moving past each other. A **gas** has particles spread far apart, moving fast and freely. Gas pressure comes from particles colliding with the container walls. **Diffusion** is the spread of particles from where they are concentrated to where they are not.

**Worked example.** Ice to steam. At -5°C, particles vibrate in place. Heat them and they vibrate harder until attractions are overcome. Ice melts at 0°C. The liquid has flowing particles. Continue heating: particles move faster, some escape the surface (evaporation). At 100°C, the rest break free and become steam.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge of how materials change with heat, pressure and combination has supported tool-making, food preparation and ochre processing for tens of thousands of years. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- All matter is particles in motion.
- Hot is fast. Cold is slow.
- Solids hold. Liquids flow. Gases fill.
- Pressure is collisions per second.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Particles are tiny but immobile balls." No. They are constantly moving.
- "Cold has no energy." No. Particles still move, just slower.
- "Gas has no mass." No. Gas particles have mass; a sealed container of gas weighs more than vacuum.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *What does "warm" mean at the particle level?* Particles are moving faster on average than in cooler matter.
- *Which state changes shape but not volume?* Liquid.
- *Why does a sealed bottle bulge in the sun?* Heating speeds up the gas particles. They collide with the walls more often and harder, raising pressure.
- *Why does perfume reach across a room?* Gas particles diffuse through air, mixing as they collide with air molecules.
- *Short trace of ice to steam.* Heat ice → vibrations overcome attractions at 0°C → liquid water with flowing particles → continued heating → particles escape as vapour at the surface → at 100°C all particles break free as steam.
