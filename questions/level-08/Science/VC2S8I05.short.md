# VC2S8I05 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Analyse processed data to identify patterns, trends, relationships and anomalies.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Patterns repeat, trends climb or fall, relationships link, anomalies are clues.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **analyse** processed data to **identify** patterns, trends, relationships and anomalies.
- You should be able to read your own graphs critically and describe what they show.

## Core content

A **pattern** is a repeating feature. A **trend** is a steady change over time. A **relationship** links two variables. An **anomaly** is a point that does not fit. Describe trends with direction, magnitude and timeframe.

Spotting relationships means plotting one variable against another and reading the shape: positive rises left to right, negative falls, horizontal cloud means no clear link. Anomalies can be measurement errors, recording errors, real outliers or signals of an event. Investigate before deleting. Correlation is a hint, not a cause.

**Worked example.** Real data from the 2009 Melbourne heatwave shows hospital admissions and excess deaths spiking on the hottest days. Pattern: yearly summer peaks. Trend: rising frequency of extreme heat days over decades. Relationship: high temperatures correlate with hospital admissions. Anomaly: a sudden, larger-than-usual spike in late January.

**Cultural context.** Long-running observation of seasons and species across Country supports detection of change. Reports from the Indigenous Knowledge Institute note shifts in flowering times, fish runs and bird arrivals identified by Traditional Owner ranger groups. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Patterns repeat. Trends climb or fall. Relationships link.
- Anomalies are clues, not litter.
- Direction, magnitude, timeframe.
- Correlation is a hint. Causation needs more.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Two things moving together must be linked." No. Coincidence is common, especially over short timeframes.
- "Anomalies are mistakes." No. Investigate first.
- "Strong correlation proves cause." No. Cause needs a mechanism and ruling out other factors.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Pattern, trend, relationship or anomaly: yearly summer ice-cream peak?* Pattern. It repeats reliably each year.
- *A proper trend statement.* "Between 2015 and 2024, average daily downloads rose by about 200 per year, an overall increase of around 40%."
- *What does a horizontal scatter cloud suggest?* No clear relationship.
- *Why investigate anomalies, not delete them?* Deleting hides faults or real signals. Investigating gives you a reason that improves the work.
- *Short summary of the heatwave example.* Hospital admissions spike on the hottest days. The yearly summer pattern, the long-term trend toward more extreme days, the heat-admission relationship and the late-January anomaly each tell a different part of the story.
