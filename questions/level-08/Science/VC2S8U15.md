## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2S8U15. Energy exists in different forms, can be transferred and transformed, and we can analyse how efficiently energy moves through a system.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Science (Physical Sciences: Forces & Energy).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Energy comes in many forms.
  - It can be transferred and transformed but not created or destroyed.
  - Efficiency tells us how much useful work we get from each input.

## Core content

**1. What energy is.** **Energy** is the capacity to do work. It is measured in **joules (J)**. Energy is real but invisible; we observe its effects, like motion, heat, sound and light. *Quick check:* What does it mean to say a system "has more energy"?

**2. Common forms.** Kinetic (motion), gravitational potential (height), elastic (stretched), thermal (heat), light, sound, chemical (in food and fuels), electrical and nuclear. *Quick check:* What two forms does a stretched bow store before it is released?

**3. Transfer vs transformation.** **Transfer** is energy moving from one object to another in the same form, like heat flowing from a hot pan to your hand. **Transformation** is energy changing form, like chemical to thermal in a candle, or electrical to light in a globe. *Quick check:* Is sunlight warming a window a transfer or a transformation?

**4. Conservation of energy.** The **law of conservation of energy** says energy is never created or destroyed; it just moves and changes form. Tracking energy through a system shows where it goes. *Quick check:* If a torch shines for an hour, where has the chemical energy in the battery gone?

**5. Worked example: pedal-powered phone charger.** Input: chemical energy in your food → kinetic energy of legs → mechanical energy in the pedals → electrical energy in the dynamo → battery energy in the phone. Some energy turns into heat in your muscles, friction in the gears, and resistance in the wires. *Follow the thinking:* identify each step → name the form → estimate the loss at each step → calculate overall efficiency.

**6. Efficiency.** **Efficiency** = useful energy out ÷ total energy in, often expressed as a percentage. A modern LED is about 30% efficient; an incandescent bulb is about 5%. *Quick check:* If a device uses 100 J and delivers 25 J usefully, what is its efficiency?

**7. Sankey diagrams.** A **Sankey diagram** shows energy flows as arrows whose width is proportional to the energy. They make losses visible. Useful for cars, light bulbs, and home heating. *Quick check:* Why is a Sankey diagram clearer than a list of numbers for tracking energy?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Sustainability Victoria publishes home and school energy audits. Power-station efficiency varies: brown-coal plants in the Latrobe Valley are around 25–30% efficient, modern combined-cycle gas around 50–60%, large solar with battery storage trades efficiency for low fuel cost and emissions.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander practices of cool burning, food preparation and shelter design demonstrate energy-efficient management of Country. Researchers including the Indigenous Knowledge Institute have published peer-reviewed work on these practices. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Greek-Australian, Italian-Australian and Vietnamese-Australian families across Melbourne use seasonal cooking, drying and preserving methods that reflect long-running attention to energy use.

## Memorable takeaways
- Energy is the capacity to do work.
- It moves and changes form, never disappears.
- Transfer same form. Transform new form.
- Efficiency = useful out / total in.
- Sankey makes losses visible.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Energy is created by power stations." No. They transform stored energy into electrical energy.
- "Efficiency over 100% is possible with the right device." No. That would break conservation of energy.
- "Heat is just wasted energy." Often, but heat itself is a form of energy and useful in heaters.
- "Renewable means efficient." No. Some renewables have lower efficiency but lower fuel cost and emissions.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *"More energy" means.* The system has more capacity to do work, whether in motion, height, heat or chemical form.
2. *Stretched bow stores.* Elastic potential energy. (Plus a small amount of gravitational potential if it is held high.)
3. *Sunlight warming a window.* Both. Light is transferred from Sun to window, then transformed into thermal energy as the glass absorbs it.
4. *Torch chemical energy after an hour.* Mostly transformed into light and heat dissipated to the surroundings; a smaller fraction lost in internal resistance and the bulb itself.
5. *Efficiency 25 of 100.* 25%.
6. *Why Sankey is clearer.* The arrow widths show proportions at a glance, making losses obvious.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Boil a kettle.* Electrical (wall) → thermal (element) → thermal (water) → kinetic (steam). Some energy lost as sound, light and heat to the room.
2. *Maribyrnong street lights.* LEDs use less electrical energy for the same brightness; they last longer, reducing replacement and waste; trade-off is higher upfront cost and recycling complexity.
3. *Café audit.* Replace older fridges (electrical → thermal removal); install LED lighting (electrical → light). Both reduce wasted electrical-thermal flows.
4. *"Free energy" device.* This is impossible because of conservation of energy. Teach that real devices always have an input, and any apparent "free energy" hides an input or violates physics.
5. *Wadawurrung cool burning.* Cool burning uses less energy and less stored fuel by burning at low intensity. Discuss with permission and credit, framing it as evidence-based land management. Use only published, community-approved material.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *System: rooftop solar plus battery in a Melbourne home.* Source: solar radiation. Solar panel converts to electrical (about 20% efficiency). Inverter converts DC to AC (about 95% efficiency). Battery stores and releases (about 90% round trip). Useful end use varies by appliance. Improvement: install a heat-pump hot-water system with timed heating to coincide with solar output, raising overall household energy efficiency.
