# VC2S8I03 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Select appropriate equipment and use it to generate and record data with attention to precision.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Match the tool to the question. Precision is not accuracy. Recording habits make or break a dataset.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **select** appropriate equipment and **use** it to **generate** and **record** data with attention to **precision**.
- You should be able to choose a tool, repeat your measurements and record them clearly.

## Core content

**Precision** is how close repeated measurements are to each other. **Accuracy** is how close they are to the true value. **Resolution** is the smallest unit your instrument can show. A miscalibrated thermometer can be very precise and very inaccurate. **Random error** is reduced by repeating. **Systematic error** is reduced by calibrating against a known reference.

Every record needs a label, a number, a unit, a time and the instrument used. Pre-printed tables prevent missing columns.

**Worked example.** To measure the density of a steel cube, use a digital calliper to 0.1 mm for each side and an electronic balance to 0.01 g for the mass. Take three independent measurements and average them. Calculate density and report units. Check the result against the known density of steel for a sense of accuracy.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander rangers across Victoria pair traditional observation with modern instruments such as data loggers and satellite telemetry. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Match instrument resolution to the question.
- Precision is repeatable. Accuracy is right.
- Repeat to fight random error.
- Label, number, unit, time, instrument.

## Watch out for these traps
- "More decimal places means more accurate." No. Adding zeros beyond your instrument's resolution is misleading.
- "Precise data is always good data." No. Precise but inaccurate readings hide a calibration problem.
- "One reading is enough if it looks reasonable." No. Repeats expose random error.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Precision vs accuracy.* Precision is how close repeated measurements are to each other. Accuracy is how close they are to the true value.
- *Five readings of 21.2°C in a fridge that should read 4°C.* Precise but inaccurate. The thermometer has a calibration problem.
- *Which error does averaging help fix?* Random error. Averaging cancels small unpredictable variations.
- *What is wrong with "23, 24, 22.5"?* Missing units, time, instrument and labels for what is being measured.
- *Short summary of the steel-cube method.* Measure each side with a digital calliper to 0.1 mm, weigh on an electronic balance to 0.01 g, repeat three times, average and calculate density. Compare to the known value to check accuracy.
