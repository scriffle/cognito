# VC2S8U11 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The rock cycle transforms rocks between sedimentary, igneous and metamorphic types over different timescales, and their properties reflect how they formed.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Three rock families form by different processes. Properties record history. The cycle has no single direction.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that the **rock cycle transforms** rocks between **sedimentary**, **igneous** and **metamorphic** types over **different timescales**.
- You should be able to read a rock's structure for clues to its origin.

## Core content

**Igneous** rocks form when molten rock cools and solidifies. Slow cooling deep underground creates large crystals (granite); fast cooling at the surface creates fine textures (basalt). **Sedimentary** rocks form when fragments, dissolved minerals or organic remains settle in layers and are compacted and cemented; they often hold fossils. **Metamorphic** rocks form when existing rocks are exposed to heat and pressure, transforming without melting; foliation is a common feature.

The rock cycle moves rocks between these families: igneous → weathered to sediment → sedimentary → heat and pressure → metamorphic → melted → igneous again.

**Worked example.** A basalt boulder near Mount Macedon. The Newer Volcanic Province in western Victoria erupted basalts as recently as a few thousand years ago. The boulder shows fine crystals (fast cooling), small holes from gas bubbles, and dark colour from iron-rich minerals. Over millions of years, weathering breaks it down, river systems transport fragments, and they settle as sediment that may form sandstone in the future.

**Cultural context.** The Mount William stone-axe quarry on Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country traded greenstone tools across south-eastern Australia for thousands of years. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Three rock families: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic.
- The cycle has no single direction.
- Heat and pressure transform without melting.
- Properties record the rock's past.

## Watch out for these traps
- "All rocks form the same way." No. The three families form by different processes.
- "The rock cycle is one-way." No. Rocks can transform along many pathways.
- "Sedimentary rocks are weak." No. Some sandstones and limestones are extremely durable.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why does granite have larger crystals than basalt?* Granite cools slowly underground, giving crystals time to grow. Basalt cools quickly at the surface.
- *Why are fossils almost always in sedimentary rocks?* Sediment protects remains during lithification. Heat and pressure in metamorphic rocks usually destroy fossils.
- *One feature in a metamorphic rock.* Foliation, where minerals are aligned in bands.
- *What process turns sediment into sedimentary rock?* Lithification: compaction by overlying weight and cementation by minerals from groundwater.
- *Short summary of the basalt boulder.* Quick cooling at the surface gave fine crystals, gas bubbles left holes, and iron minerals make it dark. Over time, weathering and transport turn fragments into future sediment.
