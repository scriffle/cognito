# VC2S8U03 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The structure of cells, tissues and organs is directly related to their function, and organ systems keep organisms alive.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Life is organised in levels. Structure follows function. Systems cooperate.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that the **structure** of cells, tissues and organs is directly related to their **function**, and that **organ systems** keep organisms alive.
- You should be able to trace one job across the levels of organisation.

## Core content

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells working together (muscle, epithelial, connective, nervous). An **organ** combines tissues to do a larger job, like the heart pumping blood. An **organ system** is a set of organs working together, such as the respiratory or digestive system.

Structure fits function at every level. Alveoli are thin and many, so gases diffuse fast across a large area. The small intestine has finger-like villi to increase absorption surface area. A leaf is thin and flat to capture light. Plants are organised too: tissues like xylem and phloem, organs like leaves and roots, and shoot and root systems.

**Worked example.** A sprint to the bus. The eye and brain decide. Skeletal muscles contract. Mitochondria in muscle cells need oxygen. Lungs pull air in. The heart pumps blood faster. Capillaries deliver oxygen to muscle cells. Carbon dioxide leaves by the same path in reverse. Multiple systems cooperate to do one job.

## Memorable takeaways
- Cells, tissues, organs, systems, organism.
- Structure follows function at every level.
- Surface area matters: alveoli, villi, root hairs.
- Systems cooperate to do one job.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Organ systems work alone." No. Most jobs need two or more systems cooperating.
- "Bigger means better." No. Many tissues, like alveoli, work because they are small and many.
- "Plants do not have organ systems." No. They have shoot and root systems with specialised tissues.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Level of the small intestine.* Organ.
- *Two tissues essential to a beating heart.* Cardiac muscle and nervous tissue. Connective tissue is also essential.
- *Shared feature of alveolus and villus.* Both maximise surface area for exchange or absorption.
- *Which organ system removes wastes from the blood?* Urinary system.
- *Short trace of oxygen from air to muscle.* Air enters the lungs, oxygen crosses the alveoli into blood, the heart pumps the blood to capillaries, and oxygen diffuses into muscle cells where mitochondria use it to release energy.
