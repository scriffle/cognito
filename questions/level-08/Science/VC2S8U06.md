- You are learning to choose a separation technique by reading what the components look like and behave like.

By the end of this you should be able to:

## Core content

**1. Pure substances vs mixtures.** A **pure substance** is made of one type of particle. A **mixture** contains two or more substances physically combined. Mixtures are very common: air, sea water, milk, bushland soil. *Quick check:* Is gold jewellery a pure substance or a mixture?

**2. Solutions and suspensions.** A **solution** is a mixture where one substance fully dissolves in another, like salt in water. A **suspension** has particles that settle out over time, like muddy water. *Quick check:* Why does sand settle in a bucket but salt does not?

**3. Filtration.** **Filtration** separates a liquid from an undissolved solid. Pour the suspension through filter paper. Particles too large to fit through are caught. *Quick check:* Why will filtration not separate salt from water?

**4. Evaporation and crystallisation.** Heat the solution. Water particles evaporate, leaving the dissolved solid behind. Used to recover sea salt for thousands of years. *Quick check:* Why is open evaporation slow?

**5. Distillation.** **Distillation** separates a liquid from another liquid or from dissolved solids by heating, condensing the vapour, and collecting it. Pure water can be produced from salt water this way. *Quick check:* Which property of water vs salt makes distillation work?

**6. Worked example: cleaning up muddy creek water.** Step 1: let it settle. Step 2: filter through a coffee filter to remove fine sediment. Step 3: distil if you need very pure water, or evaporate to recover dissolved salts. The choice of method depends on what you want and what is in the water. *Follow the thinking:* identify components → identify properties → match technique → check purity.

**7. Chromatography.** **Chromatography** separates substances by how strongly they attach to a surface and how readily they travel with a solvent. It works for ink components and food dyes. *Quick check:* Why do different ink colours travel different distances in paper chromatography?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Victorian Desalination Plant near Wonthaggi turns sea water into drinking water using reverse osmosis. EPA Victoria monitors river-water quality with separation and analysis methods.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal peoples across Australia have used careful preparation methods to remove toxins from foods, such as soaking and leaching cycad seeds and bunya nuts. These traditions show separation techniques developed long before laboratory chemistry. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant home cooks across Melbourne use solubility, filtration and crystallisation in everyday food preparation. Vietnamese, Italian and Lebanese kitchens demonstrate the same chemistry: stock-clarifying, oil separation, salt curing and pickling.

- Match the technique to the property.
- Filter for big bits.
- Evaporate or distil for dissolved bits.
- Chromatography for hidden mixtures.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Filter paper removes everything." No. Dissolved substances pass straight through.
- "Boiling kills everything." No. Boiling kills many microbes but does not remove dissolved chemicals or heavy metals.
- "Distilled water is the healthiest." No. Distilled water lacks the dissolved minerals found in tap or spring water.
- "Pure substance always means safe." No. Pure mercury is highly toxic. Purity and safety are different.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Gold jewellery.* Mixture. Most jewellery is an alloy of gold with copper or silver, which is why karat values matter.
2. *Why does sand settle but salt does not?* Sand particles are too large and dense to dissolve. Salt particles dissociate into ions that mix uniformly with water particles.
3. *Why won't filtration separate salt from water?* Salt is dissolved at the particle scale, smaller than the filter pores.
4. *Why is open evaporation slow?* The rate depends on temperature, surface area and air movement. Open air at room temperature offers limited energy and exchange.
5. *Property that makes distillation work.* The boiling point of water is much lower than that of salt. Water vaporises while salt remains.
6. *Why do ink colours travel different distances?* Each pigment has different attraction to the paper and solubility in the solvent, so they move at different rates.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Soft drink on carpet.* Blot to remove most liquid (uses absorption). Rinse and absorb again, exploiting solubility of sugar and dye in water. Vacuum or steam-clean to lift remaining dyes. Particle reason: water dissolves sugar/dye so they can be carried out.
2. *Frankston desalination.* Sea water has dissolved salts (need reverse osmosis or distillation), suspended particles (need filtration first), and may contain microbes (need disinfection). Each property points to a step in treatment.
3. *Brewery yeast removal.* Filtration with fine filters is fast and cheap but can affect flavour. Centrifugation is gentle on flavour but more expensive. Trade-off: cost vs flavour.
4. *Creek water filter test.* Issues: drinking untreated water is unsafe and may breach school policy. Safer plan: test the filter's removal of indicator dyes or measured turbidity, not for drinking.
5. *Yorta Yorta water preparation workshop.* Begin with a partnership agreement and ranger leadership. Discuss the chemistry alongside cultural meaning, do not detach the practice from its context, and use only published, community-approved material.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Rainwater purification in a remote area.* Step 1: settle in a tank to drop sediment. Step 2: pre-filter with cloth to remove organic debris. Step 3: pass through a ceramic or activated-carbon filter to remove fine particles and many chemicals. Step 4: UV or boiling to disinfect. Limit: dissolved heavy metals may not be removed without specialised resin or distillation.
