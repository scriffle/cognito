## Core content

**1. Charge, current and circuits.** **Current** is the rate at which electric charge flows, measured in amperes (A). A circuit is a closed loop that allows charge to flow from one terminal of a power source to the other. *Quick check:* What stops a circuit from working when a wire is cut?

**2. Voltage and energy.** **Voltage** (volts, V) is the energy each unit of charge gains or releases. A 12 V battery gives more energy per charge than a 1.5 V cell. *Quick check:* Why does a 9 V battery feel different from a 1.5 V battery in your hand?

**3. Resistance.** **Resistance** (ohms, Ω) is how much a component opposes current. A bulb has resistance. So does a long thin wire. Resistors are made on purpose to limit current. *Quick check:* What happens to current if resistance increases at fixed voltage?

**4. Worked example: a torch circuit.** Components: two AA cells (3 V), a switch, an LED with a 100 Ω resistor, and connecting wires. Closing the switch creates a closed loop. Current flows from the battery's positive terminal through the resistor and LED, then back. Voltage across the LED is around 2 V; the resistor drops the rest. Energy converts to light and a little heat. *Follow the thinking:* draw circuit → identify each component's role → label voltages → describe energy transfer.

**5. Series circuits.** In a **series** circuit, components are in a single line. The same current flows through each. If one part fails, the whole circuit stops. *Quick check:* Why might one bad bulb take out an entire string of old fairy lights?

**6. Parallel circuits.** In a **parallel** circuit, components have their own branch. Each branch has the full source voltage. One failure does not stop the others. *Quick check:* Why are home lighting circuits wired in parallel?

**7. Components for purposes.** Switches start and stop current. Resistors limit current. LEDs convert electrical to light energy. Capacitors store small amounts of charge. Each is chosen to do a job. *Quick check:* Why is a fuse a useful safety component?

## Watch out for these traps
- "Voltage flows around a circuit." No. Charge flows; voltage is energy per charge between two points.
- "Higher current is always better." No. Current must be matched to the components.
- "Parallel is always best." No. Series is correct for some safety and signalling tasks.
- "Mains and battery circuits are basically the same." No. Mains has hazards that demand qualified electricians.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *What stops a cut wire from working?* The loop is broken. Charge cannot complete the circuit, so no current flows.
2. *Why a 9 V battery feels different from 1.5 V.* Each charge carries more energy at higher voltage. The 9 V battery can drive more current through the same resistance, and is felt more strongly if the body is part of the circuit.
3. *Resistance up at fixed voltage.* Current decreases (Ohm's law: I = V/R).
4. *One bad bulb in old fairy lights.* In a series chain, breaking the path stops the current for all bulbs. Modern strings often add bypass paths.
5. *Why home lighting is parallel.* Each light gets full mains voltage and one failure does not take out the rest.
6. *Why a fuse is useful.* It melts and breaks the circuit if current is unsafely high, protecting people and equipment.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Two LEDs to one battery.* Parallel, so each LED sees the right voltage and one failure does not stop both. Add appropriate resistors per branch.
2. *Brimbank solar path lights.* Solar panel, rechargeable battery, a controller circuit, and an LED with light-dependent switching. Safety feature: low-voltage system with sealed enclosure.
3. *Standardised symbols.* They prevent miscommunication, support quick diagnosis, and meet Australian Standards required by law.
4. *Mains-powered class test.* Issues: shock hazard and electrocution. Alternative: use low-voltage battery circuits or supervised demonstration kits with appropriate safety controls.
5. *Wadawurrung-led microgrid.* Discuss with permission and credit. Frame the science as community-led: solar generates, battery stores, inverter delivers, controls manage demand. Use only published, community-approved material.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Sunset light.* Components: 9 V battery, light-dependent resistor (LDR), transistor as a switch, current-limiting resistor, LED. As ambient light falls, LDR resistance rises, biasing the transistor to switch the LED on. Energy flows from battery → transistor → LED, with a portion lost as heat. Diagram labels and one line on each component's role.
