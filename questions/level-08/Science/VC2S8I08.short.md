# VC2S8I08 — Science Inquiry Skills (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Communicate scientific ideas, findings and arguments by choosing formats, vocabulary and representations suited to purpose and audience.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Audience first. Format follows. Honest visuals beat dramatic ones.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **communicate** scientific ideas by **choosing** formats, vocabulary and representations suited to purpose and audience.
- You should be able to adapt one finding for two different audiences.

## Core content

A **format** is the type of communication: report, poster, slide deck, infographic, video. **Register** is how formal or technical your language is. A **representation** shows information visually: graph, table, diagram, photograph, model. Define technical words on first use. Pair every representation with a caption that names what is shown and what to take from it.

A clear scientific report has a question, a method, results with representations, an evaluation and a conclusion. Charts must use full axes unless cropping is justified and explained.

**Worked example.** A school energy-use poster for parents at open day. Audience: students and families. Purpose: persuade the school to install solar. Format: A2 poster. Representations: bar chart of monthly use, photo of the roof, simple infographic on payback time. Vocabulary: define kilowatt-hour on first use. Register: friendly, factual.

**Cultural context.** SBS Radio communicates science in over 60 languages. During COVID-19, multilingual public-health teams in Melbourne's west adapted formats and vocabulary for Vietnamese, Mandarin, Arabic and Dinka audiences. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Audience first, format second.
- Define jargon on first use.
- Pair representations with captions.
- Honest visuals beat dramatic ones.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Big words sound smart." No. Plain language sounds clear.
- "More charts means better communication." No. Each chart must earn its space.
- "Captions are decoration." No. Captions guide the reader's eye.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Assembly vs lab notebook audience.* Assembly: peers and teachers, short, big visuals, simple language. Lab notebook: detailed, technical, dated and signed.
- *Two parts of a caption.* What the reader sees and what they should take from it.
- *When to use "transpiration" vs "water moving through plants".* Use the technical word when the audience knows it or when you define it once. Use the phrase for younger or non-specialist audiences.
- *Why a cropped y-axis is a communication problem.* It exaggerates differences and hides the real scale of change.
- *Short summary of the energy-use poster example.* Identify audience and purpose, choose a poster format, pair a bar chart, photo and infographic with clear captions, define technical terms once, and review with a peer for clarity.
