# VC2S8U07 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Atomic theory explains the difference between elements, compounds and mixtures, and we represent them using symbols and chemical formulas.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Atoms build elements. Elements bond into compounds. Mixtures are physically combined. Symbols are an international shorthand.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that **atomic theory** explains the difference between **elements**, **compounds** and **mixtures**, represented by **symbols** and **formulas**.
- You should be able to read and write basic chemical shorthand.

## Core content

All matter is made of **atoms**. An **element** has only one type of atom (e.g. iron, Fe). A **compound** has two or more elements chemically bonded (e.g. water, H₂O). A **mixture** has two or more substances physically combined that keep their own properties (e.g. air).

A **chemical formula** uses symbols and numbers to show elements and ratios. H₂O means two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. The subscript counts atoms in a molecule; a coefficient like "2H₂O" counts molecules. Spelling matters: Co is cobalt; CO is carbon monoxide.

**Worked example.** Hydrogen (H) is a flammable gas. Oxygen (O) supports combustion. Bond two hydrogen atoms to one oxygen atom and the result is liquid water at room temperature. New properties emerge: it is no longer flammable. Bonding changes properties.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge of materials includes ochres (iron oxides), eucalyptus oils (organic compounds) and fibres (natural polymers). Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Atoms make elements. Elements make compounds.
- Properties change when atoms bond.
- Symbols are an international shorthand.
- Precision in names prevents accidents.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Compounds are mixtures of elements." No. Compounds are chemically bonded. Mixtures are physically combined.
- "Capital and lowercase do not matter." No. Co is cobalt; CO is carbon monoxide.
- "Air is one substance." No. Air is a mixture of gases.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is salt safe but sodium and chlorine alone are not?* Bonding changes properties. The compound is stable; the elements alone are highly reactive.
- *Element, compound or mixture: brass?* Mixture. An alloy of copper and zinc whose proportions can vary.
- *What does CaCO₃ tell you?* Calcium carbonate has one calcium atom, one carbon atom and three oxygen atoms per formula unit.
- *Oxygen atoms in 3CO₂.* Six. Two per CO₂, multiplied by three molecules.
- *Why is "table salt" less precise than NaCl?* Table salt can include additives like iodide and anti-caking agents. NaCl points to one specific compound.
