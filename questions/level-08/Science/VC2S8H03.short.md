# VC2S8H03 — Science as a Human Endeavour (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Scientific responses to real-world issues can involve ethical, environmental, social and economic trade-offs.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Real-world choices rarely have a perfect option. Use four lenses. Naming weights keeps debate honest.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that scientific responses to real-world issues can **involve** ethical, environmental, social and economic **trade-offs**.
- You should be able to weigh and explain those trade-offs in a concrete case.

## Core content

A **trade-off** is a choice where gaining one thing costs you another. A **stakeholder** is anyone affected by a decision. **Opportunity cost** is what you give up when you pick one option. Useful science decisions are examined through four lenses: ethical, environmental, social and economic.

Two reasonable people can study the same evidence and reach different conclusions because they weight effects differently. Naming your weights makes a debate honest. Tools like cost-benefit analysis and stakeholder maps make hidden judgements visible.

**Worked example.** Australia is planning offshore wind zones, including off Gippsland on Gunaikurnai Country. Ethical: how to share benefits with Traditional Owners and locals. Environmental: turbines reduce emissions but can affect seabirds and whales. Social: construction and operation jobs in the Latrobe Valley. Economic: high upfront cost, lower fuel bills over time. The decision changes depending on which lens you weight highest.

**Cultural context.** On Gunaikurnai Country, GLaWAC has signed agreements with the Victorian Government on joint park management. Trade-offs include cultural protection, tourism income and ecological restoration. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Every real decision costs something somewhere.
- Use all four lenses, not just the loudest.
- List stakeholders before listing answers.
- Showing the working beats announcing the answer.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Science gives us the right answer." No. Science narrows options. Humans choose between trade-offs.
- "If it is good for the environment, the cost does not matter." No. Cost can decide whether the project is built.
- "Trade-offs mean someone is being unfair." No. Trade-offs are unavoidable. Fairness lies in how they are decided and shared.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *What is a trade-off?* A choice where gaining one thing costs you another, with no perfect option available.
- *Why use all four lenses?* A single lens hides effects. Using ethical, environmental, social and economic lenses surfaces costs and benefits that would otherwise be missed.
- *Who is a stakeholder in a wind farm?* Anyone affected: local households, Traditional Owners, energy users, wildlife, future grid users.
- *Why name your weights?* It makes disagreement honest. Two people can read the same evidence and decide differently because they prioritise different effects.
- *Short summary of offshore wind near Gippsland.* It cuts emissions and brings jobs but raises wildlife and cost concerns. The right answer depends on whose interests are weighted and how the project is designed.
