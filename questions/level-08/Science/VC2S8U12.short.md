# VC2S8U12 — Science Understanding (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The changing positions of Earth, the Sun and the Moon cause predictable cycles including seasons, eclipses and tides.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Science.
- **Big ideas:** Day from spin. Year from orbit. Seasons from tilt. Tides from gravity.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that the **changing positions** of Earth, the Sun and the Moon cause **predictable cycles**.
- You should be able to use simple geometry to explain seasons, eclipses and tides.

## Core content

Earth **rotates** on its axis once every 24 hours and **orbits** the Sun once every 365.25 days. The axis is tilted about 23.5°, which causes the seasons: when the southern hemisphere tilts toward the Sun, Melbourne has summer; when it tilts away, winter.

The Moon orbits Earth about every 27 days, and we see phases as the lit half is angled toward us. A **solar eclipse** happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth; a **lunar eclipse** when Earth passes between the Sun and Moon. Eclipses are rare because the Moon's orbit is slightly tilted.

**Worked example.** Tides at Phillip Island. The Moon's gravity pulls water on the Earth-facing side. A second bulge appears on the opposite side. As Earth rotates, beaches pass through both bulges, giving two highs and two lows per day. The Sun adds a smaller pull. When Sun and Moon align (new and full moons), tides are larger (spring tides). At first and third quarter, tides are smaller (neap tides).

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander astronomy includes detailed knowledge of the Sun, Moon and stars. The Yolŋu people of north-east Arnhem Land have shared a description of lunar tides that aligns with Western explanations. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Day from spin. Year from orbit.
- Seasons from tilt, not distance.
- Eclipses are alignment events.
- Tides rise and fall twice a day.

## Watch out for these traps
- "It is summer because Earth is closer to the Sun." No. Tilt explains seasons.
- "The Moon makes its own light." No. It reflects sunlight.
- "Eclipses happen every month." No. The Moon's orbit is tilted.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is Melbourne cold in June even though Earth is closest to the Sun in early January?* Tilt determines how directly sunlight strikes the southern hemisphere. In June, that hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun.
- *Where is the Moon at full moon?* On the side of Earth opposite the Sun, so the Earth-facing side is fully lit.
- *Why are total solar eclipse paths narrow?* The Moon casts a small shadow at Earth's distance. Only places inside it see totality.
- *Why are spring tides larger than neap tides?* At new and full moons, the Sun and Moon pull along the same line, adding to the tide. At first and third quarter, they pull at right angles.
- *Short summary of two tides per day.* The Moon creates one bulge facing it and another on the opposite side. As Earth spins, each beach passes through both bulges and the two troughs in between.
