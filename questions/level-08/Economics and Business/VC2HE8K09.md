## Core content

**1. Why we have tax.** Tax funds services that markets do not deliver well alone: defence, health, education, roads, public broadcasting, justice, social services. It also redistributes income from higher to lower earners. *Quick check:* What is one local service in your suburb that tax funds?

**2. Types of Commonwealth tax.** **Income tax** on individuals (progressive: higher rates on higher incomes). **GST** of 10% on most goods and services. **Company tax** on business profits. Other taxes include fringe-benefits tax, excise on alcohol and tobacco, and customs duties. *Quick check:* What does "progressive" mean for tax rates?

**3. State and local tax.** States collect payroll tax, stamp duty on property, gambling and motor-vehicle taxes. Local councils collect **rates** from property owners and a small set of fees. Funding for many services is shared across levels (e.g., schools and hospitals). *Quick check:* Why do many services depend on more than one level of government?

**4. Worked example: Pay slip in Melbourne.** A worker earns $1,200 a week. Tax is withheld by the employer through PAYG and forwarded to the ATO. Their take-home is lower than the gross pay. At the end of the financial year, they lodge a tax return; the ATO reconciles their tax. If they paid too much, they receive a refund; too little, a bill. *Follow the thinking:* gross pay → tax withheld → take-home → annual reconciliation.

**5. Where the money goes.** Australian government spending categories include: social security and welfare (largest), health, education, defence, general public services, infrastructure. State and local budgets fund hospitals, schools and roads in different shares. *Quick check:* What is one example of "social security" you have seen?

**6. Tax debates.** Public debate often focuses on income-tax rates, company-tax rates, GST coverage and progressivity. There are honest disagreements about fairness, efficiency and incentives. *Quick check:* Why is "everyone wants lower tax" a misleading simplification?

**7. Tax morale and compliance.** A working tax system depends on trust: that revenue is used fairly and that compliance is enforced. The Australian Tax Office (ATO) administers it. *Quick check:* Why is "tax morale" part of the economic story?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Victorian state taxes (payroll, land, stamp duty) fund hospitals, schools and infrastructure. Local councils across Victoria collect rates that fund libraries, parks and roads.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander-owned businesses pay company tax like other businesses, and Aboriginal-led not-for-profits often deliver tax-funded services. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrants pay tax from arrival and use tax-funded services like schools, hospitals and settlement programs. Multilingual ATO resources support compliance.

## Watch out for these traps
- "GST is unfair because everyone pays it." Lower-income households pay a larger share of income on GST; rebates and concessions adjust the impact.
- "All my tax goes to roads." Social security is usually the largest single category.
- "Tax avoidance is the same as tax evasion." Avoidance is legal planning; evasion is illegal.
- "Lower tax always grows the economy." Sometimes; trade-offs depend on what is funded.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Local service tax funds.* Public library; rubbish collection; local parks; public hospital; state school.
2. *"Progressive" tax.* Higher-income earners pay a higher percentage of income on additional dollars.
3. *Why services span levels.* Different government scales fund different parts; cooperation is part of the system.
4. *Social-security example.* Age pension; family tax benefit; JobSeeker; disability support.
5. *"Everyone wants lower tax" simplification.* People also want more spending on services; lower tax usually means less spending or higher debt.
6. *Why tax morale matters.* Compliance is voluntary in large part; perceived fairness drives willingness to comply.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *"GST included".* The price includes 10% Goods and Services Tax; the seller forwards it to the ATO; revenue is shared with states.
2. *Yarra Ranges rates increase.* Funds council services like roads and libraries vs household cost-of-living pressure; service-quality trade-off.
3. *First tax return.* Get a Tax File Number; gather income statements; use myTax via the ATO; ask about deductions you can legitimately claim.
4. *Higher company tax debate.* For: companies use public infrastructure; fairer share. Against: investment effects; cost passed to consumers. Both sides agree on need for honest design.
5. *New migrant family.* Step 1: register a TFN. Step 2: use multilingual ATO resources. Step 3: ask a community legal centre or tax-help volunteer for first-year support.
