# VC2HE8K06 — Economics and Business

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HE8K06. The changing nature of work in contemporary Australia and predictions about the future of work.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Economics and Business (Work).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Work has always changed; the pace has accelerated.
  - Technology and demographics drive the change.
  - Adaptability is the most important worker skill.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Digital Literacy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Personal and Social Capability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about the **changing nature of work** in **contemporary Australia** and **predictions about the future of work**.
- You are learning to read trends and plan for them.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **automation**, **gig economy** and **lifelong learning** in your own words.
- Identify three forces changing Australian work.
- Describe one occupation likely to grow and one likely to shrink.
- Explain why uncertainty is part of any prediction about future work.

## Core content

**1. Forces of change.** Major forces: technology (especially digitalisation and AI), demographics (ageing population), globalisation (offshoring and international competition), climate (energy transition), and policy choices (industrial relations, education). *Quick check:* Which force feels most visible in your community?

**2. Automation and AI.** **Automation** is the use of machines or software to do tasks once done by people. Routine tasks (data entry, simple driving, basic transcription) are most exposed. Creative, complex, social-emotional and physical-dexterity tasks are less exposed. AI is changing this picture quickly. *Quick check:* Why are some jobs "less exposed" rather than "safe"?

**3. The gig economy.** The **gig economy** is paid work done as short tasks or contracts (rideshare, food delivery, freelance design). It offers flexibility and risks (income variability, fewer entitlements). Regulation is evolving. *Quick check:* What is one advantage and one disadvantage of gig work?

**4. Worked example: a Year 8 student in 2026.** Their parents may have careers that did not exist 25 years ago (data scientist, sustainability consultant) and others under pressure (some clerical roles). By the time the student is 30, new occupations will exist that we cannot fully predict. Skills that travel: communication, problem-solving, learning new tools, working with diverse teams, ethical judgement. *Follow the thinking:* observe trends → identify transferable skills → plan lifelong learning.

**5. Demographic change.** Australia's population is ageing. More work in health, aged care and allied services. Skill shortages in trades. More older workers continue in paid work. *Quick check:* Why does aged care employment grow with an older population?

**6. Lifelong learning.** **Lifelong learning** is updating skills across your working life. It includes formal study, on-the-job training, micro-credentials and self-directed learning. Adaptability beats locking in one set of skills. *Quick check:* What is one habit you can start now that supports lifelong learning?

**7. Predictions are uncertain.** Forecasts about future work get many specifics wrong. Broad directions (more digital, more services, more healthcare) are more reliable than specific job-counts. Honest planning names uncertainty. *Quick check:* Why do "10 jobs of the future" lists often age poorly?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Victoria's economy is shifting from manufacturing toward services, health and tech. The Latrobe Valley energy transition is a high-profile case. CSIRO and universities publish workforce-trend reports.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander employment grows in land management, ranger programs, arts, education, health and businesses listed on Supply Nation. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant workers contribute strongly across health, aged care, transport, hospitality and tech. Skilled migration policy shapes the labour market in Victoria.

## Memorable takeaways
- Work changes with technology, demographics and policy.
- Adaptability beats fixed skills.
- Gig work has flexibility and risk.
- Health and care are growing.
- Predictions are directional, not precise.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Robots will take all the jobs." Some tasks; rarely whole jobs. New jobs also emerge.
- "Trades are obsolete." Skill shortages and high pay say otherwise.
- "University is the only path." Trades, TAFE, micro-credentials and starting a business are real options.
- "Future-proof skills exist." Adaptability beats any specific skill.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Most visible force.* Often digitalisation in daily life; AI in homework; ageing in family caring; climate in conversation.
2. *Why "less exposed" not "safe".* Tasks within an exposed job can still be automated; what changes is what the worker does, not always whether the job exists.
3. *Gig advantages/disadvantages.* Flexibility and entry; instability and fewer entitlements.
4. *Why aged-care growth.* Larger older population needs more health and social services; demand for skilled workers grows.
5. *Lifelong-learning habit.* Reading something new each week; tackling a free online course each term; learning from work mentors.
6. *Why "10 jobs of the future" ages poorly.* Specific predictions need many assumptions to hold; broad directions are more reliable.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Trends 2035.* AI in everyday tools; climate transition jobs; care-economy growth.
2. *Casey Future of Work night.* A TAFE trainer; an AI ethics researcher; a careers counsellor; an Aboriginal employment officer.
3. *Trade vs university.* Earnings over time; lifestyle and physical demands; learning style; family context.
4. *Gig-worker sick leave.* For: basic protection. Against: cost and flexibility. Both can be balanced with portable entitlements.
5. *Sudanese-Australian support.* Step 1: language and digital-skills classes. Step 2: mentoring partnerships with employers. Step 3: micro-credential pathways in identified growth areas.
