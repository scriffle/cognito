# VC2HE8K03 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The different purposes and goals of businesses, different categories of business organisation according to ownership structure, types of products, target market, and how businesses can identify and take advantage of market opportunity.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Businesses exist for different reasons. Structure matches purpose. Opportunity is unmet need plus ability to deliver.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about **business purposes and goals**, **ownership structures**, **product types**, **target markets** and how businesses **find opportunity**.
- You should be able to read a business as a structure with purpose.

## Core content

Businesses pursue profit, services, social goals or cultural sustainability. **Sole trader**: one owner with personal liability. **Partnership**: two or more owners. **Company**: separate legal entity with limited liability (Pty Ltd or Ltd). **Not-for-profit**: surplus reinvested in mission. Products are **goods** (physical) or **services** (work), often combined. A **target market** is the group of customers a business focuses on.

Opportunity comes from unmet needs in the community, demographic and technology trends, customer requests and observation of competitors. Test cheaply before scaling. Ethics is part of business decisions, not separate.

**Worked example.** A 17-year-old in Box Hill notices peers want rare sneakers locally. Sole trader structure. Product: physical goods plus sourcing service. Target market: 14–18 year olds in suburban Melbourne. Opportunity: local supply gap. Trade-offs: time vs school; competition from online giants.

**Cultural context.** Supply Nation lists Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander-owned businesses across Australia. Examples include Clothing the Gaps and many in Victoria. Migrant entrepreneurs run a large share of Victorian small businesses across hospitality, retail and services. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Purposes vary; structures match purposes.
- Sole trader risks more; companies separate liability.
- Sharper targeting beats broad reach.
- Opportunity = unmet need + ability to deliver.

## Watch out for these traps
- "All businesses are about profit." Many serve other purposes too.
- "Sole trader is simplest, so it is best." It is also the riskiest for personal liability.
- "Bigger market is better." Sharper targeting often wins.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Non-profit business.* A community health clinic; op shop; neighbourhood house.
- *Structure that shields personal assets.* A company (limited liability).
- *Coffee and experience.* Both a good (the coffee) and a service (seating, music, atmosphere).
- *Unmet need in a suburb.* Late-evening transport for shift workers; affordable bilingual children's books.
- *Short summary of the sneaker resale idea.* Sole trader structure, mixed product, sharp local target market, opportunity in a supply gap, with school-time trade-offs.
