# VC2HE8S06 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Create and present descriptions and explanations, using subject-specific knowledge and terminology, with reference to sources.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Description first; explanation second. Audience and format together. Citations build credibility.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **create and present descriptions and explanations** using **subject terminology** with **source references**.
- You should be able to talk about economics clearly and credibly.

## Core content

**Description** says what is happening; **explanation** says why. Use precise terms (scarcity, opportunity cost, supply, demand, equilibrium, CPI, GDP) and define them on first use for non-experts. Choose format and tone for the audience: short video for peers, written report for assessors, public talk for parents. Cite by author/organisation, year and where accessed. Use a consistent style.

Charts and infographics help where words struggle. Use labels, units and a clear takeaway. Cite data sources on the chart itself.

**Worked example.** Explaining inflation. Year 6 audience: a 90-second animation with a price comparison. Parents at a school evening: a 200-word handout with a chart and one source. Teacher assessor: a 600-word report with multiple cited sources. Same concept; three formats.

**Cultural context.** First Nations channels include NITV, IndigenousX and the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria. Cite First Nations authors directly. SBS Radio broadcasts in over 60 languages; multilingual ATO and ASIC resources support compliance and literacy. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Description first; explanation second.
- Use precise terms; define for non-experts.
- Cite by author, year, source.
- Integrity beats persuasion.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Big words sound smart." Plain language sounds clear.
- "Long quotes prove the point." Use sparingly; reasoning is yours.
- "If it looks polished, the source is fine." Polish is not provenance.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Description vs explanation.* Description: what happened. Explanation: why.
- *Why does precise vocabulary build trust?* It signals careful thinking and lets readers follow exactly.
- *Year 8 vs council audience.* Council: more numbers and policy framing. Year 8: accessibility, examples, takeaways.
- *Why do citations help readers?* They allow follow-up, demonstrate breadth and signal honesty.
- *Short summary of explaining inflation across three formats.* Same concept tuned to the audience: a quick animation, a handout with a chart, a fully cited report.
