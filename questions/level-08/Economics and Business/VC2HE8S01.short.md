# VC2HE8S01 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Develop and apply questions to investigate contemporary economic, business, work or financial issues and systems.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Topic ≠ question. Place, time, actor, measure. Operationalise the variable.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop and apply questions** to investigate **economic, business, work or financial issues**.
- You should be able to turn an idea into something you can investigate.

## Core content

A topic is general; a question is specific. Refine by adding place, time, actor and measure. **Operationalisation** turns abstract terms into observable measures: "affordability" becomes "percentage of household income spent on rent". Different stakeholders care about different measures (workers, platforms, customers, regulators); naming them sharpens the question without making it loaded. Many economic questions weigh costs against benefits across stakeholders.

Refine when sources do not fit, variables cannot be operationalised, or bias-checks reveal loaded language.

**Worked example.** Investigating youth gig-work. Initial: Is the gig economy good for young people? Refined v1: How has the share of Year 11–12 students doing gig work changed? Refined v2: How has the share of Victorian Year 11–12 students doing food-delivery gig work changed between 2019 and 2024, based on ABS and union surveys?

**Cultural context.** Economic questions affecting First Nations communities should follow AIATSIS ethical guidelines, with community priorities shaping the question. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Topic ≠ question.
- Place, time, actor, measure.
- Operationalise the variable.
- Refining is part of the work.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If the question is interesting, it is investigable." Interest is necessary, not sufficient.
- "Stakeholders are a distraction." They shape what counts as an answer.
- "More words means stronger question." Clarity matters more than length.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *"Interested in rent" not yet a question.* It names a topic but no place, time, measure or actor.
- *Operational measure of youth unemployment.* 15–24 unemployment rate; underemployment rate; participation rate of full-time students.
- *Why does naming stakeholders strengthen questions?* It clarifies whose costs and benefits count.
- *Bias-check question.* Does my wording assume a conclusion? Does it leave out any group?
- *Short summary of refining the gig-work question.* Add a year band, a state, a sector and a data source to move from broad topic to investigable question.
