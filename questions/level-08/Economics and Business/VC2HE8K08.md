## Core content

**1. Smart shopping basics.** Compare prices across at least three sellers; read product reviews from multiple sources; check return policies; look for warranties; consider total cost (delivery, fees, ongoing costs). *Quick check:* Why is total cost different from sticker price?

**2. Plan before purchase.** Wait at least 24 hours for non-essential purchases over $50; ask whether the purchase meets a real need or want; check your budget. **Cooling-off periods** in some contracts (e.g., door-to-door sales) give you time to cancel. *Quick check:* Why does a 24-hour pause reduce impulse spending?

**3. Verify the seller.** Check the website's address (https, not http; correct spelling); look up reviews and complaints; prefer trusted platforms; verify by phone or email through known contacts. *Quick check:* Why is the spelling of a website address a red flag?

**4. Worked example: spotting a phishing scam.** You get a text claiming to be from Australia Post: "Parcel delayed. Pay $1.99 to redeliver: [link]". Red flags: small fee creates urgency; clicking the link goes to a fake site; the URL is misspelled. Smart response: do not click; go to the official Australia Post tracking site directly; report the SMS to Scamwatch. *Follow the thinking:* notice urgency → check the URL → use official channels → report.

**5. Three common patterns.** **Phishing**: fake messages from "trusted" sources asking for details. **Investment scams**: promises of high returns with low risk. **Romance scams**: building trust online before asking for money. All three exploit emotion and urgency. *Quick check:* Which emotion is most often exploited?

**6. What to do if you have been scammed.** Stop further payments immediately; contact your bank to freeze cards; change passwords; report to Scamwatch and to police if appropriate; tell trusted family; check for identity-theft signs over coming weeks. *Quick check:* Why is acting fast critical after a scam?

**7. Habits that prevent scams.** Use unique passwords; enable two-factor authentication; do not share verification codes; talk through big decisions with someone you trust; if it sounds too good to be true, it is. *Quick check:* What is one habit you can start today?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Scamwatch (run by the ACCC) and Consumer Affairs Victoria provide alerts and reporting tools. Victoria Police, banks and IDCARE Australia support victims.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** The Indigenous Consumer Assistance Network (ICAN) supports Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander consumers, including those targeted by predatory sales. Scamwatch publishes culturally adapted alerts. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual scam alerts reach communities in Vietnamese, Mandarin, Arabic, Sudanese and other languages. The Ethnic Communities' Council of Victoria runs awareness programs.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If it has reviews, it must be safe." Reviews can be fake.
- "Only older people fall for scams." Year 8s and adults of all ages have been victims.
- "Scammers are easy to spot." Many use professional design and personal information.
- "I will know if I am being scammed." Trust your gut, but verify with someone you trust.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Total cost vs sticker.* Includes delivery, fees, optional add-ons, and any ongoing subscription.
2. *Why 24-hour pause.* Impulse fades; you check whether the purchase fits your needs and budget.
3. *Misspelled website.* Common scam sign (e.g., australia-post.support); always check the real domain.
4. *Most-exploited emotion.* Urgency, plus fear of loss and excitement about gains.
5. *Why act fast.* Most fraud can be blocked within hours; delays reduce recovery chances.
6. *Habit you can start.* Pause before clicking; verify in a second channel; talk to a trusted person.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *$20 game for $5.* Check seller reviews on a trusted source; check URL spelling; compare price with reputable platforms.
2. *Frankston scam-awareness.* Phishing-text demo; bank-scam call examples; a hands-on "spot the scam" game.
3. *Bank-branch worker.* Pause the transaction; ask gentle questions about the call; suggest the customer hang up and call the bank's known number.
4. *Platform reimbursement debate.* For: platforms profit from ads, including scam ads; safety as a basic responsibility. Against: cost; identifying genuine victims is hard. Both sides agree platforms have a role.
5. *Vietnamese-speaking grandparent.* Step 1: call the family member together. Step 2: ring the bank using a number on the back of the card. Step 3: report to Scamwatch and the local community legal centre.
