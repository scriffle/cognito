# VC2HE8K07 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The legal and ethical rights and responsibilities of Australian consumers and businesses.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Rights and responsibilities go together. Law sets the floor; ethics asks more.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **legal and ethical rights and responsibilities** of **consumers and businesses**.
- You should be able to act fairly on both sides.

## Core content

The **Australian Consumer Law (ACL)** sits in the Competition and Consumer Act 2010, administered by the ACCC and Consumer Affairs Victoria. Consumer guarantees: goods must be of **acceptable quality**, **fit for purpose** and **match description**; services delivered with **due care and skill**. These cannot be excluded by contract.

Consumer responsibilities: pay agreed prices, behave respectfully, follow instructions, keep receipts, raise concerns promptly. Business responsibilities: deliver what was promised, be honest in advertising, disclose key terms, handle complaints fairly, keep customer data private. Ethics often goes further than law.

**Worked example.** A phone-bill dispute in Brunswick. The plan promised "unlimited data" but throttled after 30 GB without clear disclosure. ACL s 18 (misleading conduct) applies. Consumer steps: contact telco complaints, escalate to the Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman if unresolved. Keep records.

**Cultural context.** The Indigenous Consumer Assistance Network (ICAN) provides culturally appropriate consumer support; ACCC has acted on misleading sales practices targeting Aboriginal communities. Multilingual consumer-rights information and translated complaint forms support migrant and refugee communities. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Rights and responsibilities go together.
- ACL sets the floor.
- Goods and services have guarantees.
- Help exists; use it.

## Watch out for these traps
- "No refunds" signs override the law. They don't.
- "If you signed, you have no rights." Unfair-terms law can override one-sided clauses.
- "Complaints are pointless." Most are resolved at the first step.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Faulty laptop "no refunds".* No. ACL guarantees override store policies.
- *Customer responsibility on returns.* Take care of the product; return within a reasonable time; bring proof of purchase.
- *Why does ethics go beyond law?* Law sets the minimum; ethics aims higher.
- *First step in a dispute.* Contact the business directly with the issue and what you want.
- *Short summary of the phone-bill case.* The misleading "unlimited" claim breaches the ACL; the consumer should escalate through the telco's complaints unit, then the Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman if needed.
