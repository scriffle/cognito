# VC2HE8K08 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Strategies to become a smart consumer, including dealing with financial scams.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Compare, plan, verify. Scams use urgency, fear and trust. Total cost beats sticker price.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **strategies to become a smart consumer**, including **dealing with financial scams**.
- You should be able to keep your money and data safer.

## Core content

Smart shopping: compare across at least three sellers; read multiple reviews; check return policies and warranties; consider total cost (delivery, fees, ongoing costs). Wait 24 hours for non-essential purchases over $50. **Cooling-off periods** in some contracts (e.g., door-to-door sales) give time to cancel.

Three common scam patterns: **phishing** (fake messages from "trusted" sources), **investment scams** (high returns, low risk), **romance scams** (trust-building before money requests). All exploit emotion and urgency. Use unique passwords, two-factor authentication, and never share verification codes.

**Worked example.** A phishing scam. You get an SMS claiming to be from Australia Post: "Parcel delayed. Pay $1.99 to redeliver." Red flags: small fee creates urgency; link goes to a fake site; URL is misspelled. Smart response: do not click; go to the official Australia Post site directly; report the SMS to Scamwatch.

If scammed: stop further payments; contact your bank to freeze cards; change passwords; report to Scamwatch and police if appropriate; tell trusted family.

**Cultural context.** The Indigenous Consumer Assistance Network (ICAN) supports Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander consumers. Multilingual scam alerts reach communities in Vietnamese, Mandarin, Arabic, Sudanese and other languages. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Compare, plan, verify.
- Scams use urgency, fear and trust.
- If it sounds too good, it is.
- Stop, freeze, change, report.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If it has reviews, it must be safe." Reviews can be fake.
- "Only older people fall for scams." Anyone can be a victim.
- "Scammers are easy to spot." Many use professional design.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Total cost vs sticker.* Includes delivery, fees, add-ons and any ongoing subscription.
- *Misspelled website.* Common scam sign (e.g., australia-post.support); always check the real domain.
- *Most-exploited emotion.* Urgency, plus fear of loss and excitement about gains.
- *Why act fast.* Most fraud can be blocked within hours; delays reduce recovery chances.
- *Short summary of an Australia Post phishing scam.* Notice urgency, check the URL, use official channels, do not click, and report to Scamwatch.
