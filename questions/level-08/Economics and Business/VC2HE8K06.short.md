# VC2HE8K06 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The changing nature of work in contemporary Australia and predictions about the future of work.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Work always changes. Tech, demographics and policy drive it. Adaptability is the key skill.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about the **changing nature of work** in Australia and **predictions about the future**.
- You should be able to read trends and plan for them.

## Core content

Major forces: **technology** (digitalisation, AI), **demographics** (ageing population), **globalisation**, **climate** (energy transition), and **policy** choices. **Automation** affects routine tasks most; creative, complex, social-emotional and physical-dexterity tasks are less exposed. The **gig economy** offers flexibility and instability. **Lifelong learning** is updating skills across your working life through formal study, on-the-job training, micro-credentials and self-directed work.

Predictions are uncertain: broad directions (more digital, more services, more health) are more reliable than specific job counts.

**Worked example.** A Year 8 student in 2026. Their parents may have careers that didn't exist 25 years ago (data scientist, sustainability consultant). By the time the student is 30, new occupations will exist that we can't fully predict. Skills that travel: communication, problem-solving, learning new tools, working with diverse teams, ethical judgement.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander employment grows in land management, ranger programs, arts, education and health. Migrant workers contribute strongly across health, aged care, transport, hospitality and tech. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Work changes with technology, demographics and policy.
- Adaptability beats fixed skills.
- Health and care are growing.
- Predictions are directional, not precise.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Robots will take all the jobs." Some tasks; rarely whole jobs. New jobs also emerge.
- "Trades are obsolete." Skill shortages and high pay say otherwise.
- "Future-proof skills exist." Adaptability beats any specific skill.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why "less exposed" not "safe".* Tasks within a job can still be automated; what changes is what the worker does.
- *Gig advantages/disadvantages.* Flexibility and entry vs instability and fewer entitlements.
- *Why does aged care grow?* A larger older population needs more health and social services.
- *Lifelong-learning habit.* Weekly reading; free online courses; learning from work mentors.
- *Short summary of the 2026 Year 8.* Trends shape choices; transferable skills matter more than job titles; lifelong learning is the habit that adapts.
