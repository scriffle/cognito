# VC2HE8S02 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Locate, select and organise relevant data and information from a range of sources.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** One source is not research. Organise with labels and units. Triangulate to manage bias.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **locate, select and organise** data and information from a **range of sources**.
- You should be able to build a working dataset for your question.

## Core content

Sources include the ABS, RBA, ATO, Productivity Commission, Treasury, councils, peak industry bodies, NGOs, academic articles, news outlets and community-language media. A **primary source** is original data (ABS Census, company reports); a **secondary source** interprets (news, opinion). Selection criteria: authority, currency, method, independence, verifiability.

Organise data in a spreadsheet with consistent columns: date, value, units, source, notes. Save raw and cleaned versions. **Triangulation** uses multiple independent sources on the same point; agreement increases confidence.

**Worked example.** Investigating youth (15–24) unemployment in Greater Melbourne, 2019–2024. Sources: ABS Labour Force release (primary); Jobs and Skills Australia briefings (primary); The Conversation analyses (secondary); an academic study (secondary); a youth-advocacy report (secondary). Triangulate across at least three. Cite each by author, year, access.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS ethical guidelines apply when using data on First Nations communities. The First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria publishes its own data. Ethnic community councils and SBS provide community-specific data. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- One source is not research.
- Primary records; secondary interprets.
- Organise with labels and units.
- Cite by author, year, source.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Google says" is not a citation.
- "Government data is unbiased." Reliable on some questions; not all.
- "Anonymous sources are convenient." Often unverifiable.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why ABS is strong.* Authority, independence, disclosed methodology, regular releases.
- *News on ABS release.* Secondary; journalist interprets primary data.
- *Sign of low authority.* Anonymous author, no methodology, sensational tone.
- *Why is triangulation more than "more sources"?* Sources must be independent and address the same point.
- *Short summary of organising data on Melbourne youth unemployment.* Primary ABS data first, secondary interpretation second; consistent spreadsheet columns; raw and cleaned versions; cite each row.
