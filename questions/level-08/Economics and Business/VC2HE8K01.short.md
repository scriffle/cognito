# VC2HE8K01 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The basic economic problem of scarcity and how it necessitates economic decision-making based on costs and benefits, incentives and opportunity cost.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Wants are unlimited; resources are not. Every choice has a cost. Incentives change behaviour.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **basic economic problem** of **scarcity** and how it forces choice based on **costs and benefits**, **incentives** and **opportunity cost**.
- You should be able to make and evaluate choices like an economist.

## Core content

**Scarcity** means there are not enough resources to satisfy all wants. Every choice means a **trade-off**: what you accept losing to gain something else. **Opportunity cost** is the value of the next-best option you gave up. Decisions weigh **costs** (what you give up) against **benefits** (what you gain), both money and non-money (time, health, relationships). An **incentive** is a reason to act: higher prices encourage suppliers and discourage buyers; rebates encourage solar installation; school grades encourage study.

**Worked example.** A Year 8 student's Saturday. Options: (a) work a 5-hour shift at a Werribee café for $80; (b) play in a basketball tournament; (c) help at the family business. Each has different money, fun, skill and relationship costs and benefits. The opportunity cost of any choice is the best alternative not taken.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have long managed scarcity through seasonal cycles, sustainable harvest and shared responsibility for Country. Migrant families across Melbourne manage scarcity through extended-family support and frugal traditions. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Scarcity makes choice unavoidable.
- Every choice has an opportunity cost.
- Costs and benefits are not only money.
- Incentives change behaviour.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If it is free, it is free." Free in money does not mean free in time.
- "I had no choice." Almost every decision is a choice between alternatives.
- "Opportunity cost is whatever I imagine." It is the next-best real option.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *"Have everything I need" vs "everything I want".* Needs are limited; wants keep growing as choices and information expand.
- *Concert opportunity cost.* The best thing you could have done with the $50 and the time at the concert.
- *Government incentive.* Solar rebates; free public-transport days; school-attendance allowances.
- *Why does non-money cost matter?* Health, family time, sleep and learning shape decisions, often more than money.
- *Short summary of a Saturday choice.* List options, name costs and benefits, identify the opportunity cost, and decide with reasons.
