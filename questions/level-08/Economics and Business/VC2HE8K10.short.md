# VC2HE8K10 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Financial planning, including budgeting and achieving personal financial objectives.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** A budget is choice in writing. Saving habits beat dramatic decisions. Goals shape plans.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about **financial planning**, including **budgeting** and **achieving personal financial objectives**.
- You should be able to take control of money over time.

## Core content

A **budget** is a plan showing expected **income** and **expenses** over a month. Savings = income − expenses. "Pay yourself first" sets aside savings before spending. A **short-term goal** is within months; a **long-term goal** is a year or more. Specific, measurable, time-bound goals are easier to track.

An **emergency fund** is money saved for unexpected costs; even $100–$500 cushions shocks for students. Use free tools like ASIC Moneysmart's budget planner. Stick with one tool for at least three months.

**Worked example.** A Year 8 monthly budget. Income: $80 part-time work + $40 allowance = $120. Expenses: $20 transport, $30 phone, $20 food, $10 streaming, $10 misc = $90. Savings: $30/month. Short-term goal: $200 for school trip in 7 months. Long-term goal: $1,000 for a vehicle in 33 months.

**Cultural context.** ICAN provides culturally appropriate financial counselling. Many First Nations communities practise shared financial planning across extended family. Migrant families often plan across multiple currencies and remittance commitments. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- A budget is choice in writing.
- Income − expenses = savings.
- Short and long term goals.
- Adjust without abandoning.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I will start saving when I earn more." Saving habits build with current income.
- "Budgeting is restrictive." It is choosing where your money goes.
- "Emergency funds are for adults." Even small balances help students.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Written vs mental budget.* Written exposes patterns and forces honesty.
- *Sneaky expense.* Subscriptions, app purchases, school excursions, transport top-ups.
- *Why "save more" fails as a goal.* Vague; no amount, no timeframe, no plan.
- *Emergency-fund event.* Lost phone, urgent travel, bike repair.
- *Short summary of the Year 8 budget.* $120 in, $90 out, $30 saved; short and long term goals with timeframes; one buffer line and monthly review.
