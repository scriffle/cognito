## Core content

**1. What entrepreneurship is.** **Entrepreneurship** is the practice of identifying opportunities, organising resources and accepting risk to create new businesses, products or services. It can happen inside large organisations too. *Quick check:* Why is "starting a business" not the only kind of entrepreneurship?

**2. Habits that help.** Successful entrepreneurs often share: clear problem-solving, customer focus, resilience, willingness to learn, and a small core of trusted relationships. None of these is genetic; all can be practised. *Quick check:* Why is "resilience" different from "ignoring evidence"?

**3. Risk and innovation.** **Risk** is the chance that a venture fails or underperforms. **Innovation** is doing something new that creates value. Entrepreneurs use innovation to manage risk: testing cheaply, learning quickly, adjusting course. *Quick check:* What is one cheap test an entrepreneur could run before launching a product?

**4. Worked example: Clothing the Gaps.** Clothing the Gaps is a Victorian Aboriginal-owned business founded by Sianna and Laura Thompson. It sells apparel with health and First Nations-pride messaging. Profits fund Aboriginal-led health work. Success: revenue growth, brand recognition, community impact, employment of Aboriginal staff. Risks: navigating cultural responsibilities and commercial demands. *Follow the thinking:* identify the opportunity → choose the structure and purpose → manage cultural responsibilities → measure multiple success indicators.

**5. Measures of success.** Profit is one measure. Others include: customer satisfaction, employees retained, community impact, environmental outcomes, cultural contribution, longevity. Different entrepreneurs weight measures differently. *Quick check:* Why is "profit only" too narrow for many Australian entrepreneurs?

**6. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander entrepreneurship.** **Supply Nation** lists Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander-owned businesses. Examples: Welcome to Country (online travel and cultural-tours platform), Bunjil Energy (Aboriginal-led energy investments), Mob in Fashion. Aboriginal entrepreneurs often combine commercial goals with community and cultural responsibilities. *Quick check:* What is one barrier and one strength facing Aboriginal-owned businesses?

**7. Failure as part of the path.** Many successful entrepreneurs have failed earlier. Failing well means learning, recovering and trying again. Australian start-up communities (e.g., LaunchVic, Stone & Chalk) support the cycle. *Quick check:* Why is "fail fast" not the same as "be careless"?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** LaunchVic supports start-ups across the state. Victorian Aboriginal Chamber of Commerce (VACC) and Kinaway Chamber of Commerce Victoria support Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander businesses.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Supply Nation publishes case studies of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander-owned businesses. Indigenous Business Australia (IBA) provides finance and support. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant entrepreneurship is a major part of Victorian small business. The Multicultural Business Ministerial Council and the Ethnic Communities' Council of Victoria support diverse founders.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Entrepreneurs are born, not made." Habits and skills can be learned.
- "Success means a big exit." Many entrepreneurs sustain successful long-term businesses.
- "Aboriginal businesses are only social enterprises." Many are commercial businesses competing fully.
- "Failure equals the end." Many successful entrepreneurs failed earlier and learned from it.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why "starting a business" is not the only kind.* People can be entrepreneurial inside large organisations (intrapreneurship) or in community and cultural ventures.
2. *Resilience vs ignoring evidence.* Resilience keeps going while still learning from data; ignoring evidence is stubbornness.
3. *Cheap test.* Pre-sell a small batch; build a landing page; pilot at a school event; offer a service to friends and family before scaling.
4. *Why "profit only" is too narrow.* It misses community impact, cultural contribution, employment and environmental outcomes.
5. *Aboriginal-owned business barrier and strength.* Barrier: access to capital and procurement networks. Strength: deep community connections and cultural authority.
6. *"Fail fast" vs careless.* Fail fast tests small, learns and adjusts; careless skips the learning step.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Youth lawn-mowing service.* Ask neighbours; price one job; flyer the street; refine the offer.
2. *Brimbank youth-entrepreneur grant.* Clear problem identified; realistic plan; named mentor; community benefit.
3. *Business-mentor habits to encourage.* Customer focus; weekly small experiments; reflective practice.
4. *Promoting entrepreneurship to all.* For: skills useful in many careers. Against: not every student wants to be an entrepreneur; ensure breadth of pathways.
5. *Wadawurrung tourism expansion.* Step 1: keep cultural authority with the Wadawurrung Traditional Owners Aboriginal Corporation. Step 2: scale offerings in line with cultural protocols. Step 3: partner with regional Victoria tourism networks for distribution.
