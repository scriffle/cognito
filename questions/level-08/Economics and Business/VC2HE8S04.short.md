# VC2HE8S04 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Analyse and verify sources of data and information.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Provenance plus method first. Triangulate. Strong claims are specific and sourced.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **analyse and verify** sources of data and information.
- You should be able to grade sources for use.

## Core content

**Provenance** is where a source comes from. **Methodology** is how the information was produced. Five evaluation criteria: authority, currency, method, independence, verifiability. **Bias** is a tendency to favour one side; conflicts of interest should be disclosed and weighed.

**Triangulation** uses independent sources on the same claim. Disagreement is often a clue, not a problem. Visual sources need the same checks; cropped axes, misaligned scales and selective framing can mislead.

**Worked example.** A headline: "Most Australians are running out of money." Source A: bank PR survey of 1,000 customers online. Source B: ABS household financial-stress data. Source C: Productivity Commission research note. Verdict: B and C are more authoritative; A may be useful as an indicator but limited by sample and conflict.

**Cultural context.** Cite Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander authors directly; data should be sourced through community-approved channels (AIATSIS, IBA, Supply Nation). Multilingual fact-checking and ethnic community council reports complement mainstream sources. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Provenance + method first.
- Five criteria sharpen judgement.
- Triangulate to verify.
- Strong claims are specific and sourced.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If the influencer is famous, they must know." Fame is not authority.
- "Anonymous = unbiased." Anonymity removes accountability.
- "Newer is always better." Some claims need long series.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why does method matter as much as author?* A respected author with weak method can still be misleading.
- *Why does disclosure beat secrecy?* Readers can weigh the source themselves.
- *Why do reputable sources disagree?* Different methods or definitions; different time windows.
- *Sweeping phrase to avoid.* "Everyone knows", "always", "never", "the data prove".
- *Short summary of the budget-claim assessment.* ABS and Productivity Commission data weigh more than a bank PR survey on a "running out of money" claim; triangulate and cite.
