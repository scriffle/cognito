# VC2HE8S01 — Economics and Business

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HE8S01. Develop and apply questions to investigate contemporary economic, business, work or financial issues and systems.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Economics and Business (Investigating).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Strong questions narrow a topic into something testable.
  - Economic questions are measurable.
  - Refinement is part of investigation.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Literacy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop and apply questions** to investigate **contemporary economic, business, work or financial issues and systems**.
- You are learning to turn an idea into something you can investigate.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **investigable question**, **scope** and **measure** in your own words.
- Convert a broad topic into three increasingly investigable questions.
- Identify two measures that could answer one question.
- Justify when to refine a question.

## Core content

**1. Topic vs question.** A topic is general ("rent"). A question is specific ("How have median weekly rents in Melbourne's outer suburbs changed between 2019 and 2024, based on REIV data?"). Specificity makes investigation possible. *Quick check:* Why is "I am interested in rent" not yet a question?

**2. Add scope: place, time, actor, measure.** Refining narrows scope. Place: which suburb or LGA? Time: which period? Actor: which group (renters, landlords, councils)? Measure: rents, vacancy rates, complaints? *Quick check:* Which addition usually narrows scope the most?

**3. Operationalise the variable.** **Operationalisation** turns abstract terms into observable measures. "Affordability" might be the percentage of household income spent on rent or rent-to-income ratio. Naming the measure lets others test it. *Quick check:* What is one operational measure of "youth unemployment"?

**4. Worked example: an investigation into youth gig-work.** Initial: Is the gig economy good for young people? Refined v1: How has the share of Year 11–12 students doing gig work changed? Refined v2: How has the share of Victorian Year 11–12 students doing food-delivery gig work changed between 2019 and 2024, based on ABS and union surveys? *Follow the thinking:* draft → narrow scope → operationalise → re-check.

**5. Stakeholders shape questions.** Different stakeholders care about different measures: workers (pay, safety), platforms (revenue), customers (price), regulators (rules). A question can be neutral while still acknowledging stakeholders. *Quick check:* Why does naming stakeholders strengthen a question?

**6. Cost-benefit framing.** Many economics questions are about whether benefits exceed costs across stakeholders. Building this into the question can help. *Quick check:* What costs and benefits would you weigh for a youth-rent-control policy?

**7. When to refine.** Refine when: sources you can access do not match the question; the variable cannot be operationalised; the question has been answered already; bias-checks reveal loaded language. *Quick check:* What is one bias-check question for your draft?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Parliament of Victoria research papers, the Victorian Auditor-General reports, ABS, REIV and the Productivity Commission provide accessible primary sources for student investigation.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Economic questions affecting First Nations communities should follow AIATSIS ethical guidelines, with community priorities shaping the question and consent for data use. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual community surveys and ethnic community councils provide voices and data missing from mainstream sources.

## Memorable takeaways
- Topic ≠ question.
- Place, time, actor, measure.
- Operationalise the variable.
- Stakeholders sharpen focus.
- Refining is part of the work.

## Stretch yourself
Pick a current Victorian or Australian economic issue. Write five versions of a question, from broadest to most refined, naming what you added at each step. 250 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "More words means stronger question." Clarity matters more than length.
- "If the question is interesting, it is investigable." Interest is necessary, not sufficient.
- "Stakeholders are a distraction." They shape what counts as an answer.
- "Indigenous economic topics are off limits." They are valid with appropriate ethics and partnership.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *"Interested in rent" not yet a question.* It names a topic but no specific place, time, measure or actor.
2. *Addition that narrows most.* Adding a measure (e.g., median rent in $/week) often locks scope tighter than place or time.
3. *Operational measure of youth unemployment.* Unemployment rate among 15–24 year olds; participation rate of full-time students; underemployment rate among recent graduates.
4. *Why naming stakeholders strengthens questions.* It clarifies whose costs and benefits count and prevents one-sided framing.
5. *Costs and benefits of youth rent control.* Benefits: affordability, stability. Costs: supply reduction, landlord investment. Net effect depends on design.
6. *Bias-check question.* Does my wording assume a conclusion? Does it leave out any group?

**Try these scenarios**

1. *"Phone plans".* Q1: Are phone plans good value? Q2: How has the cost of unlimited mobile plans changed since 2020? Q3: How has the median monthly cost of unlimited mobile plans from the four largest carriers changed in Melbourne between 2020 and 2024, with adjustments for inflation?
2. *Wyndham small-business research.* What products and services are demanded by Year 8 students? How do local Wyndham food trucks set prices? What barriers do Year 8 students face in starting micro-businesses?
3. *Think-tank refinement.* Define the place; define the time; identify the data source before writing the final question.
4. *Sensitive financial research.* Reframe to focus on systemic factors, ensure consent, anonymise data, partner with community organisations.
5. *Wadawurrung economic development.* Step 1: contact the Wadawurrung Traditional Owners Aboriginal Corporation. Step 2: co-design the question. Step 3: ensure outputs benefit the community first.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Topic: cost of living.* Q1: Is cost of living too high? Q2: Has cost of living risen since 2019? Q3: How has cost of living in Greater Geelong risen between 2019 and 2024? Q4: How has the CPI for Geelong households risen between 2019 and 2024, based on ABS data, compared with wage growth? Q5 (refined): How has the gap between CPI growth and wage growth in Greater Geelong widened or narrowed between 2019 and 2024, based on ABS time-series data and Geelong-specific surveys?
