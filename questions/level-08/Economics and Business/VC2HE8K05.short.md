# VC2HE8K05 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The reasons people work and the different ways Australians can derive an income.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** People work for many reasons. Income has many forms. Unpaid work matters.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **reasons people work** and the **different ways Australians can derive an income**.
- You should be able to see work as more than a pay packet.

## Core content

Reasons to work include income, meaning, identity, social connection, skill development, contributing to community and family support. Income sources: **wage** (per hour or day), **salary** (annual fixed), **self-employed income** (sole trader, contractor), **transfer payments** (Centrelink, age pension, family tax benefits), **investment income** (interest, dividends, rent), **royalties** for creative work. Unpaid work (caring, parenting, volunteering) produces real economic value but does not show up in GDP.

**Worked example.** A Vietnamese-Australian family in Footscray. One parent earns a teacher's salary. Another runs a small grocery as a sole trader. A grandparent receives the age pension. A teenager has a part-time café wage. The family combines paid work, self-employed work, transfer payments, youth wages, and unpaid caring at home.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander work includes land management, ranger programs, cultural education, arts and health. IBA and the Indigenous Land and Sea Corporation support employment. Migrant work patterns vary by arrival pathway. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Many reasons to work.
- Wages, salaries, self-employment, transfers.
- Unpaid work has real value.
- Work shapes identity.

## Watch out for these traps
- "If you are not paid, it is not work." Unpaid work is essential.
- "Self-employment is freedom." It is also risk; many earn less than employees.
- "Transfer payments are not earned." They are funded by taxes and support people across life stages.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Wage vs salary.* Wages: per hour or day, often shift work. Salary: fixed annual, often professional roles.
- *Why is self-employed income less stable?* Demand varies; sick or holiday days are unpaid; tax and super are self-managed.
- *Why does unpaid work matter to the economy?* Carers and parents do essential work that is missing from headline economic numbers.
- *Why do we celebrate work milestones?* They mark identity, skill, and contribution to community.
- *Short summary of the Footscray household.* Paid salary, self-employed income, age pension, casual youth wages and unpaid caring combine to support the household.
