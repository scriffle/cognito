# VC2HE8K04 — Economics and Business (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Entrepreneurship and the link to business success, including Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander-owned businesses and entrepreneurs.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Economics and Business.
- **Big ideas:** Opportunity plus action plus risk. Success has many measures. Aboriginal entrepreneurship is a growing part of the economy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about **entrepreneurship** and its **link to business success**, including **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander-owned businesses**.
- You should be able to recognise entrepreneurial habits and stories.

## Core content

**Entrepreneurship** identifies opportunities, organises resources and accepts risk to create new ventures. **Innovation** creates new value; **risk** is the chance of failure. Successful entrepreneurs often share clear problem-solving, customer focus, resilience and willingness to learn. Success has many measures: profit, customer satisfaction, employee retention, community impact, cultural contribution.

**Supply Nation** lists Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander-owned businesses across Australia. **Indigenous Business Australia (IBA)** provides finance and support. The **Kinaway Chamber of Commerce Victoria** supports First Nations businesses in Victoria.

**Worked example.** Clothing the Gaps is a Victorian Aboriginal-owned business founded by Sianna and Laura Thompson. It sells apparel with health and First Nations-pride messaging; profits fund Aboriginal-led health work. Success spans revenue growth, brand recognition, community impact and Aboriginal employment. Risks: navigating cultural responsibilities and commercial demands together.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal entrepreneurs often combine commercial goals with community and cultural responsibilities. Migrant entrepreneurship is a major part of Victorian small business, supported by the Multicultural Business Ministerial Council. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Opportunity plus action plus risk.
- Habits matter more than personality.
- Success has many measures.
- Failure is information.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Entrepreneurs are born, not made." Habits and skills can be learned.
- "Aboriginal businesses are only social enterprises." Many are commercial businesses competing fully.
- "Failure equals the end." Many successful entrepreneurs failed earlier.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is "starting a business" not the only kind?* Intrapreneurship within organisations; community and cultural ventures.
- *Resilience vs ignoring evidence.* Resilience keeps going while learning; ignoring is stubbornness.
- *Cheap test.* Pre-sell a small batch; build a landing page; pilot at a school event.
- *Aboriginal-owned business barrier and strength.* Capital and procurement access (barrier); deep community connections and cultural authority (strength).
- *Short summary of Clothing the Gaps.* Victorian Aboriginal-owned apparel business with profit reinvested in health, measured by revenue, employment and community impact; balancing cultural responsibilities and commercial growth.
