## Core content

**1. The legal framework.** The **Australian Consumer Law (ACL)** sits in the Competition and Consumer Act 2010. Administered by the ACCC nationally and Consumer Affairs Victoria in this state. It covers misleading conduct, consumer guarantees, unfair terms and product safety. *Quick check:* Why is "buyer beware" not enough in modern markets?

**2. Consumer guarantees.** Goods must be of **acceptable quality**, **fit for purpose** and **match description**. Services must be **delivered with due care and skill**. These cannot be excluded by contract. *Quick check:* Can a store sell you a faulty laptop and refuse a refund "because the receipt says no refunds"?

**3. Consumer responsibilities.** Pay agreed prices, behave respectfully, follow product instructions, keep receipts, raise concerns promptly. Fairness is a two-way street. *Quick check:* What is one responsibility a customer has when returning a product?

**4. Business responsibilities.** Deliver what was promised, be honest in advertising, disclose key terms, handle complaints fairly, keep customer data private under the Privacy Act 1988. *Quick check:* What is one consequence of misleading advertising for a business?

**5. Worked example: a phone-bill dispute.** A consumer in Brunswick signs up for a phone plan that promises "unlimited data" but is throttled after 30 GB without clear disclosure. ACL protections: misleading conduct (s 18 of the ACL); the consumer can seek remedy via the telco's complaints process, then the Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman. Business responsibility: clear disclosure. Consumer responsibility: read the key terms and raise concerns promptly. *Follow the thinking:* identify the issue → name the right → use the right channel → keep records.

**6. Ethics beyond the law.** Ethical expectations often exceed legal duties: paying suppliers on time, treating staff well, sourcing ethically, considering environmental impact, respecting cultural protocols. *Quick check:* Why does ethics often go further than the law?

**7. Where to seek help.** Consumer Affairs Victoria, ACCC, Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman, Australian Financial Complaints Authority, Community Legal Centres. Most issues are resolved before formal complaints. *Quick check:* What is the first step in a consumer dispute?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Consumer Affairs Victoria publishes plain-language guides on rights and responsibilities and runs a complaints process. The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) covers federal matters.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** The Indigenous Consumer Assistance Network (ICAN) provides culturally appropriate consumer support. Cases of misleading sales practices targeting Aboriginal communities have led to ACCC enforcement actions. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual consumer-rights information and translated complaint forms support migrant and refugee communities across Melbourne.

## Watch out for these traps
- "No refunds" signs override the law. They don't.
- "If you signed, you have no rights." Unfair-terms law can override one-sided clauses.
- "Small businesses are exempt." Most ACL provisions apply to all businesses.
- "Complaints are pointless." Most are resolved at the first step.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why "buyer beware" is not enough.* Power and information are uneven; modern law protects against misleading conduct.
2. *Faulty laptop "no refunds".* No. ACL guarantees override store policies.
3. *Customer responsibility on returns.* Take care of the product; return within a reasonable time; bring proof of purchase if you have it.
4. *Consequence of misleading advertising.* Investigation; remedies for affected consumers; fines and reputational damage.
5. *Why ethics goes beyond law.* Law sets minimum; ethics aims at higher standards (e.g., on environment, fairness, social impact).
6. *First step in a dispute.* Contact the business directly with the issue and what you want.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Faulty online headphones.* Contact the seller with photos and proof of purchase; request a remedy (repair, refund, replacement); if refused, escalate to Consumer Affairs Victoria.
2. *Casey consumer-rights night.* Online shopping rights; phone-and-internet plans; financial scams.
3. *Café complaint handling.* Listen and acknowledge; offer a remedy where appropriate; record the issue for future improvement.
4. *Emissions disclosure debate.* For: informed choice; reputational alignment. Against: complexity, cost. Both sides can be addressed with standardised reporting.
5. *Vietnamese-speaking family bank dispute.* Step 1: contact the bank's complaints unit. Step 2: ask for interpreter support. Step 3: escalate to AFCA with the help of a community legal centre.

Consumer right: accurate information. Business responsibility: honest advertising. Outcome: enforceable undertakings, refunds, fines. Source: ACCC media release.
