# VC2M8N03 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M8N03. Convert between fractions and terminating or recurring decimals, using digital tools as appropriate.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Fractions and decimals are two ways of writing the same rational number.
  - A fraction terminates when its denominator (in simplest form) has only factors of 2 and 5.
  - A fraction recurs when division produces a repeating remainder pattern.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Critical and Creative Thinking; Numeracy; Digital Literacy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **convert fractions to terminating or recurring decimals** and to **convert decimals (including recurring) back to fractions**.
- You are learning why some fractions terminate and others repeat forever.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Convert any fraction to its decimal form by division.
- Identify whether a fraction will terminate or recur before dividing.
- Use dot notation to write recurring decimals correctly.
- Convert a terminating decimal to a fraction in simplest form.
- Convert a recurring decimal to a fraction using an algebraic method.

## Core content

**1. Fractions to decimals by division.** To convert a fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator. 3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375. This decimal stops — it **terminates**. 1/3 = 1 ÷ 3 = 0.333… — this decimal repeats forever and is called a **recurring** decimal. *Quick check:* Convert 5/8 to a decimal. Does it terminate or recur?

**2. Terminating decimals — why they stop.** A fraction in simplest form terminates if and only if its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5. Why? Our number system is base 10 = 2 × 5. Denominators built from 2s and 5s divide evenly into powers of 10. Examples: 1/4 (4 = 2²) → 0.25; 3/20 (20 = 2² × 5) → 0.15. *Quick check:* Without dividing, will 7/40 terminate? Explain.

**3. Recurring decimals — why they repeat.** If the denominator has a prime factor other than 2 or 5, the division will never end neatly. The remainders eventually repeat, creating a cycle. 1/7 = 0.142857142857… with a repeating block of 6 digits. We write this as 0.1̇4̇2̇8̇5̇7̇ (dots over the first and last digits of the repeating block). *Quick check:* Convert 2/11 to a decimal and identify the repeating block.

**4. Dot notation for recurring decimals.** Place a dot over the first and last digit of the shortest repeating block. 1/3 = 0.3̇ (one digit repeats). 1/6 = 0.16̇ (only the 6 repeats; the 1 does not). 5/12 = 0.416̇ (the 6 repeats). *Quick check:* Write 4/9 as a recurring decimal with correct dot notation.

**5. Terminating decimals back to fractions.** Read the place value: 0.375 = 375/1000. Simplify: divide both by 125 → 3/8. Shortcut: count the decimal places (3), put the digits over 10³ = 1000, simplify. *Quick check:* Convert 0.24 to a fraction in simplest form.

**6. Recurring decimals back to fractions — algebraic method.**
Convert 0.7̇2̇ (0.727272…) to a fraction.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Let x = 0.727272…
- The repeating block has 2 digits, so multiply by 100: 100x = 72.727272…
- Subtract: 100x − x = 72.7272… − 0.7272… = 72.
- 99x = 72, so x = 72/99 = 8/11.

**7. Worked example — mixed conversions.**
(a) Convert 5/6 to a decimal: 5 ÷ 6 = 0.83̇. (b) Will 11/16 terminate? 16 = 2⁴ — only factor of 2 — yes. 11 ÷ 16 = 0.6875. (c) Convert 0.1̇8̇ to a fraction: let x = 0.1818…; 100x = 18.1818…; 99x = 18; x = 18/99 = 2/11.

*Quick check:* Convert 0.6̇ to a fraction.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG) displays batting averages to two or three decimal places — these are fractions (runs ÷ innings) converted to terminating or recurring decimals and then rounded. The ASX (Australian Securities Exchange) quotes share prices in decimal dollars, converted from fractional cent movements. VicRoads fuel-price boards show decimals (e.g. 178.9 cents per litre) that represent fractions of a cent.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Proportional sharing of resources — dividing a harvest among families — produces fractions that in Western notation may terminate or recur. Understanding these conversions helps students read statistical reports about First Nations communities, such as those published by the Yoorrook Justice Commission, where data is often presented in decimal percentages derived from fractional proportions. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Currency conversions between the Australian dollar and currencies used by Melbourne's diaspora communities (Vietnamese dong, Indian rupee, Chinese yuan) often produce recurring decimals. Understanding that these decimals represent exact fractions builds confidence in financial calculations across all backgrounds.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You score 17 out of 23 on a quiz. Convert to a decimal (round to 3 decimal places). Is the exact decimal terminating or recurring? Explain why by examining the denominator.
2. **Civic.** A council report says 5/12 of residents use public transport. Convert to a decimal with correct dot notation. A journalist rounds this to 0.42. By how much does the rounded value differ from the exact value?
3. **Vocational.** A machinist in Dandenong sets a lathe to cut pieces 0.375 inches thick. Express this as a fraction of an inch in simplest form. Then convert 7/16 inch to a decimal for the digital readout.
4. **Ethical.** A student says "0.999… is almost 1 but not quite." Use the algebraic method to convert 0.9̇ to a fraction. What do you find? Discuss whether "almost" is the right word.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A class investigates which unit fractions (1/n) from n = 2 to n = 12 produce terminating decimals and which produce recurring. List both groups and identify the pattern in the denominators.

## Memorable takeaways
- Every fraction is either a terminating or a recurring decimal — no other type.
- Terminates when the simplified denominator has only factors of 2 and/or 5.
- Recurs when the denominator has any other prime factor.
- Dot notation marks the repeating block — dots over the first and last repeating digits.
- The algebraic method (multiply, subtract, solve) converts any recurring decimal back to a fraction.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Forgetting to simplify the fraction.** 375/1000 is not the final answer — simplify to 3/8.
- **Placing dots incorrectly.** In 1/6 = 0.16̇, only the 6 repeats. The dot goes on the 6 only, not on the 1.
- **Confusing "non-terminating" with "irrational."** Recurring decimals are infinite but they are still rational (they can be written as fractions). Only non-repeating infinite decimals (like π) are irrational.
- **Rounding a recurring decimal and calling it exact.** 1/3 ≠ 0.33 or 0.333. The exact value is 0.3̇.
