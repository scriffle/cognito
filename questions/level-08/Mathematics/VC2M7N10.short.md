# VC2M7N10 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving rational numbers and percentages, including financial contexts such as 'best buys'; formulate problems, choosing representations and efficient calculation strategies, designing algorithms and using digital tools as appropriate; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, justifying choices made about the representation.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Mathematical modelling turns real situations into solvable calculations. Percentages and unit rates enable fair comparison. Choosing strategy matters as much as arithmetic.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **use mathematical modelling** to solve practical problems involving rational numbers, percentages and financial contexts.
- You should be able to formulate, solve, interpret and communicate.

## Core content

The **modelling cycle**: Real problem → Formulate (identify variables, choose operations) → Solve (calculate) → Interpret (what does the number mean?) → Communicate (explain, justify, include units).

**Percentage of a quantity.** 15% of $240: 0.15 × 240 = $36. **Percentage decrease:** a $80 item at 25% off = 80 × 0.75 = $60. **Percentage increase:** $150 with 10% rise = 150 × 1.10 = $165.

**Unit pricing (best buys).** Compare by finding price per unit. Cereal A: 500 g for $5.50 = $0.011/g. Cereal B: 750 g for $7.80 = $0.0104/g. Cereal B is the better buy.

**Designing an algorithm.** For best-buy problems: (1) list products and sizes, (2) calculate unit price for each, (3) compare, (4) state and justify the best buy.

**Worked example.** Pack A: 6 cupcakes for $9.00 = $1.50 each. Pack B: 10 for $14.00 = $1.40 each. Pack B saves $0.10 per cupcake — a 6.7% saving. Communicated: "Pack B is approximately 6.7% cheaper per cupcake."

**Victorian and cultural context.** Australian Consumer Law requires unit-price labels on supermarket shelves. Aboriginal community-controlled organisations publish annual reports using percentages to communicate financial data. Market stalls across Melbourne present real comparison-shopping contexts daily.

## Memorable takeaways
- Modelling = formulate → solve → interpret → communicate.
- Unit price = total price ÷ quantity.
- Percentage change = (difference ÷ original) × 100.
- A number without units and context is not a finished answer.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Bigger packs are always better value." Not always — check unit price.
- "50% extra free = 50% off." 50% extra drops unit price by 33%; 50% off drops it by 50%.
- "20% up then 20% down returns to the start." $100 → $120 → $96. It does not.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *35% of 600.* 210.
- *$45 shirt, 20% off.* $36.
- *Juice A: 2 L/$4.20 vs Juice B: 3 L/$5.70.* A = $2.10/L, B = $1.90/L. Juice B is cheaper.
- *Why state units and context?* So the reader knows what the number means and can act on it.
- *Solving vs interpreting.* Solving gives a number; interpreting explains what it means in the real situation.
