# VC2M7A06 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Manipulate formulas involving several variables using digital tools, and describe the effect of systematic variation in the values of the variables.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Algebra).
- **Big ideas:** A formula links variables. Change one systematically, hold the rest constant, and describe what happens. Spreadsheets make this fast.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **substitute into, rearrange and explore formulas with several variables** using spreadsheets and other digital tools.
- You should be able to describe the effect of changing one variable at a time.

## Core content

A **formula** is an equation linking several **variables**. In A = l × w, the three variables are area, length and width. The variable alone on one side is the **subject**. To evaluate, substitute known values: if l = 8 and w = 5, then A = 40.

To **rearrange**, do the same operation to both sides. Making w the subject of A = l × w: divide both sides by l to get w = A ÷ l.

**Systematic variation** means changing one variable in regular steps while holding the others constant. In A = l × w with w fixed at 5, let l run from 1 to 10. A increases by 5 each step — a linear relationship. A **spreadsheet** lets you test dozens of values instantly: enter l-values in one column, type the formula in the next and drag down.

**Worked example — speed, distance, time.** d = s × t. Hold s = 60 km/h and vary t from 1 to 6: d goes 60, 120, 180, 240, 300, 360. Each extra hour adds 60 km — constant increase, straight-line graph. Rearrange for t: t = d ÷ s. If d = 210 and s = 60, then t = 3.5 hours.

After varying, describe the effect in words: "When t increases by 1 hour (s fixed at 60), d increases by 60 km." State whether the relationship is linear or non-linear. For A = πr², increasing r by 1 each time gives non-constant jumps in A — that relationship is non-linear.

**Victorian context.** VicRoads speed-distance-time formulas are embedded in learner-driver education. The Bureau of Meteorology uses multi-variable formulas to model Melbourne's weather. Gunditjmara eel-trap engineering at Budj Bim involves relationships between water flow, channel width and trap capacity — variables shifting with seasonal rainfall. ## Memorable takeaways
- A formula links variables — change one, watch the others respond.
- Rearranging swaps the subject: what you do to one side, do to the other.
- Systematic variation means one variable at a time, everything else constant.
- Spreadsheets make "what if?" fast and visual.

## Watch out for these traps
- Forgetting order of operations when substituting — in v = u + at, calculate a × t first.
- Changing two variables at once — you cannot tell which caused the change.
- Assuming a formula perfectly models reality at all values.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Subject of v = u + at.* v.
- *Find v when u = 10, a = 3, t = 4.* v = 10 + 12 = 22.
- *Rearrange v = u + at for t.* t = (v − u) ÷ a.
- *Doubling w in A = l × w.* Every A value doubles — doubling a factor doubles the product.
- *Is the increase in A = πr² constant?* No. The jumps grow as r increases — non-linear.
