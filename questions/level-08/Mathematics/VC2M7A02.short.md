# VC2M7A02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Apply the associative, commutative and distributive laws to aid mental and written computation, and formulate algebraic expressions using constants, variables, operations and brackets.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Number laws let you rearrange and regroup calculations. The same laws work with letters as with numbers. Writing algebraic expressions generalises arithmetic.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **apply the associative, commutative and distributive laws** to simplify computation.
- You should be able to formulate and simplify algebraic expressions using constants, variables, operations and brackets.

## Core content

The **commutative law** says you can swap the order of addition or multiplication: *a + b = b + a* and *a × b = b × a*. This does not work for subtraction or division.

The **associative law** says you can regroup addition or multiplication: *(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)*. Example: (4 × 25) × 7 = 100 × 7 = 700 — much faster.

The **distributive law** says multiplication spreads over addition: *a(b + c) = ab + ac*. Example: 6 × 53 = 6 × 50 + 6 × 3 = 318. This is the most powerful law for mental maths and for algebra.

**From numbers to algebra.** The same laws apply to letters. *3x + 5x = (3 + 5)x = 8x* uses the distributive law in reverse (collecting like terms). Brackets matter: *3(n + 4) = 3n + 12*, which is different from *3n + 4*.

**Worked example — tram fares.** Zone 1 costs $a, Zone 2 costs $b. Group 1: 4 Zone 1, 3 Zone 2 → *4a + 3b*. Group 2: 2 Zone 1, 5 Zone 2 → *2a + 5b*. Combined: *(4a + 3b) + (2a + 5b) = 6a + 8b*. Commutative law lets you swap terms, then collect like terms.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal trade networks across south-eastern Australia involved systematic exchange — greenstone axes from Wurundjeri Country traded for possum-skin cloaks, shell and ochre — encoding proportional and additive reasoning. Melbourne's myki fares, Queen Victoria Market pricing and AFL statistics all rely on these laws in everyday computation.

## Memorable takeaways
- Commutative: swap order (+ and × only).
- Associative: regroup (+ and × only).
- Distributive: multiply across a bracket.
- Collecting like terms uses the distributive law in reverse.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Subtraction is commutative." It is not: 10 − 3 ≠ 3 − 10.
- "3(n + 4) is the same as 3n + 4." You must distribute: 3n + 12.
- "Like terms need the same number." They need the same variable part.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Does 12 − 5 equal 5 − 12?* No — subtraction is not commutative.
- *Regroup 5 + 47 + 15.* (5 + 15) + 47 = 67.
- *7 × 98 distributively.* 7(100 − 2) = 700 − 14 = 686.
- *Simplify 4y + 9y.* 13y.
- *Expand 4(x + 7).* 4x + 28.
