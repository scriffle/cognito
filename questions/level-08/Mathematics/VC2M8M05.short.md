# VC2M8M05 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Recognise and use rates to solve problems involving the comparison of two related quantities of different units of measure.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** A rate compares two quantities with different units. Rates are everywhere: speed, pay, price per kilogram. Understanding rates lets you compare, convert and make decisions.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **recognise and use rates** to solve problems comparing two quantities with **different units**.
- You should be able to calculate unit rates and use them for comparison and conversion.

## Core content

A **rate** compares two quantities with **different units** — the word "per" signals a rate. Speed (km/h), price ($/kg), flow (L/min) are all rates. A **ratio** compares quantities with the *same* unit.

To find a **unit rate**, divide: 240 km in 3 hours → 240 ÷ 3 = 80 km/h. Once you know the rate, find missing quantities by multiplying or dividing: distance = speed × time; cost = price rate × quantity.

**Comparing rates:** Express each as a unit rate. Shop A: $7.00 for 2 kg = $3.50/kg. Shop B: $9.90 for 3 kg = $3.30/kg. Shop B is cheaper.

**Converting rates:** 60 km/h → m/s: multiply by 1000 (km to m), divide by 3600 (h to s) ≈ 16.7 m/s.

**Worked example.** Two households track water use. Household A: 1 400 L for 4 people = 350 L/person/week. Household B: 1 050 L for 3 people = 350 L/person/week. Same rate — total litres alone is misleading without per-person comparison.

**Victorian context.** VicRoads posts speed limits in km/h. Melbourne Water sets household targets in litres per person per day. The Transport Accident Commission uses speed rates for stopping-distance calculations. Cultural burning requires burn-rate planning (hectares per day).

## Memorable takeaways
- A rate compares different-unit quantities — look for "per."
- Unit rate = first quantity ÷ second quantity.
- To compare fairly, convert to the same unit rate.
- Always write both units (e.g. km/h, not just "80").

## Watch out for these traps
- Confusing rate and ratio — rates have different units, ratios have the same unit.
- Dividing in the wrong order — speed = distance ÷ time, not the reverse.
- Forgetting to convert units before comparing — you cannot compare km/h to m/s directly.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Is "$4.50 per litre" a rate or ratio?* Rate — dollars and litres are different units.
- *Speed for 350 km in 5 hours.* 350 ÷ 5 = 70 km/h.
- *Water flow at 15 L/min for 8 minutes.* 15 × 8 = 120 L.
- *Convert 5 m/s to km/h.* 5 × 3600 ÷ 1000 = 18 km/h.
- *Fuel for 350 km at 8 L/100 km.* 8 × 350 ÷ 100 = 28 L.
