# VC2M7N03 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Find equivalent representations of rational numbers and represent positive and negative rational numbers and mixed numbers on a number line.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** A rational number can be written as a fraction, decimal or percentage. Negative rationals extend the number line left of zero. Placing numbers accurately builds number sense.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **find equivalent representations of rational numbers** and to **place positive and negative rationals on a number line**.
- You should be able to convert between fractions, decimals and percentages and order rational numbers.

## Core content

A **rational number** is any number that can be written as a/b where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0. This includes whole numbers, fractions, terminating decimals and repeating decimals.

**Equivalent fractions** represent the same value: 2/4 = 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%. To convert: fraction → decimal (divide), decimal → percentage (× 100), percentage → fraction (write over 100, simplify).

A **mixed number** like 2 3/4 combines a whole number and a fraction. It equals the improper fraction 11/4 because 2 × 4 + 3 = 11. Both represent 2.75.

**Negative rational numbers** sit left of zero on the number line. Further left means smaller: −2.5 < −1.3 < 0 < 0.7.

**Worked example.** Place −1.5, −3/4, 0, 2/3, 1.25 and 2 1/4 on a number line. Convert to decimals: −1.5, −0.75, 0, 0.667, 1.25, 2.25. Mark intervals and place each point. Check order: −1.5 < −0.75 < 0 < 0.667 < 1.25 < 2.25 ✓.

Different forms suit different contexts. Fractions stay exact (1/3 beats 0.333...). Decimals are easy to compare. Percentages communicate proportions clearly.

**Victorian and cultural context.** The Bureau of Meteorology reports temperatures as positive and negative decimals daily. First Nations seasonal calendars divide the year into unequal parts — an intuitive application of fractional thinking. Market traders at Queen Victoria Market use fractions, decimals and percentages interchangeably.

## Memorable takeaways
- Rational number = integer ÷ non-zero integer.
- Fractions, decimals and percentages are three faces of one value.
- Negative numbers sit left of zero — further left means smaller.
- Convert to a common form for easy ordering.

## Watch out for these traps
- "−0.5 < −0.75 because 5 < 75." Wrong — on a number line, −0.5 is to the right of −0.75, so −0.5 is larger.
- "1/3 = 0.33." That is rounded. The exact decimal is 0.333... (repeating).
- "Mixed numbers and improper fractions are different amounts." They are the same value: 2 3/4 = 11/4.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Is −2.5 rational?* Yes — it can be written as −5/2.
- *Three fractions equivalent to 3/5.* 6/10, 9/15, 12/20.
- *Convert 7/20.* 0.35 = 35%.
- *Convert 3 2/5 to an improper fraction.* 17/5.
- *Which is larger, −3/4 or −1/2?* −1/2, because it is closer to zero.
