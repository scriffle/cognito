# VC2M8M04 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M8M04. Solve problems involving time and duration, including using 12- and 24-hour time across multiple time zones.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Measurement).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Time is measured in non-decimal units — 60 seconds per minute, 60 minutes per hour, 24 hours per day.
  - 12-hour and 24-hour formats represent the same moments differently.
  - Time zones mean the same instant has different clock readings in different places.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Intercultural Capability; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **solve problems involving time and duration**, including converting between 12- and 24-hour time and working across multiple time zones.
- You are learning that time calculations require careful attention to the base-60 system and zone offsets.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Convert between 12-hour (am/pm) and 24-hour time.
- Calculate duration (elapsed time) between two times, including across midnight.
- Add and subtract time intervals.
- Solve problems involving Australian and international time zones.

## Core content

**1. 12-hour and 24-hour time.** In 12-hour time, the day splits into am (midnight to noon) and pm (noon to midnight). In 24-hour time, hours run 00:00 to 23:59. Conversions: 1:00 pm = 13:00, 9:45 am = 09:45, midnight = 00:00, noon = 12:00. *Quick check:* Convert 3:27 pm to 24-hour time.

**2. Calculating duration.** Duration = end time − start time. From 09:15 to 14:50: 14:50 − 09:15 = 5 hours 35 minutes. When subtraction gives negative minutes, borrow 1 hour (= 60 min). From 08:10 to 11:45: 11:45 − 08:10 = 3 h 35 min. *Quick check:* A movie starts at 7:40 pm and finishes at 10:05 pm. How long is it?

**3. Duration across midnight.** Split into two parts: time before midnight + time after. From 22:30 to 02:15 the next day: 22:30 to 00:00 = 1 h 30 min, 00:00 to 02:15 = 2 h 15 min. Total = 3 h 45 min. *Quick check:* A night shift runs from 11:00 pm to 6:30 am. How long is it?

**4. Adding and subtracting time intervals.** Time uses base 60, not base 10. 2 h 45 min + 1 h 30 min = 3 h 75 min = 4 h 15 min (convert 75 min to 1 h 15 min). Similarly: 5 h 10 min − 2 h 40 min = 4 h 70 min − 2 h 40 min = 2 h 30 min. *Quick check:* Add 3 hours 50 minutes and 2 hours 25 minutes.

**5. Australian time zones.** Australia has three main time zones (in standard time): AEST (UTC+10, eastern states including Victoria), ACST (UTC+9:30, SA and NT), AWST (UTC+8, WA). During daylight saving: AEDT = UTC+11, ACDT = UTC+10:30. When it is 3:00 pm AEDT in Melbourne, it is 2:30 pm ACDT in Adelaide and 12:00 pm AWST in Perth. *Quick check:* It is 9:00 am in Melbourne (AEDT). What time is it in Perth (AWST)?

**6. Worked example: scheduling an international video call.**

A Victorian school arranges a video call with a partner school in Tokyo (UTC+9) and London (UTC+0). Melbourne is on AEDT (UTC+11). They want to call at 4:00 pm Melbourne time.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Melbourne 4:00 pm AEDT = 16:00 UTC+11.
- Convert to UTC: 16:00 − 11 = 05:00 UTC.
- Tokyo (UTC+9): 05:00 + 9 = 14:00 = 2:00 pm. ✓ (school hours)
- London (UTC+0): 05:00 = 5:00 am. ✗ (too early)
- Adjust: try Melbourne 8:00 pm (20:00 AEDT) → UTC 09:00 → Tokyo 18:00 (6 pm) → London 09:00 (9 am). Better for London, but late for Tokyo.
- Compromise: Melbourne 5:00 pm → UTC 06:00 → Tokyo 15:00 (3 pm) ✓ → London 06:00 (6 am). Still early for London — time-zone challenges require trade-offs.

**7. Daylight saving and date-line effects.** Daylight saving shifts clocks forward 1 hour in spring and back in autumn (in Victoria: first Sunday in October to first Sunday in April). The International Date Line (roughly 180° longitude) means crossing it changes the date. These add complexity to scheduling. *Quick check:* If daylight saving ends in Melbourne on 6 April and clocks go back 1 hour, what happens to the time difference between Melbourne and Perth?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** V/Line and Metro Trains Melbourne use 24-hour timetables. The Australian Open tennis (Melbourne Park) broadcasts to multiple time zones — scheduling matches for global TV audiences involves the calculations above. Melbourne Airport departures boards show both local and destination times.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** First Nations seasonal calendars — such as the Wurundjeri seven-season calendar or the D'harawal calendar from south-eastern Australia — mark time through environmental indicators (flowering, animal behaviour, star positions) rather than clock hours, representing a fundamentally different but equally valid approach to tracking duration and cycles. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Families with connections to other countries navigate time zones daily for calls home. Victorian students with family in India (UTC+5:30), China (UTC+8), the Pacific Islands (UTC+12/+13) and the Middle East (UTC+3/+4) solve real time-zone problems regularly.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You catch the 07:48 train from Flinders Street. The journey takes 1 hour 23 minutes. What time do you arrive? Express in both 12-hour and 24-hour format.
2. **Civic.** The City of Wyndham schedules a council meeting from 6:30 pm to 9:15 pm. A councillor needs to leave 45 minutes early. What time does she leave? How long was she at the meeting?
3. **Vocational.** A nurse at the Royal Melbourne Hospital starts a night shift at 22:00 and finishes at 06:30 the next morning. Calculate the shift duration. If she takes a 30-minute break, how many hours does she work?
4. **Ethical.** A company schedules a mandatory online meeting at 9:00 am London time (UTC+0). Employees in Melbourne (AEDT, UTC+11) and Los Angeles (UTC−8) must attend. Calculate the local time for each city and discuss whether this is fair.
5. **Cross-cultural.** Your family plans a video call with relatives in Delhi (UTC+5:30), Auckland (UTC+13 during NZDT) and Melbourne (AEDT, UTC+11). Find a 30-minute window between 7:00 am and 10:00 pm in all three cities.

## Memorable takeaways
- 24-hour time: add 12 to pm hours (except 12 pm = 12:00); midnight = 00:00.
- Duration: subtract start from end; borrow 60 minutes if needed.
- Across midnight: split into before and after midnight, then add.
- Time zones: find the UTC offset for each location and add/subtract from UTC.
- Daylight saving changes offsets by 1 hour — check the dates.

## Stretch yourself
A flight departs Melbourne (AEDT, UTC+11) at 23:45 on Wednesday and arrives in Los Angeles (PST, UTC−8) at 18:30 on the same Wednesday (local time). Calculate the flight duration (hint: convert both times to UTC first). The return flight departs Los Angeles at 23:00 Wednesday (PST) and the flight duration is the same. ## Watch out for these traps
- "I subtracted times like decimals." 14:50 − 09:15 ≠ 5.35 hours. Minutes are base 60: 50 − 15 = 35 minutes.
- "I forgot to account for daylight saving." Always check whether DST is in effect for the dates in question. Melbourne shifts between AEST (+10) and AEDT (+11).
- "I added time zones instead of subtracting." To find UTC from local time, subtract the offset (e.g. 16:00 AEDT − 11 = 05:00 UTC). To find local time from UTC, add the offset.
- "Midnight is 24:00." Standard notation uses 00:00 for midnight starting a new day. 24:00 is sometimes used to mean "end of the previous day" but is not standard.
