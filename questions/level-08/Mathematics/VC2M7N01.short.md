# VC2M7N01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Describe the relationship between perfect square numbers and square roots, and use squares of numbers and square roots of perfect square numbers to solve problems.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** A perfect square is an integer times itself. Square roots reverse squaring. Recognising square numbers helps you estimate and solve problems.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **describe the relationship between perfect squares and square roots** and to use them in problems.
- You should be able to list perfect squares, find square roots and estimate roots of non-perfect squares.

## Core content

A **perfect square** is a whole number that results from multiplying an integer by itself: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144. When you write 7², you mean 7 × 7 = 49.

The **square root** undoes squaring. If 8² = 64, then √64 = 8. Squaring and square-rooting are **inverse operations** — each reverses the other, like addition and subtraction.

Square roots solve area problems. A square garden bed with area 36 m² has side length √36 = 6 m. A square mosaic of 225 tiles has √225 = 15 tiles per row.

**Worked example.** A square herb garden has area 196 m². Side = √196. Since 14² = 196, the side length is 14 m.

**Estimating non-perfect-square roots.** Where is √50? Since 7² = 49 and 8² = 64, √50 is between 7 and 8 — closer to 7. Refining: 7.1² = 50.41, so √50 ≈ 7.07.

**Victorian and cultural context.** AMSI in Melbourne publishes free square-number resources. First Nations design traditions, including Gunditjmara stone-house foundations at Budj Bim, encode spatial relationships that connect to area problems. Islamic geometric art in Melbourne galleries builds tessellations from square grids.

## Memorable takeaways
- Perfect square = integer × itself.
- Square root undoes squaring.
- Know 1² to 15² — they appear everywhere.
- Estimate roots by squeezing between consecutive perfect squares.

## Watch out for these traps
- "√49 = 7 and −7." At this level, take the principal (positive) root: √49 = 7.
- "Halve a number to find its root." Halving 64 gives 32, not 8. Square root is not halving.
- "64 is a perfect square because it is even." Being even is not enough — 50 is even but not a perfect square.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Is 50 a perfect square?* No — 7² = 49 and 8² = 64, so no integer squared gives 50.
- *What is 12²?* 144.
- *What is √121?* 11, because 11 × 11 = 121.
- *If √n = 9, what is n?* 81, because 9² = 81.
- *Between which two whole numbers does √30 lie?* 5 and 6, because 5² = 25 and 6² = 36.
