# VC2M7ST02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Create different types of displays of numerical data, including dot plots and stem-and-leaf plots, using software where appropriate; describe and compare the distribution of data, commenting on the shape, centre and spread including outliers and determining the range, median, mean and mode.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** A good display makes shape, centre and spread visible at a glance. Dot plots and stem-and-leaf plots show every value while revealing the distribution.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **create dot plots and stem-and-leaf plots** and to **describe and compare distributions** using shape, centre and spread.
- You should be able to identify outliers and explain their effect on summary statistics.

## Core content

A **dot plot** places a dot above a number line for each value. Repeated values stack. It suits small to medium data sets and makes clusters, gaps and outliers visible.

A **stem-and-leaf plot** splits each value into a **stem** (leading digits) and a **leaf** (last digit). Leaves are written in order on each row. Always include a **key** (e.g. 4 | 7 = 47). It works like a sideways histogram but retains every original value.

**Describing a distribution.** Comment on three things: **shape** (symmetric, positively skewed, negatively skewed), **centre** (mean, median, mode), and **spread** (range, outliers). Positively skewed means the tail stretches right; negatively skewed means the tail stretches left.

**Worked example.** January daily maxima for two Melbourne suburbs displayed in a back-to-back stem-and-leaf plot. Suburb A (inner): median 29°C, range 17, positively skewed by extreme heat. Suburb B (coastal): median 26°C, range 9, roughly symmetric. Suburb A is hotter and more variable.

**Outliers** sit far from the main cluster. They pull the mean away from the median. When outliers are present, the median is a better measure of centre.

**Victorian context.** The Bureau of Meteorology publishes daily temperature and rainfall data for Victorian stations — ideal for creating these displays. The ABS and VicHealth use data displays to communicate patterns in health and population statistics.

## Memorable takeaways
- Dot plots show every value — look for clusters, gaps, outliers.
- Stem-and-leaf plots retain all values and reveal shape.
- Describe distributions: shape, centre, spread.
- Outliers affect the mean more than the median.
- Always include a key on stem-and-leaf plots.

## Watch out for these traps
- Forgetting to order leaves in a stem-and-leaf plot — unordered leaves misrepresent shape.
- Leaving out the key — without it, no one can read the plot.
- Confusing skew direction — the tail names the skew (tail right = positively skewed).

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Mode on a dot plot.* The tallest stack of dots.
- *Recording 38 with key 3 | 5 = 35.* Stem 3, leaf 8.
- *House prices clustered low with a few high sales.* Positively (right) skewed.
- *Median dot in 15 data points.* The 8th dot from either end.
- *Dot plot or stem-and-leaf for 50 test scores?* Stem-and-leaf — 50 values split naturally into stems and leaves, and the shape shows clearly.
