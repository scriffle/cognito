# VC2M8ST02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Analyse and report on the distribution of data from primary and secondary sources using random and non-random sampling techniques.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** How you select your sample shapes the distribution you observe. Random sampling reduces bias; non-random sampling can introduce it. Reporting means describing shape, centre and spread while acknowledging the method's influence.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **analyse and report on data distributions** using **random and non-random sampling** from primary and secondary sources.
- You should be able to evaluate how the sampling technique affects the data.

## Core content

**Random sampling** gives every member a known chance of selection — minimises bias. Types: simple random, systematic, stratified. **Non-random sampling** depends on availability or choice — quicker but riskier. Types: convenience (nearby people), voluntary (self-selected), purposive (researcher-chosen).

**Primary data** you collect yourself; **secondary data** others collected (ABS, BoM). For secondary data, always check: who collected it, how, and what method was used.

**Analyse** by creating a display and describing **shape, centre, spread, outliers**. Then ask: could the sampling method explain any features?

**Worked example.** Travel times for Year 8 students. Random sample of 20: median 26.5 min, range 47, roughly symmetric. Convenience sample (early arrivers only): median 13 min, range 20, clustered low. The convenience sample captured only short commutes — the method caused the difference.

**Reporting** must include: question, population, sampling type, sample size, display, statistics, and a statement about how the method may have influenced results.

**Victorian context.** VicHealth uses stratified random sampling for the Victorian Population Health Survey. The ABS Labour Force Survey is random. Social media polls are voluntary — often unrepresentative. Non-random sampling has historically underrepresented Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples in Australian data.

## Memorable takeaways
- Random sampling minimises bias; non-random is quicker but riskier.
- The sampling method shapes the distribution you observe.
- Always name your method and discuss its influence on results.
- Compare random and non-random to see bias in action.

## Watch out for these traps
- Assuming voluntary responses represent everyone — people with strong opinions self-select.
- Ignoring who is missing from the sample — they may differ systematically.
- Treating secondary data as automatically trustworthy — always check the method.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Radio call-in vote — random or non-random?* Non-random (voluntary) — only motivated listeners call.
- *Convenience sample vs random — which to trust?* Random — every member had equal chance.
- *Why might a voluntary survey overestimate screen time?* Heavy users are more likely to respond.
- *Why name the sampling method in a report?* So readers can judge whether results are representative.
- *Stratification helps remote communities because...* It guarantees proportional representation from every subgroup.
