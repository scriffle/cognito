# VC2M8N04 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M8N04. Use the 4 operations with integers and with rational numbers, choosing and using efficient mental and written strategies, and digital tools where appropriate, and making estimates for these computations.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**
  - The four operations apply to negative numbers and fractions just as they do to positive whole numbers — with extra sign rules.
  - Choosing between mental, written and digital strategies depends on the numbers and the context.
  - Estimation is your safety net — it catches errors before they stick.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Critical and Creative Thinking; Numeracy; Personal and Social Capability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **apply all four operations to integers and rational numbers** (positive and negative fractions and decimals) and to **choose the most efficient strategy** for each situation.
- You are learning that estimation is a thinking tool, not a shortcut.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Multiply and divide integers, applying sign rules correctly.
- Perform all four operations with negative fractions and decimals.
- Apply BODMAS to expressions involving integers and rational numbers.
- Estimate answers before calculating and check for reasonableness.

## Core content

**1. Multiplying integers — sign rules.** Positive × positive = positive. Negative × negative = positive. Positive × negative = negative. Negative × positive = negative. Summary: same signs → positive; different signs → negative. (−3) × (−4) = +12. (−5) × 2 = −10. *Quick check:* Calculate (−7) × (−6).

**2. Dividing integers — sign rules.** The same sign rules apply. Same signs → positive; different signs → negative. (−20) ÷ (−4) = +5. (−18) ÷ 3 = −6. *Quick check:* Calculate 36 ÷ (−9).

**3. Operations with negative fractions.** Apply the same sign rules, then the usual fraction rules. (−2/3) × (3/4) = −6/12 = −1/2 (different signs → negative). (−1/2) + (−1/4) = −2/4 + (−1/4) = −3/4. *Quick check:* Calculate (−3/5) ÷ (−1/2).

**4. Operations with negative decimals.** Treat the numbers as positive, calculate, then apply the sign rule. (−2.4) × (−1.5): calculate 2.4 × 1.5 = 3.6; same signs → +3.6. (−7.2) ÷ 0.9 = −8 (different signs → negative). *Quick check:* Calculate (−0.6) × 4.5.

**5. BODMAS with integers and rational numbers.** Order of operations does not change. Brackets first, then indices, then multiplication/division (left to right), then addition/subtraction (left to right). (−3)² = 9 (square first: (−3) × (−3) = 9). But −3² = −9 (square the 3 first, then apply the negative). *Quick check:* Calculate (−2)³ + 5 × (−3).

**6. Worked example — multi-step with rational numbers.**
Calculate: (−1/2)² × 8 − (−3)

*Follow the thinking:*
- Brackets and orders first: (−1/2)² = 1/4 (negative × negative = positive).
- Multiply: 1/4 × 8 = 2.
- Subtract a negative: 2 − (−3) = 2 + 3 = 5.
- Estimate check: (about 0.25) × 8 ≈ 2; 2 + 3 = 5. Matches.

**7. Estimation strategies.** Round to friendly numbers. (−4.7) × 3.2 ≈ (−5) × 3 = −15. Exact: −15.04. Close enough to confirm the sign and magnitude. For fractions: (−7/8) × 16 ≈ (−1) × 16 = −16. Exact: −14. Reasonable. *Quick check:* Estimate (−2.9) × (−4.1), then calculate exactly.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Melbourne's winter mornings regularly produce negative temperatures in the outer east and north (Kinglake, Coldstream). BOM weather records show temperature changes as positive and negative values. The ASX tracks daily share-price movements as positive (gain) or negative (loss) decimals — investors perform all four operations with these. Engineers at the Port of Melbourne calculate tidal variations above and below mean sea level using negative values.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Environmental monitoring programs led by Traditional Owner corporations (Gunaikurnai, Dja Dja Wurrung) track seasonal variables — temperature drops below zero, changes in river levels above and below baseline. These measurements are recorded as integers and rational numbers in scientific datasets. Reading and interpreting this data requires fluency with operations on negative numbers. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** International students and families from northern-hemisphere backgrounds bring lived experience of deeply negative temperatures. Currency exchange calculations between Australian dollars and other currencies often produce negative net changes. Financial literacy programs across multicultural Melbourne use positive and negative rational numbers to model income, expenses and debt.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A freezer is set to −18°C. The power fails and the temperature rises by 2.5°C per hour for 6 hours. What is the temperature after 6 hours? Write the calculation as an expression involving integers and decimals.
2. **Civic.** A council's budget shows: revenue $4.2 million, expenses $4.85 million. Express the balance as a rational number. If the deficit must be covered equally across 7 wards, what is each ward's share (as a negative decimal)?
3. **Vocational.** A diver descends from the surface (0 m) at a rate of −1.5 m per minute for 8 minutes, rests, then ascends at +0.9 m per minute for 10 minutes. Calculate the final depth. Is the diver still below sea level?
4. **Ethical.** A student claims (−2)⁴ = −16. Show the correct calculation and explain the common error.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A class tracks the daily temperature change in Moscow (provided as data: +3, −5, −2, +1, −4, +2, −1 °C over 7 days). Calculate the total change and the mean daily change. Express both as rational numbers.

## Memorable takeaways
- Same signs multiply/divide to give positive; different signs give negative.
- The sign rules for multiplication and division are the same.
- (−a)² is positive; −a² is negative. Brackets matter.
- BODMAS applies fully to integers and rational numbers.
- Estimate first: check sign and magnitude before committing to the final answer.

## Stretch yourself

Show all working. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Confusing (−3)² and −3².** (−3)² = 9 because you square the entire negative number. −3² = −9 because the square applies to 3 only, then the negative is applied. Brackets make all the difference.
- **Sign errors in division of fractions.** (−2/3) ÷ (−1/4) = (−2/3) × (−4/1) = +8/3. Two negatives make a positive — do not forget to flip and then check the signs.
- **Dropping negatives mid-calculation.** In multi-step problems, write every sign explicitly. A missing negative can flip your entire answer.
- **Not estimating.** If you calculate (−4.7) × 3.2 and get +15.04 (forgot the sign), an estimate of (−5) × 3 = −15 would immediately flag the positive answer as wrong.
