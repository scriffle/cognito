# VC2M7SP02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Classify triangles, quadrilaterals and other polygons according to their side and angle properties; identify and reason about relationships.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Shapes are classified by measurable properties, not appearance. Side lengths and angles determine the category. Classification reveals hierarchical relationships.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **classify polygons** by **side and angle properties** and to reason about relationships between shape categories.
- You should be able to explain why a square is a special rectangle and a rectangle is a special parallelogram.

## Core content

A **polygon** is a closed flat shape with straight sides. A **regular** polygon has all sides and all angles equal.

**Triangles by sides:** equilateral (all equal), isosceles (two equal), scalene (none equal). **By angles:** acute (all < 90°), right (one = 90°), obtuse (one > 90°). The interior angles of every triangle sum to **180°**.

**Quadrilaterals:** **Square** — four equal sides, four right angles. **Rectangle** — opposite sides equal, four right angles. **Rhombus** — four equal sides, opposite angles equal. **Parallelogram** — opposite sides equal and parallel. **Trapezium** — exactly one pair of parallel sides. **Kite** — two pairs of adjacent equal sides.

**Hierarchical relationships.** Every square is a rectangle (meets all rectangle requirements plus more). Every rectangle is a parallelogram. Classification follows from definitions, not appearance.

**Worked example — Flinders Street Station tiles.** Square tiles: four equal sides, four 90° angles. Rectangular tiles: opposite sides equal, four 90° angles. Hexagonal border tiles: six equal sides, interior angles of 120°. *Follow the thinking:* identify side lengths → check angles → apply definition → classify.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal shield designs from south-eastern Australia use symmetrical geometric shapes — diamonds, triangles and nested polygons — with precise proportional relationships. Flinders Street Station tiles, the Royal Exhibition Building facade and basalt columns at the Organ Pipes National Park all demonstrate polygon properties in Victorian life.

## Memorable takeaways
- Classify by properties (sides and angles), not by how the shape looks.
- Triangle angles always sum to 180°.
- Quadrilateral classification is hierarchical — a square is a special rectangle.
- Regular polygons have all sides and all angles equal.

## Watch out for these traps
- "A square is not a rectangle." It is — it meets all rectangle requirements.
- "Diamonds are a shape category." The correct terms are rhombus or kite.
- "An obtuse triangle can have two obtuse angles." Impossible — the angle sum would exceed 180°.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Is a circle a polygon?* No — polygons have straight sides.
- *Triangle with sides 5, 5, 8.* Isosceles — two sides are equal.
- *Four equal sides, no right angles.* Rhombus.
- *Third angle when two are 90° and 35°.* 180° − 90° − 35° = 55°.
- *Is every parallelogram a rectangle?* No — rectangles require four right angles; parallelograms only need opposite angles equal.
