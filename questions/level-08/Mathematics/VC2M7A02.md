# VC2M7A02 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M7A02. Apply the associative, commutative and distributive laws to aid mental and written computation, and formulate algebraic expressions using constants, variables, operations and brackets.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Algebra).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Number laws let you rearrange and regroup calculations to make them easier.
  - The same laws work with letters (algebra) as with numbers.
  - Writing algebraic expressions is a way of generalising arithmetic.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Sustainability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **apply the associative, commutative and distributive laws** to simplify mental and written computation.
- You are learning to **formulate algebraic expressions** using constants, variables, operations and brackets.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- State each law in words and with symbols.
- Use the laws to rearrange calculations for easier mental arithmetic.
- Write and simplify algebraic expressions.
- Recognise when a law does and does not apply.

## Core content

**1. The commutative law.** **Addition** and **multiplication** can be done in any order: *a + b = b + a* and *a × b = b × a*. This does not work for subtraction or division. Example: 37 + 58 is easier rearranged as 58 + 37 if you prefer adding to a larger number. *Quick check:* Does 12 − 5 equal 5 − 12? Why not?

**2. The associative law.** When you add or multiply three or more numbers, you can **regroup** them: *(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)* and *(a × b) × c = a × (b × c)*. Example: (4 × 25) × 7 = 100 × 7 = 700 — much faster than 4 × 25 × 7 done left to right. *Quick check:* Regroup 5 + 47 + 15 to make the addition easier.

**3. The distributive law.** Multiplication distributes over addition: *a(b + c) = ab + ac*. This is the most powerful law for mental maths and algebra. Example: 6 × 53 = 6 × 50 + 6 × 3 = 300 + 18 = 318. *Quick check:* Use the distributive law to calculate 7 × 98 mentally.

**4. Using the laws together.** Mental maths champion strategies combine all three laws. To calculate 25 × 36: rewrite 36 as 4 × 9, then (25 × 4) × 9 = 100 × 9 = 900 (associative and commutative). *Quick check:* Calculate 50 × 18 by breaking 18 into two parts.

**5. From arithmetic to algebra.** The same laws apply to letters. If *a = 3* and *b = 5*, then *a + b = b + a = 8*. More importantly, you can simplify expressions without knowing the values: *3x + 5x = (3 + 5)x = 8x* uses the distributive law in reverse ("factoring out *x*"). *Quick check:* Simplify *4y + 9y*.

**6. Worked example — tram-fare calculation.** A group of students catches a Melbourne tram. Zone 1 costs $a per trip and Zone 2 costs $b. Four students travel Zone 1 and three travel Zone 2. Total cost = *4a + 3b*. If a second group of 2 Zone 1 and 5 Zone 2 students joins: combined cost = *(4a + 3b) + (2a + 5b) = 6a + 8b*. *Follow the thinking:* commutative law lets you swap terms → collect like terms → simplified expression.

**7. Formulating expressions with brackets.** "Five more than double a number" → *2n + 5*. "Triple the sum of a number and four" → *3(n + 4)*. The brackets matter: *3(n + 4) = 3n + 12* by the distributive law, which is different from *3n + 4*. *Quick check:* Expand *4(x + 7)*.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Melbourne's myki fare system, Melbourne Market wholesale pricing (bulk × unit-price calculations) and AFL statistics (total disposals = kicks + handballs, aggregated across players) all rely on these arithmetic laws in everyday computation.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal trade networks across south-eastern Australia involved systematic exchange — quantities of greenstone axes from Wurundjeri Country traded for possum-skin cloaks, shell and ochre. These exchanges encoded proportional and additive reasoning across multiple Nations. AIATSIS publishes material on Indigenous trade and quantitative systems. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Market traders at Queen Victoria Market and Footscray Market use mental arithmetic strategies daily — regrouping prices, distributing discounts across items and combining totals. Students from diverse backgrounds bring strong mental-computation traditions.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You buy 4 mangoes at $3 each and 6 bananas at $2 each at Footscray Market. Write an expression and simplify.
2. **Civic.** Maribyrnong City Council orders 15 park benches at $d each and 15 bins at $e each. Rewrite the total cost using the distributive law.
3. **Vocational.** A carpentry apprentice calculates timber lengths: 8 planks of *x* metres and 5 planks of *x* metres. Simplify the total length expression.
4. **Ethical.** A retailer advertises "30% off everything." Write an expression for the sale price of an item costing $p. Discuss whether the distributive law helps consumers check discounts mentally.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A school fundraiser sells lamingtons at $a each and dim sims at $b each. On Monday they sell 20 lamingtons and 30 dim sims; on Tuesday, 15 lamingtons and 25 dim sims. Write and simplify the combined revenue expression.

## Memorable takeaways
- Commutative: swap the order (addition and multiplication only).
- Associative: regroup (addition and multiplication only).
- Distributive: multiply across a bracket.
- The same laws work for letters as for numbers.
- Collecting like terms uses the distributive law in reverse.

## Stretch yourself
A school canteen sells three items: pies at $a, juice at $b and fruit at $c. On Monday, sales are 40 pies, 55 juices and 30 fruits. On Tuesday, sales are 35 pies, 45 juices and 50 fruits. Write algebraic expressions for each day's revenue. Combine them into one expression and simplify by collecting like terms. Then use the distributive law to factorise the combined pie revenue. If *a = 5*, *b = 3*, *c = 2*, substitute to find the two-day total. Show every step and name the law you use at each point. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Subtraction is commutative." It is not: 10 − 3 ≠ 3 − 10.
- "Division is associative." It is not: (24 ÷ 6) ÷ 2 ≠ 24 ÷ (6 ÷ 2).
- "3(n + 4) is the same as 3n + 4." You must distribute: 3(n + 4) = 3n + 12.
- "Like terms need the same number." Like terms need the same variable part — *3x* and *7x* are like terms regardless of the coefficients.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Does 12 − 5 equal 5 − 12?* No. Subtraction is not commutative: 12 − 5 = 7 but 5 − 12 = −7.
2. *Regroup 5 + 47 + 15.* (5 + 15) + 47 = 20 + 47 = 67.
3. *7 × 98 using the distributive law.* 7 × (100 − 2) = 700 − 14 = 686.
4. *50 × 18.* 50 × (10 + 8) = 500 + 400 = 900. Or 50 × 2 × 9 = 100 × 9 = 900.
5. *Simplify 4y + 9y.* (4 + 9)y = 13y.
6. *Expand 4(x + 7).* 4x + 28.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Market shopping.* 4 × 3 + 6 × 2 = 12 + 12 = $24.
2. *Council order.* 15d + 15e = 15(d + e) — distributive law.
3. *Timber.* 8x + 5x = 13x metres.
4. *Discount.* Sale price = p − 0.3p = 0.7p (or p(1 − 0.3)). The distributive law helps consumers see that "30% off" means "multiply by 0.7."
5. *Fundraiser.* (20a + 30b) + (15a + 25b) = 35a + 55b.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Monday: 40a + 55b + 30c. Tuesday: 35a + 45b + 50c. Combined: 75a + 100b + 80c (commutative to rearrange, then collect like terms). Pie revenue factorised: 75a = 75 × a. Substituting a = 5, b = 3, c = 2: 75(5) + 100(3) + 80(2) = 375 + 300 + 160 = $835. Laws named at each step.
