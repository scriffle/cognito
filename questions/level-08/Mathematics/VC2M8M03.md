## Core content

**1. Circle vocabulary.** The **radius** (r) is the distance from centre to edge. The **diameter** (d) is the distance across through the centre: d = 2r. The **circumference** (C) is the perimeter — the distance around the circle. *Quick check:* A circle has radius 7 cm. What is its diameter?

**2. Pi (π).** **π** is the ratio of circumference to diameter for every circle: π = C ÷ d ≈ 3.14159... It is irrational (never-ending, non-repeating). For calculations, use your calculator's π key or approximate as 3.14. *Quick check:* If a circle has diameter 10 cm, what is the exact circumference in terms of π?

**3. Circumference formula.** C = πd or equivalently C = 2πr. Example: radius 5 m → C = 2 × π × 5 = 10π ≈ 31.4 m. *Quick check:* A circular pond has diameter 8 m. What length of edging is needed?

**4. Area formula.** A = πr². The area depends on the radius squared — doubling the radius quadruples the area. Example: r = 6 cm → A = π × 6² = 36π ≈ 113.1 cm². *Quick check:* A circular pizza has radius 15 cm. What is its area?

**5. Semicircles and quarter-circles.** A **semicircle** has half the area and half the curved perimeter, but its full perimeter includes the straight diameter. Semicircle area = ½πr². Semicircle perimeter = πr + 2r (curved part + diameter). A **quarter-circle** similarly: area = ¼πr², perimeter = ½πr + 2r. *Quick check:* A semicircular window has diameter 1.2 m. What is its area?

**6. Worked example: circular running track.**

An athletics track at a Melbourne school has two straight sections (100 m each) and two semicircular ends. The radius of each semicircle is 32 m.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Circumference of one full circle = 2π × 32 = 64π m.
- Two semicircles = one full circle = 64π ≈ 201.1 m.
- Two straights = 2 × 100 = 200 m.
- Total track length = 200 + 201.1 = 401.1 m.
- This is why a standard 400 m track uses a slightly smaller radius (about 36.5 m for the inner lane with straights of 84.39 m).

**7. Working backwards.** If you know the circumference, find the radius: r = C ÷ (2π). If you know the area: r = √(A ÷ π). Example: a circular garden has area 50 m². r = √(50 ÷ π) = √15.92 ≈ 3.99 m. *Quick check:* A wheel has circumference 2.5 m. What is its radius?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The MCG (Melbourne Cricket Ground) is approximately oval — its area is estimated using semicircle-and-rectangle models. The Royal Exhibition Building dome is a hemisphere whose base circle has circumference calculations built into its restoration plans. Melbourne's roundabouts (e.g. the Hoddle Street–Johnston Street intersection) involve circular geometry for lane markings and kerb measurements.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Circular and semicircular motifs appear throughout First Nations art — concentric circles represent waterholes, meeting places and Country in many published traditions. The stone arrangements at Wurdi Youang (Wadawurrung Country, near Geelong) form an egg-shaped ring whose geometry researchers have studied for astronomical alignment. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Circular geometry is central to Islamic art (arabesques), Indian mandala design and Chinese moon-gate architecture — all represented in Melbourne's cultural landscape. Pi Day (March 14) is celebrated in Victorian schools as a cross-cultural maths event.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A bicycle wheel has diameter 66 cm. How far does the bike travel in one full revolution? How many revolutions are needed to travel 1 km?
2. **Civic.** The City of Ballarat installs a circular fountain with radius 3.5 m in a town square. Calculate the area of the fountain base and the length of decorative edging needed around it.
3. **Vocational.** A pizza shop in Carlton cuts a pizza of diameter 30 cm into 8 equal slices. Calculate the area of the whole pizza and the area of one slice. What is the length of crust (curved edge) on one slice?
4. **Ethical.** A land developer advertises a "quarter-acre circular block." A quarter acre is approximately 1 012 m². Calculate the radius. Is a circular block practical for building? Discuss advantages and disadvantages.
5. **Cross-cultural.** You are designing a circular labyrinth for a school wellness garden (inspired by walking labyrinths in multiple cultures). The outer radius is 6 m. The path is 0.8 m wide and spirals inward. Calculate the area of the outer circle and the area of the first (outermost) ring of path.

## Memorable takeaways
- C = πd = 2πr. A = πr².
- π ≈ 3.14159 — the same ratio for every circle.
- Doubling the radius quadruples the area (because r is squared).
- Semicircle perimeter includes the straight edge (diameter).
- Work backwards: r = C ÷ (2π) or r = √(A ÷ π).

## Stretch yourself
A landscape architect designs a circular memorial garden with a 4 m radius path surrounding it. The garden has radius 8 m. Calculate: (a) the area of the garden, (b) the area of the path (the ring between radius 8 m and 12 m), (c) the circumference of the outer edge of the path, and (d) the cost of paving the path at $75 per m². If the council later decides to add a semicircular extension (radius 3 m) to the garden's north side, recalculate the total landscaped area. Show all working. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "I used the diameter in the area formula." A = πr², not πd². If given the diameter, halve it first to get the radius.
- "Semicircle perimeter is just half the circumference." The perimeter of a semicircle = half the circumference + the diameter (the straight edge). You need both parts.
- "Doubling the radius doubles the area." No — area depends on r², so doubling r quadruples the area.
- "π = 3.14 exactly." π is irrational and non-terminating. 3.14 is an approximation. Use your calculator's π key for accuracy, then round at the end.
