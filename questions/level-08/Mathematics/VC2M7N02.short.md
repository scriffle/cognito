# VC2M7N02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Represent natural numbers in expanded notation using powers of 10, and as products of powers of prime numbers using exponent notation.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Place value is built on powers of 10. Every natural number greater than 1 has a unique prime factorisation. Exponent notation compresses repeated multiplication.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **write natural numbers in expanded notation using powers of 10** and as **products of prime powers**.
- You should be able to switch between representations depending on the problem.

## Core content

Our number system is **base 10**. Each place is worth 10 times the one to its right: 10⁰ = 1, 10¹ = 10, 10² = 100, 10³ = 1 000. **Expanded notation** breaks a number into these parts: 4 703 = 4 × 10³ + 7 × 10² + 3 × 10⁰.

A **prime number** has exactly two factors: 1 and itself (e.g. 2, 3, 5, 7, 11). A **composite number** has more than two factors. The number 1 is neither prime nor composite.

**Prime factorisation** breaks a composite number into a product of primes. The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic guarantees this factorisation is unique. Use a **factor tree** or **repeated division** to find it.

**Worked example: 3 600.** Expanded notation: 3 × 10³ + 6 × 10². Prime factorisation by repeated division: 3 600 ÷ 2 = 1 800 → ÷ 2 = 900 → ÷ 2 = 450 → ÷ 2 = 225 → ÷ 3 = 75 → ÷ 3 = 25 → ÷ 5 = 5 → ÷ 5 = 1. Result: 2⁴ × 3² × 5².

**Why two representations?** Expanded notation reveals place value — useful for rounding and ordering. Prime factorisation reveals building blocks — useful for HCF, LCM and simplifying fractions.

**Victorian and cultural context.** The Australian Synchrotron in Clayton uses powers of 10 across many orders of magnitude. First Nations counting systems, including Torres Strait Islander grouping by twos and fives, show that base systems are a human design choice. Melbourne's multicultural classrooms can explore Babylonian (base 60) and Mayan (base 20) systems alongside base 10.

## Memorable takeaways
- Place value is powers of 10 in disguise.
- 10⁰ = 1 — not 0.
- Every natural number > 1 is either prime or a unique product of primes.
- Factor trees and repeated division reach the same answer.

## Watch out for these traps
- "10⁰ = 0." Any non-zero base to the power 0 equals 1.
- "1 is prime." It has only one factor, so it does not qualify.
- "12 = 2 × 6 is a prime factorisation." 6 is not prime — keep going: 12 = 2² × 3.
