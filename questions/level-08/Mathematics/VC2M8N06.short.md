# VC2M8N06 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving rational numbers and percentages, including financial contexts involving profit and loss; formulate problems, choosing efficient mental and written calculation strategies and using digital tools where appropriate; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the context, reviewing the appropriateness of the model.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Rational numbers model real financial situations. Percentage increase and decrease underpin profit, loss, discount and markup. Efficient strategies suit different complexity levels.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **model practical problems** involving **percentages and rational numbers**, especially profit and loss.
- You should be able to choose efficient strategies, interpret results in context and review the model.

## Core content

A **rational number** can be written as *a/b* — this includes terminating and repeating decimals and all percentages. Financial contexts use all forms: 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%.

**Percentage of an amount.** 15% of $240 = 240 × 0.15 = $36. Mental shortcut: 10% + 5%.

**Percentage increase** (profit, markup, GST): multiply by (1 + rate). A 60% markup on $40: $40 × 1.60 = $64. GST (10%): multiply by 1.10. **Percentage decrease** (loss, discount): multiply by (1 − rate). 30% off $120: $120 × 0.70 = $84.

**Successive percentages compound.** A 10% increase then 10% decrease: 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99 — a 1% loss, not back to the original.

**Worked example — Queen Victoria Market.** Avocados bought at $2.40, sold at $4.00 (66.7% markup). Friday discount 25%: $4.00 × 0.75 = $3.00 (still $0.60 profit). Selling 80 full-price + 20 discounted: total profit = $128 + $12 = $140. *Follow the thinking:* identify prices → calculate per-unit profit → apply discount → aggregate.

**Reviewing.** Always ask: is the percentage on the correct base? Are there hidden costs? Does the model account for wastage?

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal community enterprises like the Koorie Heritage Trust use percentage-based financial modelling for pricing and sustainability. Queen Victoria Market, Chadstone sales and the Australian GST all involve percentage calculations Victorians encounter daily.

## Memorable takeaways
- Increase: × (1 + rate). Decrease: × (1 − rate).
- Profit % is on cost price, not selling price.
- Successive percentages compound — they do not simply add or cancel.
- Review: is the percentage applied to the correct base?

## Watch out for these traps
- "Profit % is on selling price." Standard convention is cost price.
- "20% up then 20% down returns to original." No: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96.
- "50% off then 20% off = 70% off." No: 0.50 × 0.80 = 0.40, so 60% off.
