# VC2M7M03 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Describe the relationship between π and the circumference, radius and diameter of a circle.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Every circle shares the same ratio of circumference to diameter — that ratio is π. Understanding π lets you move between circumference, diameter and radius.

## What you are learning
- You are learning how **π links the circumference, diameter and radius** of every circle.
- You should be able to use π to calculate unknown measurements and explain why the relationship works.

## Core content

Take any circle and divide its circumference (the distance around) by its diameter (the distance across through the centre). The answer is always **π ≈ 3.14159…** — an **irrational number** that never terminates or repeats.

The **diameter** (d) passes through the centre; the **radius** (r) goes from centre to edge, so d = 2r. The **circumference** (C) is the perimeter. Because π = C ÷ d, we get **C = πd** or equivalently **C = 2πr**.

To find circumference, substitute the known value. A wheel with diameter 70 cm: C = π × 70 ≈ 219.9 cm. To find diameter from circumference, rearrange: d = C ÷ π.

**Worked example.** A circular interpretive sign along the Birrarung (Yarra River) trail has circumference 188.5 cm. Diameter = 188.5 ÷ π ≈ 60.0 cm. Always state units and rounding.

**Why π is constant.** All circles are similar — scaling up or down changes circumference and diameter by the same factor, so their ratio stays fixed. This makes π universal.

**Victorian context.** The MCG centre circle, the State Library reading room dome, and the Australian Synchrotron's circular particle paths all rely on precise circumference calculations. Globally, Babylonian, Egyptian, Chinese and Indian mathematicians all approximated π centuries before the modern symbol was adopted.

## Memorable takeaways
- π is the ratio of circumference to diameter — the same for every circle.
- C = πd = 2πr.
- π is irrational: it never terminates or repeats.
- Always state units and rounding.

## Watch out for these traps
- Confusing radius and diameter — the diameter is twice the radius.
- Saying π = 3.14 exactly — that is a rounded approximation.
- Forgetting units — a bare number means nothing without cm, m or mm.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *How many diameters fit along the circumference?* Just over three — approximately π.
- *Diameter when radius is 5 cm.* d = 2 × 5 = 10 cm.
- *Circumference when r = 3 m.* C = 2 × 3.14 × 3 = 18.84 m.
- *Diameter of wheel with C = 2.1 m.* d = 2.1 ÷ 3.14 ≈ 0.669 m.
- *Double the diameter — what happens to C?* It also doubles, because C = πd and π is constant.
