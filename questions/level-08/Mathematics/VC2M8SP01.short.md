# VC2M8SP01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Identify the conditions for congruence and similarity of triangles and explain the conditions for other sets of common shapes to be congruent or similar, including those formed by transformations.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Congruent = same shape and size. Similar = same shape, proportional size. Minimum conditions (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS) guarantee triangle congruence without checking everything.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **identify conditions for congruence and similarity** of triangles and other shapes, including those formed by transformations.
- You should know the five congruence tests and the three similarity conditions.

## Core content

**Congruent (≅):** same shape, same size. Every matching side and angle equal. Produced by reflections, rotations and translations.

**Triangle congruence conditions** (any one is sufficient):
- **SSS** — three sides equal.
- **SAS** — two sides and included angle.
- **ASA** — two angles and included side.
- **AAS** — two angles and a non-included side.
- **RHS** — right angle, hypotenuse, one other side.

**SSA is NOT sufficient** — it can produce two different triangles (the ambiguous case).

**Similar (∼):** same shape, possibly different size. All angles equal; all sides in the same ratio (scale factor). Produced by enlargement (dilation).

**Triangle similarity conditions:** AAA (or AA), SSS-ratio, SAS-ratio.

**Worked example (SAS).** Two triangular beds share fence AB = 8 m. Both have a second side = 5 m and included angle = 65°. By SAS, the triangles are congruent → equal area.

**Transformations.** Reflect/rotate/translate → congruent. Enlarge → similar. Combination of dilation + rigid motion → similar.

**Victorian and cultural context.** The NGV's Great Hall uses congruent triangular stained-glass panels. First Nations art traditions use repeated congruent motifs through reflection and rotation (possum-skin cloaks, cross-hatching). Islamic geometric tiling in Melbourne mosques achieves pattern through congruent tiles and rigid transformations.

## Memorable takeaways
- Congruence: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS.
- SSA does NOT work (ambiguous case).
- Similarity: AA, SSS-ratio, SAS-ratio.
- Rigid transformations → congruent. Enlargement → similar.

## Watch out for these traps
- "SSA proves congruence." Two triangles can exist with the same SSA measurements.
- "Same area = congruent." 3×6 and 2×9 both have area 18 but are not congruent.
- "All equilateral triangles are congruent." They are similar (AA), only congruent if same side length.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Two circles, same radius — congruent?* Yes.
- *Sides 5, 7, 9 both triangles — condition?* SSS → congruent.
- *Sides 3,4,5 and 6,8,10 — similar?* Yes, scale factor 2 (SSS-ratio).
- *Angles 40°, 60°, 80° both triangles?* Similar by AAA.
- *Reflected then enlarged ×2?* Similar (not congruent — size changed).
