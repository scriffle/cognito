# VC2M7M05 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Demonstrate that the interior angle sum of a triangle in the plane is 180° and apply this to determine the interior angle sum of other shapes and the size of unknown angles.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** The angles in any flat triangle sum to exactly 180°. Any polygon splits into triangles, giving the formula (n − 2) × 180° for its angle sum.

## What you are learning
- You are learning that the **interior angle sum of a triangle is 180°** and how to extend this to **any polygon**.
- You should be able to calculate unknown angles using the angle-sum property.

## Core content

Draw any triangle on a flat surface, tear off the three corners and arrange them along a straight line — they fit exactly, making **180°**. This is true for every triangle: equilateral, isosceles, scalene, right-angled.

A **quadrilateral** splits into two triangles (draw one diagonal), so its angle sum is 2 × 180° = **360°**. In general, a polygon with **n sides** splits into **(n − 2) triangles**, giving an angle sum of **(n − 2) × 180°**. Pentagon: 540°. Hexagon: 720°. Octagon: 1080°.

In a **regular polygon** (all sides and angles equal), each angle = (n − 2) × 180° ÷ n. Regular hexagon: 120°. Regular octagon: 135°.

**Worked example.** A pentagonal park shelter has four known angles: 108°, 108°, 120°, 95°. Angle sum = 540°. Known total = 431°. Unknown angle = 540° − 431° = 109°.

**Why it matters.** The triangle is the only rigid polygon — every structure from roof trusses to bridge supports uses triangulation. The hexagonal bluestone pavers on Melbourne's laneways and the octagonal bandstand in Fitzroy Gardens demonstrate polygon geometry in built form.

## Memorable takeaways
- Triangle angle sum = 180°.
- Polygon angle sum = (n − 2) × 180°.
- Quadrilateral: 360°. Pentagon: 540°. Hexagon: 720°.
- Regular polygon angle = (n − 2) × 180° ÷ n.
- The triangle is the building block for all polygon angle sums.

## Watch out for these traps
- Assuming the formula works on curved surfaces — it is for flat (Euclidean) planes only.
- Confusing interior and exterior angles — they are supplements, not the same thing.
- Miscounting triangles — always draw diagonals from one vertex; the count is n − 2.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Third angle of a 50°, 70° triangle.* 180° − 50° − 70° = 60°.
- *Can a triangle have two obtuse angles?* No — they would already exceed 180°.
- *Fourth angle of a quadrilateral with 90°, 90°, 110°.* 360° − 290° = 70°.
- *Octagon angle sum.* (8 − 2) × 180° = 1080°.
- *Each angle of a regular pentagon.* 540° ÷ 5 = 108°.
