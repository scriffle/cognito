# VC2M7N09 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Recognise, represent and solve problems involving ratios.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** A ratio compares quantities of the same kind. Equivalent ratios scale every part by the same factor. Ratios appear in recipes, maps, mixtures and sharing.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **recognise, represent and solve problems involving ratios**.
- You should be able to simplify ratios, find equivalent ratios, and divide quantities in a given ratio.

## Core content

A **ratio** compares two or more quantities measured in the same unit. Flour to sugar = 2 : 3 means for every 2 parts flour there are 3 parts sugar. Order matters.

**Simplifying:** divide all parts by the highest common factor. 12 : 18 → divide by 6 → 2 : 3.

**Equivalent ratios:** multiply or divide every part by the same number. 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 10 : 15.

**Dividing a quantity in a ratio.** Share $200 in 3 : 2. Add parts: 5. One part: $40. Allocate: $120 and $80.

**Three-part ratios.** Concrete uses sand, gravel, cement as 3 : 2 : 1. For 60 kg: 6 parts, each 10 kg → 30 kg, 20 kg, 10 kg.

**Worked example.** A damper recipe uses flour and water in 5 : 2 for 700 g total. Parts = 7, one part = 100 g. Flour = 500 g, water = 200 g. To double: 1 000 g and 400 g. Ratio unchanged: 5 : 2 ✓.

**Ratio vs fraction vs rate.** Ratio 2 : 3 → fraction of flour is 2/5 (part of the whole). A rate compares different units (km/h). Related but distinct.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Melbourne's coffee culture uses milk-to-espresso ratios. The NGV uses scale ratios in catalogues. Traditional First Nations food preparation — grinding seeds, mixing ochre pigments — involves proportional mixing. Multicultural cuisines (Vietnamese, Lebanese, Indian) all rely on ingredient ratios.

## Memorable takeaways
- Ratio compares like quantities — order matters.
- Simplify by dividing all parts by the HCF.
- To divide in a ratio: add parts, find one part, allocate.
- 2 : 3 means 2 out of 5 total parts, not 2 out of 3.

## Watch out for these traps
- "2 : 3 means 2 out of 3." No — the total is 5 parts. The fraction is 2/5.
- "Order doesn't matter." 2 : 3 is different from 3 : 2.
- "Ratios must be whole numbers." 1.5 : 2 is valid but usually converted to 3 : 4.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *12 girls and 18 boys.* Ratio = 12 : 18 = 2 : 3.
- *Simplify 45 : 30.* 3 : 2.
- *56 lollies in ratio 3 : 4.* 7 parts, one part = 8. Person A: 24, Person B: 32.
- *800 mL paint in ratio 1 : 2 : 5, how much blue?* 8 parts, one = 100 mL. Blue = 200 mL.
- *Fraction of cats if ratio cats : dogs = 4 : 7.* 4/11.
