# VC2M8M03 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve problems involving the circumference and area of a circle using formulas and appropriate units.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Pi links diameter to circumference in every circle. C = πd = 2πr. A = πr². These formulas extend to semicircles and quarter-circles.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate the circumference and area of circles** using formulas and appropriate units.
- You should be able to apply π to full circles, semicircles and quarter-circles.

## Core content

**Circle vocabulary.** Radius (r): centre to edge. Diameter (d = 2r): across through centre. Circumference (C): distance around.

**Pi (π ≈ 3.14159).** The ratio C ÷ d for every circle. Irrational — use your calculator's π key.

**Circumference:** C = πd = 2πr. Radius 5 m → C = 10π ≈ 31.4 m.

**Area:** A = πr². Radius 6 cm → A = 36π ≈ 113.1 cm². Doubling the radius quadruples the area.

**Semicircles and quarter-circles.** Semicircle area = ½πr². Semicircle perimeter = πr + 2r (half the curved circumference + the straight diameter). Quarter-circle: area = ¼πr², perimeter = ½πr + 2r.

**Worked example.** Athletics track: two straights (100 m each) + two semicircular ends (r = 32 m). Full circle circumference = 2π × 32 = 64π ≈ 201.1 m. Total track ≈ 200 + 201.1 = 401.1 m.

**Working backwards.** From circumference: r = C ÷ (2π). From area: r = √(A ÷ π).

**Victorian and cultural context.** The MCG's area is estimated using semicircle-and-rectangle models. The stone arrangement at Wurdi Youang (Wadawurrung Country) forms an egg-shaped ring studied for geometric and astronomical properties. Circular motifs in First Nations art (concentric circles for waterholes and meeting places) connect to geometric understanding. Islamic arabesques and Chinese moon gates in Melbourne demonstrate circular geometry across cultures.

## Memorable takeaways
- C = πd = 2πr. A = πr².
- π is the same for every circle (≈ 3.14159).
- Doubling radius quadruples area (r² effect).
- Semicircle perimeter includes the straight diameter edge.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Used diameter in A = πr²." Halve the diameter first — A = πr², not πd².
- "Semicircle perimeter is just half the circumference." Add the diameter (straight edge) too.
- "π = 3.14 exactly." It is irrational and non-terminating — 3.14 is an approximation.
