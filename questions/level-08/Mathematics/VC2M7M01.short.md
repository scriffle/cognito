# VC2M7M01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Establish the formulas for areas of rectangles, triangles and parallelograms and use these in problem-solving.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Measurement).
- **Big ideas:** Area counts unit squares inside a shape. Every triangle and parallelogram formula traces back to the rectangle. Derive, don't just memorise.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **derive and apply area formulas** for rectangles, triangles and parallelograms.
- You should be able to break composite shapes into these three types and calculate total area.

## Core content

**Area** is the surface inside a flat shape, measured in **square units** (cm², m²). A rectangle 5 cm × 3 cm holds 15 unit squares, so **A = l × w**.

A **parallelogram** can be cut and rearranged into a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height. So **A = b × h**. The height must be at right angles to the base, not along the slant side.

A **triangle** is half a parallelogram. Copy the triangle, flip and attach — you get a parallelogram. So **A = ½ × b × h**, where h is the perpendicular height from base to opposite vertex.

**Worked example — composite shape.** A display board: rectangle 120 cm × 80 cm with a triangular top (base 120 cm, height 30 cm). Rectangle: 120 × 80 = 9600 cm². Triangle: ½ × 120 × 30 = 1800 cm². Total: 11 400 cm² = 1.14 m².

For **composite shapes**, break them into rectangles, triangles and parallelograms. Find each area, then add — or subtract if there is a cut-out.

**Victorian context.** Federation Square's pinwheel tiling is built from triangles and parallelograms. Gunditjmara stone-house foundations at Budj Bim are roughly rectangular; archaeologists calculate floor areas to estimate dwelling size. Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung possum-skin cloaks are assembled from measured rectangular and triangular panels. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Rectangle: A = l × w. Parallelogram: A = b × h. Triangle: A = ½ × b × h.
- Height is always perpendicular to the base.
- Every formula traces back to the rectangle.
- For composites: break, calculate, combine.

## Watch out for these traps
- Using the slant side instead of the perpendicular height — the height must be at right angles.
- Forgetting to halve for triangles — a triangle is half a parallelogram.
- Mixing cm² and m² — convert lengths first, or divide cm² by 10 000.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why square units?* Area is two-dimensional, so the unit is squared.
- *Rectangle 12 m × 7 m.* A = 84 m².
- *Parallelogram base 9, height 4.* A = 36 cm².
- *Triangle base 10, height 6.* A = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm².
- *Garden bed: rectangle 8 × 3 minus triangle ½ × 3 × 2.* 24 − 3 = 21 m².
