# VC2M7N07 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Find percentages of quantities and express one quantity as a percentage of another, with and without digital tools.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:** A percentage is a fraction out of 100. Finding a percentage of a quantity means multiplying. Expressing a part as a percentage means dividing by the whole and multiplying by 100.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate percentages of quantities** and **express one quantity as a percentage of another**.
- You should be able to use mental shortcuts, written methods and calculators as appropriate.

## Core content

**Per cent** means "per hundred." 25% = 25/100 = 1/4 = 0.25. To convert: percentage → decimal, divide by 100. Fraction → percentage, divide then multiply by 100.

**Finding a percentage of a quantity:** multiply by the decimal form. 15% of $240 = 0.15 × 240 = $36. Mental shortcut: 10% = $24, 5% = $12, total $36.

**Expressing one quantity as a percentage of another:** (part ÷ whole) × 100. "45 out of 180" → 45 ÷ 180 × 100 = 25%.

**Common mental strategies:** 50% = halve. 25% = halve twice. 10% = divide by 10. 1% = divide by 100. Build others from these: 35% = 30% + 5%.

**Worked example — exam results.** Maths: 68/80 = 85%. Science: 54/75 = 72%. Percentages let you compare fairly even when totals differ.

**Percentages above 100%** occur when a quantity exceeds the original. A plant that grew from 40 cm to 52 cm is now at 52/40 × 100 = 130% of its original height.

**Victorian context.** The ABS publishes Victorian data in percentages. GST is 10% of the price on every receipt. The Yoorrook Justice Commission uses percentages to describe disparities in health and justice outcomes for First Nations Victorians — understanding these numbers is essential to reading the evidence. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Per cent means per hundred — a universal comparison tool.
- To find a percentage of a quantity: multiply by the decimal form.
- To express a part as a percentage: (part ÷ whole) × 100.
- Mental shortcuts (10%, 1%, halving) build speed and confidence.

## Watch out for these traps
- Confusing "percentage of" (the amount) with "percentage off" (the remaining price after discount).
- Applying sequential percentage changes is not the same as adding them — 25% off then 10% on ≠ 15% off.
- Small numbers can produce dramatic percentages — always consider the absolute size.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *40% as fraction and decimal.* 2/5; 0.40.
- *12% of 350.* 0.12 × 350 = 42.
- *27 out of 60 as a percentage.* 45%.
- *35% of 200 mentally.* 10% = 20, 30% = 60, 5% = 10. Total: 70.
- *Plant at 130%.* 52 ÷ 40 × 100 = 130% — a 30% increase from the original.
