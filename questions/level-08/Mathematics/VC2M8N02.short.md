# VC2M8N02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Establish and apply the exponent laws with positive integer exponents and the zero exponent, using exponent notation with numbers.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:** An exponent is shorthand for repeated multiplication. The laws follow logically from that definition. Any non-zero number to the power of zero equals 1.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **establish and apply the exponent laws** — product, quotient, power of a power, power of a product, and zero exponent.
- You should be able to simplify numerical expressions using these laws.

## Core content

**Exponent notation:** 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32. The base is 2, the exponent is 5.

**Product law:** same base, add exponents. aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ. Example: 2³ × 2⁴ = 2⁷. Why? You are combining repeated multiplications into one string.

**Quotient law:** same base, subtract exponents. aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ. Example: 3⁵ ÷ 3² = 3³. Why? You cancel common factors.

**Power of a power:** multiply exponents. (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐˣⁿ. Example: (2³)⁴ = 2¹².

**Power of a product:** (a × b)ⁿ = aⁿ × bⁿ. Example: (2 × 5)³ = 2³ × 5³ = 1000 = 10³.

**Zero exponent:** a⁰ = 1 (a ≠ 0). Proof: a³ ÷ a³ = a⁰ by the quotient law, and anything divided by itself is 1.

**Worked example.** Simplify (2³ × 2²)² ÷ 2⁴. Inside brackets: 2⁵ (product law). Power: (2⁵)² = 2¹⁰. Divide: 2¹⁰ ÷ 2⁴ = 2⁶ = 64.

## Memorable takeaways
- Product law: same base, add exponents.
- Quotient law: same base, subtract exponents.
- Power of a power: multiply exponents.
- Zero exponent: a⁰ = 1 (from the quotient law).

## Watch out for these traps
- (2³)⁴ = 2¹² (multiply), not 2⁷ (don't add for power of a power).
- 2³ × 3² cannot use the product law — bases must be the same.
- a⁰ = 1, not 0. The proof comes from a ÷ a = 1.
