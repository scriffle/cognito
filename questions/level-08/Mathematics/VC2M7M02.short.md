# VC2M7M02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve problems involving the volume of right prisms including rectangular and triangular prisms, using established formulas and appropriate units.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Measurement).
- **Big ideas:** Volume measures three-dimensional space. A right prism has a uniform cross-section. V = area of base × length works for every right prism.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate the volume of rectangular and triangular prisms** using established formulas.
- You should be able to convert between cm³, m³ and litres.

## Core content

**Volume** is the space a three-dimensional object fills, measured in **cubic units** (cm³, m³). A **right prism** has two identical parallel bases connected by rectangular sides that are perpendicular to the bases. If you slice it anywhere parallel to the base, you always get the same shape.

For a **rectangular prism** (box): **V = l × w × h**. A box 8 × 5 × 3 cm has V = 120 cm³ — three layers of 40 unit cubes.

For a **triangular prism**: the cross-section is a triangle with area ½ × b × h. Multiply by the prism's length: **V = ½ × b × h × l**.

The general rule is **V = area of base × length** — it works for every right prism.

**Worked example — water trough.** Triangular cross-section: base 80 cm, height 40 cm. Length 200 cm. Area = ½ × 80 × 40 = 1600 cm². Volume = 1600 × 200 = 320 000 cm³. Convert: 320 000 ÷ 1000 = 320 litres.

**Key conversions:** 1 litre = 1000 cm³. 1 m³ = 1000 litres. 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³.

**Victorian context.** Melbourne Water manages reservoir volumes in megalitres. Gunditjmara eel traps at Budj Bim channel water through stone-walled races functioning as rough rectangular prisms — the volume of water determines harvest capacity. Across many Nations, coolamon carrying vessels depend on length, width and depth for their capacity. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Volume is three-dimensional: cubic units, not square units.
- V = area of base × length works for all right prisms.
- Rectangular prism: V = l × w × h. Triangular prism: V = ½ × b × h × l.
- 1 litre = 1000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1000 litres.

## Watch out for these traps
- Confusing area (cm²) with volume (cm³) — volume needs three measurements.
- Using the slant side instead of the perpendicular height for the triangular cross-section.
- Forgetting unit conversions — divide cm³ by 1000 for litres; multiply m³ by 1000 for litres.
