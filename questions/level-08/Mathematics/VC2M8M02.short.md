# VC2M8M02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve problems involving the volume and capacity of right prisms using appropriate units.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Volume measures 3D space. For any right prism: V = area of cross-section × height. Capacity links volume to litres.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate the volume and capacity of right prisms** using appropriate units.
- You should recognise that one formula (V = A × h) works for all right prisms.

## Core content

A **right prism** has a uniform cross-section with straight sides perpendicular to the base — cereal boxes, triangular tents, hexagonal pencils.

**Volume formula:** V = area of cross-section × height (or length). Rectangular prism: V = l × w × h. Triangular prism: V = ½ × b × h × length.

**Other prisms.** Find the cross-section area using the appropriate formula (trapezium, hexagon, L-shape), then multiply by length.

**Volume and capacity conversions.** 1 cm³ = 1 mL. 1 000 cm³ = 1 L. 1 m³ = 1 000 L. A fish tank 60 × 30 × 40 cm = 72 000 cm³ = 72 L.

**Worked example.** Garden path: 8 m × 1.2 m × 0.1 m. Cross-section area = 1.2 × 0.1 = 0.12 m². V = 0.12 × 8 = 0.96 m³. Order 1 m³ of concrete (allowing for waste).

**Units.** Matchbox: cm³. Room: m³. Reservoir: megalitres. Medicine: mL.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Melbourne Water reports reservoir capacity in megalitres. The Gunditjmara eel channels at Budj Bim manage water volumes through engineered weirs and holding ponds — 6,600+ years of volume-management engineering. Cooking across Melbourne's cultures uses capacity daily (mL, L).

## Memorable takeaways
- V = area of cross-section × height — works for all right prisms.
- 1 cm³ = 1 mL. 1 000 cm³ = 1 L. 1 m³ = 1 000 L.
- Identify the cross-section first.
- Convert all dimensions to the same unit before calculating.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Forgot the ½ in a triangular prism." Missing it doubles your answer.
- "Mixed cm and m." Convert to one unit first.
- "Volume and capacity are different." They measure the same space in different units.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Rectangular prism 5 × 4 × 10 cm.* V = 200 cm³.
- *Tent: triangle base 3 m, height 2 m, length 4 m.* V = ½ × 3 × 2 × 4 = 12 m³.
- *Tank 2 × 1 × 0.5 m.* V = 1 m³ = 1 000 L.
- *Trapezoidal gutter (8+12)/2 × 5 cm², length 300 cm.* V = 50 × 300 = 15 000 cm³ = 15 L.
- *Shipping container units?* Cubic metres (m³).
