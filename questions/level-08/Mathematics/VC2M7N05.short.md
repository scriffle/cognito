# VC2M7N05 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Multiply and divide fractions and decimals using efficient mental and written strategies, and digital tools.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:** Multiplying fractions means finding a fraction of a fraction. Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal. Decimals follow the same logic in base ten.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **multiply and divide fractions and decimals** efficiently — choosing between mental, written and digital strategies.
- You should be able to estimate answers and simplify results.

## Core content

**Multiplying fractions:** multiply numerators together and denominators together. 2/3 × 4/5 = 8/15. **Cross-cancel** common factors first to keep numbers small: in 4/9 × 3/8, cancel to get 1/3 × 1/2 = 1/6.

For **mixed numbers**, convert to improper fractions first. 2½ × 1⅓ = 5/2 × 4/3 = 20/6 = 3⅓.

**Dividing fractions:** keep the first fraction, flip the second (its **reciprocal**), then multiply. 3/4 ÷ 2/5 = 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = 1⅞.

**Multiplying decimals:** multiply as whole numbers, then count the total decimal places in both factors and place the point. 0.4 × 0.3 = 0.12 (two decimal places). Always estimate first: 2.5 × 3.4 ≈ 7.5, exact answer 8.50.

**Dividing decimals:** make the divisor a whole number. 3.6 ÷ 0.4 → multiply both by 10 → 36 ÷ 4 = 9.

**Worked example — scaling a recipe.** A recipe for 4 needs 2¾ cups of flour. For 6 people: scale factor = 6/4 = 3/2. Flour: 11/4 × 3/2 = 33/8 = 4⅛ cups. Decimal check: 2.75 × 1.5 = 4.125. Matches.

**Victorian context.** Queen Victoria Market sells produce by fractional and decimal quantities. Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung and Yorta Yorta communities practise equal sharing of harvests — dividing a whole into equal parts is practical fraction work. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Multiply fractions: tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms.
- Divide fractions: keep, flip, multiply.
- Cross-cancel before multiplying to keep numbers small.
- For decimals: count places (multiplication) or equalise the divisor (division).

## Watch out for these traps
- "Of" means multiply, not add — 2/3 of 3/4 is 2/3 × 3/4.
- Flip the second fraction when dividing, not the first.
- Estimate first — if 2.5 × 3.4 gives 85, something is wrong (should be near 7.5).

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *3/4 × 2/7.* 6/28 = 3/14.
- *5/6 × 3/10.* Cross-cancel to get 1/4.
- *5/6 ÷ 1/3.* 5/6 × 3/1 = 5/2 = 2½.
- *1.2 × 0.05.* 0.06 (three decimal places).
- *4.5 ÷ 0.15.* 450 ÷ 15 = 30.
