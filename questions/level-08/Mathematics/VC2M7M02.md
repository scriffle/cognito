## Core content

**1. What is volume?** **Volume** is the amount of space a three-dimensional object occupies, measured in **cubic units** — cm³, m³. One cubic centimetre (1 cm³) is a cube with 1 cm edges. One litre equals 1000 cm³. *Quick check:* How many cubic centimetres in one litre?

**2. What is a right prism?** A **prism** has two identical parallel faces (the **bases** or **cross-sections**) connected by rectangular sides. A **right** prism has sides perpendicular to the bases — it does not lean. If you sliced through a right prism anywhere parallel to the base, you would always get the same shape. *Quick check:* Is a Toblerone box a prism? What shape is its cross-section?

**3. Volume of a rectangular prism.** A rectangular prism (box) has a rectangle as its cross-section. The formula is **V = l × w × h**. A box 8 cm × 5 cm × 3 cm has volume 8 × 5 × 3 = 120 cm³. You can think of it as 3 layers of 8 × 5 = 40 unit cubes each. *Quick check:* A shipping container is 6 m × 2.4 m × 2.6 m. What is its volume?

**4. Volume of a triangular prism.** The cross-section is a triangle with base b and perpendicular height h. The area of that triangle is ½ × b × h. Multiply by the length l of the prism: **V = ½ × b × h × l**. *Quick check:* A triangular prism has base 6 cm, height 4 cm and length 10 cm. What is its volume?

**5. The general rule: V = area of base × length.** This works for every right prism. Find the cross-sectional area (using the appropriate area formula) and multiply by the distance between the two bases. The rectangular and triangular prism formulas are just special cases. *Quick check:* A prism has a cross-section with area 25 cm² and length 14 cm. What is its volume?

**6. Worked example — water trough.**
A cattle trough is a triangular prism: the triangular cross-section has base 80 cm and height 40 cm; the trough is 200 cm long.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Cross-section area: A = ½ × 80 × 40 = 1600 cm².
- Volume: V = 1600 × 200 = 320 000 cm³.
- Convert to litres: 320 000 ÷ 1000 = 320 litres.

**7. Units and conversions.** Choose units to match the problem. For a pencil case, cm³ is sensible. For a swimming pool, m³ or litres. Key conversions: 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1000 litres; 1 litre = 1000 cm³. Always state your unit in the answer. *Quick check:* A fish tank holds 0.06 m³ of water. How many litres is that?

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** Your school locker is a rectangular prism 30 cm wide, 40 cm deep and 120 cm tall. Calculate its volume in cm³ and convert to litres. Could you fit a 50-litre bag inside?
2. **Civic.** The Shire of Surf Coast installs a triangular-prism-shaped drainage channel: cross-section base 60 cm, height 30 cm, channel length 25 m. Calculate the volume in cm³, then convert to litres to determine the channel's capacity after a storm.
3. **Vocational.** A concreter in Werribee pours a rectangular slab 8 m × 4 m × 0.15 m. Calculate the volume in m³ and the number of litres of concrete needed. If concrete costs $260 per m³, find the total cost.
4. **Ethical.** A packaging company claims its triangular-prism box holds 500 cm³. The box has base 8 cm, height 5 cm and length 24 cm. Check the claim. Is it accurate?
5. **Cross-cultural.** A school builds a raised garden bed shaped as a rectangular prism (3 m × 1.2 m × 0.4 m) and fills it with soil. Calculate the volume in m³ and litres. If bagged soil comes in 25-litre bags, how many bags are needed?

## Memorable takeaways
- Volume measures three-dimensional space in cubic units.
- A right prism has a uniform cross-section — the same shape all the way through.
- V = area of base × length works for every right prism.
- Rectangular prism: V = l × w × h. Triangular prism: V = ½ × b × h × l.
- 1 litre = 1000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1000 litres.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Confusing area and volume.** Area is flat (cm²); volume is three-dimensional (cm³). A prism problem needs three measurements, not two.
- **Using the slant height instead of the perpendicular height.** For a triangular-prism cross-section, the height must be at right angles to the base — just as in area calculations.
- **Forgetting unit conversions.** If lengths are in centimetres but the answer needs litres, divide cm³ by 1000. If lengths are in metres and you need litres, multiply m³ by 1000.
- **Mixing up dimensions.** In V = ½ × b × h × l, b and h belong to the triangular cross-section; l is the length of the prism. Do not confuse the triangle's height with the prism's length.
