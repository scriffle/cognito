## Core content

**1. What is a perfect square?** A **perfect square** is a whole number that results from multiplying an integer by itself. For example, 9 = 3 × 3. The first twelve perfect squares are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144. *Quick check:* Is 50 a perfect square? How do you know?

**2. The squaring operation.** When you write 7², you mean 7 × 7 = 49. The small "2" is an **exponent** (or **index**), and the operation is called **squaring** because a square with side length 7 units has an area of 49 square units. *Quick check:* What is 12²?

**3. What is a square root?** The **square root** of a number undoes squaring. If 8² = 64, then √64 = 8. The symbol √ is called a **radical**. Every positive number has a positive square root, but only perfect squares have whole-number square roots. *Quick check:* What is √121?

**4. The relationship between squaring and square roots.** Squaring and taking a square root are **inverse operations** — each undoes the other. If you square 5 to get 25, then √25 brings you back to 5. This mirrors how addition undoes subtraction and multiplication undoes division. *Quick check:* If √n = 9, what is n?

**5. Using squares and square roots in problems.** A square garden bed has area 36 m². What is the side length? Side = √36 = 6 m. If a mosaic uses 225 identical square tiles arranged in a square, each row has √225 = 15 tiles. *Quick check:* A square room has area 81 m². What is the length of one wall?

**6. Worked example: finding an unknown side length.**

A landscape architect at the Royal Botanic Gardens Melbourne is designing a square herb garden with an area of 196 m².

*Follow the thinking:*
- Area of a square = side × side = side².
- So side = √Area = √196.
- List perfect squares: 10² = 100, 11² = 121, 12² = 144, 13² = 169, 14² = 196. Yes — 14² = 196.
- Side length = 14 m.

**7. Estimating square roots of non-perfect squares.** Where does √50 sit? Since 7² = 49 and 8² = 64, √50 is between 7 and 8, closer to 7. You can refine: 7.0² = 49.00, 7.1² = 50.41 — so √50 ≈ 7.07. This estimation skill helps you check calculator answers and judge reasonableness. *Quick check:* Between which two whole numbers does √30 lie?

## Watch out for these traps
- "The square root of 49 is 7 and −7." At this level we take the **principal (positive) square root**, so √49 = 7.
- "64 is a perfect square because it is even." Being even is not enough — 50 is even but not a perfect square. A number is a perfect square because an integer times itself gives that number.
- "You can just halve a number to find its square root." Halving 64 gives 32, not 8. Square root is not the same as halving.
- "√100 = 50." That is halving, not square-rooting. √100 = 10 because 10 × 10 = 100.
