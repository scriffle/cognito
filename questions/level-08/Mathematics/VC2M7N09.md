# VC2M7N09 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M7N09. Recognise, represent and solve problems involving ratios.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**
  - A ratio compares two or more quantities of the same kind.
  - Equivalent ratios behave like equivalent fractions — multiply or divide both parts by the same number.
  - Ratios appear in recipes, maps, mixtures, scales and sharing.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **recognise, represent and solve problems involving ratios**.
- You are learning to simplify ratios, find equivalent ratios, and divide quantities in a given ratio.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Write a ratio from a word problem and simplify it.
- Find equivalent ratios by scaling up or down.
- Divide a quantity into parts according to a given ratio.
- Distinguish ratios from fractions and rates.

## Core content

**1. What is a ratio?** A **ratio** compares two or more quantities measured in the same unit. If a mixture uses 2 cups of flour and 3 cups of sugar, the ratio of flour to sugar is 2 : 3. Order matters: 2 : 3 is not the same as 3 : 2. *Quick check:* A class has 12 girls and 18 boys. Write the ratio of girls to boys.

**2. Simplifying ratios.** Divide all parts by their highest common factor. 12 : 18 → divide both by 6 → 2 : 3. This is the simplest form. *Quick check:* Simplify 45 : 30.

**3. Equivalent ratios.** Multiply or divide every part by the same number. 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 6 : 9 = 10 : 15. This is identical to the logic of equivalent fractions. *Quick check:* Write two ratios equivalent to 5 : 2.

**4. Dividing a quantity in a given ratio.** To share $200 in the ratio 3 : 2, add the parts (3 + 2 = 5), find the value of one part ($200 ÷ 5 = $40), then allocate: 3 × $40 = $120 and 2 × $40 = $80. Check: $120 + $80 = $200. ✓ *Quick check:* Share 56 lollies in the ratio 3 : 4. How many does each person get?

**5. Ratios with three or more parts.** A concrete mix uses sand, gravel and cement in the ratio 3 : 2 : 1. For 60 kg total: 6 parts, each part = 10 kg. Sand = 30 kg, gravel = 20 kg, cement = 10 kg. *Quick check:* A paint mix uses red, blue and white in the ratio 1 : 2 : 5. How much blue for 800 mL total?

**6. Worked example: scaling a recipe.**

A school canteen recipe for damper makes 8 serves using flour and water in the ratio 5 : 2 (by mass). The total dough mass is 700 g.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Total parts = 5 + 2 = 7.
- One part = 700 g ÷ 7 = 100 g.
- Flour = 5 × 100 = 500 g. Water = 2 × 100 = 200 g.
- For 16 serves (double): multiply both by 2 → flour = 1 000 g, water = 400 g.
- Check ratio: 1 000 : 400 = 5 : 2. ✓

**7. Ratio vs fraction vs rate.** A ratio compares like quantities (flour to sugar). A **fraction** is a part of a whole (2/5 of the mixture is flour). A **rate** compares different units (km per hour). They are related but not identical: the ratio 2 : 3 means the fraction of flour is 2/5 and the fraction of sugar is 3/5. *Quick check:* If the ratio of cats to dogs in a shelter is 4 : 7, what fraction of the animals are cats?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The NGV (National Gallery of Victoria) uses scale ratios to reproduce artworks in catalogues. Melbourne's coffee culture relies on milk-to-espresso ratios — a flat white is typically 1 : 5 espresso to steamed milk. Engineers Australia publishes concrete-mix ratios used on Victorian construction sites.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Traditional food preparation across many First Nations involves proportional mixing — grinding seeds, combining ochre pigments in specific proportions, and mixing resins. The Gunditjmara eel-trap system at Budj Bim channels water through weirs whose dimensions maintain flow ratios suited to eel migration. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Cuisines represented in Melbourne — Vietnamese pho broth, Lebanese za'atar, Indian spice blends — all rely on precise ingredient ratios. Exploring cultural recipes is a practical way to learn ratio thinking.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A cordial bottle says to mix concentrate and water in the ratio 1 : 4. You want to make 1.5 litres. How much concentrate and how much water?
2. **Civic.** The City of Casey plans a new park with grassed area to garden beds in the ratio 7 : 3. The total area is 5 000 m². Calculate each area.
3. **Vocational.** A concreter in Geelong mixes sand, gravel and cement in the ratio 3 : 2 : 1 for a driveway. She needs 1 800 kg of mix. How much of each material?
4. **Ethical.** Two students contribute to a joint project in the ratio of hours worked (8 : 12). Should the marks be split in the same ratio? Discuss fairness.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A Vietnamese bakery in Richmond makes bánh mì dough using a flour-to-water ratio of 5 : 3. For 2 400 g of dough, how much flour and water?

## Memorable takeaways
- A ratio compares quantities of the same kind — order matters.
- Simplify by dividing all parts by the HCF.
- To divide in a ratio: add parts, find one part, allocate.
- Equivalent ratios scale every part by the same factor.
- Ratios, fractions and rates are related but distinct.

## Stretch yourself
A landscape architect is designing a sensory garden for a Melbourne primary school. The garden bed, path and water feature are in the ratio 5 : 3 : 2. The total area is 120 m². Calculate the area of each section. The budget is allocated in the same ratio from a total of $45 000. Find each budget allocation. If the water feature's area is later increased by 20% (taken from the garden bed), find the new ratio in simplest form. Show all working and explain each step. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "A ratio of 2 : 3 means 2 out of 3." No — it means 2 out of 5 total parts. The fraction is 2/5, not 2/3.
- "You can add ratios like fractions." Ratios do not add in the same way. 2 : 3 plus 2 : 3 is not 4 : 6 in the addition sense — if you double a recipe you still have the ratio 2 : 3, just with larger quantities.
- "The order doesn't matter." 2 : 3 (flour to sugar) is different from 3 : 2 (sugar to flour). Always state what each number refers to.
- "Ratios must be whole numbers." Ratios like 1.5 : 2 are valid but are usually converted to whole numbers (3 : 4) by multiplying through.
