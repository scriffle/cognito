# VC2M7P01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Identify the sample space for single-stage experiments; assign probabilities to the possible outcomes and predict relative frequencies for related experiments.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** The sample space is the complete list of possible outcomes. Probability ranges from 0 to 1. Predicted frequency connects theory to expected results.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **identify sample spaces**, **assign probabilities** and **predict relative frequencies** for single-stage experiments.
- You should be able to move between theoretical probability and expected experimental results.

## Core content

A **single-stage experiment** is one action with defined outcomes — rolling a die, flipping a coin, drawing a card. The **sample space** (S) lists every possible outcome. For a die: S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.

**Assigning probabilities.** If outcomes are equally likely: P(outcome) = 1 ÷ total outcomes. A fair die: P(any number) = 1/6. Rules: every probability is between 0 and 1; all probabilities sum to 1.

**Events.** An event is a subset of the sample space. "Even number" = {2, 4, 6}. P(even) = 3/6 = 1/2.

**Expressing probability.** P = 2/10 = 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%. Fractions are exact; decimals and percentages help comparison.

**Predicted relative frequency.** Expected count = probability × number of trials. A die rolled 300 times: expected threes = 1/6 × 300 = 50. Actual results will vary — that variation is randomness, not error.

**Worked example.** Fair die, 300 rolls. P(3) = 1/6. Expected frequency = 50. Interpretation: about 50, with variation expected.

**Victorian and cultural context.** Bureau of Meteorology forecasts state "70% chance of rain" — direct probability communication. Traditional First Nations games involving chance (throwing games, seed games) demonstrate engagement with randomness and fairness. Dice games across cultures — Korean yut, Indian pachisi — show universal interest in formalising chance.

## Memorable takeaways
- Always list the sample space first.
- P(event) = favourable ÷ total (equally likely outcomes).
- Probabilities sum to 1.
- Predicted frequency = probability × trials.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Two outcomes means probability is always 1/2." Only if equally likely — a meteor strike tomorrow is not 50/50.
- "After 5 heads, tails is due." The gambler's fallacy — each flip is independent.
- "Predicted frequency is exact." It is an expectation; actual results will spread around it.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Spinner S = {R, B, G, Y}. P(Blue)?* 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%.
- *Bag: 3 red, 2 blue, 5 green. P(blue)?* 2/10 = 1/5.
- *P(greater than 4) on a fair die?* 2/6 = 1/3.
- *Die rolled 300 times, expected threes?* 50.
- *Is two-heads-in-a-row probability 1/2?* No — sample space for two flips is {HH, HT, TH, TT}. P(HH) = 1/4.
