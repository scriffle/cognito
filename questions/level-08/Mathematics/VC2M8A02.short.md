# VC2M8A02 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Graph linear relations on the Cartesian plane using digital tools where appropriate; solve linear equations and one-variable inequalities using graphical and algebraic techniques; verify solutions by substitution.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** A linear relation produces a straight-line graph. You can solve equations algebraically or graphically. Inequalities describe a range of solutions.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **graph linear relations** and **solve equations and inequalities** using graphical and algebraic methods.
- You should be able to verify every solution by substitution.

## Core content

A **linear relation** has the form *y = mx + c*: *m* is the **gradient** (steepness), *c* is the **y-intercept** (where the line crosses the y-axis). Create a table of at least three values, plot the points and draw the straight line.

**Solving equations graphically.** To solve *2x + 1 = 7*, graph *y = 2x + 1* and *y = 7*. The x-coordinate where they intersect is the solution: *x = 3*.

**Solving algebraically.** Inverse operations: *2x + 1 = 7 → 2x = 6 → x = 3*. Always verify: *2(3) + 1 = 7*.

**Inequalities** use <, >, ≤ or ≥. Solve like an equation, but **reverse the sign** if you multiply or divide by a negative. *2x + 1 > 7 → x > 3*. Show on a number line: open circle at 3, arrow right.

**Worked example — myki fare comparison.** Myki Money: *C₁ = 2t*. Myki Pass: *C₂ = 10*. Graph both. They cross at *t = 5*. Inequality: *2t < 10 → t < 5*. Myki Money is cheaper for fewer than 5 trips. Verify: *2(4) = 8 < 10*. *Follow the thinking:* model → graph → find crossover → write inequality → verify.

**Digital tools.** GeoGebra and Desmos let you graph instantly, adjust viewing windows and compare multiple lines. Use them to check hand-drawn graphs.

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung seasonal knowledge tracks how resources change over time — relationships between time and abundance. PTV myki structures, Melbourne Water usage tiers and Victorian electricity tariffs all generate linear relations students encounter in daily life.

## Memorable takeaways
- Linear relation: *y = mx + c* graphs as a straight line.
- Gradient *m* = steepness; y-intercept *c* = where line crosses y-axis.
- Solve graphically (read intersection) or algebraically (inverse operations).
- Inequalities give a range — show on a number line.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Multiply by a negative, keep the sign." You must reverse the inequality sign.
- "Two points define the line reliably." Plot three — the third confirms correctness.
- "Gradient and y-intercept are the same thing." Gradient is steepness; y-intercept is starting height.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *y = 3x − 2: gradient and intercept.* Gradient 3, y-intercept −2.
- *Solve 5x − 3 = 22.* 5x = 25 → x = 5. Check: 5(5) − 3 = 22.
- *Solve 3x − 4 ≤ 8.* 3x ≤ 12 → x ≤ 4. Closed circle at 4, arrow left.
- *Myki crossover.* Lines meet at t = 5; myki Money cheaper for fewer than 5 trips.
- *Solve −x + 4 = 1 graphically.* Lines y = −x + 4 and y = 1 meet at x = 3.
