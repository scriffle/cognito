# VC2M7N07 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M7N07. Find percentages of quantities and express one quantity as a percentage of another, with and without digital tools.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**
  - A percentage is a fraction out of 100 — a universal way to compare parts of different wholes.
  - Finding a percentage of a quantity means multiplying by a decimal or fraction.
  - Expressing one quantity as a percentage of another means dividing and converting.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Critical and Creative Thinking; Numeracy; Ethical Capability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate percentages of quantities** and to **express one quantity as a percentage of another**.
- You are learning that percentages make comparisons fair, even when the totals are different.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Convert between percentages, fractions and decimals fluently.
- Calculate a given percentage of a quantity (e.g. 15% of $240).
- Express one quantity as a percentage of another (e.g. 36 out of 150 as a percentage).
- Use mental shortcuts, written methods and digital tools as appropriate.

## Core content

**1. What is a percentage?** **Per cent** means "per hundred." 25% means 25 out of 100, which equals the fraction 25/100 = 1/4, or the decimal 0.25. Every percentage can be written as a fraction and a decimal. *Quick check:* Write 40% as a fraction in simplest form and as a decimal.

**2. Key conversions.** Percentage → decimal: divide by 100 (move the point two places left). 35% = 0.35. Decimal → percentage: multiply by 100. 0.08 = 8%. Fraction → percentage: divide numerator by denominator, then multiply by 100. 3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%. *Quick check:* Convert 5/6 to a percentage (round to one decimal place).

**3. Finding a percentage of a quantity.** Multiply the quantity by the decimal (or fraction) form of the percentage. 15% of $240 = 0.15 × 240 = $36. Mental shortcut: find 10% first ($24), then half of that for 5% ($12), then add: $36. *Quick check:* Find 12% of 350.

**4. Expressing one quantity as a percentage of another.** Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. "What percentage is 45 out of 180?" → 45 ÷ 180 = 0.25 → 0.25 × 100 = 25%. The formula is: percentage = (part ÷ whole) × 100. *Quick check:* Express 27 out of 60 as a percentage.

**5. Mental strategies for common percentages.** 50% = halve. 25% = halve twice. 10% = divide by 10. 1% = divide by 100. Build other percentages from these: 15% = 10% + 5%. 33⅓% = divide by 3. These shortcuts are faster than reaching for a calculator. *Quick check:* Find 35% of 200 using a mental strategy.

**6. Worked example — exam results.**
A student scores 68 out of 80 on a maths test and 54 out of 75 on a science test. Which is the better result?

*Follow the thinking:*
- Maths: 68 ÷ 80 = 0.85 = 85%.
- Science: 54 ÷ 75 = 0.72 = 72%.
- Maths is the stronger result (85% > 72%), even though the raw scores are hard to compare directly because the tests had different totals.

**7. Percentages greater than 100%.** If a quantity increases beyond the original, the percentage exceeds 100. A shop that sold 120 items last month and 156 this month has sold 156/120 × 100 = 130% of last month's total — a 30% increase. *Quick check:* A plant was 40 cm tall and is now 52 cm. Express the current height as a percentage of the original.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) publishes Victorian data in percentages — unemployment rates, population growth, school completion rates. GST in Australia is 10% of the price; every receipt shows this calculation. VicHealth reports use percentages to compare health outcomes across local government areas. The Reserve Bank of Australia sets the cash rate as a percentage that affects mortgage repayments across Victoria.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** The Yoorrook Justice Commission reports use percentages to describe disparities in health, education and justice outcomes for First Nations Victorians — understanding these percentages is essential to reading the evidence. Aboriginal community-controlled organisations like the Victorian Aboriginal Health Service track health indicators as percentages to measure progress and advocate for resources. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multicultural communities across Melbourne encounter percentages in everyday finance — interest rates on savings, discount sales, nutritional labels showing percentage daily intake. Understanding percentages protects consumers and supports informed decision-making regardless of background.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A pair of shoes originally costs $120. A sale offers 25% off. After the discount, a 10% GST is added. Calculate the final price. Is the final price the same as applying 15% off the original? Explain.
2. **Civic.** The City of Greater Geelong reports that 78% of its 260 000 residents are satisfied with local parks. How many residents is that? If the target is 85%, how many more residents need to be satisfied?
3. **Vocational.** A café owner in Brunswick finds that 38 out of 200 customers ordered the new menu item. Express this as a percentage. If the target is 25% and the café serves 200 customers per day, has the target been met?
4. **Ethical.** A news headline says "Crime up 50%!" The actual numbers are 4 incidents last year and 6 this year. Discuss whether the percentage is technically correct and whether it gives a fair picture.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A school with 850 students surveys language backgrounds: 42% English only, 18% Vietnamese, 12% Mandarin, 8% Arabic, and the rest other languages. Calculate the number of students in each group and the percentage and number in the "other" group.

## Memorable takeaways
- Per cent means per hundred — a universal comparison tool.
- To find a percentage of a quantity: multiply by the decimal form.
- To express a part as a percentage: (part ÷ whole) × 100.
- Mental shortcuts (10%, 1%, halving) build speed and number sense.
- Percentages make unequal totals comparable — that is their power.

## Stretch yourself
A school in Ballarat runs a sustainability audit. Last year the school used 120 000 litres of water. This year, after installing rainwater tanks, usage dropped to 97 200 litres. Calculate the decrease as a percentage of last year's usage. The school also reduced electricity use from 85 000 kWh to 72 250 kWh. Calculate that percentage decrease. The school's target is a 20% reduction across both resources. Determine whether the target was met for each, and calculate the combined percentage reduction if you treat water and electricity as equally weighted. Show all working. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Percentage of vs percentage off.** 25% of $120 is $30 (the discount). 25% off $120 is $120 − $30 = $90 (the sale price). Read carefully.
- **Applying percentage changes sequentially is not the same as adding them.** 25% off then 10% on is not the same as 15% off. The 10% is applied to the already-reduced price.
- **Forgetting which is the "whole."** In "what percentage is 45 of 180," the 180 is the whole (denominator). Putting them the wrong way around gives a very different answer.
- **Small numbers, big percentages.** A rise from 2 to 3 is a 50% increase, but the change is just 1 unit. Always consider the absolute numbers alongside the percentage.

## Try drawing this

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *40% as fraction and decimal.* 40/100 = 2/5; 0.40.
2. *5/6 as a percentage.* 5 ÷ 6 = 0.8333… = 83.3%.
3. *12% of 350.* 0.12 × 350 = 42.
4. *27 out of 60 as a percentage.* 27 ÷ 60 = 0.45 = 45%.

6. *Plant height as percentage.* 52 ÷ 40 = 1.3 = 130%.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Shoes.* 25% off $120 = $30 discount → $90. GST 10% of $90 = $9 → $99. If you applied 15% off: 0.15 × $120 = $18 → $102. Not the same — because 10% GST is calculated on the reduced price, not the original.
2. *Geelong parks.* 78% of 260 000 = 202 800 satisfied. For 85%: 0.85 × 260 000 = 221 000. Need 221 000 − 202 800 = 18 200 more.
3. *Brunswick café.* 38 ÷ 200 = 0.19 = 19%. Target is 25%, so 19% < 25% — target not met.
4. *Crime headline.* Technically correct (6 is 50% more than 4). But 2 extra incidents in a large population may not represent a meaningful trend. Absolute numbers matter alongside the percentage.
5. *Language survey.* English: 0.42 × 850 = 357. Vietnamese: 0.18 × 850 = 153. Mandarin: 0.12 × 850 = 102. Arabic: 0.08 × 850 = 68. Other: 100% − 80% = 20% → 0.20 × 850 = 170.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Water decrease: 120 000 − 97 200 = 22 800. Percentage: 22 800 ÷ 120 000 × 100 = 19%. Electricity decrease: 85 000 − 72 250 = 12 750. Percentage: 12 750 ÷ 85 000 × 100 = 15%. Water target (20%): not quite met (19%). Electricity target: not met (15%). Combined equally weighted: (19% + 15%) ÷ 2 = 17%. Overall target of 20% not met, but water is close. The school could target further electricity savings.
