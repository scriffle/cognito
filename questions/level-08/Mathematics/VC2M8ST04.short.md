# VC2M8ST04 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Plan and conduct statistical investigations involving samples of a population; use ethical and fair methods to make inferences about the population and report findings, acknowledging uncertainty.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** A well-designed investigation uses fair sampling to infer things about the population. Inference means going beyond your sample. Acknowledging uncertainty is good reasoning, not weakness.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **plan and conduct statistical investigations** using samples, **make inferences** about the population, and **acknowledge uncertainty**.
- You should apply ethical and fair methods throughout.

## Core content

**Statistical inference** means using sample results to draw conclusions about the whole population. If 72% of a random sample prefer a new timetable, you infer approximately 72% of the whole school does — but acknowledge it might not be exact.

**Fair investigation:** clear question, defined population, random sampling (everyone has a chance), appropriate sample size, ethical practices. **Ethical methods:** informed consent, anonymity, no harm, voluntary participation, honest reporting.

**Conducting:** collect according to plan, check for errors, note the **response rate** (low rates introduce bias even in random samples).

**Making inferences:** State clearly: "Based on our sample of [n], we estimate approximately [finding] for the population." Add uncertainty: "A different sample could give a slightly different result."

**Worked example.** "How many hours per week do Year 8 students spend on homework?" Random sample of 30, 27 responded. Median = 5 h, mean = 5.8 h, positively skewed. Inference: typical student does 5–6 hours/week. Uncertainty: true median likely between 4 and 7 hours.

**Victorian context.** The ABS reports margins of error on all surveys. The Yoorrook Justice Commission gathers data alongside community testimony — acknowledging statistics alone cannot capture the full picture. AIATSIS guidelines set ethical standards for research with First Nations communities.

## Memorable takeaways
- Inference = going beyond the sample to conclude about the population.
- Always say "Based on our sample of [n], we estimate..."
- Acknowledge uncertainty: different samples give slightly different results.
- Ethical research: consent, anonymity, voluntary, honest.
- Low response rates undermine even random samples.

## Watch out for these traps
- Stating results as certainties — always use "estimate" and "approximately."
- Ignoring non-response — those who refuse may differ from those who respond.
- Confusing sample statistics with population parameters — they are estimates, not the same thing.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Describing vs inferring?* Describing reports the sample; inferring draws a conclusion about the population.
- *Why say "approximately"?* Sampling variability means a different sample might give a different result.
- *Response rate of 20/50 — problem?* Yes — responders may differ from non-responders, introducing bias.
- *Better communication?* "Based on our sample of 40, we estimate around 65–70%" beats "68% of students like maths."
- *English-only survey limitation?* Excludes non-English speakers; inferences apply only to English-proficient respondents.
