# VC2M7N06 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Use the 4 operations with positive rational numbers, including fractions and decimals, to solve problems using efficient mental and written calculation strategies.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:** The four operations work together in multi-step problems. Fractions, decimals and whole numbers are all rational numbers. Choosing the right operation and strategy matters as much as the arithmetic.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **combine addition, subtraction, multiplication and division with fractions and decimals** to solve multi-step problems.
- You should be able to estimate, calculate and check for reasonableness.

## Core content

A **rational number** is any number that can be written as a fraction a/b (b ≠ 0). This includes whole numbers (5 = 5/1), fractions (3/4) and decimals (0.75). At Level 7 you work with positive rational numbers.

**Adding and subtracting fractions** requires a common denominator. 2/3 + 1/4: convert to 8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12. For decimals, line up the decimal points.

**BODMAS** applies to fractions and decimals: brackets first, then orders, then multiplication and division (left to right), then addition and subtraction. In 1/2 + 3/4 × 2/3, multiply first (3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2), then add (1/2 + 1/2 = 1).

When a problem mixes fractions and decimals, convert to a common form. ½ of $7.60 = 0.5 × 7.60 = $3.80.

**Worked example — school fundraiser.** 150 sausages at $2.50 = $375. 80 drinks at $1.75 = $140. Total income: $515. Total costs: $185.50 + $52.00 = $237.50. Profit: $515 − $237.50 = $277.50. Profit per item (230 sold): $277.50 ÷ 230 ≈ $1.21.

**Estimation** catches errors. Round to friendly numbers before calculating. If your answer is wildly different from the estimate, recheck.

**Victorian context.** Melbourne's myki system runs on decimal subtraction — every trip deducts a fare from your balance. Yorta Yorta communities along the Murray divide fish catches among families, a practical multi-step problem of division and sometimes fractional shares. ## Memorable takeaways
- The four operations work the same for fractions and decimals as for whole numbers.
- BODMAS applies — multiply and divide before adding and subtracting.
- Convert to a common form when fractions and decimals are mixed.
- Estimate first, calculate second, check third.

## Watch out for these traps
- Ignoring BODMAS with fractions — multiply before you add.
- Rounding too early in multi-step problems — keep full precision until the end.
- Mixing up "of" (multiply) and "off" (subtract) — ¼ of $20 is $5; ¼ off $20 is $15.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Is 2.5 rational?* Yes. 2.5 = 5/2.
- *3/5 − 1/4.* 12/20 − 5/20 = 7/20.
- *0.5 + 0.25 × 4.* Multiply first: 1. Then add: 1.5.
- *¾ of $12.40.* 0.75 × 12.40 = $9.30.
- *Estimate 4.9 × 3.1.* Estimate: 15. Exact: 15.19.
