# VC2M8M07 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving ratios and rates, including distance-time problems for travel at a constant speed and financial contexts; formulate problems; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, reviewing the appropriateness of the model.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Modelling turns messy real problems into manageable calculations. Distance-time and financial rates follow the same cycle: formulate, solve, interpret, communicate, review.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to use **mathematical modelling** to solve problems involving **ratios, rates, distance-time and financial contexts**.
- You should be able to formulate, solve, interpret, communicate and review your model.

## Core content

The modelling cycle: **Formulate → Solve → Interpret → Communicate → Review**. The review step asks: "Does this make sense? What did I assume?"

**Distance-time:** For constant speed, **D = S × T**. Rearrange for any unknown. For multi-stage journeys, calculate each stage separately. **Average speed = total distance ÷ total time** (not the average of the speeds — more time at slower speed pulls it down).

**Financial rates:** Percentages are rates per 100. Discount: 20% off $85 → pay $68. **Simple interest: I = PRT** (Principal × Rate as decimal × Time). $500 at 3% for 2 years → $30 interest.

**Worked example.** Melbourne to Ballarat (115 km) at 80 km/h, car uses 9 L/100 km, petrol $1.92/L. Time = 115 ÷ 80 ≈ 1 h 26 min. Fuel = 9 × 115 ÷ 100 = 10.35 L. Cost = 10.35 × $1.92 ≈ $19.87. Review: constant 80 km/h is optimistic (traffic, speed zones). Model gives a lower-bound estimate.

**Victorian context.** V/Line timetables use distance-time models. VicRoads travel-time signs. The First Home Owner Grant and stamp duty are financial-rate models. MoneySmart (ASIC) provides student financial literacy resources.

## Memorable takeaways
- D = S × T — rearrange for any unknown.
- Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.
- Percentage: multiply by the decimal (25% = 0.25).
- Simple interest: I = PRT.
- Always review: "Does this make sense? What did I assume?"

## Watch out for these traps
- Averaging speeds directly — more time at slower speed pulls the average down.
- Successive discounts are not additive — 50% then 20% off = 60% off, not 70%.
- Forgetting to convert percentage to decimal — 4.5% is 0.045, not 4.5.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Train at 90 km/h for 2.5 h.* D = 225 km.
- *25% off $120.* Pay $90.
- *$1 000 at 4.5% for 3 years.* I = $135.
- *Cycling 4.5 km at 18 km/h then 4.5 km at 12 km/h.* Average speed = 9 ÷ 0.625 = 14.4 km/h.
- *50% off then 20% off $100.* Final = $40 (60% total discount, not 70%).
