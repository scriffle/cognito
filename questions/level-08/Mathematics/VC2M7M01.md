# VC2M7M01 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M7M01. Establish the formulas for areas of rectangles, triangles and parallelograms and use these in problem-solving.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Measurement).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Area measures how much flat surface a shape covers.
  - Every area formula for triangles and parallelograms traces back to the rectangle.
  - Understanding where a formula comes from means you can use it confidently in new situations.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Critical and Creative Thinking; Numeracy; Sustainability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **establish (derive) and apply the area formulas** for rectangles, triangles and parallelograms.
- You are learning that formulas are not rules to memorise blindly — they come from reasoning you can see and repeat.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Explain why A = l × w gives the area of a rectangle.
- Show how cutting a rectangle gives the triangle formula A = ½ × b × h.
- Show how reshaping a parallelogram gives A = b × h.
- Solve area problems involving composite shapes and real-world contexts.

## Core content

**1. What is area?** **Area** is the amount of surface inside a flat (two-dimensional) shape, measured in **square units** — cm², m², km². One square centimetre is a 1 cm × 1 cm square. *Quick check:* Why is area measured in square units rather than plain centimetres?

**2. Area of a rectangle.** A rectangle 5 cm long and 3 cm wide can be covered by 5 × 3 = 15 unit squares. The formula is **A = l × w** (length × width). This works because you are counting rows of unit squares. *Quick check:* A rectangle is 12 m by 7 m. What is its area?

**3. Area of a parallelogram.** A **parallelogram** has two pairs of parallel sides, but the sides are not at right angles. Imagine slicing off the slanted triangle at one end and sliding it to the other end — you get a rectangle. The base stays the same and the height is the perpendicular distance between the parallel sides. So **A = b × h**. *Quick check:* A parallelogram has base 9 cm and height 4 cm. What is its area?

**4. Area of a triangle.** Draw any triangle. Copy it, flip the copy and attach it along one side — you form a parallelogram. The triangle is exactly half that parallelogram. So **A = ½ × b × h**, where b is the base and h is the **perpendicular height** (the shortest distance from the base to the opposite vertex). *Quick check:* A triangle has base 10 cm and height 6 cm. What is its area?

**5. Perpendicular height matters.** The height must be measured at right angles to the base, not along a slanted side. In a right-angled triangle, one of the shorter sides is the height. In other triangles, you may need to draw the height inside or outside the triangle. *Quick check:* A triangle has sides 5 cm, 12 cm and 13 cm, with a right angle between the 5 cm and 12 cm sides. Which two measurements do you use for base and height?

**6. Worked example — composite shape.**
A school wall display board is shaped like a rectangle (120 cm × 80 cm) with a triangular extension on top (base 120 cm, height 30 cm).

*Follow the thinking:*
- Rectangle area: A₁ = 120 × 80 = 9600 cm².
- Triangle area: A₂ = ½ × 120 × 30 = 1800 cm².
- Total area: 9600 + 1800 = 11 400 cm².
- Convert to m²: 11 400 ÷ 10 000 = 1.14 m².

**7. Problem-solving strategies.** For composite shapes, break them into rectangles, triangles and parallelograms. Find each area, then add (or subtract if there is a hole). Label your diagram, show the formula, substitute, calculate and include units at every step. *Quick check:* A rectangular garden bed (8 m × 3 m) has a triangular flower patch removed (base 3 m, height 2 m). What area remains?

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You want to cover a bedroom wall (3.2 m wide, 2.4 m high) with paint. The wall has a rectangular window (1.2 m × 0.8 m). Calculate the area to be painted.
2. **Civic.** The City of Maribyrnong plans a triangular community garden on a corner block with base 18 m and perpendicular height 12 m. Calculate the area and the number of 1 m² raised beds that fit.
3. **Vocational.** A carpenter in Ballarat cuts a parallelogram-shaped tabletop with base 1.5 m and height 0.8 m from a rectangular sheet 1.5 m × 1.0 m. What area of timber is wasted?
4. **Ethical.** A developer claims a block of land is 500 m². The block is a parallelogram with base 25 m and height 18 m. Check the claim and explain whether it is accurate.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A school quilt project combines rectangular panels (20 cm × 15 cm) and right-angled triangular panels (base 15 cm, height 20 cm). Calculate the area of one rectangle and one triangle, and find the total area if the quilt uses 12 rectangles and 8 triangles.

## Memorable takeaways
- Area counts how many unit squares fit inside a shape.
- Rectangle: A = l × w. Parallelogram: A = b × h. Triangle: A = ½ × b × h.
- Every formula traces back to the rectangle — you can derive them, not just memorise them.
- Height is always perpendicular to the base.
- For composite shapes: break, calculate, combine.

## Stretch yourself
A school in Shepparton redesigns its courtyard. The courtyard is a rectangle 20 m × 15 m. In one corner, a triangular garden bed is marked out (base 6 m along one wall, perpendicular height 4 m). In the opposite corner, a parallelogram-shaped stage area is built (base 5 m, height 3 m). The remaining area is paved at $38 per square metre. Calculate the paving area and total paving cost. If the budget is $10 000, determine whether the project is within budget and by how much. Show all formulas, substitutions and units. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Using the slant side instead of the perpendicular height.** The height of a triangle or parallelogram must be at right angles to the base. The slant side is longer and gives a wrong answer.
- **Forgetting to halve for triangles.** A triangle is half a parallelogram — leaving out the ½ doubles the answer.
- **Mixing up units.** If lengths are in centimetres, area is in cm². If you need m², convert lengths to metres first or divide cm² by 10 000.
- **Adding instead of subtracting for cut-outs.** When a shape has a hole or a piece removed, subtract the removed area from the total.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why square units?* Area measures two-dimensional space — length × width — so the unit is squared (cm × cm = cm²).
2. *Rectangle 12 m × 7 m.* A = 12 × 7 = 84 m².
3. *Parallelogram base 9, height 4.* A = 9 × 4 = 36 cm².
4. *Triangle base 10, height 6.* A = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm².
5. *Right-angled triangle 5-12-13.* Use the two sides forming the right angle: base 12 cm, height 5 cm (or vice versa). A = ½ × 12 × 5 = 30 cm².
6. *Garden bed remaining area.* Rectangle: 8 × 3 = 24 m². Triangle: ½ × 3 × 2 = 3 m². Remaining: 24 − 3 = 21 m².

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Bedroom wall.* Wall: 3.2 × 2.4 = 7.68 m². Window: 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 m². Paint area: 7.68 − 0.96 = 6.72 m².
2. *Maribyrnong garden.* A = ½ × 18 × 12 = 108 m². Up to 108 raised beds of 1 m² each (in practice fewer due to paths).
3. *Ballarat carpenter.* Parallelogram: 1.5 × 0.8 = 1.2 m². Rectangle sheet: 1.5 × 1.0 = 1.5 m². Waste: 1.5 − 1.2 = 0.3 m².
4. *Developer claim.* A = 25 × 18 = 450 m², not 500 m². The claim overstates the area by 50 m².
5. *School quilt.* Rectangle: 20 × 15 = 300 cm². Triangle: ½ × 15 × 20 = 150 cm². Total: 12 × 300 + 8 × 150 = 3600 + 1200 = 4800 cm².

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Courtyard: 20 × 15 = 300 m². Triangle garden: ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 m². Parallelogram stage: 5 × 3 = 15 m². Paving area: 300 − 12 − 15 = 273 m². Cost: 273 × $38 = $10 374. Over budget by $374. The school could slightly reduce the paved area or seek a lower quote.
