# VC2M7M06 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving ratios of lengths, areas and volumes; formulate problems, interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, justifying choices made about the representation.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** When you scale a length by factor k, area scales by k² and volume by k³. Mathematical modelling means formulating, solving, interpreting and communicating.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to use **mathematical modelling** to solve practical problems involving **ratios of lengths, areas and volumes**.
- You should be able to formulate, solve, interpret and communicate solutions in context.

## Core content

**Mathematical modelling** translates a real-world problem into mathematics, solves it, then interprets the result back in context. The cycle: **formulate → solve → interpret → communicate**.

A **ratio** compares two quantities. In a 1 : 24 scale model, every length is 1/24 of the real length. But **areas scale by k²** and **volumes scale by k³**, because area involves two dimensions and volume involves three.

So if k = 2 (you double every length): area becomes 2² = 4 times larger; volume becomes 2³ = 8 times larger. This is why doubling a garden bed's dimensions does not just double the soil you need — it multiplies it by eight.

**Worked example.** A school garden bed is 2 m × 1 m × 0.3 m deep. All lengths are doubled (k = 2). New area = 4 × 2 = 8 m². New soil volume = 8 × 0.6 = 4.8 m³. The school needs eight times the soil, not twice.

Always **state your assumptions** (e.g. "I modelled the tank as a cylinder"), include **units** at every step, and **check** whether the answer is reasonable.

**Victorian context.** Architects at the Melbourne School of Design build scale models; Melbourne Water uses models to plan reservoir capacity. The engineered eel channels at Budj Bim on Gunditjmara Country involve proportioning of channels, pools and walls — practical scaling refined over millennia.

## Memorable takeaways
- Scale length by k → area by k² → volume by k³.
- Modelling cycle: formulate, solve, interpret, communicate.
- Doubling lengths gives 4× area and 8× volume.
- Always state assumptions and units.

## Watch out for these traps
- Thinking area doubles when length doubles — it quadruples (k²).
- Thinking volume doubles when length doubles — it multiplies by eight (k³).
- Giving a number without context — always interpret in the real situation.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Map: 3 cm at 1 : 50 000.* 3 × 50 000 = 150 000 cm = 1.5 km.
- *Plan area: 6 cm² at 1 : 100.* 6 × 100² = 60 000 cm² = 6 m².
- *Model tank: 0.5 L at 1 : 10.* 0.5 × 10³ = 500 L.
- *Garden bed with k = 2.* Area × 4, volume × 8.
- *Why state assumptions?* So others can check if your model is reasonable.
