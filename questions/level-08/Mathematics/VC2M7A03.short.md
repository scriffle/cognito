# VC2M7A03 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Solve one-variable linear equations of increasing complexity with natural number solutions; verify equation solutions by substitution.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** A linear equation states two expressions are equal. Solving means finding the variable's value. Substitution checks the answer.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **solve one-variable linear equations** where solutions are natural numbers.
- You should be able to verify every solution by substituting it back into the original equation.

## Core content

An **equation** says two things are equal: *3x + 5 = 20*. It is **linear** because the variable has no power higher than 1. A **solution** is the value that makes both sides equal.

**One-step equations** use a single inverse operation. *x + 7 = 12* → subtract 7 → *x = 5*. *4n = 28* → divide by 4 → *n = 7*.

**Two-step equations** undo operations in reverse order. *2x + 3 = 17* → subtract 3 → *2x = 14* → divide by 2 → *x = 7*.

**Equations with brackets.** Expand first, then solve. *3(n + 2) = 21* → *3n + 6 = 21* → *3n = 15* → *n = 5*. Or divide both sides by 3 first: *n + 2 = 7* → *n = 5*.

**Worked example — Melbourne Aquarium tickets.** A school buys 24 student tickets at $n each plus a $30 booking fee. Total: $582. Equation: *24n + 30 = 582*. Solve: *24n = 552 → n = 23*. Check: *24(23) + 30 = 582*. Confirmed. *Follow the thinking:* translate → write equation → isolate variable → verify.

**Verifying by substitution** catches arithmetic errors. Always plug your answer back into the original equation. If both sides match, the solution is correct.

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung and Bunurong/Boon Wurrung seasonal calendars encoded timing relationships — quantitative reasoning with knowns and unknowns. Melbourne Zoo pricing, PTV myki fares and AFL ticket bundles all generate one-variable equations in daily life.

## Memorable takeaways
- An equation says two sides are equal.
- Inverse operations undo what was done to the variable.
- Work step by step — reverse the order of operations.
- Always verify by substitution.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Do both sides differently." Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.
- "Skip the check." Substitution catches errors before they reach your final answer.
- "3(n + 2) equals 3n + 2." You must distribute: 3n + 6.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Solve m − 9 = 15.* Add 9: m = 24.
- *Solve 5y − 4 = 31.* Add 4: 5y = 35. Divide by 5: y = 7.
- *Solve 4(x − 3) = 20.* Expand: 4x − 12 = 20 → 4x = 32 → x = 8.
- *Aquarium tickets.* 24n + 30 = 582 → n = 23. Check: 24(23) + 30 = 582.
- *Why verify?* Substitution confirms the solution satisfies the original equation and catches arithmetic mistakes.
