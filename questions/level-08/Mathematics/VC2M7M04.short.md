# VC2M7M04 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Identify corresponding, alternate and co-interior relationships between angles formed when parallel lines are crossed by a transversal; use them to solve problems and explain reasons.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** When a transversal crosses parallel lines, predictable angle relationships appear. Knowing them lets you find unknown angles with reasoning, not measurement.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **identify and use the angle relationships** that form when **parallel lines are crossed by a transversal**.
- You should be able to calculate unknown angles and explain your reasoning using correct terminology.

## Core content

Two **parallel** lines (marked ‖) crossed by a **transversal** create eight angles at two intersection points — four at each.

**Corresponding angles** sit in the same position at each intersection. They are **equal**. **Alternate angles** sit on opposite sides of the transversal, between the parallels. They are **equal** (trace a Z or S shape to spot them). **Co-interior angles** sit on the same side of the transversal, between the parallels. They are **supplementary** — they add to **180°**. **Vertically opposite angles** at any intersection are always equal.

**Worked example.** Two parallel tram tracks are crossed by a street. One angle at the upper crossing is 55°. Corresponding angle at the lower crossing = 55° (equal). Co-interior angle on the same side = 180° − 55° = 125°. Alternate angle = 55°. Always name the rule you use.

**Combining rules.** If you know one of the eight angles, you can find all the others by chaining corresponding, alternate, co-interior and vertically opposite relationships.

**Victorian context.** Melbourne's Hoddle Grid has parallel streets crossed by diagonal lanes — real transversal patterns. The roof trusses of Flinders Street Station rely on angle relationships in parallel structures. AMSI uses these principles in structural design education.

## Memorable takeaways
- Corresponding angles are equal.
- Alternate angles are equal.
- Co-interior angles sum to 180°.
- Vertically opposite angles are always equal.
- State the rule at every step.

## Watch out for these traps
- Mixing up alternate and co-interior — alternate are on opposite sides (equal); co-interior are on the same side (sum to 180°).
- Forgetting the parallel condition — these rules only hold when the lines are parallel.
- Not stating reasons — always name the relationship you used.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Corresponding angle to 65°.* 65° (corresponding angles are equal).
- *Alternate angle to 110°.* 110° (alternate angles are equal).
- *Co-interior partner of 72°.* 180° − 72° = 108°.
- *All eight angles when one is 40°.* Four angles of 40° and four of 140°.
- *Can one angle determine all eight?* Yes, when the lines are parallel — via the four relationships.
