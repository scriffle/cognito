# VC2M7N08 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M7N08. Compare, order and solve problems involving addition and subtraction of integers.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**

- You are learning that negative numbers are not strange — they describe real situations every day.

- Solve real-world problems that involve integer addition and subtraction.

## Core content

**1. What are integers?** **Integers** are whole numbers that can be positive, negative or zero: … −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 … They do not include fractions or decimals. Every integer has a position on the **number line**, with negative numbers to the left of zero and positive numbers to the right. *Quick check:* Is −7 an integer? Is 3.5?

**2. Comparing and ordering integers.** On the number line, a number further to the right is greater. So 3 > −5, and −2 > −8. To order a set of integers, place them on the number line and read left to right (ascending) or right to left (descending). *Quick check:* Order these from smallest to largest: 4, −1, 0, −6, 2.

**3. Adding integers — same sign.** When both numbers have the same sign, add the absolute values and keep the sign. (+5) + (+3) = +8. (−4) + (−6) = −10. *Quick check:* Calculate (−7) + (−3).

**4. Adding integers — different signs.** When the signs are different, subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger and take the sign of the larger. (+8) + (−3) = +5. (−9) + (+4) = −5. Think of it as a tug-of-war between the positive and negative teams. *Quick check:* Calculate (−12) + (+7).

**5. Subtracting integers.** Subtracting is the same as adding the opposite. Change the subtraction sign to addition and flip the sign of the second number: a − b = a + (−b). So (+6) − (+9) = (+6) + (−9) = −3. And (+3) − (−4) = (+3) + (+4) = +7. *Quick check:* Calculate (−5) − (−8).

**6. Worked example — temperature change.**
At 6 am in Mount Hotham, the temperature is −4°C. By 2 pm it has risen 11 degrees. By midnight it drops 9 degrees from the 2 pm reading.

*Follow the thinking:*
- 2 pm temperature: (−4) + (+11) = +7°C.
- Midnight temperature: (+7) + (−9) = −2°C.
- Total change from 6 am to midnight: (−2) − (−4) = (−2) + (+4) = +2°C. The temperature is 2 degrees higher at midnight than at 6 am.

**7. Integers in real life.** Bank accounts: deposits are positive, withdrawals negative. Altitude: above sea level is positive, below sea level is negative. Golf: under par is negative, over par is positive. History: BCE dates can be treated as negative years on a timeline. *Quick check:* A bank balance is $120. After a withdrawal of $85 and a deposit of $40, what is the new balance?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Bureau of Meteorology records sub-zero temperatures across the Victorian Alps — Mount Hotham, Falls Creek, Mount Buller. Melbourne itself occasionally reaches 0°C on winter mornings. The MCG scoreboard uses integers when a cricket team is "behind" or "ahead" by runs. Victorian financial literacy programs use positive and negative values to teach budgeting — income as positive, expenses as negative.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal astronomical knowledge tracks seasonal changes that in Western measurement involve temperature shifts below zero — Gunaikurnai knowledge of frost patterns in Gippsland, for instance, links cold-season observations to land management. Deep Time timelines extending tens of thousands of years into the past use negative numbers in Western dating systems (e.g. 65 000 BCE), though First Nations frameworks understand time differently. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Students from families with connections to northern-hemisphere countries experience negative temperatures as everyday reality — Scandinavian, Canadian, Russian and northern Chinese communities in Melbourne bring lived understanding of sub-zero conditions. Financial literacy in migrant communities involves tracking income and expenses as integer operations.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** At 7 am the temperature in Ballarat is −3°C. It rises 8 degrees by noon, then drops 5 degrees by 6 pm. What is the temperature at noon and at 6 pm? What is the overall change from 7 am to 6 pm?
2. **Civic.** A council in Alpine Shire records overnight temperatures for a week: −6, −4, −2, 0, 1, −3, −5 (°C). Order these from coldest to warmest and calculate the range (highest − lowest).
3. **Vocational.** A scuba diver at Portsea Pier descends from sea level (0 m) to −12 m, then ascends 5 m to examine a reef, then descends another 3 m. What is the diver's final depth? Write each step as integer addition.
4. **Ethical.** A student says "subtracting a negative is cheating — you're just adding." Explain why subtracting a negative really does equal adding, and give a real-world example to convince them.
5. **Cross-cultural.** A class plots historical events on a timeline using integers: founding of Rome (−753), birth of the Common Era (0), fall of the Western Roman Empire (+476), arrival of the First Fleet (+1788). Calculate the number of years between the founding of Rome and the fall of the Western Roman Empire.

## Memorable takeaways
- Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers and zero.
- On the number line, right is greater — even −2 is greater than −8.
- Adding a negative is the same as subtracting.
- Subtracting a negative is the same as adding.
- Temperature, altitude, finance and timelines all use integer reasoning.

## Stretch yourself
A mountaineer in New Zealand starts at a base camp at an altitude of 850 m above sea level. She climbs 1200 m on day one, descends 350 m into a valley on day two, climbs 900 m on day three and descends 480 m on day four. Calculate her altitude at the end of each day. The summit is at 3754 m. How many more metres does she need to climb from her day-four position? Meanwhile, a diver off the Victorian coast starts at sea level and descends to −18 m. Express the total vertical distance between the diver and the mountaineer's day-four position as a single integer subtraction. Show all working. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Thinking −3 is greater than −1.** On the number line, −1 is to the right of −3, so −1 > −3. A common error is to compare the digits (3 > 1) and forget the signs.
- **Confusing subtraction of a negative with subtraction of a positive.** 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8. But 5 − 3 = 2. The brackets and the double negative matter.
- **Forgetting that 0 is an integer.** Zero is neither positive nor negative. It is the boundary between them and it is an integer.
- **Losing track of signs in multi-step problems.** Write out each step on the number line or in brackets. Rushing through signs is the most common source of integer errors.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Ballarat temperature.* Noon: (−3) + 8 = 5°C. 6 pm: 5 + (−5) = 0°C. Overall change: 0 − (−3) = +3°C.
2. *Alpine Shire.* Ordered: −6, −5, −4, −3, −2, 0, 1. Range: 1 − (−6) = 7°C.
3. *Portsea diver.* Start: 0. Step 1: 0 + (−12) = −12. Step 2: (−12) + 5 = −7. Step 3: (−7) + (−3) = −10. Final depth: −10 m.
4. *Subtracting a negative.* If you owe someone $5 and they cancel the debt, your wealth goes up by $5. Cancelling a negative (subtracting it) has the same effect as adding a positive. 0 − (−5) = 0 + 5 = 5.
5. *Rome timeline.* Fall of Western Roman Empire (+476) minus founding of Rome (−753): 476 − (−753) = 476 + 753 = 1229 years.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Day 1: 850 + 1200 = 2050 m. Day 2: 2050 − 350 = 1700 m. Day 3: 1700 + 900 = 2600 m. Day 4: 2600 − 480 = 2120 m. To summit: 3754 − 2120 = 1634 m. Diver: −18 m. Distance between: 2120 − (−18) = 2120 + 18 = 2138 m vertical distance.
