# VC2M7ST01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Acquire data sets for discrete and continuous numerical variables and calculate the range, median, mean and mode; make and justify decisions about which measures of central tendency provide useful insights into the nature of the distribution of data.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Mean, median and mode each summarise data differently. The range measures spread. Choosing the right measure depends on the data shape and the question.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **calculate the range, median, mean and mode** and to **decide which measure is most useful** for a given situation.
- You should be able to distinguish discrete from continuous variables and explain how outliers affect each measure.

## Core content

A **discrete** variable takes countable values (goals scored); a **continuous** variable can take any value in a range (height, temperature).

**Mean** = sum of values ÷ number of values. It uses every data point, so it is sensitive to **outliers**. **Median** = middle value when data is ordered. If even-numbered, average the two middle values. It resists outliers. **Mode** = most frequent value. A set can have no mode, one mode, or several. **Range** = maximum − minimum. It measures spread, not centre.

**Worked example.** A class records money raised: $5, $8, $10, $10, $10, $12, $15, $200. Mean = $33.75 (dragged up by the $200 outlier). Median = $10. Mode = $10. The median or mode best represents a typical student here.

**When to use which.** Mean for roughly symmetric data with no extremes. Median when data is skewed or has outliers. Mode for the most common value. The ABS reports **median** house prices for Victorian suburbs because a few expensive sales skew the mean.

**Victorian context.** The ABS, VicHealth and the Yoorrook Justice Commission all rely on summary statistics to report on housing, wellbeing and justice outcomes. Understanding which measure is reported — and why — is a critical numeracy skill.

## Memorable takeaways
- Mean: add and divide — sensitive to outliers.
- Median: middle value — resistant to outliers.
- Mode: most frequent — can have more than one.
- Range: max − min — spread, not centre.
- Choose the measure that fits the data and the question.

## Watch out for these traps
- Assuming the mean is always best — it is misleading when data is skewed.
- Forgetting to order data before finding the median.
- Confusing range (spread) with a measure of centre.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Is "number of pets" discrete or continuous?* Discrete — whole-number counts.
- *Mean of 3, 5, 7, 9, 11.* 35 ÷ 5 = 7.
- *Median of 2, 4, 6, 8, 100.* 6.
- *Mode of 3, 5, 5, 7, 8, 8, 8, 10.* 8.
- *House prices with one outlier — which measure?* Median — it resists the outlier's pull.
