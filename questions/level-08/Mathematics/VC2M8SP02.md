## Core content

**1. Angle sum of a quadrilateral.** Draw one diagonal — it splits the quadrilateral into two triangles. Each triangle has angle sum 180°, so the quadrilateral's angle sum = 2 × 180° = 360°. *Quick check:* Three angles of a quadrilateral are 95°, 80° and 110°. What is the fourth?

**2. Parallelogram properties via congruent triangles.** In parallelogram ABCD, diagonal AC creates △ABC and △CDA. Because AB ∥ CD (alternate angles equal) and AD ∥ BC (alternate angles equal), and AC is common, the two triangles are congruent by ASA. Consequences: AB = CD, AD = BC (opposite sides equal); ∠A = ∠C, ∠B = ∠D (opposite angles equal); diagonals bisect each other. *Quick check:* In a parallelogram, one angle is 70°. What are the other three angles?

**3. Rectangle properties.** A rectangle is a parallelogram with all angles 90°. The congruent-triangle argument still applies, and additionally the diagonals are equal in length (proved by RHS on the triangles formed). *Quick check:* A rectangle has diagonals of length 10 cm. What is the length from the centre to each corner?

**4. Rhombus properties.** A rhombus is a parallelogram with all sides equal. Its diagonals are perpendicular bisectors of each other (proved by SSS on the four small triangles formed). The diagonals also bisect the vertex angles. *Quick check:* In a rhombus, one diagonal is 12 cm and the other is 16 cm. What is the side length?

**5. Square properties.** A square is both a rectangle and a rhombus — it inherits all properties of both. All sides equal, all angles 90°, diagonals equal and perpendicular, diagonals bisect each other and bisect vertex angles. *Quick check:* A square has side 8 cm. What is the length of its diagonal?

**6. Worked example: finding unknown angles in a parallelogram.**

In parallelogram PQRS, ∠P = 3x + 10° and ∠Q = 2x + 40°. Find all angles.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Co-interior angles (same side of a transversal between parallel lines) are supplementary: ∠P + ∠Q = 180°.
- 3x + 10 + 2x + 40 = 180 → 5x + 50 = 180 → 5x = 130 → x = 26.
- ∠P = 3(26) + 10 = 88°. ∠Q = 2(26) + 40 = 92°.
- Opposite angles equal: ∠R = 88°, ∠S = 92°.
- Check: 88 + 92 + 88 + 92 = 360°. ✓

**7. The quadrilateral hierarchy.** Every square is a rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram and quadrilateral. Every rectangle is a parallelogram. Every rhombus is a parallelogram. Not every parallelogram is a rectangle or rhombus. A trapezium has at least one pair of parallel sides. Understanding this hierarchy prevents over-claiming or under-claiming properties. *Quick check:* Is every rectangle a rhombus? Explain.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The grid layout of Melbourne's CBD (Hoddle Grid) creates rectangular and parallelogram-shaped blocks — surveyors used angle properties to ensure perpendicularity. The Eureka Tower's base is a parallelogram shape. Victorian roofing trusses use triangulated quadrilateral frames whose stability depends on the rigid-triangle principle.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Traditional weaving (dilly bags, fish traps, mats) across many First Nations communities produces parallelogram and rhombus patterns through the crossing of fibres at consistent angles. The diamond/rhombus motif appears widely in published Aboriginal art as a symbol with cultural meaning. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Quadrilateral geometry is central to textile design worldwide — Scottish tartans use parallelogram repeats, Japanese sashiko stitching creates rhombus grids, and Persian rug patterns exploit quadrilateral symmetry. All are studied in Melbourne design schools.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** A picture frame is meant to be rectangular. You measure the diagonals: one is 52 cm and the other is 51 cm. Is the frame truly rectangular? Explain using the diagonal property of rectangles.
2. **Civic.** A landscape architect at the City of Moreland designs a rhombus-shaped garden bed with diagonals 6 m and 8 m. Calculate the side length of the rhombus and its area.
3. **Vocational.** A builder checks whether a concrete slab is a true parallelogram by measuring both diagonals and checking they bisect each other. If the diagonals are 4.8 m and 3.6 m and they cross at their midpoints, is it a parallelogram? Could it also be a rectangle?
4. **Ethical.** A classmate says "a square is not a rectangle because rectangles have two long sides and two short sides." Explain why this is incorrect using the mathematical definition and the quadrilateral hierarchy.
5. **Cross-cultural.** You are studying a parallelogram tile pattern in an Islamic art display at the NGV. Each tile has angles 60° and 120°. Verify the angle sum is 360° and explain why opposite angles must be equal using the congruent-triangle proof.

## Memorable takeaways
- Quadrilateral angle sum = 360° (two triangles).
- A diagonal splits a parallelogram into two congruent triangles (ASA).
- Opposite sides and opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal.
- Diagonals bisect each other in all parallelograms; are equal in rectangles; are perpendicular in rhombuses.
- Square = rectangle + rhombus (inherits all properties of both).

## Stretch yourself
Prove that the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular. Start by drawing rhombus ABCD with diagonals intersecting at M. Identify the four triangles formed. Use SSS to prove △ABM ≅ △CBM. Conclude that ∠AMB = ∠CMB, and since they form a straight line, each must be 90°. Then: a quadrilateral has vertices at A(0,0), B(6,0), C(8,4), D(2,4). Verify it is a parallelogram by showing opposite sides are equal. Check whether it is a rectangle (are diagonals equal?). Show all working and explain each reasoning step. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "A quadrilateral's angles add to 180°." No — that is a triangle. Quadrilateral angle sum = 360°.
- "Diagonals of a parallelogram are always equal." Only in rectangles. In a general parallelogram, diagonals bisect each other but are not necessarily equal.
- "A rhombus has no right angles." A square is a special rhombus that does have right angles.
- "I only need to state the property." At this level, you need to explain reasoning — use congruent triangles or angle properties to justify your answer.
