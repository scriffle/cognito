# VC2M8N02 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M8N02. Establish and apply the exponent laws with positive integer exponents and the zero exponent, using exponent notation with numbers.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**
  - An exponent is shorthand for repeated multiplication.
  - The exponent laws follow logically from that definition — they are not arbitrary rules.
  - The zero exponent (a⁰ = 1) is a natural consequence of the division law.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Critical and Creative Thinking; Numeracy; Digital Literacy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **understand, establish and apply the exponent (index) laws** with positive integer exponents, and to explain why any non-zero number raised to the power of zero equals 1.
- You are learning that exponent notation compresses large calculations into compact expressions.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Write repeated multiplication in exponent form and expand exponent form back to multiplication.
- State and apply the product law, quotient law, power-of-a-power law, and power-of-a-product law.
- Explain and apply the zero exponent rule.
- Simplify numerical expressions using the exponent laws.

## Core content

**1. Exponent notation.** 2⁵ means 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32. The **base** is 2, the **exponent** (or index) is 5. Exponent notation is shorthand for repeated multiplication of the same factor. *Quick check:* Write 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 in exponent form and evaluate it.

**2. Product law (multiplying powers with the same base).** When you multiply powers with the same base, add the exponents: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ. Why? 2³ × 2⁴ = (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = 2⁷. You are just combining the repeated multiplications. *Quick check:* Simplify 5⁴ × 5³.

**3. Quotient law (dividing powers with the same base).** When you divide powers with the same base, subtract the exponents: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (where m > n). Why? 3⁵ ÷ 3² = (3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3) ÷ (3 × 3) = 3 × 3 × 3 = 3³. You cancel common factors. *Quick check:* Simplify 7⁶ ÷ 7².

**4. Power of a power.** When you raise a power to another power, multiply the exponents: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐˣⁿ. Why? (2³)⁴ means 2³ × 2³ × 2³ × 2³ = 2³⁺³⁺³⁺³ = 2¹². *Quick check:* Simplify (4²)³.

**5. Power of a product.** When you raise a product to a power, each factor gets the exponent: (a × b)ⁿ = aⁿ × bⁿ. Why? (2 × 5)³ = (2 × 5) × (2 × 5) × (2 × 5) = 2³ × 5³ = 8 × 125 = 1000. Check: 10³ = 1000. *Quick check:* Simplify (3 × 4)² and verify by calculating 12².

**6. Zero exponent.** Any non-zero number raised to the power of zero equals 1: a⁰ = 1 (where a ≠ 0). Why? By the quotient law, a³ ÷ a³ = a³⁻³ = a⁰. But anything divided by itself is 1. So a⁰ = 1.

*Follow the thinking:*
- 5³ ÷ 5³ = 125 ÷ 125 = 1.
- By the quotient law: 5³⁻³ = 5⁰.
- Therefore 5⁰ = 1.

*Quick check:* What is 100⁰? What about 1⁰?

**7. Worked example — simplifying a multi-step expression.**
Simplify: (2³ × 2²)² ÷ 2⁴.

*Follow the thinking:*
- Inside the brackets: 2³ × 2² = 2⁵ (product law).
- Apply the power: (2⁵)² = 2¹⁰ (power-of-a-power law).
- Divide: 2¹⁰ ÷ 2⁴ = 2⁶ = 64 (quotient law).

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** Your phone storage is 128 GB. Express 128 as a power of 2. If each photo is approximately 2³ MB, how many photos (as a power of 2) fit in 128 GB? (Hint: 1 GB = 2¹⁰ MB.)
2. **Civic.** A council's population doubles every 20 years. If the current population is 40 000, write a formula using exponents for the population after n doubling periods. Predict the population after 60 years (3 doubling periods).
3. **Vocational.** A lab technician dilutes a bacteria sample by a factor of 10 each hour. After 5 hours, the concentration is the original divided by 10⁵. If the original count was 10⁸ bacteria per mL, write the final concentration as a power of 10.
4. **Ethical.** A student writes 2³ × 3² = 6⁵. Explain why this is wrong. What law has been misapplied?
5. **Cross-cultural.** A class investigates paper folding. Each fold doubles the layers: after n folds there are 2ⁿ layers. Calculate the number of layers after 7 folds. If the paper starts at 0.1 mm thick, use exponent notation to find the total thickness.

## Memorable takeaways
- An exponent counts how many times the base is multiplied by itself.
- Product law: same base, add exponents (aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ).
- Quotient law: same base, subtract exponents (aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ).
- Power of a power: multiply exponents ((aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐˣⁿ).
- Zero exponent: a⁰ = 1 because a ÷ a = 1.

## Watch out for these traps
- **Adding instead of multiplying for "power of a power."** (2³)⁴ = 2¹² (multiply 3 × 4), not 2⁷ (which is adding 3 + 4).
- **Applying product law to different bases.** 2³ × 3² cannot be simplified using the product law — the bases must be the same.
- **Thinking a⁰ = 0.** The zero exponent gives 1, not 0. The proof comes from the quotient law: a ÷ a = 1.
- **Confusing 2³ with 2 × 3.** 2³ = 8 (repeated multiplication), not 6 (single multiplication). The exponent is not a multiplier of the base.
