# VC2M8N01 — Mathematics (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Recognise irrational numbers in applied contexts, including π and numbers that develop from the square root of positive real numbers that are not perfect squares, and recognise that irrational numbers cannot develop from the division of integer values by natural numbers.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics.
- **Big ideas:** Not all numbers can be written as fractions — those that cannot are irrational. π and square roots of non-perfect-squares are irrational: their decimals never terminate and never repeat.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **recognise irrational numbers** in applied contexts and understand **why they cannot be written as fractions**.
- You should be able to distinguish irrational from rational numbers when they arise in measurement and geometry.

## Core content

A **rational number** can be written as p/q (integer ÷ natural number). All terminating decimals (0.125 = 1/8) and all repeating decimals (0.333... = 1/3) are rational.

An **irrational number** cannot be written as any fraction. Its decimal goes on forever with no repeating block. You will never find integers p and q that work.

**π ≈ 3.14159265...** is irrational (proven 1761). 22/7 is a rational approximation — close but not equal. **Square roots of non-perfect-squares** are irrational: √2 ≈ 1.41421..., √3 ≈ 1.73205..., √5 ≈ 2.23606... Perfect squares (4, 9, 16, 25...) have rational roots: √4 = 2, √9 = 3.

**Why √2 is irrational (idea).** Assume √2 = p/q in simplest form. Then p² = 2q², forcing both p and q to be even — contradicting "simplest form." No such fraction exists.

**Where they appear.** Circles (π in C = πd), diagonals of squares (side × √2), heights of equilateral triangles (side × √3/2), and Pythagoras' theorem whenever the hypotenuse involves a non-perfect-square sum.

**Victorian context.** Engineers at the Australian Synchrotron work with π in circular-path calculations. Ancient Indian mathematicians (Sulba Sutras, c. 800 BCE) approximated √2 for altar construction — predating the Greek formal proof by centuries.

## Memorable takeaways
- Rational = fraction. Irrational = not a fraction.
- Irrational decimals never terminate, never repeat.
- π, √2, √3, √5 are irrational. √4, √9, √25 are rational.
- In practice we round, but the exact value has no fraction form.

## Watch out for these traps
- Thinking all square roots are irrational — perfect squares give rational roots.
- Thinking 22/7 = π — it is an approximation, not exact.
- Thinking long repeating decimals are irrational — 1/7 = 0.142857... repeats and is rational.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Is 0.125 rational?* Yes — 0.125 = 1/8.
- *√49 and √50?* √49 = 7 (rational). √50 is irrational.
- *Is 22/7 equal to π?* No. 22/7 ≈ 3.142857 (repeating); π ≈ 3.141592 (non-repeating).
- *Diagonal of a 1 m square.* √2 m — irrational.
- *Can two irrational legs give a rational hypotenuse?* Yes: legs √2 and √2 give c = √4 = 2.
