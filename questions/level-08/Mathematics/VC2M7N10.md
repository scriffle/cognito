# VC2M7N10 — Mathematics

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2M7N10. Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving rational numbers and percentages, including financial contexts such as 'best buys'; formulate problems, choosing representations and efficient calculation strategies, designing algorithms and using digital tools as appropriate; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, justifying choices made about the representation.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Years 7–8, Mathematics (Number).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Mathematical modelling turns real situations into calculations you can solve and then interpret.
  - Percentages and unit rates are tools for fair comparison.
  - Choosing the right strategy matters as much as getting the arithmetic right.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Numeracy; Critical and Creative Thinking; Personal and Social Capability; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems** involving rational numbers and percentages, including comparing prices and financial decisions.
- You are learning a four-step cycle: formulate → solve → interpret → communicate.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Formulate a real problem as a mathematical model (identify variables, choose operations).
- Calculate percentages, percentage increases/decreases and unit rates.
- Compare products using unit pricing ("best buy").
- Interpret your answer in context and justify your method.

## Core content

**1. The modelling cycle.** Real problem → **Formulate** (choose variables, write expressions) → **Solve** (calculate) → **Interpret** (what does the number mean in context?) → **Communicate** (explain and justify). This cycle repeats: if the answer seems unreasonable, revisit your formulation. *Quick check:* What is the difference between solving and interpreting?

**2. Percentage of a quantity.** To find 15% of $240: convert to a decimal (0.15) and multiply: 0.15 × 240 = $36. Alternatively: 10% = $24, 5% = $12, so 15% = $36. Choose the method that suits you. *Quick check:* Find 35% of 600.

**3. Percentage increase and decrease.** A $80 item with 25% off: discount = 0.25 × 80 = $20, sale price = $60. Alternatively: sale price = 80 × 0.75 = $60. For a 10% increase on $150: new price = 150 × 1.10 = $165. *Quick check:* A $45 shirt is marked 20% off. What is the sale price?

**4. Unit pricing and best buys.** To compare products of different sizes, find the **unit price** (price per unit of quantity). A 500 g box of cereal costs $5.50 → $5.50 ÷ 500 = $0.011 per gram. A 750 g box costs $7.80 → $7.80 ÷ 750 = $0.0104 per gram. The larger box is the better buy per gram. *Quick check:* Juice A: 2 L for $4.20. Juice B: 3 L for $5.70. Which is the best buy?

**5. Designing an algorithm.** An algorithm is a step-by-step procedure. For best-buy problems: (1) identify products and their sizes, (2) calculate unit price for each, (3) compare, (4) state the best buy and justify. Digital tools (spreadsheets) can automate steps 2 and 3 for many products. *Quick check:* Write three steps to compare the cost of two brands of laundry detergent.

**6. Worked example: school fundraiser pricing.**

A Year 7 class sells two cupcake packs for a fundraiser. Pack A: 6 cupcakes for $9.00. Pack B: 10 cupcakes for $14.00. Which is the better value for buyers? What percentage saving does the better pack offer compared to the other?

*Follow the thinking:*
- Unit price A: $9.00 ÷ 6 = $1.50 per cupcake.
- Unit price B: $14.00 ÷ 10 = $1.40 per cupcake.
- Pack B is cheaper per cupcake.
- Saving per cupcake: $1.50 − $1.40 = $0.10.
- Percentage saving: $0.10 ÷ $1.50 × 100 = 6.67% (to 2 d.p.).
- Communicate: "Pack B saves buyers approximately 6.7% per cupcake compared to Pack A."

**7. Interpreting and communicating.** A mathematical answer is not finished until you explain what it means in context. "The answer is 0.75" is incomplete. "The sale price is $0.75 per 100 g, making Brand X 12% cheaper than Brand Y" is a communicated solution. Justify your choice of method. *Quick check:* Why should you state units and context when communicating a solution?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Coles and Woolworths supermarkets in Victoria display unit prices on shelf labels (required by Australian Consumer Law). The ACCC (Australian Competition and Consumer Commission) enforces unit-pricing rules. VicHealth publishes health-cost data as percentages for public communication.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal community-controlled organisations manage budgets, grants and service contracts that require percentage-based financial reporting. The Koorie Heritage Trust and First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria publish annual reports with financial data — real contexts where percentages communicate proportional spending. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** International students and new arrivals in Melbourne use percentage and unit-price skills to compare products across unfamiliar brands. Market stalls at Queen Victoria Market, Footscray Market and Dandenong Market all present comparison-shopping contexts.

## Try these scenarios

1. **Everyday.** You are buying laundry detergent. Brand A: 2 L for $11.00. Brand B: 3 L for $15.60. Calculate the unit price for each and identify the best buy. What percentage cheaper is the better option?
2. **Civic.** The Shire of Surf Coast reports that visitor numbers increased by 18% from 2023 to 2024. If 2023 visitors totalled 1 250 000, how many visited in 2024? Interpret what this means for local services.
3. **Vocational.** A café owner in Brunswick calculates that ingredients cost 35% of the selling price for each item. If a sandwich sells for $12.50, what is the ingredient cost? If she wants a 60% gross margin, what should the selling price be for ingredients costing $4.20?
4. **Ethical.** A "50% extra free" pack contains 750 mL (up from 500 mL) at the same price. A classmate says this is the same as 50% off. Is it? Use unit pricing to explain.
5. **Cross-cultural.** You are helping a family compare mobile plans. Plan A: $35/month for 20 GB. Plan B: $50/month for 35 GB. Calculate cost per GB. Which is the best buy? What other factors might influence the decision?

## Memorable takeaways
- Modelling = formulate → solve → interpret → communicate.
- Unit price = total price ÷ quantity — the fairest comparison tool.
- Percentage of a quantity: multiply by the decimal form.
- Percentage change: (difference ÷ original) × 100.
- A number without context and units is not a finished answer.

## Stretch yourself
A school canteen manager has a $500 weekly budget for fruit. She sources from two suppliers. Supplier A: apples at $3.20/kg, bananas at $2.80/kg, oranges at $4.50/kg. Supplier B: apples at $2.90/kg, bananas at $3.10/kg, oranges at $4.20/kg. She needs 40 kg of apples, 30 kg of bananas and 20 kg of oranges per week. Calculate the total cost from each supplier. Determine which supplier is cheaper overall and by what percentage. Then explore: is there a smarter strategy (splitting the order between suppliers)? Calculate the optimal split and the total saving compared to using the more expensive supplier alone. Show all working and justify your strategy. 250–300 words.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Bigger packs are always better value." Not always — check the unit price. Promotional pricing sometimes makes smaller packs cheaper per unit.
- "50% extra free is the same as 50% off." 50% extra free means you get 1.5× the product for the same price (unit price drops by 33%). A 50% discount halves the price (unit price drops by 50%). They are different.
- "I just need the answer." In modelling, interpreting and communicating are part of the answer. A bare number with no context is incomplete.
- "Percentage increase and decrease are symmetric." A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does not return to the original: $100 → $120 → $96.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Solving vs interpreting.* Solving produces a number; interpreting explains what that number means in the real situation.
2. *35% of 600.* 0.35 × 600 = 210.
3. *$45 shirt, 20% off.* Sale price = 45 × 0.80 = $36.
4. *Juice best buy.* A: $4.20 ÷ 2 = $2.10/L. B: $5.70 ÷ 3 = $1.90/L. Juice B is the best buy.
5. *Three steps for detergent comparison.* (1) Note price and volume of each. (2) Calculate price per litre. (3) Compare and state which is cheaper per litre.
6. *Why state units and context?* So the reader knows what the number represents and can act on it. "$1.90" alone is meaningless without "per litre" and "for Juice B."

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Detergent.* A: $11.00 ÷ 2 = $5.50/L. B: $15.60 ÷ 3 = $5.20/L. Brand B is the best buy. Percentage cheaper: ($5.50 − $5.20) ÷ $5.50 × 100 = 5.5%.
2. *Surf Coast visitors.* 2024 visitors = 1 250 000 × 1.18 = 1 475 000. Interpretation: 225 000 additional visitors means more demand on parking, waste and emergency services.
3. *Café.* Ingredient cost = 0.35 × $12.50 = $4.38. For 60% margin: selling price = $4.20 ÷ 0.40 = $10.50.
4. *50% extra free vs 50% off.* Original: 500 mL for price P. Unit price = P/500. With 50% extra: 750 mL for P. New unit price = P/750 (33% reduction). With 50% off: 500 mL for P/2. Unit price = P/1 000 (50% reduction). They are not the same — 50% off is a bigger saving per mL.
5. *Mobile plans.* A: $35 ÷ 20 = $1.75/GB. B: $50 ÷ 35 = $1.43/GB. Plan B is the best buy per GB. Other factors: data needs, coverage, contract length, included calls.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** Supplier A total: (40 × $3.20) + (30 × $2.80) + (20 × $4.50) = $128 + $84 + $90 = $302. Supplier B total: (40 × $2.90) + (30 × $3.10) + (20 × $4.20) = $116 + $93 + $84 = $293. Supplier B is cheaper by $9, which is 3.0% less. Optimal split: buy apples and oranges from B (cheaper) and bananas from A (cheaper). Split cost: (40 × $2.90) + (30 × $2.80) + (20 × $4.20) = $116 + $84 + $84 = $284. Saving vs Supplier A alone: $18 (6.0%). Strategy justified: compare each item individually and buy from the cheaper source.
