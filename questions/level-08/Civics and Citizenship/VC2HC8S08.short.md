# VC2HC8S08 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Develop an analysis of civics and citizenship issues with knowledge and evidence using multiple methods of communication.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Analysis is more than opinion. Audience first, format second. Accessibility is part of the message.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop an analysis** with **knowledge and evidence**, using **multiple methods of communication**.
- You should be able to make a case people can read, watch or hear.

## Core content

**Analysis** breaks an issue into parts, examines evidence, considers perspectives, and reaches a justified conclusion. A solid analysis names a **claim**, supports it with **evidence**, and explains the **reasoning** that links them. Civic analysis fits many formats: report, essay, poster, infographic, podcast, video, presentation. Cite at least three reputable sources, including one primary source. A reasoned conclusion names limits or alternatives.

Accessibility (plain English, multilingual versions, alt text, captions) makes analysis available to more people.

**Worked example.** A proposed bike lane in inner Melbourne. Audience 1: peers — 90-second video with three facts. Audience 2: council — 200-word submission with two sources cited. Audience 3: primary class — picture-book page with a simple map. Same analysis, three artefacts.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander voices in civic communication include NITV and IndigenousX. SBS Radio broadcasts in over 60 languages. Cite community sources directly. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Analysis is more than opinion.
- Claim, evidence, reasoning.
- Audience first, format second.
- Cite at least three sources.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Quoting equals analysing." Use quotes sparingly; the reasoning is yours.
- "Citations are decoration." They are the basis for trust.
- "One format suits all audiences." Tailor it.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why does "what evidence shows" beat "what I think"?* Evidence allows others to test the claim; opinion alone cannot be checked.
- *Missing reasoning.* The argument fails to explain why the evidence supports the claim.
- *Why does citing strengthen analysis?* It makes claims checkable and shows you have read more than one perspective.
- *Why are captions a civic accessibility issue?* They make information available to deaf and hard-of-hearing audiences.
- *Short summary of a bike-lane analysis.* The same claim and evidence reach peers, council and younger audiences through three formats tuned to each.
