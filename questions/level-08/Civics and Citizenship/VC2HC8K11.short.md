# VC2HC8K11 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** How groups express their identities, including religious and cultural identity, and how this expression can influence their perceptions of others and others' perception of them.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Identity is layered. Expression builds belonging. Perception is shaped, not fixed.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **how groups express their identities** and how that expression **shapes perception** in both directions.
- You should be able to read identity as a two-way street.

## Core content

A person can hold national, cultural, religious, gender, family, occupational and personal identities. Groups share some layers. **Expression** appears in clothing, food, language, festivals, art, music, place-names and shared spaces. Expression strengthens self-perception and shapes how others perceive a group.

**Stereotypes** are oversimplified beliefs about a group. They can affect everyday behaviour and institutions. Personal contact, accurate information, plural media representation and intercultural programs reduce their power.

**Worked example.** The AFL grand final. Multiple expressions sit alongside each other: Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Welcome to Country, the national anthem, team songs, fan colours, multicultural performers. Together they communicate a national event that includes many groups, with First Nations recognition first.

**Cultural context.** Public festivals like the Antipodes Festival (Greek), Diwali at Federation Square, Vietnamese Tết in Footscray and Lunar New Year demonstrate identity expression in shared public space. Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Wadawurrung and other Victorian peoples express identity through Welcomes, dual-named places, art and language revitalisation. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Identity is layered.
- Expression builds belonging.
- Perception is shaped, not fixed.
- Stereotypes simplify; description specifies.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Stereotypes are just first guesses." They shape institutions and behaviour.
- "Only minorities have identities." Everyone has identities.
- "Welcome to Country is just ceremony." It is a culturally significant practice with meaning beyond formality.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why multiple identities at once?* People belong to many groups; each adds a layer.
- *Stereotype vs description.* A description fits a specific person or event. A stereotype generalises a group, often unfairly.
- *Why do migrant communities keep traditions strongly?* Traditions provide continuity and a way to pass identity to the next generation in a new place.
- *Why can first impressions mislead?* They are limited and filtered through prior expectations.
- *Short summary of the AFL grand final example.* Multiple expressions coexist: First Nations Welcome, national anthem, team and fan identities, multicultural performers. Each signals a group; together they signal a shared event.
