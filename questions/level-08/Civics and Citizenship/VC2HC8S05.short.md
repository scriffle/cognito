# VC2HC8S05 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Explain how political actors exercise power in democratic institutions.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Power is exercised, not just held. Many actors, many forms. Accountability needs visibility.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **explain** how **political actors exercise power** in democratic institutions.
- You should be able to read real power moves, not just diagrams.

## Core content

**Political actors** include elected representatives, executive office-holders, public servants, judges, lobby groups, party officials and journalists. Forms of power: **legislative** (passing laws), **executive** (running government), **judicial** (interpreting laws), **soft** (influence through media, expertise, public opinion), and **resource** (controlling budgets and people).

Power is exercised through specific acts. Limits and checks include the Constitution, courts, parliament, media and voters. **Hard power** is formal authority; **soft power** is influence. Both shape outcomes. Every exercise of power can be questioned, recorded and reviewed.

**Worked example.** A federal Treasurer at budget time. Sets priorities (executive), drafts bills with Treasury (resource and legislative), negotiates with caucus and the Opposition (party and political), defends decisions in parliament (legislative), and explains the budget on television (soft). Each act is visible and reviewable.

**Cultural context.** The First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria exercises authority within its mandate and engages on treaty. Traditional Owner corporations exercise power over land management decisions in their Country. Multicultural advisory bodies and faith leaders exercise soft power through advice and advocacy. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Power is exercised, not just held.
- Hard and soft both shape outcomes.
- Limits and checks are part of the system.
- Accountability needs visibility.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Only the elected hold power." Many unelected actors exercise real power.
- "Soft power is not real power." It often determines what gets onto the agenda.
- "Aboriginal organisations are advisory only." Some exercise statutory and treaty-based power.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *PM's two main powers.* Executive (running government) and legislative influence (leading the majority party).
- *Why "exercise" not "hold".* Holding a role does not show what actually happens; exercising it is observable.
- *Why does soft power come first?* It shapes the agenda before formal votes.
- *Why does accountability need visibility?* Hidden power use cannot be challenged.
- *Short summary of a Treasurer's powers.* Sets priorities, drafts and negotiates legislation, defends decisions in parliament, and communicates publicly. Each is reviewable.
