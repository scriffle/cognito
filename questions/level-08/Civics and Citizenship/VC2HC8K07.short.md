# VC2HC8K07 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The characteristics of laws and how laws are made in Australia through parliaments (statutory law) and through the courts (common law).
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Parliament makes statutes. Courts develop common law. Both work together.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **characteristics** of laws and **how laws are made** through **parliaments** (statutory law) and **courts** (common law).
- You should be able to tell the two main sources apart.

## Core content

A **law** is a general rule that applies to everyone, can be enforced, was made by a recognised process, and applies prospectively. **Statutory law** comes from parliament: a bill is introduced, debated in both houses, voted on and given royal assent, becoming an **Act**. **Common law** comes from courts: judges apply **precedent** (past decisions on similar facts) to reach new ones. Higher-court decisions bind lower courts.

Parliament can override common law by passing a statute. Courts interpret statutes when their words are unclear. Federal matters are listed in s 51 of the Constitution; most other areas are State (schools, hospitals, traffic).

**Worked example.** Negligence. The 1932 case Donoghue v Stevenson established a duty of care between manufacturers and consumers. Australian courts adopted and developed this in Grant v Australian Knitting Mills (1936). Today the principle is supplemented by statutes like the Civil Liability Act 2002 (Vic), which set specific rules for negligence claims.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal customary law existed and continues in Aboriginal communities. Australian law has grown to recognise some aspects, e.g., native title since Mabo v Queensland (No. 2) (1992). Victorian treaty negotiations are exploring further recognition. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Laws are general rules.
- Parliament: statutes.
- Courts: common law and precedent.
- They interact and layer.

## Watch out for these traps
- "All law comes from parliament." No. Common-law principles develop through court decisions.
- "Statutes always win." Usually, but courts interpret them.
- "Aboriginal customary law has no place." Limited recognition exists since Mabo.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why does secret law contradict democracy?* People cannot follow rules they cannot see.
- *Role of precedent.* Predictability and equal treatment. Similar facts get similar outcomes.
- *Why does parliament sometimes codify common law?* To make the rule clearer, to add detail, or to change parts of it.
- *Who makes road rules?* Mostly States. Victoria's road rules are state legislation.
- *Short summary of negligence law.* Donoghue v Stevenson (1932) established duty of care via common law. The Civil Liability Act 2002 (Vic) builds on this. Courts apply both together.
