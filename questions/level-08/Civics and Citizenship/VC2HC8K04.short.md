# VC2HC8K04 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** How citizens are informed about and can participate in Australia's democracy, including use of the electoral system, contact with their elected representatives, use of lobby groups, interest groups and direct action.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Voting is the floor, not the ceiling. Information is the fuel. Pressure can be organised legally.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **how citizens are informed about and can participate** in Australia's democracy.
- You should be able to use the channels available to you, ethically and effectively.

## Core content

Voting is compulsory for citizens over 18. Information sources include public broadcasters (ABC, SBS), commercial news, social media, Hansard and AEC materials. Effective citizen contact with MPs names the issue, gives your address, and asks for a specific action.

A **lobby group** aims to influence policy. An **interest group** represents shared concerns. **Direct action** is collective action like protests, marches and petitions, legal when peaceful. Digital tools (e-petitions, online town-halls) expand reach but raise misinformation risks.

**Worked example.** Imagine a proposed freeway through Footscray. Citizens can read council planning documents, attend public consultations, write to their local council, State MP and federal MP, join a neighbourhood association, contact an environmental NGO, and take peaceful direct action through protests or letter campaigns. The mix multiplies impact.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander participation grows through bodies like the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria and advocacy organisations. The Ethnic Communities' Council of Victoria supports multicultural engagement, and SBS Radio broadcasts in over 60 languages. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Voting is the floor.
- Information is the fuel.
- Contact: name, address, specific ask.
- Groups multiply individual voices.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Voting is enough." No. Decisions between elections need participation too.
- "Direct action is illegal." No. Peaceful protest is a legal right.
- "Online petitions do nothing." Mixed; they help when paired with other channels.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is voting necessary but not sufficient?* It legitimises representatives but does not direct day-to-day decisions.
- *Why is social media alone not enough?* Algorithms favour engagement over accuracy; cross-checking with primary sources matters.
- *Why does a specific ask make contact more effective?* It gives the recipient something concrete to consider or do.
- *Why is "peaceful" key in direct action?* It keeps the action legal and protects participants while still pressuring decision-makers.
- *Short summary for a Footscray freeway issue.* Read planning documents, attend consultations, contact MPs at three levels, join a local group and a wider NGO, and take peaceful, legal action together.
