# VC2HC8S01 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Develop and refine questions to investigate contemporary political, legal and civic issues.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Topic ≠ question. Place, time, actor, measure. Refine until it leads to research.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop and refine questions** to investigate **contemporary political, legal and civic issues**.
- You should be able to turn a topic into a specific question someone could investigate.

## Core content

A topic is general (e.g., "voting"). A **question** is specific and answerable. Good civics questions are specific about place, time and people; use available sources; allow multiple perspectives; and have a clear **scope**. The narrowing funnel: start with a topic → add place → add time → add actor → add measure → result is a refined question. Replace loaded language with neutral framing. Name **stakeholders** to shape who you ask and how you interpret results.

**Worked example.** Housing affordability. Start broad. Add place (Melbourne's outer suburbs). Add time (since 2020). Add a civic angle (state and local government planning). Add a measure (rental costs, vacancy rates, approvals). Refined question: "How have state and local government planning decisions affected rental affordability in three Melbourne outer suburbs since 2020?"

**Cultural context.** Civic questions affecting First Nations communities follow AIATSIS guidelines on respectful research, with consent and community involvement. Questions about migrant communities benefit from community sources (multilingual media, council diversity strategies) and researchers who speak community languages. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Topic ≠ question.
- Place, time, actor, measure.
- Neutral wording.
- Refine until it leads to real research.

## Watch out for these traps
- "More words means better question." Not always. Clarity matters more than length.
- "A neutral question has no view." Neutral framing avoids loaded language but still asks a real question.
- "Search engines answer questions instantly." Investigable questions need more than one source.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is "I want to study housing" not enough?* It does not name place, time, people or what you want to find out.
- *Why does loaded language weaken a question?* It assumes a conclusion before evidence is examined.
- *Stakeholders in a public-housing question.* Current tenants, applicants, neighbours, council, state government, advocacy groups.
- *Sign of vagueness.* You cannot decide which sources to look at, or any source could answer it.
- *Short summary of refining a housing question.* Broad topic → add Melbourne's outer suburbs, since 2020, state and local planning, rental and vacancy measures → a specific investigable question.
