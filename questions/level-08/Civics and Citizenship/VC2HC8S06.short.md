# VC2HC8S06 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Engage in democratic decision-making, such as reaching consensus and voting.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Democratic means real say plus rules. Consensus listens; voting decides. Process legitimacy beats popular outcome.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **engage in democratic decision-making**, including **consensus** and **voting**.
- You should be able to choose the right process for the decision in front of you.

## Core content

**Consensus** is reached when everyone in the group can live with the decision. **Majority vote** decides faster when a defined majority is reached. A **quorum** is the minimum number of people needed to make a binding decision. Australia uses **preferential voting** for the House, **proportional voting** for the Senate, and **first-past-the-post** in some other contexts.

A decision is **legitimate** when people see the process as fair: everyone could speak, the rule was clear in advance, and the result was recorded.

**Worked example.** A Year 8 representative council in a Melbourne school spends a small budget. Check quorum, open discussion, allow small-group conversations, run a clarifying round, try consensus on simple items, vote on the contested item, and record the result in minutes.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander decision-making traditions often emphasise consensus, listening and inclusion of Elders. The First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria uses elected representation to negotiate treaty. Many multicultural community organisations blend consensus and voting. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Democratic means real say plus rules.
- Consensus listens; voting decides.
- Voting systems shape who wins.
- Legitimate process beats popular outcome.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Consensus means everyone agrees." It means everyone can live with the decision.
- "Loudest voice wins." That is the opposite of democratic.
- "Procedure is bureaucracy." It is what makes decisions trustworthy.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Consensus strength/limit.* Strength: builds buy-in and includes minority views. Limit: slow; can stall on hard choices.
- *Simple vs absolute majority.* Simple: more votes than any other option. Absolute: more than half of all eligible voters.
- *Why a quorum?* It ensures enough people are present that the decision represents the group.
- *Why does preferential voting encourage broader appeals?* Candidates need to be acceptable as a second or third preference.
- *Short summary of a school-council decision.* Check quorum, discuss openly, use consensus where possible, vote on contested items, record the result.
