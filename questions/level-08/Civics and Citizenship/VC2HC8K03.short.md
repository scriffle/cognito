# VC2HC8K03 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The Australian Constitution and the roles and interactions of key institutions and political actors in Australia's democracy, including the Prime Minister, the Governor-General, the Commonwealth parliament, the Ministers and Departments, State and Territory governments, and the High Court of Australia.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** The Constitution sets ground rules. Three branches check each other. Federal shares with States.

## What you are learning
- You are learning about the **Australian Constitution** and the **roles and interactions** of key **institutions and political actors**.
- You should be able to explain who does what and how they work together.

## Core content

A **constitution** is a set of rules for how a country is governed. The Australian Constitution, in force since 1901, created the federation and divided power between the Commonwealth and the States. The **legislature** (parliament, two houses) makes laws. The **executive** (Prime Minister, Ministers and Departments) carries them out. The **judiciary** (courts, headed by the High Court) interprets them.

The **Prime Minister** is the leader of the party with majority support in the House of Representatives. The **Governor-General** represents the King and gives royal assent to laws, normally on the elected government's advice. The Commonwealth covers defence, immigration and taxation; States cover schools, hospitals and police. The High Court can rule whether a law is consistent with the Constitution.

**Worked example.** A federal online-safety bill. A Minister drafts it; the House of Representatives debates and votes; the Senate debates and votes; the Governor-General gives royal assent; the High Court can later review it for constitutional validity.

**Cultural context.** The Constitution was written without Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander consultation. The 1967 referendum changed s 51(xxvi). The Uluru Statement (2017) and the 2023 Voice referendum are recent moments. In Victoria, the First Peoples' Assembly is negotiating a treaty. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Constitution sets the ground rules.
- Three branches check each other.
- Federal shares with States.
- High Court has the final say on the Constitution.

## Watch out for these traps
- "The Prime Minister is head of state." No. The King is, represented by the Governor-General.
- "All laws are made by the PM." No. Parliament passes laws; the PM leads the government that proposes most bills.
- "The Constitution is just history." No. It actively shapes today's politics.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why does parliament have two houses?* The House of Representatives represents populations; the Senate gives equal representation to each State.
- *Why is "ceremonial" not "no power"?* The Governor-General has reserve powers used in constitutional crises, even if rarely.
- *Which level of government runs a Victorian public school?* The State government.
- *Why can interpreting the Constitution reshape the country?* New interpretations change what is legally allowed, sometimes recognising rights or invalidating laws.
- *Short summary of how a federal law is made.* A Minister proposes a bill; both houses debate and vote; the Governor-General gives royal assent; the High Court can later review for constitutional validity.
