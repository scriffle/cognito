# VC2HC8S07 — Civics and Citizenship

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HC8S07. Examine methods of civic participation in a local/state/national issue and explain why and how they would participate in civic processes in a particular way.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship (Participating in civic processes).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Different issues call for different methods.
  - Choosing how to participate is itself civic skill.
  - Reasoned justification beats default action.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Personal and Social Capability; Critical and Creative Thinking; Ethical Capability.

- You are learning to **examine methods of civic participation** in a **local/state/national issue** and **explain why and how** you would **participate in a particular way**.

## Core content

**1. Civic participation has many shapes.** Methods include voting, joining a group, writing to representatives, attending consultations, submitting to committees, signing petitions, attending peaceful protests, volunteering, donating, running for office, jury service, and using social media to mobilise. *Quick check:* Why is voting only one of many methods?

**2. Match method to issue.** Local issues (e.g., a pedestrian crossing) often suit council channels and local groups. State issues (e.g., school funding) suit state MPs, unions and education advocacy groups. National issues (e.g., climate policy) suit federal MPs, large NGOs and coordinated campaigns. *Quick check:* What is one mismatch you should avoid?

**3. Considering stakeholders.** A **stakeholder** is anyone affected. Listing stakeholders helps you choose allies and anticipate opposition. *Quick check:* Why does listing stakeholders make a plan stronger?

**4. Worked example: a state-level issue, school food.** Imagine you want healthier and more affordable food options in Victorian secondary schools. Methods: write to your state MP; submit to an inquiry on student wellbeing; join or start a student-led campaign; engage parents through the school council; coordinate with peers across schools via VicSRC; write a brief op-ed for a school or local newspaper. Justify the mix: state government has the policy lever; peer coordination shows breadth; written submissions create a paper trail. *Follow the thinking:* understand the lever → choose two or three methods → plan steps → measure progress.

**5. Setting a strategy.** A **strategy** combines methods, targets the right decision-makers, and uses a realistic timeline. It names what success looks like and what you will do if the first plan does not work. *Quick check:* Why does a strategy include a back-up plan?

**6. Working with partners.** Coalitions across schools, faiths or community groups often achieve more than going alone. Identify partners early and agree shared messages and division of labour. *Quick check:* What is one risk of working in coalition?

**7. Reflecting on participation.** After action, review what worked and what did not. Civic participation is a skill that improves with practice. *Quick check:* What is one personal habit that makes participation sustainable?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** VicSRC supports Year 8 students to participate in state-level conversations. The Department of Education and the Victorian Council of Social Service publish inquiry opportunities. Local councils across Victoria run online and in-person consultations.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander young people participate through bodies such as the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria's youth engagement and Reconciliation Australia's Schools Reconciliation Challenge. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual peer networks and faith-based youth groups in Melbourne support civic participation in many languages. The Ethnic Communities' Council of Victoria coordinates Multicultural Youth Network activities.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Voting is enough." Decisions between elections still need participation.
- "More methods is always better." Spread too thin can dilute impact.
- "Direct action is the only effective method." Often, the combination of channels works best.
- "Only adults participate effectively." Year 8 students have shaped real Victorian decisions.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Voting as one method.* Voting elects representatives; most policy choices happen between elections, requiring other methods.
2. *Mismatch to avoid.* Writing to a federal MP about a council bylaw, or to a councillor about a federal policy. Identify the right level first.
3. *Why list stakeholders.* It reveals allies, opponents and people whose concerns must be addressed.
4. *Why include a back-up plan.* First attempts may fail; readiness keeps the campaign moving.
5. *Risk of coalitions.* Coordination cost; managing disagreement among members.
6. *Sustainable personal habit.* Choose a small ongoing commitment (a regular meeting, weekly letter, monthly volunteering).

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Park lighting.* Local issue: petition with neighbours; contact council; attend the next council meeting and speak. Start with the petition to demonstrate support.
2. *Cardinia youth consultation.* Online survey responses; an in-person meeting with the youth advisory officer; a coordinated school-group submission.
3. *State mental-health campaign.* Submission to a state inquiry; coordinated MP visits; awareness day with stories from young people. Timeline: 12 weeks.
4. *14-year-olds and protests.* Safety and supervision considerations; permission and family communication; informed participation versus tokenism.
5. *Wadawurrung-led environmental campaign.* Step 1: contact the Wadawurrung Traditional Owners Aboriginal Corporation. Step 2: ask how schools can support, not lead. Step 3: ensure their direction shapes the work.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Issue: better Victorian bus services to a regional school.* Methods: petition to the local council and state Transport Minister; meeting with the local State MP; coordinated emails from parents and students. Milestones: petition submitted by week 3; MP meeting by week 5. Success: a meeting scheduled and a written response that names a possible action. Sources cited.
