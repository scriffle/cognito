## Core content

**1. The Australian Constitution as foundation.** The Constitution sets the structure of government, divides powers between the Commonwealth and States, and contains a few express rights. It is rigid: changes need a referendum. *Quick check:* Why is having a rigid foundation useful for a legal system?

**2. Rule of law.** The **rule of law** means everyone — including the government — is subject to the same laws, applied by independent courts. Laws should be public, prospective (forward-looking) and applied consistently. *Quick check:* What is one practical sign that the rule of law is working in a country?

**3. Separation of powers.** Three branches share power: legislature (parliament makes laws), executive (PM and Ministers carry them out), judiciary (courts interpret them). Independent courts are essential to the rule of law. *Quick check:* What goes wrong if a Prime Minister can choose how a court rules?

**4. Court hierarchy in Victoria.** The **Magistrates' Court** handles minor crimes and small civil cases. The **County Court** handles more serious crimes and bigger civil cases. The **Supreme Court** handles the most serious matters and hears appeals from lower courts. The **High Court of Australia** is the final appeal and the only court that can interpret the Constitution. *Quick check:* Why does the system need a hierarchy of courts?

**5. Federal courts.** The **Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia** handles federal matters like family law and migration. The **Federal Court of Australia** handles matters under federal law (e.g., consumer protection, corporations). The High Court sits at the top. *Quick check:* Which court would handle a complaint about misleading advertising under Commonwealth consumer law?

**6. Worked example: a serious assault case in Melbourne.** Where it starts depends on severity. A simple assault may go through the Magistrates' Court for a quick hearing. A serious indictable assault is committed to the County Court for trial before a judge and jury. An appeal on a point of law can go to the Court of Appeal in the Supreme Court. The High Court grants special leave to appeal only in significant cases. *Follow the thinking:* charge → committal → trial court → appeal → final court.

**7. Why the rule of law matters.** Rule of law gives certainty (you can know the rules in advance), accountability (officials can be challenged), and equality (laws apply to everyone). Without it, power becomes arbitrary. *Quick check:* Why does an independent judiciary matter for ordinary citizens, not just politicians?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Victoria's court system sits at William Street, Melbourne (Supreme Court), with specialist divisions like the Koori Court (specifically for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander defendants under conditions like guilty pleas, with Elder and respected-person involvement).
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** The Koori Court, the Victorian Aboriginal Legal Service, and the work of the First Peoples' Assembly all engage with the legal system. Aboriginal customary law is recognised in limited ways through evolving doctrines (such as native title since Mabo, 1992). Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Court interpreter services, multilingual legal-aid clinics in Melbourne's south-east and west, and community legal centres support participation by people from non-English-speaking backgrounds.

## Watch out for these traps
- "The Constitution lists all rights." No. It contains only a few express rights; most come from legislation or common law.
- "Courts make the law." Mostly they interpret and apply it; courts can develop the common law, but parliament makes statute law.
- "Rule of law means strict laws." No. It means the same laws apply to everyone, fairly and predictably.
- "The Koori Court is a separate system." No. It is part of the Victorian Magistrates' Court with culturally responsive procedures.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why a rigid foundation is useful.* Stability and predictability. Citizens and institutions can plan around durable rules.
2. *Practical sign the rule of law is working.* Government decisions can be challenged in court, and the same laws apply to public officials and citizens.
3. *PM choosing court rulings.* Decisions stop being based on law and become political. Predictability and equality collapse.
4. *Why a court hierarchy.* Specialisation by seriousness, manageable workloads, and the ability to correct errors through appeals.
5. *Misleading advertising under Commonwealth law.* Federal Court of Australia (or related federal jurisdiction).
6. *Why independence matters to citizens.* It protects ordinary people from arbitrary or politically motivated treatment, not just politicians.
