# VC2HC8K09 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** How Australia's secular democracy and pluralist, multi-faith society draws upon diverse cultural origins, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures, Christian and Western heritage, and the cultures of other migrant communities.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** Secular protects, not bans, faith. Pluralism is a public habit. First Nations are the deepest layer.

## What you are learning
- You are learning how Australia's **secular democracy** and **pluralist, multi-faith society** draws upon **diverse cultural origins**.
- You should be able to read Australian society as a layered, multi-source whole.

## Core content

**Secular** democracy means government does not adopt or favour any particular religion; Section 116 of the Constitution forbids the Commonwealth from establishing a state religion. **Pluralist** means many communities share public life. **Multi-faith** means many religious traditions coexist with secular and non-religious people.

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have lived on this continent for at least 65,000 years, shaping languages, knowledge, art, governance and law (e.g., native title since Mabo, 1992). Christian and Western traditions shaped institutions like parliament, courts and common law. Migrant communities have continued shaping Australia, especially since 1945.

**Worked example.** Melbourne's suburbs reflect waves of migration: Italian and Greek heritage in Brunswick and Oakleigh, Vietnamese in Springvale and Footscray, Chinese in Box Hill, Indian in Wyndham, Sudanese in Sunshine. Lunar New Year, Diwali, Vietnamese Tết, Greek Easter, Ramadan and Eid are public events.

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Boon Wurrung/Bunurong, Wadawurrung, Gunaikurnai and other Victorian peoples actively shape policy through Traditional Owner corporations and the First Peoples' Assembly. Acknowledgement of Country is standard in Victorian public institutions. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Secular protects, not bans, faith.
- Pluralism is a public habit.
- First Nations are the deepest layer.
- Migration keeps adding.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Secular means religion is banned." No. Government does not favour one.
- "Multicultural is recent." No. Migration has shaped Australia for over 200 years.
- "First Nations contribute only to art." No. Land management, science, law and policy all benefit.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Secular vs anti-religion.* Secular means no state religion and equal treatment for all faiths. Anti-religion would treat religion as bad in general.
- *Pluralist sign in your suburb.* Multilingual signage; restaurants from many traditions; community language schools; multi-faith places of worship nearby.
- *Why "deepest layer" for First Nations.* They predate every other Australian tradition by tens of thousands of years and still shape Australia today.
- *Why "ongoing work".* Tensions and inequality persist; active institutions and habits maintain pluralism.
- *Short summary of Acknowledgement of Country.* It recognises First Nations as original custodians, builds respect, and signals that Australia's institutions include First Nations voices.
