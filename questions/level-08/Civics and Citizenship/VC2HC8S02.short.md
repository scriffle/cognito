# VC2HC8S02 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Analyse contemporary issues by locating, selecting, explaining and comparing information, data and ideas from a range of sources.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** One source is not research. Primary records, secondary interprets. Triangulate to manage bias.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **analyse** issues by **locating, selecting, explaining and comparing** sources.
- You should be able to do real research that goes beyond one search result.

## Core content

A **primary source** is original material from the time or event: Hansard, court judgments, bills, ABS statistics, witness accounts. A **secondary source** interprets or summarises: news articles, textbook chapters, analyses. Selection is judgement: check the publisher, date, corrections and stated purpose. **Bias** is a tendency to favour one side; all sources have perspectives, so use a mix to **triangulate**.

Comparing sources reveals where they agree (likely facts), where they disagree (contested interpretation), and where they have gaps.

**Worked example.** Should the federal voting age be lowered to 16? Locate: the Parliamentary Library brief; an ABC News article; a Youth Affairs Council of Victoria report; a Conversation opinion; an academic study. Compare: shared facts (current voting age, examples from other countries) and contested claims (effects on turnout and democratic engagement). Justify which source carries more weight for your specific question.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS guidelines support respectful research with First Nations communities. Multilingual community media (SBS Radio, ethnic newspapers in Vietnamese, Mandarin, Arabic) provides voices missed by mainstream sources. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- One source is not research.
- Primary records, secondary interprets.
- Compare to reveal contest.
- Triangulate to manage bias.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Government sources are unbiased." Less likely to fabricate, but still frame.
- "Two opposing views means truth is in the middle." Not always.
- "If a source is recent, it is best." Recency helps fast-moving issues; older primary sources still matter.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Hansard as primary.* It is the institution's verbatim record at the time.
- *News report on a parliamentary vote.* Secondary; the journalist interprets the event. Hansard or the bill text is primary.
- *Risk of one source.* You will miss other perspectives, errors and gaps.
- *Why reputable sources differ on the same data.* Different emphases, definitions or interpretations.
- *Short summary of investigating voting-age reform.* Use a mix of parliamentary, news, advocacy and academic sources; compare on specific claims; justify the source that best fits each part of your question.
