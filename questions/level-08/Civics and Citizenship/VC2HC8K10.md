- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship (Citizenship, diversity and identity).

By the end of this you should be able to:

## Core content

**1. Why values matter to a democracy.** A democracy needs more than laws. It also needs people who treat each other as equals, accept losing an election, and respect institutions even when they disagree. Shared values make day-to-day cooperation possible. *Quick check:* What is one democratic action that requires trust in shared values?

**2. Freedom.** **Freedom** means people can act, speak, gather and worship without unreasonable restriction. It is balanced against the freedoms of others. *Quick check:* When does one person's freedom limit another's?

**3. Respect.** **Respect** is treating people as worthy of dignity regardless of who they are. In a diverse society, respect lets very different people share schools, streets and parliament. *Quick check:* What is the difference between respect and agreement?

**4. Fairness.** **Fairness** means similar people get similar treatment, and rules are applied consistently. It does not mean everyone gets the same outcome, but that processes are open and reasons are given. *Quick check:* Why does "fair process" matter even when an outcome is disappointing?

**5. Equality of opportunity.** **Equality of opportunity** means people have a real chance to participate, succeed and access services regardless of background. This often needs more support for some, not less, to level the field. *Quick check:* Why is "everyone treated identically" not always equal opportunity?

**6. Worked example: a multicultural Year 8 cohort in Melbourne.** Imagine a class with Anglo-Australian, Vietnamese, Sudanese, Greek and Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung students. Freedom: each chooses how to express identity (clothing, language, faith). Respect: students learn each other's names and pronunciations. Fairness: school rules apply to everyone the same way. Equality of opportunity: EAL support for students still learning English; cultural-leave provisions; access to scholarships. Result: stronger social cohesion than rules alone could create. *Follow the thinking:* identify the value → name the practice → describe the outcome.

**7. Social cohesion and resilience.** **Social cohesion** is the sense that "we" includes everyone in the country, even people who disagree. **Resilience** is the ability of a society to absorb shocks (pandemics, economic downturns, terror attacks) without breaking apart. Both rest on shared values. *Quick check:* What is one Australian example of a shock the country handled cohesively?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** The Scanlon Foundation's Mapping Social Cohesion reports track Australian cohesion year by year. The Victorian Multicultural Commission and Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission support diverse communities.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have demonstrated resilience over generations and continue to lead initiatives that strengthen Australian society, including the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria and Reconciliation Australia. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant settlement services (e.g., AMES Australia, Settlement Services International) help new arrivals build belonging. Faith communities and sports clubs often provide first networks for newcomers.

## Memorable takeaways
- Laws need shared values to live.
- Freedom is balanced, not unlimited.
- Respect is wider than agreement.
- Fair process matters as much as outcome.
- Equality of opportunity may need different support for different people.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Cohesion means agreement." No. It means coexisting respectfully across disagreement.
- "Equality means identical treatment." No. It often requires different support to reach the same opportunity.
- "Values are private; laws are public." Both shape public life.
- "Shared values cannot include diverse identities." Diverse identities and shared values strengthen each other.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Democratic action needing trust.* Accepting the result of an election, even one you lost, requires trust that institutions will treat you fairly next time.
2. *When freedom limits.* When my action harms another person or their freedom, e.g., shouting hate speech that threatens a fellow citizen.
3. *Respect vs agreement.* Respect: treating someone with dignity. Agreement: thinking they are correct. You can respect without agreeing.
4. *Why fair process matters when outcome is disappointing.* You can accept losing if the rules were applied fairly. Without fair process, losers will distrust the system.
5. *Why identical treatment is not equal opportunity.* A wheelchair user, an EAL student and a hearing-impaired student all need different supports to access the same education.
6. *Australian shock handled cohesively.* COVID-19 saw widespread compliance with public-health measures and community support networks, alongside real tensions that needed addressing.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *New student with limited English.* Buddy system; bilingual welcome card; EAL support classes; staff use plain English in the first weeks.
2. *Brimbank social-cohesion projects.* Multi-faith community garden; intercultural sports league; multilingual story-times at libraries.
3. *Junior teacher inclusion plan.* Freedom: students choose how to present work. Respect: agreed classroom norms. Fairness: clear assessment criteria. Equality of opportunity: differentiated tasks and supports.
4. *All opinions and respect.* For: people deserve to be heard, even when wrong. Against: respect is for people, not for harmful claims; some claims (e.g., racism) should be challenged.
5. *Sudanese-Australian family.* Welcome event with translation; pairing with a host family for the first term; ensure school policies recognise cultural-leave needs.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Institution: Victorian public library system.* Freedom: open access to information for everyone. Respect: multilingual collections and inclusive staff training. Fairness: same rules for all visitors. Equality of opportunity: free internet, study spaces and resources for those without home access. Improvement area: outreach to communities not yet using libraries. External pressure: funding cuts that limit programs. Sources cited.
