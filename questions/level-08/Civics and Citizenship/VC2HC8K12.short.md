# VC2HC8K12 — Civics and Citizenship (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Various experiences and perspectives of national identity and citizenship, including the connections of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples to Country and Place, and of different migrant groups.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Civics and Citizenship.
- **Big ideas:** One country, many stories. Country is more than land. Migration adds, does not replace.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **various experiences and perspectives of national identity and citizenship**, including First Nations connections to **Country** and migrant journeys.
- You should be able to listen to multiple voices on what being Australian means.

## Core content

**Citizenship** is both a legal status and a lived experience: voting rights and a sense of belonging. **Country** in First Nations contexts is more than land; it includes seas, skies, kinship, language, stories and responsibilities of care. Migration brings different experiences depending on the era, reasons and policies of the time. Italian and Greek post-war, Vietnamese refugee, South Asian skilled and African humanitarian arrivals have each shaped Australia.

Hard history is part of the story: dispossession, the White Australia Policy, the Stolen Generations, racial discrimination. Recent responses include Reconciliation Australia, the Uluru Statement (2017) and the First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria.

**Worked example.** Three perspectives on Footscray. Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country runs through it, recognised in public spaces. Anglo-Australian working-class history shapes terraces and union halls. Vietnamese migration since the 1970s shapes food, business and language. Sudanese, Ethiopian and Eritrean communities have added newer threads. Each is a real Footscray story.

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Boon Wurrung, Wadawurrung, Gunaikurnai and other Victorian peoples maintain language, Country and governance through Traditional Owner corporations. The First Peoples' Assembly is negotiating treaty. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- One country, many stories.
- Country is more than land.
- Citizenship is legal and lived.
- Hard history needs honesty.

## Watch out for these traps
- "There is one Australian story." No. There are many overlapping stories.
- "First Nations peoples lost their connection to Country." No. Connection continues.
- "Migrants assimilate completely." Most integrate, keeping aspects of heritage.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *One legal right unique to citizens.* Voting in federal elections.
- *Why is "Country" capitalised?* It marks a specific First Nations concept distinct from generic land.
- *Why do "good/bad migrant" frames mislead?* They reduce complex stories to simple labels and ignore structural conditions.
- *Why does acknowledging hard history matter?* Without it, trust and reconciliation rest on incomplete foundations.
- *Short summary of three perspectives on Footscray.* Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country, colonial working-class history, post-1970s Vietnamese migration and recent African arrivals all describe a real Footscray, each foregrounding different threads.
