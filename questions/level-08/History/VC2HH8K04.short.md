# VC2HH8K04 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Patterns of continuity and change caused by significant events between 600 CE and 1750 CE, including the transformation of the Roman world and the spread of major worldviews.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** Medieval and early-modern worlds reshaped everything. Religions and ideas spread across vast distances. Continuity sits beside change.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **continuity and change** caused by **significant events between 600 CE and 1750 CE**, including the **transformation of the Roman world** and the **spread of major worldviews**.
- You should be able to read a long stretch of world history.

## Core content

The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 CE; the Eastern (Byzantine) Empire endured until 1453. New kingdoms — Franks, Visigoths, Anglo-Saxons — formed in Western Europe; Byzantine preserved Roman law and Greek learning.

**Rise of Islam** from 610 CE: by 750 the Caliphate stretched Spain to Central Asia. Baghdad and Cordoba became centres of science, philosophy and trade. **Tang and Song China** (from 618 CE) developed gunpowder, printing, the compass and paper money; Silk Roads and maritime trade linked China globally. **Medieval Europe** organised through feudalism and Catholic Church; **Crusades** (1096–1291) and **Black Death** (1346–53) reshaped politics and economy.

**Worldviews** — Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, Hinduism, Confucianism — spread through trade, conquest, missionaries and migration. The **Renaissance** (from 14th century) and **Gutenberg's printing press** (c. 1450) accelerated literacy and the **Reformation** (from 1517). From 1492, European voyages drove sustained contact between continents — trade, disease, slavery, colonisation and the **Columbian Exchange** of plants, animals and ideas.

**Cultural context.** Across this period, First Nations Australia maintained continuous societies — trade networks, lore and ecological management — independent of Eurasian events until the late 1700s. Many Victorian families trace direct heritage to Byzantine, Islamic, Tang–Song, medieval European and Renaissance Italian societies. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Many worlds, not one.
- Religions and ideas travel.
- Continuity sits beside change.
- Print and trade transformed life.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Europe drove history." Many regions led at different times.
- "Aboriginal Australia stopped while the world moved." It continued, deeply.
- "All change is progress." Plague, slavery and colonisation also reshaped lives.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why "Rome fell" is half the story.* The eastern empire continued; western kingdoms inherited Roman institutions.
- *Printing's effect.* Cheap books; literacy growth; faster spread of ideas and dissent.
- *Black Death and labour.* Population loss raised wages and shifted peasant–landlord relations.
- *Worldviews with merchants.* Trade networks carried texts, teachers and ritual objects.
- *Short summary 600–1750.* Many regions led at different times; religions and technologies spread along trade networks; from 1492 sustained contact between continents reshaped the world unevenly.
