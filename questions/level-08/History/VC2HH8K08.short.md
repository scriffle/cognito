# VC2HH8K08 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The features and structures of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities and their continuity and change over time, such as connection to Country and Place, spirituality and kinship systems.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** Communities are organised around Country, spirituality and kinship. Continuity is strong; change is real. Diversity is the rule.

A note before you begin. Specific cultural knowledge belongs to communities. Engage only with published, community-approved material.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **features and structures** of **First Nations communities** and their **continuity and change** over time — **connection to Country and Place**, **spirituality** and **kinship**.
- You should be able to read community structures with respect for diversity.

## Core content

**Connection to Country and Place.** Each Nation has long, specific relationships with Country. Identity, responsibility and rights flow from those relationships.

**Spirituality.** Lore, ceremony, song-cycles, ancestral beings and sacred sites shape daily life. Spirituality is not separate from law or ecology.

**Kinship systems.** Kinship orders relationships, marriage, ceremony and responsibilities. **Moieties** (two-part divisions), **skin groups** and **totems** structure who relates to whom. Systems vary across Nations.

**Community features**: Elders' authority; consensus decision-making; gendered knowledge; intergenerational teaching; ceremony and trade with other Nations.

**Continuity since 1788**: language revival; ceremony; kinship; connection to Country; Native Title; Aboriginal Community Controlled Organisations; cultural enterprises. **Change since 1788**: colonisation and dispossession; missions and reserves; Stolen Generations; urbanisation; treaty processes; growing political voice.

**Worked example.** The Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation represents Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Traditional Owners — Elders, members and staff — and undertakes cultural-heritage protection, land management, treaty work and Welcome to Country. Continuity meets contemporary law.

**Cultural context.** Community structures are diverse; authority lies with Traditional Owners. The First Peoples' Assembly, Yoorrook Justice Commission and Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations lead in Victoria. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Country, spirituality and kinship.
- Continuity is strong.
- Change is real.
- Authority lies with community.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Communities all share one structure." Diversity is the rule.
- "Kinship is just family." It is governance and law.
- "Continuity ended at colonisation." It continues, often strengthening.
