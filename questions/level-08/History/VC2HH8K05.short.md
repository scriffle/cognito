# VC2HH8K05 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Features of the pre-modern world, such as social structures, contact and conflict.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** Pre-modern societies had layered social structures. Contact spread goods, people and ideas. Conflict was woven into politics and culture.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **features of the pre-modern world** — **social structures, contact and conflict**.
- You should be able to compare societies on common features.

## Core content

Most pre-modern societies were stratified: rulers and nobles; priests and scholars; merchants and artisans; farmers and labourers; enslaved people. Some had powerful women rulers; most limited women's public roles. Kinship and clan ties mattered everywhere.

**Cities, villages and nomads.** Cities concentrated power and trade; villages grew most food; nomadic peoples (Mongols, Bedouin, Tuareg) traded across vast distances.

**Contact zones**: Silk Roads, Trans-Saharan caravans, Indian Ocean monsoon routes, Pacific voyaging networks, Mesoamerican corridors — moving languages, religions, diseases and technologies.

**Conflict.** Wars over territory, succession, religion and resources shaped politics. Empires expanded and collapsed (Roman, Byzantine, Tang, Abbasid, Mongol, Aztec, Inca, Songhai).

**Worked example: Mongol Empire (1206–1368).** Genghis Khan unified Mongol tribes; Pax Mongolica enabled Silk-Roads exchange (papermaking, gunpowder spread west); brutal conquests killed millions.

**Worked example: Mali Empire.** Trans-Saharan trade carried gold, salt and enslaved people, plus Islam and scholarship. Mansa Musa's 1324 hajj displayed African wealth and learning.

**Cultural context.** Across the pre-modern period, Sahul societies continued with stratified social structures, trade networks (ochre, pearl-shell, stone-axe heads), lore and inter-nation diplomacy. Many Victorian families trace heritage to these empires and contact zones. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Pre-modern worlds were stratified.
- Contact moved more than goods.
- Conflict shaped culture.
- Many regions led at different times.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Knights and castles equals 'medieval'." Many medieval worlds had no knights.
- "Pre-modern Africa was isolated." It had vast networks.
- "Aboriginal Australia wasn't part of pre-modern world." It had its own complex networks.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why nomads were pivotal.* They moved goods, animals and information across regions.
- *Indian Ocean route beyond goods.* Religions, languages, scholars, ships, ideas.
- *Why empires collapse.* Overextension, succession crises, disease, climate, revolt.
- *Mansa Musa's hajj.* Revealed West African wealth and Muslim scholarship.
- *Short summary of pre-modern features.* Stratified societies traded along great networks and fought over territory and ideas; empires rose and fell across every inhabited continent.
