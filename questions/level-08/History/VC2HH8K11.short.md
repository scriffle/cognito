## Core content

A **continuous culture** has unbroken transmission of language, lore, kinship and practice across generations on Country. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples meet this over **65,000+ years**.

**Evidence**: archaeological dating (Madjedbebe, Lake Mungo, Devil's Lair); genetic studies; oral accounts aligning with sea-level rise; intact languages, lore and ceremonial practice today.

**19th-century interpretations** framed First Nations as "vanishing" or "primitive" — wrong and harmful, fuelling Stolen Generations and dispossession. **20th-century shifts**: Mungo Man and Mungo Lady (1968, 1974); 1967 referendum; Mabo (1992); Native Title Act 1993. **21st-century shifts**: Madjedbebe at 65,000 years; Aboriginal-led research; *Dark Emu* (2014); cultural-burning policy; Uluru Statement from the Heart (2017); Yoorrook Justice Commission.

**Worked example.** Earlier narratives said Aboriginal Australia had "no agriculture". New evidence — Budj Bim aquaculture, native-grain cropping at Brewarrina — has rewritten textbooks.

Recognising deep continuity supports Native Title, treaty, cultural heritage and self-determination. Misrepresentation continues to do real harm.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal scholars, authors and Corporations lead interpretation; AIATSIS publishes peer-reviewed work. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Continuous, not just oldest.
- Evidence evolves.
- 19th-century narratives were wrong.
- Aboriginal-led research drives change.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Old textbooks were objective." They reflected colonial assumptions.
- "The story is settled." It evolves with evidence and voice.
- "Interpretation is just academic." It shapes lives.
