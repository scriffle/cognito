## Core content

**1. Social structures.** Most pre-modern societies were stratified: rulers and nobles; priests and scholars; merchants and artisans; farmers and labourers; enslaved people. Some had powerful women rulers; many limited women's public roles. Kinship and clan ties mattered everywhere. *Quick check:* Why is "everyone was a peasant" not accurate?

**2. Cities, villages and nomads.** Cities concentrated power, religion and trade. Villages grew most of the food. Nomadic peoples (Mongols, Bedouin, Tuareg) moved livestock and traded across vast distances. *Quick check:* Why were nomadic groups often pivotal in trade?

**3. Contact zones.** Trade routes — Silk Roads (Eurasia), Trans-Saharan caravans, Indian Ocean monsoon routes, Pacific voyaging networks, Mesoamerican corridors — connected societies and carried languages, religions, diseases and technologies. *Quick check:* What is one thing the Indian Ocean route moved beyond goods?

**4. Conflict.** Wars over territory, succession, religion and resources shaped politics. Empires expanded and collapsed (Roman, Byzantine, Tang, Abbasid, Mongol, Aztec, Inca, Songhai). *Quick check:* Why do empires also collapse, not just expand?

**5. Worked example: Mongol Empire (1206–1368).** Genghis Khan unified Mongol tribes and built the largest contiguous land empire. Outcomes: Pax Mongolica enabled Silk-Roads trade and ideas exchange (papermaking, gunpowder spread west); brutal conquests killed millions. *Follow the thinking:* one empire, many effects, mixed legacy.

**6. Worked example: contact between West Africa and the Mediterranean.** Trans-Saharan trade carried gold, salt and enslaved people, plus Islam, scholarship and architectural ideas. Mali's emperor Mansa Musa's hajj (1324) displayed African wealth and learning. *Quick check:* What does Mansa Musa's hajj reveal about pre-modern Africa?

**7. Conflict and culture.** War shaped art (epic poetry, monuments), law (codes, treaties), economy (taxation) and religion (crusades, jihads, missionary movements). *Quick check:* Why is treating conflict as "outside culture" wrong?

- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Across the pre-modern period, Sahul societies continued — with their own social structures, trade networks (ochre, pearl-shell, stone-axe heads), lore and inter-nation diplomacy. ## Watch out for these traps
- "Knights and castles equals 'medieval'." Many medieval worlds had no knights.
- "Trade was simple barter." Sophisticated finance and credit existed.
- "Pre-modern Africa was isolated." Trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean networks connected it widely.
- "Aboriginal Australia wasn't part of pre-modern world." It had its own complex networks.
