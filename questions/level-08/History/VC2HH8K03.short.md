# VC2HH8K03 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The different methods and sources of evidence used by historians and archaeologists to investigate early societies, and the importance of archaeology and conserving the remains, material culture and heritage of these societies.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** History is built from evidence. Different methods reveal different things. Conservation is ethical work.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **methods and sources of evidence** used by historians and archaeologists and the **importance of conserving** heritage.
- You should be able to read history as detective work.

## Core content

A **source** is anything giving evidence about the past — written texts, objects, art, oral accounts, photographs, landscapes, human remains. **Primary sources** were created during the time studied; **secondary sources** were created later.

**Archaeology methods**: **survey** (finding sites); **excavation** in layers; **dating** (stratigraphy, radiocarbon, OSL, DNA); **analysis** (bones, plants, ceramics, isotopes); **recording** (photography, drawing, GIS). Strong history triangulates across written, visual, material, oral, landscape and scientific evidence.

**Conservation** protects fragile sites and objects. UNESCO World Heritage and Australia's Aboriginal Heritage Acts give legal protection.

**Worked example: Madjedbebe (Mirarr Country, NT).** Stratified excavation; OSL dating of sand grains; pollen, charcoal and stone-tool analysis; Mirarr Traditional Owners co-led research. Result: 65,000+ years of occupation.

**Worked example: Pompeii.** A 79 CE eruption preserved a Roman town; excavation since 1748 reveals daily life; conservation now uses 3D scanning and stabilisation.

**Ethical issues**: who owns the past, who digs and where remains belong. In Australia, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander remains and cultural objects are increasingly repatriated under the Return of Cultural Heritage program.

**Cultural context.** Traditional Owners hold authority over cultural heritage on Country; AIATSIS publishes the Code of Ethics. Migrant community archives (Italian Australian Institute; Chinese Museum Melbourne; Jewish Museum of Australia) preserve diverse histories. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Sources are evidence; types vary.
- Methods reveal different layers.
- Triangulation strengthens history.
- Communities hold authority over heritage.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Old objects speak for themselves." They need context.
- "Digging fast is good." Slow, layered work preserves evidence.
- "Cultural objects are property." They are also relationships and responsibilities.
