# VC2HH8K17 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The role, contribution and achievements of a significant individual or group to change an ancient society.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** Individuals and groups can change a society, but never alone. Role, contribution and achievement are three different questions. Change is enabled by the conditions of the time.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **role, contribution and achievements** of a **significant individual or group** in changing an ancient society.
- You should be able to separate what a person *did* from what *changed* because of them.

## Core content

*Role* asks what position someone held; *contribution* asks what they added; *achievement* asks what was actually accomplished and lasted. Keep the three separate.

**Agency and conditions.** **Agency** is the capacity to cause change, but people always act inside conditions they did not create — wars, trade, technology, beliefs. Strong answers weigh both the person and the moment.

**Worked example: Augustus (63 BCE – 14 CE).** After decades of civil war, Octavian — titled **Augustus** — turned the Roman Republic into one-man rule. Role: first citizen, then effectively emperor. Contributions: ended civil wars, reorganised the army, reformed taxation, built roads and aqueducts. Achievement: roughly 200 years of relative stability (the *Pax Romana*). He relied on the army, allies and a war-weary population — not himself alone.

**Worked example: a group.** The reforms attributed to **Cleisthenes** (c. 508 BCE) reorganised Athens, but the change was carried by thousands of ordinary citizens. Groups — councils, guilds, armies — change societies as surely as named individuals.

**Significance is not approval.** Qin Shi Huang unified China and standardised script, currency and weights, but at great human cost. A figure can be significant *and* harshly judged. Always ask who wrote a source, when, and why — Augustus partly shaped his own record.

**Cultural context.** First Nations societies also remember significant individuals and groups, but who counts as significant, and how the story is told, is determined by community. Greek, Italian, Chinese and Egyptian-Australian families in Victoria carry living memory of these societies. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Role, contribution and achievement are three separate questions.
- Agency always works inside conditions.
- Groups change societies, not only individuals.
- Significance is not the same as approval.

## Watch out for these traps
- "One great leader changed everything." Conditions and supporters always matter.
- "Significant means good." Significance measures impact, not virtue.
- "If a source survives, it must be true." Surviving sources often serve the powerful.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Contribution vs achievement.* A contribution is what a person adds; an achievement is the change that resulted and lasted.
- *Augustus's lasting change.* The Principate and roughly two centuries of relative stability.
- *Significant and harshly judged.* Yes — Qin Shi Huang changed China enormously and is also judged for forced labour and censorship.
- *Why caution with a ruler's autobiography.* It is written to shape the ruler's own reputation.
- *Short summary.* Individuals and groups cause change through their role and contributions, but only inside conditions they did not create — and significance measures impact, not virtue.
