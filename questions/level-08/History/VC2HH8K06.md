## Core content

**1. Country and Place.** **Country** is the lands, waters, skies, plants, animals, ancestors and people in relationship. **Place** is a specific part of Country. *Quick check:* Why is "Country" a relational, not just geographic, term?

**2. Diversity of knowledge.** Australia has hundreds of First Nations, each with distinct languages, lore and accounts of creation. There is no single "Aboriginal story". *Quick check:* Why is "the Aboriginal Dreamtime" a misleading singular phrase?

**3. Oral accounts.** Senior knowledge holders transmit accounts through speech, song and ceremony. Some are public; many are restricted by Lore. *Quick check:* What does it mean that some knowledge is restricted?

**4. Stories.** Stories transmit law, ecology, kinship, geography and history. They are taught, performed and lived. *Quick check:* Why are stories also law?

**5. Artworks.** Painting, carving, weaving, body design, song-cycle and dance carry knowledge. The Papunya Tula art movement (from 1971), Yolŋu bark paintings, Wadawurrung weaving and Possum-skin cloak traditions are widely published examples. *Quick check:* Why do artworks count as primary sources?

**6. Worked example: Bunjil and the Birrarung.** Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung and other Kulin Nation accounts of Bunjil (the eagle creator) and the Birrarung (Yarra) are taught through community-led programs and published in works such as those of the First Peoples' Assembly and the Koorie Heritage Trust. *Follow the thinking:* community authority + published sources + classroom use.

**7. Protocols for engagement.** Use published, community-approved material; cite authors and corporations; do not record or photograph without permission; do not reproduce restricted knowledge; pay where appropriate. AIATSIS protocols and the Victorian Aboriginal Heritage Act 2006 apply. *Quick check:* Why is "I saw it on a website" not enough?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Koorie Heritage Trust; First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria; Bunjilaka at Melbourne Museum; published works by Bruce Pascoe, Tony Birch, Aunty Joy Murphy Wandin and others.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Authority lies with Traditional Owners and Aboriginal Corporations. AIATSIS publishes guidance.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Schools in diverse communities benefit from clear protocols that protect cultural knowledge while supporting respectful learning.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Dreamtime is a single religion." It is a colonial generalisation; many distinct lores exist.
- "Stories are myths." They are law, ecology and history.
- "Anyone can retell a story." Authority and protocols matter.
- "Old equals universal." Many accounts belong to specific Country and people.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why Country is relational.* It links lands, waters, skies, plants, animals, ancestors and people in ongoing responsibility.
2. *Why "the Aboriginal Dreamtime" is misleading.* It homogenises many distinct lores into one phrase.
3. *What restricted knowledge means.* Some knowledge is reserved by Lore for specific people or settings.
4. *Why stories are law.* They transmit and enforce relationships, rights and responsibilities.
5. *Why artworks are primary.* They were made by knowledge holders and carry meaning directly.
6. *Why a website is not enough.* It may not be authorised; check the source's authority and permissions.
