## Core content

First Nations occupation reaches back at least **65,000 years**. Across that span the climate, sea levels, flora and fauna shifted dramatically.

**Major changes**: megafauna extinctions (around 45,000 years ago); the **Last Glacial Maximum** (about 21,000 years ago) — cooler, drier, lower seas; **sea-level rise** at the end of the Ice Age — Tasmania bridge flooded around 14,000 years ago; Torres Strait formed about 8,000 years ago.

**Responses through technology**: stone tools; grinding stones; fire-stick farming; fish-traps (Budj Bim, Brewarrina); seasonal harvest calendars; bush-fire management; bush medicines. **Through lore**: rules for harvesting, movement, ceremony — adaptive technology in cultural form. **Through movement**: seasonal mobility; ceremonial gatherings; trade routes for food, stone, ochre and ideas.

**Worked example: memory of sea-level rise.** Nunn and Reid (2016) showed Aboriginal oral accounts along several coastlines describe landscapes that flooded thousands of years ago (Spencer Gulf, Great Barrier Reef coast, Kimberley) — matching geological evidence.

**Worked example: Budj Bim eel aquaculture.** Gunditjmara Country: an engineered system stretching back at least 6,600 years (UNESCO World Heritage), managing water flow, eel migration and seasonal harvest.

**Cultural context.** Knowledge of Deep Time is held by communities; cite Aboriginal authors and Corporations. Schools in diverse communities can draw on published deep-history work to ground learning. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Watch out for these traps
- "First Nations were passive." Active management is everywhere.
- "Memory doesn't last thousands of years." Aligned oral accounts suggest it can.
- "Aboriginal Australia is small-scale." Large engineered systems exist.
