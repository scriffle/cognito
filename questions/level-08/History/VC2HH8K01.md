## Core content

**1. Human evolution in brief.** Modern humans (Homo sapiens) evolved in Africa roughly 300,000 years ago. Earlier hominins (Homo erectus, Neanderthals, Denisovans) lived across Africa, Europe and Asia. *Quick check:* Why is "humans evolved from apes" too simple?

**2. Out of Africa migrations.** Multiple waves of Homo sapiens left Africa from about 70,000 years ago, reaching Europe, Asia, Australia and eventually the Americas. *Quick check:* Why is dating these movements still being refined?

**3. Sahul.** During the last Ice Age, lower sea levels joined Australia, New Guinea and Tasmania into one landmass called **Sahul**. Asia and the islands to the north formed **Sunda**. Between them lay deep water — **Wallacea**. *Quick check:* Why is reaching Sahul evidence of advanced seafaring?

**4. Populating Sahul.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples are among the longest continuous cultures on Earth. Archaeological evidence at Madjedbebe (Mirarr Country, NT) dates occupation to at least 65,000 years ago. Sites at Lake Mungo (Paakantji, Ngyiampaa and Mutthi Mutthi Country) document Aboriginal life and ceremony from at least 40,000 years ago. *Quick check:* What does Madjedbebe's age suggest about voyaging?

**5. Establishing societies.** Across Sahul, distinct nations developed languages, lore, songlines, technologies and economies adapted to varied environments — from arid interiors to wet tropics and southern temperate forests. *Quick check:* Why is "one culture" the wrong framing for First Nations Australia?

**6. Worked example: Lake Mungo.** Mungo Lady (cremated burial) and Mungo Man (red-ochre burial), dated at least 40,000 years old, are among the world's earliest documented ceremonial burials. Discovered by Jim Bowler in 1968–1974 on the lands of three Traditional Owner groups, the remains were returned to community. *Follow the thinking:* evidence + community + interpretation.

**7. Interpretations evolve.** New dating, DNA analysis and Aboriginal-led research keep changing the timeline. Older "single-wave" stories have given way to multiple-route, multiple-time interpretations. *Quick check:* Why is "the textbook is final" rarely true in this area?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Museums Victoria's First Peoples exhibition; the Koorie Heritage Trust; Bunjilaka galleries.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Many First Nations describe their existence on Country since the Dreaming, not via migration. Both worldviews — community story and archaeological dating — are taught together. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migration is the deep human story. Modern Victoria is also a migration society, layered on top of First Nations Country.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Humans evolved from chimps." We share an ancestor; we did not evolve from modern apes.
- "Aboriginal arrival is recent." Evidence points to 65,000+ years.
- "First Nations is one culture." It is hundreds of distinct nations.
- "Oral history isn't evidence." It is — and often aligns with archaeology.
