## Core content

**1. Deep Time on Sahul.** First Nations occupation reaches back at least 65,000 years. Across that span the climate, sea levels, flora and fauna shifted dramatically. *Quick check:* What does "Deep Time" mean in plain English?

**2. Major environmental changes.** **Megafauna extinctions** (around 45,000 years ago). **The Last Glacial Maximum** (about 21,000 years ago) — cooler, drier, lower sea levels. **Sea-level rise** at the end of the Ice Age (from about 18,000 years ago) — the land bridge to Tasmania flooded around 14,000 years ago; Torres Strait formed about 8,000 years ago. **The wet–dry tropical cycles** of the Holocene. *Quick check:* Why did so much coastal Country disappear under the sea?

**3. Responses through technology.** Stone tools and grinding stones; fire-stick farming; fish-traps (Budj Bim, Brewarrina); seasonal harvest calendars; bush-fire management; bush medicines. *Quick check:* How is fire-stick farming a climate response?

**4. Responses through lore.** Lore set rules for harvesting, movement, gathering and ceremony — including responses to drought and abundance. *Quick check:* Why is lore an adaptive technology?

**5. Responses through movement.** Mobility across seasons and Country, ceremonial gatherings linking distant Nations, and trade routes that moved food, stone, ochre and ideas. *Quick check:* Why did mobility protect food security?

**6. Worked example: memory of sea-level rise.** Researchers (e.g., Nunn and Reid 2016) have shown that Aboriginal oral accounts along several coastlines describe landscapes that flooded thousands of years ago — Spencer Gulf, the Great Barrier Reef coast, the Kimberley. These match geological evidence. *Follow the thinking:* community memory aligns with science across thousands of years.

**7. Worked example: Budj Bim eel aquaculture.** Gunditjmara Country: an engineered eel-aquaculture and stone-house system stretching back at least 6,600 years. UNESCO World Heritage. Responds to environment by managing water flow, eel migration and seasonal harvest. *Quick check:* Why is Budj Bim a strong counter-example to "no farming in Aboriginal Australia"?

## Watch out for these traps
- "First Nations were passive." Active management is everywhere.
- "Memory doesn't last thousands of years." Aligned oral accounts suggest it can.
- "Aboriginal Australia is small-scale." Large engineered systems exist.
- "Climate change is new." First Nations have responded to climate shifts for millennia.
