# VC2HH8K02 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Development and features of early societies, such as the development of technologies, agriculture, trade, social groups, religious beliefs and laws.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** Early societies developed in many places, in many ways. Technology, food, trade and ideas reshaped life. Continuity and change run side by side.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **development and features of early societies** — technologies, agriculture, trade, social groups, beliefs, laws.
- You should be able to compare societies and read change over time.

## Core content

Most of human history was foraging. From about 12,000 years ago, **agriculture** emerged independently in the Fertile Crescent, China, Mesoamerica, the Andes, the Sahel and the New Guinea Highlands.

**Technologies**: polished stone tools, pottery, weaving, metallurgy (copper, bronze, iron), writing, the wheel, irrigation — different combinations in different societies. **Trade** moved obsidian, salt, copper, shells, textiles and ideas; the Silk Roads linked China through Central Asia to the Mediterranean. **Social structures** ranged from stratified (rulers, priests, warriors, farmers, slaves) to more egalitarian. **Belief systems** — polytheistic, animist, monotheistic, ancestor-focused — shaped art, law and politics.

**Worked example: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander societies.** Sophisticated stone tools, fish-traps (Budj Bim, Brewarrina), fire and land management, complex kinship and lore, trade routes (pearl-shell, ochre, stone-axe heads), languages and seasonal calendars.

**Worked example: Mesopotamia.** Sumer (modern Iraq) from about 4000 BCE: cities around irrigated farming; writing (cuneiform) supported trade and law (Code of Hammurabi); ziggurats anchored religion.

**Cultural context.** Budj Bim Cultural Landscape (Gunditjmara Country) is World Heritage-listed for eel aquaculture going back at least 6,600 years. Victorian classrooms include living connections to Greek, Egyptian, Chinese, Indian, Mesopotamian and Andean heritage. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Many societies, many paths.
- Agriculture changed everything.
- Trade carried ideas.
- Belief systems shaped life.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Agriculture meant progress." It brought benefits and harms.
- "Only literate societies are sophisticated." Orality is its own information system.
- "Indigenous economies were hunter-gathering only." Many also farmed and traded.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why agriculture was a turning point.* Surplus enabled larger settlements, stratification and specialised work.
- *Why "wheel not universal".* Many societies developed other technologies first.
- *What trade reveals.* Long-distance exchange of goods and ideas.
- *Why Hammurabi is useful.* Codified laws give a window into values and disputes.
- *Short summary of Budj Bim vs Sumer.* Stone fish-traps and clan lore vs irrigation, writing and city-states; both built complex societies with trade and ritual.
