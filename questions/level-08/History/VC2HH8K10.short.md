# VC2HH8K10 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Cultural protocols for maintaining and preserving Country and Place, ancestral remains, cultural artefacts and artworks and shared responsibility for their maintenance, continuing use and preservation.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** Protocols translate respect. Country, remains and objects all need protection. Responsibility is shared, with community authority.

A note before you begin. Specific cultural knowledge belongs to communities. Engage only with published, community-approved material.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **cultural protocols** for protecting **Country, ancestral remains, artefacts and artworks**, and how **responsibility is shared**.
- You should be able to act respectfully in classrooms, museums and on Country.

## Core content

**Why protocols matter.** They protect cultural knowledge, sacred sites and community authority. Good intentions are not enough.

**Country and Place**: Welcome and Acknowledgement; permission for access; cultural-heritage assessments before development; protecting rock art and ceremonial places. **Ancestral remains**: return to community is the norm; the Return of Cultural Heritage program supports repatriation. **Artefacts**: documentation, conservation, restricted access where required, repatriation; museums consult Traditional Owners. **Artworks**: Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property (ICIP) protections; consent for reproduction; correct attribution; fair payment.

**Shared responsibility.** Traditional Owners hold authority. Councils protect places, museums care for objects, schools teach respectfully, the public follows protocols.

**Worked example: Victorian Aboriginal Heritage Act 2006.** The Act protects Aboriginal places and objects. Cultural Heritage Management Plans are required for many works. Registered Aboriginal Parties (RAPs) speak for Country.

**Cultural context.** AIATSIS Code of Ethics; ICIP guidance from Terri Janke and Co; Maiam nayri Wingara Data Sovereignty principles. Migrant heritage organisations work with overlapping protocols. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Protocols translate respect.
- Repatriation is the norm.
- Responsibility is shared, with community authority.
- Law backs protocol.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Protocols are red tape." They are how respect happens.
- "If it's on the internet it's free to use." Permissions still apply.
- "One community can speak for all First Nations." Each Country has its own authority.
