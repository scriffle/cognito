## Core content

**1. What is a source?** A **source** is anything that gives evidence about the past — written texts, objects, art, oral accounts, photographs, landscapes, human remains. **Primary sources** were created during the time studied; **secondary sources** were created later. *Quick check:* Is a 21st-century historian's book a primary or secondary source on ancient Rome?

**2. Archaeology methods.** **Survey** (finding sites). **Excavation** (digging in layers). **Dating**: stratigraphy, radiocarbon, OSL (luminescence), DNA. **Analysis**: bones, plant remains, ceramics, isotopes. **Recording**: photography, drawing, GIS. *Quick check:* Why is layer-by-layer digging important?

**3. Sources of evidence types.** Written, visual, material, oral, landscape, scientific. Strong history triangulates across types. *Quick check:* Why do oral traditions count as evidence?

**4. Conservation.** Sites and objects are fragile. Conservation protects them for the future. UNESCO World Heritage and Australia's Aboriginal Heritage Acts give legal protection. *Quick check:* What is one threat to a heritage site today?

**5. Worked example: Madjedbebe (Mirarr Country, NT).** Excavation in stratified layers; OSL dating of grains of sand; pollen, charcoal and stone-tool analysis; Mirarr Traditional Owners co-led research. Results: 65,000+ years of human occupation. *Follow the thinking:* method + community + interpretation.

**6. Worked example: Pompeii.** A volcanic eruption in 79 CE preserved an entire Roman town. Excavation since 1748 reveals daily life. Modern conservation work uses 3D scanning and stabilisation. *Quick check:* Why is Pompeii a "time capsule" site?

**7. Ethical issues.** Who owns the past? Who gets to dig? Where do remains belong? In Australia, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander remains and cultural objects are increasingly repatriated to community. The Return of Cultural Heritage program supports this. *Quick check:* Why is repatriation an ethical priority?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Museums Victoria; Aboriginal Heritage Council; Heritage Victoria; National Trust Victoria. The Victorian Aboriginal Heritage Act 2006 protects places, objects and ancestral remains.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Traditional Owners hold authority over cultural heritage on Country. AIATSIS publishes the Code of Ethics for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Research. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant histories are increasingly preserved through community archives (Italian Australian Institute; Chinese Museum Melbourne; Jewish Museum of Australia).

## Watch out for these traps
- "Old objects speak for themselves." They require context and interpretation.
- "Digging fast is good." Slow, layer-by-layer work preserves evidence.
- "Cultural objects are property." They are often relationships and responsibilities.
- "Old means less protection needed." Older sites are often more fragile.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *21st-century book on ancient Rome.* Secondary.
2. *Why layer-by-layer.* Lower layers are older; mixing destroys timeline evidence.
3. *Oral traditions as evidence.* They carry long-term information about Country, events and law.
4. *Threat to a heritage site.* Climate change; development; tourism; theft.
5. *Why Pompeii is a "time capsule".* Sudden burial preserved buildings, people and objects.
6. *Repatriation priority.* Cultural respect; community authority; law.
