## Core content

**1. From Rome to Romes.** The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 CE; the Eastern (Byzantine) Empire endured until 1453. New kingdoms (Franks, Visigoths, Anglo-Saxons) emerged in Western Europe; the Byzantine Empire preserved Roman law and Greek learning. *Quick check:* Why is "Rome fell" only half the story?

**2. The rise and spread of Islam.** From 610 CE in Arabia, Islam expanded rapidly. By 750 CE the Caliphate stretched from Spain to Central Asia. Cities like Baghdad and Cordoba became centres of science, philosophy and trade. *Quick check:* Why was the "House of Wisdom" in Baghdad significant?

**3. The Tang and Song in China.** From 618 CE the Tang and then the Song produced major innovations — gunpowder, printing, the compass, paper money. The Silk Roads and maritime trade linked China to the world. *Quick check:* Why did printing reshape society?

**4. Medieval Europe transformed.** Feudalism organised society. The Catholic Church dominated learning. The Crusades (1096–1291) and the Black Death (1346–1353) reshaped politics, economy and ideas. *Quick check:* How did the Black Death change labour markets?

**5. Spread of worldviews.** Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, Hinduism and Confucianism spread through trade, conquest, missionaries and migration. Each shaped law, art and learning differently. *Quick check:* Why do worldviews "travel" with merchants?

**6. The Renaissance and printing.** From the 14th century, the Italian Renaissance recovered ancient learning. Gutenberg's printing press (c. 1450) accelerated literacy and the Reformation (from 1517). *Quick check:* Why was print like an early internet?

**7. Early-modern globalisation.** From 1492, European voyages opened sustained contact between continents — bringing trade, disease, slavery, colonisation and exchange of plants, animals and ideas. The "Columbian Exchange" reshaped diets worldwide. *Follow the thinking:* events from 600 to 1750 connected the world unevenly.

- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Across this period (Deep Time continuing), First Nations Australia maintained continuous societies — trade networks, lore and ecological management — independent of Eurasian events until the late 1700s. ## Watch out for these traps
- "Europe drove history." Many regions led at different times.
- "Religion is private." It shaped law, art, politics, science.
- "Aboriginal Australia stopped while the world moved." It continued, deeply.
- "All change is progress." Plague, slavery and colonisation also reshaped lives.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why "Rome fell" is half the story.* The eastern empire continued; western kingdoms inherited Roman institutions.
2. *House of Wisdom.* Translation, mathematics, astronomy and philosophy under the Abbasid Caliphate.
3. *Printing's effect.* Cheap books; literacy growth; faster spread of ideas and dissent.
4. *Black Death and labour.* Population loss raised wages and changed peasant–landlord relations.
5. *Worldviews travelling with merchants.* Networks carried texts, teachers and ritual objects.
6. *Print like early internet.* Cheap copying broke information monopolies.
