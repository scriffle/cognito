# VC2HH8K09 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Significant Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples' beliefs and values that shaped, and continue to shape, everyday life, such as the relationships with Country and Place, land, trade, technologies and stories.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** Beliefs and values guide daily life. Country, trade, technology and story connect. Values shape the present.

A note before you begin. Specific cultural knowledge belongs to communities. Engage only with published, community-approved material.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **beliefs and values** that shape **everyday life**, including relationships with **Country, land, trade, technologies and stories**.
- You should be able to read values as lived practice.

## Core content

Across diverse Nations, common values recur: **custodial responsibility** to Country; **reciprocity** with kin and Country; **respect for Elders and Lore**; **truthful speaking**; **patience and time-depth**; **collective wellbeing**.

**Country and Place.** Daily decisions respect Country. Welcome to Country (Traditional Owners) and Acknowledgement (visitors) are public expressions.

**Land**: seasonal calendars (Gariwerd six-season; D'harawal six-season); fire-stick farming; protecting sacred sites; harvesting in season.

**Trade**: long-distance exchange of ochre, pearl-shell, stone-axe heads and fibres, woven with ceremony — trading partners were also ceremonial partners.

**Technologies**: tools designed for sustainable use across generations; innovation tested by Lore.

**Stories**: transmit Lore, ecology, kinship, geography and history; some restricted by Lore.

**Worked example: Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung today.** Welcome to Country; Birrarung cultural-heritage protection; language reclamation through the Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation; partnership with Parks Victoria.

**Cultural context.** Values are lived; authority lies with community. The First Peoples' Assembly, Koorie Heritage Trust and Yoorrook Justice Commission lead in Victoria. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Custodial responsibility runs through everything.
- Country, trade, tech and story connect.
- Values are present-tense.
- Authority lies with community.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Values are abstract." They are practiced daily.
- "Welcome and Acknowledgement are the same." Only Traditional Owners give Welcome.
- "Story is just entertainment." It carries Lore.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Custody vs ownership.* Custody is ongoing responsibility under Lore; ownership is a market right.
- *Why seasons are more detailed.* Calendars track plants, animals, water and weather together.
- *Why trade is more than goods.* It is also ceremony, kinship and obligation.
- *Sustainable design in values.* Tools and harvesting must protect Country for future generations.
- *Short summary of Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung today.* Custodial responsibility shows up in Welcome, Birrarung protection, language revival and treaty work — values lived in the present.
