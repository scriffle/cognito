# VC2HH8K16 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Causes and consequences of contacts and conflicts within and/or with other societies, resulting in developments such as the conquest of other lands, the expansion of trade and peace treaties.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** Contact was driven by trade, migration and ambition. Conflict arose from competition for resources, territory and power. Both produced lasting consequences — empires, trade networks, cultural exchange and destruction.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **causes and consequences of contacts and conflicts** within and between ancient societies.
- You should be able to explain why contact sometimes led to trade and sometimes to conquest.

## Core content

Ancient societies made contact through **trade** (seeking goods unavailable locally), **migration** (population pressure, opportunity), **diplomacy** (marriage alliances, embassies) and **exploration**. Not all contact was peaceful, but not all was violent.

**Conflicts** arose from competition for fertile land, water, trade routes and strategic positions — but also from dynastic ambition, religious difference and internal disputes such as civil wars and succession crises.

**Worked example: Rome and Carthage (264–146 BCE).** Both sought control of the western Mediterranean. Three wars (the Punic Wars) ended with Rome's total destruction of Carthage. Cause: trade and territorial competition. Consequence: Rome became a Mediterranean empire. The consequences reshaped politics, economy and culture for centuries.

**Trade as peaceful contact.** The Silk Road (from c. 200 BCE) connected China, Central Asia, Persia, India and the Mediterranean — carrying silk, spices, glassware, religions and disease. The Indian Ocean network linked East Africa, Arabia, India and South-East Asia. Trade spread ideas as well as goods.

**Peace treaties.** The earliest known treaty — between Ramesses II (Egypt) and Hattusili III (Hittite) around 1259 BCE — established borders, prisoner exchanges and mutual defence. Diplomacy was a tool of statecraft alongside war.

**First Nations contact networks.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples maintained continent-wide exchange networks for tens of thousands of years — moving greenstone, ochre, shells, songs and ceremony. The Pituri trade route crossed thousands of kilometres. Contact was governed by kinship obligation and lore. These networks show that sophisticated exchange and diplomacy are not uniquely "civilised" inventions. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Contact was driven by trade, migration, diplomacy and ambition.
- Conflict arose from competition — but also honour, religion and internal disputes.
- Peace treaties are as significant as battles.
- First Nations trade networks show long-distance exchange governed by lore.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Contact means conquest." Many contacts were peaceful and mutually beneficial.
- "Ancient trade was simple." Long-distance networks were sophisticated and governed by agreements.
- "Aboriginal Australia was isolated." Continent-wide trade operated for tens of thousands of years.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why seek contact for tin.* Bronze requires tin; without local supply, trade is essential for military and economic power.
- *Why trade spreads religion.* Merchants carry beliefs, build temples abroad, and create communities that attract converts.
- *Why both sides benefit from a peace treaty.* War is expensive; peace allows trade, rebuilding and security.
- *What First Nations trade networks show.* Complex society, long-distance organisation and sustained inter-group cooperation over millennia.
- *Short summary.* Ancient contacts and conflicts were driven by trade, ambition and competition. The Punic Wars show how rivalry leads to empire; the Silk Road shows how trade spreads culture; First Nations networks show exchange governed by kinship and lore across tens of thousands of years.
