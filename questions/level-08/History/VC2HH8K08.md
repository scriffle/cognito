## Core content

**1. Connection to Country and Place.** Each Nation has long, specific relationships with Country and Place. Identity, responsibility and rights flow from those relationships. *Quick check:* Why does asking "where are you from?" matter in many First Nations contexts?

**2. Spirituality.** Lore, ceremony, song-cycles, ancestral beings and sacred sites shape daily life. Spirituality is not separate from law or ecology. *Quick check:* Why is "spirituality is private belief" too narrow?

**3. Kinship systems.** Kinship orders relationships, marriage, ceremony and responsibilities. **Moieties** (two-part divisions), **skin groups** and **totems** structure who relates to whom and how. Systems vary across Nations. *Quick check:* Why is a strong kinship system also a strong governance system?

**4. Community features.** Elders' authority; consensus decision-making; gendered knowledge; intergenerational teaching; ceremony and trade with other Nations. *Quick check:* What is one role of Elders today?

**5. Continuity since 1788.** Language revival; ceremony; kinship; connection to Country; Native Title; Aboriginal Community Controlled Organisations; cultural enterprises. *Quick check:* What is one Victorian example of continuity in 2026?

**6. Change since 1788.** Colonisation and dispossession; missions and reserves; Stolen Generations; modern urbanisation; treaty processes; growing political voice. *Quick check:* Why is "change" not always loss?

**7. Worked example: Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung community structure.** The Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation represents Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Traditional Owners. It includes Elders, members and staff; engages in cultural-heritage protection, land management, treaty work and welcome to Country. *Follow the thinking:* community structure today reflects deep continuity and contemporary law.

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria; Yoorrook Justice Commission; Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations; Aboriginal Community Controlled Organisations (ACCOs).
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Community structures are diverse and authority lies with Traditional Owners. AIATSIS publishes guidance. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Victorian schools can support respectful engagement with local Aboriginal community structures while celebrating diverse heritage.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Communities all share one structure." Diversity is the rule.
- "Kinship is just family." It is governance and law.
- "Continuity ended at colonisation." It continues, often strengthening.
- "Specific knowledge is open." Authority and protocols apply.
