# VC2HH8K06 — History (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The diversity of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples' knowledge and understanding of the creation of Country and Place and people in oral accounts, stories and artworks.
- **Year / Area:** Years 7–8, History.
- **Big ideas:** Country and Place have creation accounts taught through speech, story and artwork. Knowledge is diverse — many Nations. Stories and artworks are evidence and authority.

A note before you begin. Specific cultural knowledge belongs to communities. Engage only with published, community-approved material.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **diversity** of **First Nations knowledge** of the **creation of Country, Place and people** in **oral accounts, stories and artworks**.
- You should be able to read First Nations knowledge with respect.

## Core content

**Country** is the lands, waters, skies, plants, animals, ancestors and people in relationship. **Place** is a specific part of Country.

Australia has hundreds of First Nations, each with distinct languages, lore and accounts of creation. There is no single "Aboriginal story". Senior knowledge holders transmit **oral accounts** through speech, song and ceremony — some public, many restricted by Lore. **Stories** transmit law, ecology, kinship, geography and history. **Artworks** — painting, carving, weaving, body design, song-cycle, dance — carry knowledge. Papunya Tula (from 1971), Yolŋu bark paintings, Wadawurrung weaving and Possum-skin cloak traditions are widely published examples.

**Worked example.** Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung and other Kulin Nation accounts of Bunjil (the eagle creator) and the Birrarung (Yarra) are taught through community-led programs and published in works of the First Peoples' Assembly and the Koorie Heritage Trust.

**Protocols.** Use published, community-approved material; cite authors and corporations; do not record or photograph without permission; do not reproduce restricted knowledge; pay where appropriate. AIATSIS protocols and the Victorian Aboriginal Heritage Act 2006 apply.

**Cultural context.** Authority lies with Traditional Owners and Aboriginal Corporations. Schools in diverse communities benefit from clear protocols. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Country is relational.
- Knowledge is diverse.
- Stories carry law.
- Protocols matter.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Dreamtime is a single religion." It is a colonial generalisation.
- "Stories are myths." They are law, ecology and history.
- "Anyone can retell a story." Authority and protocols matter.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why Country is relational.* It links lands, waters, skies, plants, animals, ancestors and people in ongoing responsibility.
- *Why "Dreamtime" singular misleads.* It homogenises many distinct lores.
- *Why stories are law.* They transmit and enforce relationships, rights and responsibilities.
- *Why artworks are primary.* They were made by knowledge holders and carry meaning directly.
- *Short summary of respectful engagement.* Use community-approved sources, credit authors and corporations, follow Lore, and respect restricted knowledge.
