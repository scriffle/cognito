# VC2HG8K13 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The spiritual, aesthetic and cultural value of landscapes and landforms for people, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Landscapes mean more than their shapes. Three kinds of value overlap. First Nations connections are continuing.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **spiritual, aesthetic and cultural value** of landscapes and landforms.
- You should be able to see landscapes as more than land.

## Core content

**Spiritual value** is sacred or religious meaning. **Aesthetic value** is sensory beauty and awe. **Cultural value** is community identity, history and practice. The same landform can hold all three. Sacred sites like Uluru (Anangu Country), Wilpena Pound / Ikara (Adnyamathanha Country) and the Grampians / Gariwerd (Jardwadjali and Djab Wurrung Country) have specific cultural protocols.

Respectful access follows Traditional Owner direction. Tourism can support protection but can also damage sites; climate change is altering landscapes; honest management names values explicitly.

**Worked example.** Wilsons Promontory. Spiritual: Bunurong/Boon Wurrung and Gunaikurnai connections to Country. Aesthetic: rugged coast, granite peaks, beaches. Cultural: walking and family-camping traditions. Joint management increasingly includes Traditional Owners.

**Cultural context.** Joint management is increasing across Victoria; the Gunaikurnai and Victorian Government Joint Management Plan covers parts of East Gippsland. Migrant communities form aesthetic and cultural attachments to Australian landscapes. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Three kinds of value, often combined.
- Sacred sites need protocols.
- Aesthetic value supports wellbeing.
- Management names values explicitly.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Spiritual = religious." Spiritual value can be broader.
- "Aesthetic is just pretty." It includes awe and connection.
- "Tourism is always harmful." Well-designed tourism can support protection.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why are values not "extras"?* They shape how people relate to and protect places.
- *Why was climbing Uluru closed?* Anangu Traditional Owners requested closure on cultural grounds; agreed by the Board in 2017 and effective in 2019.
- *How does aesthetic value support mental health?* Awe reduces stress; visual quality boosts mood.
- *Practical respectful step.* Stay on marked paths; ask permission before photography; follow ranger and Traditional Owner direction.
- *Short summary of Wilsons Promontory.* A single landscape with overlapping spiritual, aesthetic and cultural values; respectful access follows Traditional Owner and Parks Victoria direction.
