## Core content

**1. What urbanisation is.** **Urbanisation** is the process by which the share of people living in cities grows. The world is more than half urban; many countries are still urbanising rapidly. *Quick check:* Why is "more people in cities" not the same as "fewer people in rural areas"?

**2. Causes.** **Push factors** drive people from rural areas: low wages, drought, conflict, lack of services. **Pull factors** attract people to cities: jobs, services, education, family ties. Birth rates and policy also matter. *Quick check:* What is one push and one pull factor in Indonesia or China?

**3. Patterns.** Cities grow through migration, natural increase and reclassification. **Megacities** (10 million+) like Jakarta, Mumbai, Shanghai concentrate growth. Many smaller cities also grow rapidly. *Quick check:* What is one Australian connection to a megacity?

**4. Worked example: urbanisation in Indonesia.** Causes: jobs in Jakarta, Surabaya and Bandung; rural-urban wage gap; education access; coastal-trade hubs. Patterns: Java is highly urbanised; outer islands urbanise more slowly. Environmental impacts: air pollution; flooding from land subsidence in Jakarta; deforestation in surrounding areas. Economic impacts: GDP growth; informal-sector expansion; income inequality. Social impacts: housing pressure; new lifestyles; rural-urban family networks. Response: Indonesia is moving its capital to Nusantara to relieve Jakarta. *Follow the thinking:* causes → patterns → impacts → response.

**5. Impacts in detail.** Environmental: air and water pollution; heat islands; biodiversity loss in urban fringes. Economic: productivity gains; informal economy; inequality. Social: cultural change; family-network strain; new opportunities. *Quick check:* What is one environmental impact often overlooked?

**6. Comparison.** Different countries follow different paths: China's planned urbanisation; India's mixed pattern; USA's earlier urbanisation now plateauing; Indonesia's rapid recent growth. *Quick check:* Why does comparison reveal more than single-country study?

**7. Implications for Australia.** Australia is highly urbanised; its trade and migration connect closely to urbanising Asian countries. *Quick check:* What is one Australian sector affected by Asian urbanisation?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Melbourne is part of a global network of cities. Trade with Indonesia, China and India shapes parts of Victoria's economy.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Many Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people live in cities; the share has grown over decades. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Many Melbourne residents have lived urbanisation first-hand in their home countries before migrating. Their experience informs Melbourne's own urban future.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Cities are bad." They are also engines of opportunity.
- "Rural and urban are separate." Networks connect them.
- "Megacities are the whole story." Smaller cities matter too.
- "Migration is the only cause." Natural increase and reclassification also drive growth.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *"More in cities" not same as "fewer in rural".* Rural populations can grow in absolute numbers even while the urban share rises.
2. *Push and pull example.* Indonesia: rural low wages and natural disasters push; Jakarta jobs and education pull.
3. *Australian megacity connection.* Trade with Shanghai and Mumbai; migration links; education exports to Jakarta.
4. *Overlooked environmental impact.* Urban heat island; loss of fringe biodiversity; groundwater impacts.
5. *Why comparison reveals more.* It exposes how policy and history shape similar pressures differently.
6. *Australian sector affected.* Agriculture exports; education (international students); minerals.

4. *Slow rapid urbanisation debate.* For: protect environment and services. Against: limit opportunity. Both sides can be reconciled with planning.
5. *Ho Chi Minh City vs Melbourne.* Density; transport mix; family ties spanning both; food culture.
