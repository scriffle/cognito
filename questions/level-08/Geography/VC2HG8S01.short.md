# VC2HG8S01 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Develop questions for a geographical inquiry related to a phenomenon, issue or challenge.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Topic ≠ question. Place, scale, time, measure. Concepts frame questions.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **develop questions for a geographical inquiry**.
- You should be able to ask questions a geographer could investigate.

## Core content

A topic is general; an **inquiry question** is specific. Refining narrows scope by adding place, scale, time and measure. Geographic concepts (space, place, interconnection, change, sustainability) help frame questions. Criteria: specific, investigable, open, neutral, operationalisable. Iterate as you learn; community priorities can shape the question.

**Worked example.** A flood-risk question. Initial: Are floods getting worse? Refined v1: Has flooding in regional Victoria become more frequent since 2000? Refined v2: How has the frequency of major flooding in the Goulburn-Murray region changed between 2000 and 2024, based on Bureau of Meteorology and floodplain-management data?

**Cultural context.** Inquiries affecting Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities should follow AIATSIS guidelines. The First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria publishes community-approved materials. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Topic ≠ question.
- Place, scale, time, measure.
- Concepts frame questions.
- Iteration is part of inquiry.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Bigger question is stronger." Specific questions get tested.
- "Specific = narrow." Specific means clear; still substantive.
- "Indigenous topics are off-limits." Valid with appropriate ethics.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *"Bushfires" as topic.* Names a phenomenon but no place, time, measure or actor.
- *Most overlooked criterion.* Operationalisability.
- *Space question about your suburb.* How are services distributed?
- *Why is iteration not failure?* Refining responds to evidence.
- *Short summary of the flood-risk refinement.* From "are floods worse?" to a specific, sourced, measured regional question over a defined time window.
