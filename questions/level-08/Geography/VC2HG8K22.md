# VC2HG8K22 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG8K22. How space, place, interconnection, change and sustainability can be applied to understand the process of urbanisation, and its impacts on places.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Geography (Changing nations).
- **Big ideas:**

  - Each concept opens different questions.
  - Sustainability brings the long view.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Sustainability; Critical and Creative Thinking; Literacy.

## What you are learning
- You are learning how **space, place, interconnection, change and sustainability** can be applied to **understand urbanisation** and its **impacts on places**.
- You are learning to use concepts as analytical tools.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **space**, **place**, **interconnection**, **change** and **sustainability** in your own words.
- Apply each concept to one urbanising city.
- Combine concepts in a short analysis.
- Discuss why sustainability is the long view.

## Core content

**1. Five geographic concepts.** **Space**: location, distribution, distance, scale. **Place**: what makes somewhere distinct. **Interconnection**: how places and people affect each other. **Change**: how features shift over time. **Sustainability**: whether processes can continue without harming future options. *Quick check:* Why does each concept open different questions?

**2. Space and urbanisation.** Cities grow in patterns: outward (sprawl), upward (high-rise), or both. Distribution of services, transport and housing matters. *Quick check:* What is one space question about Melbourne's growth?

**3. Place and urbanisation.** Different cities feel different: Tokyo's transit; Mumbai's density; Melbourne's coffee culture; Jakarta's coastal sprawl. Place is shaped by history, culture and geography. *Quick check:* What is one Melbourne feature that makes it feel like Melbourne?

**4. Interconnection and urbanisation.** Cities depend on rural areas, other cities and global trade. Food, water, energy, capital and people flow in and out. *Quick check:* What is one interconnection your city depends on daily?

**5. Worked example: applying concepts to Jakarta.** Space: spread over Java's coast, with a dense core. Place: cultural and political capital of Indonesia, sinking due to land subsidence. Interconnection: trade hub for Indonesia and ASEAN. Change: rapid growth and capital-relocation plan to Nusantara. Sustainability: water and land issues threaten long-term liveability. Combining concepts gives a fuller picture than any one alone. *Follow the thinking:* apply each concept → identify a tension → propose a question.

**6. Change and sustainability.** Change is what is happening; sustainability is whether it can continue. Many urbanising cities face both fast change and sustainability challenges (water, energy, climate). *Quick check:* Why does change without sustainability assessment risk problems?

**7. Implications for Australia.** Australian cities are highly urbanised. Applying these concepts reveals trade-offs (density vs sprawl; affordability vs liveability; growth vs sustainability). *Quick check:* What is one Australian trade-off best seen with these concepts?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Plan Melbourne uses geographic-concept thinking implicitly: integrated land-use, transport and environment. Council planning frameworks apply concepts at local scale.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** First Nations geographic concepts (Country, kinship, seasonal indicators) add depth and challenge Western frames. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant communities have lived urbanisation in many forms; their insight applies to Melbourne's future.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Concepts are jargon." They organise thinking.
- "Sustainability is environmental only." It includes economic and social too.
- "One concept is enough." Combination reveals the picture.
- "First Nations concepts are alternative." They are complementary.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why concepts open different questions.* They focus attention on different features and prevent superficial description.
2. *Space question about Melbourne's growth.* How is density distributed across the metro area? Where do services lag population?
3. *Melbourne place feature.* Coffee culture; sports traditions; multicultural festivals; the bay.
4. *Daily interconnection.* Food from rural Victoria; water from upstream catchments; goods from overseas.
5. *Change without sustainability assessment.* Fast solutions may create long-term problems (e.g., car-dependent suburbs).
6. *Australian trade-off seen with concepts.* Density vs sprawl; growth vs sustainability; affordability vs amenity.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Five concepts in a paragraph.* Choose one suburb and apply each in turn; combine into one observation.
2. *Greater Geelong growth questions.* How will density be distributed? How will sense of place be protected? What sustainability targets apply?
3. *Junior planner studying Jakarta.* Space (sprawl vs core); change (capital relocation); sustainability (water).
4. *Sustainability outweighing other concepts debate.* For: long-term liveability. Against: immediate needs (housing, employment). Both sides can be balanced in policy.
5. *Wadawurrung Geelong project.* Step 1: contact the Wadawurrung Traditional Owners Aboriginal Corporation. Step 2: integrate First Nations concepts with Plan Melbourne framework. Step 3: report-back to community.

Interconnection: trade hub, diaspora ties globally including Australia. Change: rapid growth; new transport projects. Sustainability: water and coastal-flood risks. Australian connection: diaspora and trade. Sources cited.
