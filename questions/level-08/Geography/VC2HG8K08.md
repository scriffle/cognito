# VC2HG8K08 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG8K08. The influence of environmental quality on people's perceptions of the liveability of places.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Geography (Place and liveability).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Environmental quality shapes daily life.
  - Air, water, noise, green space and climate matter.
  - Perception varies but measurable factors converge.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Sustainability; Health and Physical Education; Critical and Creative Thinking.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **influence of environmental quality on perceptions of liveability**.
- You are learning to see environment as part of what makes a place feel liveable.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **environmental quality**, **urban heat island** and **green space** in your own words.
- List five environmental factors that affect liveability.
- Identify two ways poor environmental quality affects health and wellbeing.
- Describe one Victorian initiative that improves environmental quality.

## Core content

**1. What environmental quality covers.** Air quality; water quality; noise; green space; climate and microclimate; biodiversity; safety from hazards. Each contributes to whether a place feels good to live in. *Quick check:* Why is air quality often invisible but important?

**2. Air quality.** Measured by particulate matter (PM2.5, PM10), nitrogen dioxide, ozone. Affected by traffic, industry, bushfire smoke and household heating. Poor air harms respiratory and cardiovascular health. *Quick check:* What is one Victorian event that recently raised concerns about air quality?

**3. Urban heat island.** **Urban heat islands** are city areas warmer than surrounding land due to dark surfaces, low canopy and density of heat sources. They worsen heatwaves and increase cooling costs. Tree canopies and reflective surfaces help. *Quick check:* Why are some Melbourne suburbs hotter than others on the same day?

**4. Green space.** Parks, gardens, street trees and natural areas provide cooling, biodiversity, mental-health benefits and community space. Distribution of green space is uneven across Melbourne. *Quick check:* What is one mental-health benefit of accessible green space?

**5. Worked example: comparing two Melbourne suburbs.** Eastern suburbs with high canopy and lower density (Boroondara): cooler microclimate, more parks, higher house prices. Western and northern growth areas (Wyndham, Hume): lower canopy cover, hotter days, expanding population. Liveability perception differs partly due to these environmental factors. *Follow the thinking:* identify factor → measure → describe impact → propose response.

**6. Noise and other factors.** Aircraft noise near airports; traffic noise on main roads; industrial noise. Solid policy reduces exposure (sound walls, planning). *Quick check:* Why does noise affect wellbeing beyond annoyance?

**7. Initiatives to improve environmental quality.** Plan Melbourne's 20-minute neighbourhood; Greening the West and other canopy programs; EPA Victoria air-quality monitoring; cool-burning partnerships; water-sensitive urban design. *Quick check:* What is one example of a Victorian canopy program?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** EPA Victoria monitors air quality; the Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action publishes environmental-quality data. Local councils (e.g., Brimbank, Wyndham) lead canopy programs.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Traditional Owner-led land management contributes to environmental quality through cool burning, waterway care and reintroduction of native species. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual communities help maintain community gardens, neighbourhood-clean-up programs and faith-based environmental initiatives across Melbourne.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Environment is just nature." It includes urban environments too.
- "Air quality is the same across a city." It varies block by block.
- "Trees are decoration." Canopy provides cooling and health benefits.
- "Noise is just annoying." Chronic noise harms sleep and cardiovascular health.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why invisible-but-important air quality.* You cannot see particulate matter; health impacts accumulate over time.
2. *Recent Victorian air-quality event.* Bushfire smoke in 2019–20; major industrial incidents; major heatwave events with smog.
3. *Why some Melbourne suburbs are hotter.* Less canopy, more dark surfaces, denser construction, fewer waterways.
4. *Green-space mental-health benefit.* Lower stress, better mood, recovery from attention fatigue, social connection.
5. *Why noise affects wellbeing.* Sleep disruption; chronic stress; cardiovascular impact.
6. *Victorian canopy program example.* Greening the West; council-level canopy targets in Brimbank, Wyndham, Whittlesea.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Three suburb factors.* Canopy cover; traffic noise; proximity to green space.
2. *Hume canopy priorities.* Streets with low existing canopy; school surroundings; bus-stop and footpath shade.
3. *Junior environmental officer.* Multilingual messaging; live air-quality data; partnership with schools and councils.
4. *Minimum canopy targets debate.* For: equity and resilience. Against: cost and design constraints. Both sides can be reconciled with phased targets.
5. *Vietnamese-Australian community-gardens.* Provide land access; co-design with community elders; bilingual signage and events.

Community-led: school-yard greening programs. Sources cited.
