# VC2HG8K01 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The classification of water as a renewable resource, the forms that it takes as a resource in the water cycle, and the ways in which flows of water connect and change places.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Water is renewable but not unlimited. The water cycle moves water through many forms. Flows connect and change places.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **classification of water as renewable**, its **forms** in the **water cycle**, and how **flows connect places**.
- You should be able to see water as a moving, shared resource.

## Core content

A **renewable resource** can be replenished naturally within a human timeframe. Water is renewable through the **water cycle**: evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, runoff and groundwater recharge. Forms include liquid (rivers, lakes, oceans, groundwater), solid (snow, ice, glaciers) and gas (water vapour, clouds). A **catchment** is an area that drains to a single river system. Water flow connects uplands, plains, towns and coasts.

Humans reshape flows through dams, weirs, irrigation, stormwater systems and pipelines. Each change has benefits and costs.

**Worked example.** The Yarra River. Rises on Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country east of Melbourne; flows through forests, the Yarra Valley, suburbs and into Port Phillip Bay. Each stage changes water quality, temperature and use. Forests filter; farms add nutrients; cities discharge stormwater.

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Bunurong/Boon Wurrung, Wadawurrung, Yorta Yorta and other Victorian peoples have managed waterways for tens of thousands of years. The eel-harvest aquaculture at Budj Bim (Gunditjmara Country) is a UNESCO World Heritage example. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Water is renewable but can be scarce.
- Three forms in the cycle.
- Catchments collect to rivers.
- Flows connect and change places.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Renewable means infinite." It means recyclable in human timeframes.
- "All water is fresh water." Most is salt.
- "Aboriginal water knowledge is folklore." It is observation-based, contemporary and complex.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Form in atmosphere most.* Gas (water vapour) and droplets in clouds.
- *Step a hot day speeds up.* Evaporation and transpiration.

- *Dam trade-off.* Storage and power vs habitat loss and fish-passage blockage.
