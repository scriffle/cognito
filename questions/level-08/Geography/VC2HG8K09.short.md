# VC2HG8K09 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The influence of social connectedness and community identity on people's perceptions of the liveability of places, including the cultural connectedness of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples to Country and Place.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Connection makes places feel liveable. Country is more than land. Recognition strengthens belonging.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **influence of social connectedness and community identity** on **liveability**, including **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander connections to Country**.
- You should be able to read liveability through belonging as well as services.

## Core content

**Social connectedness** is how strongly people feel linked to others around them. **Community identity** is how a place sees itself. **Country** in First Nations contexts includes land, water, sky, plants, animals, stories, ancestors and responsibilities. The Yarra River Protection Act 2017 formally recognises Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung connection to Birrarung.

A well-serviced suburb can feel unliveable if you do not belong; a less-serviced area can feel deeply liveable when you do. Public recognition (place-naming, signage, Acknowledgement of Country, multilingual signs) strengthens belonging.

**Worked example.** Footscray as a multicultural place. Identity shaped by Vietnamese, Sudanese, Italian and other communities; Footscray Market; football traditions; Maribyrnong River. Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country runs through it. Long-term residents and new arrivals feel different aspects of the place; liveability depends on belonging, networks and recognition.

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Bunurong/Boon Wurrung, Wadawurrung, Gunaikurnai and other Victorian peoples lead joint-management of Country and shape state-level reform through treaty negotiations. Festivals, faith centres and community organisations across Melbourne support community identity. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Connection makes places feel liveable.
- Identity grows from history and habits.
- Country is more than land.
- Recognition strengthens connection.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Community is just where you live." It includes faith, family, sport, language.
- "Country is just land." It includes water, sky, stories, ancestors.
- "Recognition is symbolic." It also has practical and legal effects.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Small town vs big city.* Closer ties and sense of belonging trade off against services and opportunities.
- *Why is "Country" capitalised?* It marks a specific First Nations concept distinct from general land.
- *Why does "I love it here" matter?* Belonging predicts staying and contributing more than convenience does.
- *Daily recognition example.* Welcome to Country at events; dual-named places; multilingual signage.
- *Short summary of Footscray identity.* Layered Wurundjeri Country and multiple migrant-community markers; belonging varies by who you are and how long you have lived there.
