## What you are learning
- You are learning the **causes, impacts and responses** to an **atmospheric or hydrometeorological hazard**.
- You should be able to trace a hazard from cause to consequence.

## Core content

A **hazard** is a natural event with potential to harm; a **disaster** is when the hazard meets exposed and vulnerable people. Atmospheric and hydrometeorological hazards include floods, droughts, storms (cyclones), thunderstorms, hailstorms and heatwaves. Causes are mostly natural; human factors (land use, building standards, climate change) increase exposure.

Four phases: **mitigation** (planning rules, levees, education); **preparedness** (early-warning, kits, plans); **response** (evacuation, rescue, shelter); **recovery** (clean-up, rebuild, mental-health support).

**Worked example.** 2022 northern Victoria floods. Cause: La Niña rainfall; saturated ground; full reservoirs. Direct impacts: home damage, livestock losses, road closures in Echuca, Rochester, Shepparton. Indirect: mental-health strain, insurance costs, school interruption. Responses: emergency evacuation, sandbagging, SES and ADF support, disaster payments, build-back-better planning.

**Cultural context.** Aboriginal-led cool burning reduces wildfire intensity. Traditional knowledge of flood-prone areas informs land use in some catchments. Multilingual emergency communication has expanded after 2022 and 2024 events. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Hazard plus exposure equals disaster.
- Four phases: mitigate, prepare, respond, recover.
- Invest before, recover faster.
- Climate change shifts the picture.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Natural disaster." Often misleading; human factors shape outcomes.
- "Past data predicts future hazards." Climate change shifts the baseline.
- "Recovery is just rebuilding." Mental health and community fabric matter too.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Same flood, different damage.* Differences in land use, building standards, warnings and community capacity.
- *Why does "natural disaster" mislead?* Outcomes depend on human factors.
- *Underestimated indirect impact.* Mental health; long-term insurance and housing affordability shifts.
- *Climate change shift.* More extreme rainfall; longer fire seasons; sea-level rise increasing coastal flooding.
- *Short summary of the 2022 Victorian floods.* La Niña rainfall caused widespread flooding in northern towns; impacts spanned property and mental health; multi-phase responses are still shaping rebuilding.
