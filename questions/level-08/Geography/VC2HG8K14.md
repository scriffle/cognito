- You are learning to read processes in the shapes around you.

By the end of this you should be able to:

## Core content

**1. What geomorphology is.** **Geomorphology** is the study of how landforms are made and changed. It uses processes from physics, chemistry and biology. Time matters: some changes are slow (millions of years); some fast (a single flood). *Quick check:* Why is a landform "not finished"?

**2. Tectonic and volcanic processes.** Plate tectonics builds mountains, opens rifts and triggers volcanoes. Recent volcanism in western Victoria (Newer Volcanic Province) shaped basalt plains and cones over the last few million years. *Quick check:* What is one Victorian landform that came from volcanism?

**3. Weathering and erosion.** **Weathering** breaks rock in place (mechanical, chemical, biological). **Erosion** moves the weathered material by water, wind, ice or gravity. **Deposition** lays the material down to form new landforms (beaches, dunes, deltas). *Quick check:* What is one example of weathering you can see locally?

**4. Worked example: the Twelve Apostles.** Coastal limestone cliffs along the Great Ocean Road. Process: wave erosion at the base undercuts cliffs; weather weakens limestone; sea stacks form and eventually fall. The number has changed over decades as stacks collapse and new ones form. *Follow the thinking:* identify rock → identify process → trace change → predict future.

**5. River and floodplain processes.** Rivers erode in their upper reaches, transport material in their middle reaches and deposit on floodplains. Meanders, oxbow lakes and deltas reflect these processes. The Yarra, Goulburn and Murray show classic examples. *Quick check:* Why do floodplains rebuild after floods?

**6. Mass movement.** Landslides, slumps and rockfalls happen on slopes where gravity exceeds friction. Rainfall, vegetation loss and earthquakes can trigger them. *Quick check:* Why do recently cleared hills slip more easily?

**7. Local landform study.** Picking a local landform — a creek, a beach, a hill — and tracing its processes builds geographic skill. Field observation, mapping and photography are key. *Quick check:* What is one local landform you could study?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Victoria offers diverse local studies: volcanic plains (Western Victoria), coastal cliffs (Great Ocean Road), alpine peaks (Mount Buller, Mount Bogong), karst caves (Buchan), river valleys (Yarra, Goulburn).
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** First Nations land managers in Victoria have observed geomorphological change over generations; oral histories include flooding and volcanic events. The Indigenous Knowledge Institute publishes peer-reviewed work on these. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant communities arriving from many landforms (volcanic, mountainous, monsoonal) bring different geomorphological literacies.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Landforms are static." They are dynamic, often slowly.
- "Erosion is bad." It is normal; some erosion creates valued landforms.
- "Weathering = erosion." Weathering breaks in place; erosion moves material.
- "Aboriginal knowledge is just stories." It is observation-based and increasingly published.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *"Not finished" landform.* Ongoing processes continue to shape it; today's shape is a snapshot.
2. *Victorian volcanic landform.* Mount Eccles/Budj Bim; Tower Hill; basalt plains around Hamilton.
3. *Local weathering example.* Cracks in pavements; salt damage on coastal buildings; tree-roots breaking rocks.
4. *Why floodplains rebuild.* Floods deposit silt and sand, rebuilding fertile soils.
5. *Why cleared hills slip.* Roots stabilise soil; clearance reduces stability.
6. *Local landform to study.* A nearby creek bend; cliff; sand dune; rocky outcrop.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Creek or hill.* Erosion by water; weathering by rain and temperature; deposition during floods.
2. *Surf Coast cliff safety.* Setbacks from cliff edge; signage; regular geological survey.
3. *Junior geographer at a Victorian beach.* Sand grain size; wave direction; vegetation cover.
4. *Tourist access to fragile landforms.* For: education and connection. Against: damage and litter. Both sides can be reconciled with boardwalks and limits.
5. *Wadawurrung volcanic-plains knowledge.* Acknowledgement of Country; permission to take notes; follow up with community-approved materials.
