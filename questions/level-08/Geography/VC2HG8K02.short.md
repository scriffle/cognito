# VC2HG8K02 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The environmental, economic, cultural, spiritual and aesthetic uses and value of water, including for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and peoples of the Asia region.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Water has many kinds of value beyond use. Different cultures relate to water differently. Best management balances values.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **five kinds of value** of water and how **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples** and **peoples of the Asia region** value water.
- You should be able to see water as more than a utility.

## Core content

**Environmental** value: ecosystem support, biodiversity. **Economic** value: irrigation, hydro, drinking water, tourism. **Cultural** value: festivals, gatherings, identity. **Spiritual** value: sacred sites, ceremonies. **Aesthetic** value: scenic beauty, recreation. The same river holds all five. The Yarra River Protection (Wilip-gin Birrarung murron) Act 2017 recognises Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung connections to the Birrarung.

The Mekong supports food, transport and culture across Vietnam, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia. The Ganges is sacred in Hindu culture. Monsoonal flows shape life across South and Southeast Asia.

**Worked example.** The Murray-Darling Basin. Environmental: floodplain ecology, native fish. Economic: irrigated agriculture. Cultural: Yorta Yorta, Wiradjuri, Barkindji and other First Nations Peoples' law. Spiritual: significant sites. Aesthetic: paddle steamers at Echuca. Trade-offs: water allocations between extractive and environmental uses are contested.

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Wadawurrung, Yorta Yorta, Gunaikurnai and other Victorian peoples lead joint management. Migrant communities bring water values from Asia: Vietnamese, Cambodian, Indian, Sri Lankan, Pakistani communities maintain water-related festivals. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Five kinds of value.
- Aboriginal water values are legal and current.
- Asian water knowledge shapes Melbourne.
- Trade-offs are real.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Water value is just economic." Four other kinds matter.
- "Spiritual value is private." It can shape law and management.
- "Asian water knowledge is foreign." Many Australians bring it with them.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Victorian wetland with migratory birds.* Western Treatment Plant; Western Port; Edithvale-Seaford Wetlands.
- *Yarra River Protection Act recognition.* Recognises Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country with a Birrarung Council including Traditional Owner representation.
- *Mekong dam tensions.* Upstream dams reduce downstream flows, affecting fisheries and agriculture in multiple countries.
- *Why "best use of water" is rarely one answer.* Values compete; communities and ecosystems need different things.
- *Short summary of Murray-Darling values.* All five values present; managing trade-offs between irrigation and environmental flows is the central challenge.
