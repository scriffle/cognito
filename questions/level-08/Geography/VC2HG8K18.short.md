# VC2HG8K18 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Causes and patterns of urbanisation in urbanising countries, and its environmental, economic and social impacts, including a study of Indonesia or the United States of America, or China or India.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Urbanisation is the world's biggest spatial shift. Patterns vary by country. Impacts span environment, economy and society.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **causes and patterns of urbanisation** and its **impacts**, with a country study.
- You should be able to read urbanisation as global and country-specific.

## Core content

**Urbanisation** is the rising share of people living in cities. **Push factors** (low rural wages, drought, conflict) drive migration; **pull factors** (jobs, services, education) attract. Cities grow through migration, natural increase and reclassification. **Megacities** (10 million+) include Jakarta, Mumbai, Shanghai.

Environmental impacts: pollution, heat islands, biodiversity loss. Economic impacts: GDP growth, informal-sector expansion, inequality. Social impacts: housing pressure, cultural change, family-network strain.

**Worked example.** Indonesia. Causes: rural-urban wage gap; coastal trade hubs; education access. Patterns: Java highly urbanised; outer islands slower. Impacts: Jakarta land subsidence and flooding; GDP growth; new lifestyles. Response: capital move to Nusantara.

**Cultural context.** Many Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people live in cities; the share has grown over decades. Many Melbourne residents have lived urbanisation first-hand before migrating. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Urbanisation is the world's biggest spatial shift.
- Push and pull factors drive it.
- Patterns vary by country.
- Comparison reveals more.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Cities are bad." They are also engines of opportunity.
- "Rural and urban are separate." Networks connect them.
- "Migration is the only cause." Natural increase and reclassification also drive growth.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *"More in cities" not same as "fewer in rural".* Rural populations can grow in absolute numbers even as the urban share rises.
- *Push and pull example.* Indonesia: rural low wages push; Jakarta jobs and education pull.
- *Overlooked environmental impact.* Urban heat island; fringe biodiversity loss; groundwater impacts.
- *Australian sector affected by Asian urbanisation.* Agriculture exports; education; minerals.
- *Short summary of Indonesia urbanisation.* Rapid coastal-city growth, megacity stress in Jakarta, capital relocation to Nusantara, and trade and education ties with Australia.
