## Core content

**1. Five kinds of value.** **Environmental** (supports ecosystems); **economic** (irrigation, hydro, tourism); **cultural** (community gatherings, festivals); **spiritual** (sacred sites, ceremonies); **aesthetic** (scenic beauty, recreation). The same river can hold all five. *Quick check:* Why do all five matter, not just economic?

**2. Environmental value.** Healthy waterways support fish, birds, vegetation and floodplain ecology. Wetlands like the Western Treatment Plant (Werribee, Victoria) host migratory birds protected under international agreements. *Quick check:* What is one Victorian wetland that hosts migratory birds?

**3. Economic value.** Water supports irrigation (Goulburn Valley), hydro-electricity (Snowy Hydro 2.0), drinking water (Melbourne metro), tourism (Murray paddleboats), recreation (boating, fishing). *Quick check:* What is one Victorian industry that depends on water flow?

**4. Cultural value for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples.** Waterways are central to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander law, language and ceremony. Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country includes the Birrarung (Yarra). The Yorta Yorta Nation has a deep relationship with the Murray. The Yarra River Protection (Wilip-gin Birrarung murron) Act 2017 recognises Aboriginal cultural values. *Quick check:* What does the recent Yarra River Protection Act recognise?

**5. Worked example: water value of the Murray-Darling Basin.** Environmental: floodplain ecology, native fish, river red gums. Economic: irrigated agriculture (wine, dairy, fruit). Cultural: Yorta Yorta, Wiradjuri, Barkindji and other First Nations Peoples' law and continuing connection. Spiritual: significant sites along the rivers. Aesthetic: paddle steamers and tourism in Echuca. Trade-offs: water allocations between extractive and environmental uses are contested. *Follow the thinking:* identify the place → list each kind of value → name the trade-offs.

**6. Water in the Asia region.** The Mekong River supports food security, transport and culture across Vietnam, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia and beyond. The Ganges River is sacred in Hindu culture. Monsoonal flows shape life across South and Southeast Asia. *Quick check:* Why might dam construction upstream of the Mekong create regional tension?

**7. Implications for management.** Decisions about flows, allocations and protection must balance the five kinds of value, plus the views of different peoples. Honest management names trade-offs. *Quick check:* Why is "best use of water" rarely a single answer?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Melbourne Water, the Victorian Environmental Water Holder and Traditional Owner corporations collaborate on Victorian waterway management. The Yarra River Protection Act 2017 is a global benchmark in Indigenous-led waterway recognition.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Wadawurrung, Yorta Yorta, Gunaikurnai and other Victorian peoples lead joint management of significant waterways. AIATSIS and the Indigenous Knowledge Institute publish peer-reviewed work. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant communities in Victoria bring water values from across Asia: Vietnamese, Cambodian, Indian, Sri Lankan, Pakistani communities maintain festivals and traditions that link to water. Friday prayer washing and Hindu sacred-water practices appear in Melbourne suburbs.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Water value is just economic." Four other kinds matter equally.
- "Spiritual value is private." Spiritual value can shape law and management.
- "Asian water knowledge is foreign." Many Australians bring it with them.
- "All values can be perfectly balanced." Trade-offs are real; management chooses.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why all five matter.* Economic alone ignores ecological, cultural, spiritual and aesthetic dimensions that shape community wellbeing and legitimate use.
2. *Victorian wetland with migratory birds.* Western Treatment Plant; Western Port; Edithvale-Seaford Wetlands.
3. *Victorian industry depending on water.* Goulburn Valley irrigation; Yarra Valley wineries; Melbourne CBD hospitality.
4. *Yarra River Protection Act recognition.* Recognises Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country and creates a Birrarung Council with Traditional Owner representation.
5. *Mekong dam tensions.* Upstream dams reduce downstream flows, affecting fisheries and agriculture in multiple countries.
6. *Why "best use of water" is rarely one answer.* Values compete; communities and ecosystems need different things.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Local Melbourne creek.* Environment (habitat); economy (property values); culture (community gatherings); spiritual (peace, reflection); aesthetic (scenic views).
2. *Maribyrnong creek restoration consultation.* Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung and Bunurong Traditional Owners; local environmental groups; nearby residents from diverse backgrounds.
3. *Junior water-management officer.* Brief in plain language; offer interpreters; include Acknowledgement of Country; allow time for community input.
4. *Indigenous water rights debate.* For: recognition and management improvement. Against: complexity and existing user rights. Both sides agree on need for clear processes.
5. *Vietnamese-Australian Maribyrnong festivals.* Permits in advance; community partnerships; signage that acknowledges both Traditional Owners and the festival community.
