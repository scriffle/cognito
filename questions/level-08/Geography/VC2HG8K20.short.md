# VC2HG8K20 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The comparison of urban patterns in highly urbanised countries, including a comparison of Australia and another country.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Highly urbanised countries are not identical. City patterns vary. Comparison reveals options.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **compare urban patterns** in highly urbanised countries, including **Australia** and **another country**.
- You should be able to read city shape against the world.

## Core content

A **highly urbanised** country generally has 75%+ of its population in cities; Australia is around 86%. Most Australian cities are coastal, mid-sized by global standards, with extensive suburbs, moderate density and car dominance. Australia has no **primate city**: population spreads across capitals.

Comparison cases: Japan (Tokyo megalopolis, high density, transit dominant); Singapore (very high density, integrated planning); the Netherlands (water management and density); Canada (coastal spread). Different shapes, different lessons.

**Worked example.** Australia vs Japan. Australia: coastal cities, spread population, moderate density, car-dominant suburbs, recent migration diversity. Japan: Tokyo-Yokohama megalopolis plus Osaka-Kobe and Nagoya, very high density, transit dominant, long urban history, ageing population. Both highly urbanised, different shapes.

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Bunurong/Boon Wurrung and Wadawurrung Country underlies Melbourne. Multicultural migration shapes contemporary Melbourne. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Highly urbanised, but different shapes.
- Australia has no primate city.
- Comparisons reveal choices.
- Lessons need adaptation.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Highly urbanised cities look the same." History and policy create big differences.
- "Density is always good." Trade-offs apply.
- "Copy what works directly." Local context matters.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why is Australia highly urbanised?* Most people live near the coast; inland is arid.
- *Why no primate city?* Federal structure and multiple state capitals.
- *Why is one comparison not enough?* Each reveals only part of the picture.
- *Why does First Nations input matter in planning?* Improves environmental outcomes and legitimacy.
- *Short summary of Australia vs Japan.* Both highly urbanised; Japan offers density and transit lessons; Australia's coastal-spread shape reflects different geography and history.
