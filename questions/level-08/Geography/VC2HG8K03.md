## Core content

**1. Distribution.** **Distribution** is where water is found across Australia. Most rainfall and rivers are in northern and eastern Australia. Central and western Australia are mostly arid. Most Australians live where annual rainfall is moderate, near the coast. *Quick check:* Why is northern Australia not heavily irrigated despite high rainfall?

**2. Variability.** **Variability** is how much water amounts swing from year to year. Australia has the most variable river flows in the developed world. Droughts and floods are normal. The Bureau of Meteorology Australia tracks this through indices like ENSO (El Niño Southern Oscillation). *Quick check:* What does a "wet decade followed by a dry decade" tell us about Australian climate?

**3. Climate-change projections.** Climate models project: drier conditions for southern Australia (especially Victoria and south-east), more extreme rainfall events in northern Australia, longer droughts, more bushfire-favouring conditions, and reduced snowpack in alpine regions. *Quick check:* Why is "more extreme" more important than "more or less" rainfall on average?

**4. Worked example: Victoria vs Top End.** Victoria: temperate rainfall, drying trend in winter and spring, smaller reservoirs, intensive use; projected drier futures plus heat. Northern Territory's Top End: monsoonal heavy summer rain, sparse population, large water bodies; projected more intense storms but greater variability. *Follow the thinking:* describe distribution → identify variability → state climate projection → name management implication.

**5. Snowpack and rivers.** Alpine snowpack in Victoria's Bogong High Plains feeds rivers like the Kiewa and Mitta Mitta. Reduced snow shortens spring melt, affecting summer flows. Snowy Hydro relies on this water for hydroelectric generation. *Quick check:* Why does less snow affect summer river flows?

**6. Groundwater.** Many Australians depend on **groundwater** in aquifers like the Great Artesian Basin. Recharge is slow; extraction can outpace it. Climate change reduces recharge in many areas. *Quick check:* Why is groundwater not a "backup" for surface-water shortages indefinitely?

**7. Implications for places.** Cities (Melbourne, Adelaide, Perth) have invested in desalination and recycling. Farming regions are diversifying crops and water-saving infrastructure. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities lead some catchment-management projects. *Quick check:* Why are some communities investing in multiple water sources?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Melbourne's water supply includes the Thomson, Upper Yarra, Cardinia, Maroondah and Sugarloaf reservoirs, plus the Victorian Desalination Plant at Wonthaggi. The Bureau of Meteorology Australia and DEECA publish projections.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Traditional Owner corporations lead joint catchment-management programs across Victoria. Cool-burning practices reduce wildfire-driven water-quality risks. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant communities use water-saving practices from many climates; multilingual water-efficiency campaigns support diverse Melbourne households.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Drought-flood cycles are unusual." Variability is normal in Australia.
- "More rainfall is always good." Distribution and timing matter.
- "Desalination solves everything." It is one tool; energy and emissions also matter.
- "Climate projections are precise." They are ranges; uncertainty is named.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why northern Australia is not heavily irrigated.* High rainfall is seasonal; soil and infrastructure limit large-scale irrigation; cultural and environmental considerations matter.
2. *Wet decade then dry.* Variability is large; climate operates over decades, not seasons.
3. *Why "more extreme" matters.* Extremes (heavy floods, long droughts) cause damage and stress that averages hide.
4. *Less snow → summer flows.* Snowpack acts like a frozen reservoir; less snow means less melt to feed rivers in summer.
5. *Why groundwater is not infinite.* Recharge is slow; extraction can exceed it; once aquifers fall, recovery takes decades.
6. *Multiple sources for resilience.* Reduces risk; smooths supply when one source fails.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Melbourne vs Darwin.* Melbourne: lower, more even rainfall. Darwin: high seasonal monsoon rain. Both: variability year-to-year. Both: climate-change pressures.
2. *Hume water strategy.* Population growth; climate change; demand-management; multiple supply sources.
3. *Melbourne Water 30-year scenario.* Population projection; climate projection; demand assumptions; investment options.
4. *Desalination debate.* For: reliable, climate-independent. Against: energy cost; emissions; capital cost. Both sides can be reconciled with renewable-powered desal as backup.
5. *Wurundjeri engagement.* Step 1: contact the Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation. Step 2: invite leadership of consultation. Step 3: ensure outputs respect Country and Wurundjeri priorities.
