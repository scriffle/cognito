## Core content

**1. Three kinds of value.** **Spiritual value** is the sacred or religious meaning of a place. **Aesthetic value** is the visual or sensory beauty that moves people. **Cultural value** is what a place holds for a community's identity, history and practice. The same landform can hold all three. *Quick check:* Why are these values not just "extras"?

**2. Spiritual value for First Nations.** Many landforms are sacred sites with specific cultural protocols. Uluru in Pitjantjatjara and Yankunytjatjara Country; Wilpena Pound (Ikara) in Adnyamathanha Country; Mount Buninyong on Wadawurrung Country. Access and behaviour rules apply. *Quick check:* Why was climbing Uluru closed to visitors?

**3. Aesthetic value.** Landscapes such as the Twelve Apostles, the Grampians (Gariwerd) and the Australian Alps draw visitors for their beauty. Aesthetic value supports tourism but also wellbeing through experiences of awe. *Quick check:* How does aesthetic value support mental health?

**4. Cultural value.** Landforms hold community memory: sites of historical events; pilgrimage routes; agricultural heritage; recreational traditions. They appear in art, photography and writing. *Quick check:* What is one Australian landform with strong cultural value beyond First Nations connections?

**5. Worked example: Wilsons Promontory.** Spiritual: Bunurong/Boon Wurrung and Gunaikurnai connections to Country; specific knowledge community-held. Aesthetic: rugged coast, granite peaks, sweeping beaches; major tourist appeal. Cultural: walking and bushwalking traditions; family camping memories. *Follow the thinking:* identify landform → name each value → consider who holds each.

**6. Respectful access.** Some landforms have restricted access for cultural reasons; some welcome respectful visiting with guidance. Following Traditional Owner direction is part of respect. *Quick check:* What is one practical step a visitor can take to be respectful?

**7. Tension and change.** Tourism brings income but can damage sites; climate change is altering landscapes; community values can clash with development. Honest management balances these. *Quick check:* Why do values need to be named explicitly in management plans?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Parks Victoria manages many significant landscapes (Wilsons Promontory, Grampians/Gariwerd, Buchan Caves, Twelve Apostles). Traditional Owner corporations co-manage many.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Joint management is increasing; the Gunaikurnai and Victorian Government Joint Management Plan covers parts of East Gippsland. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant communities form aesthetic and cultural attachments to Australian landscapes; bushwalking, surfing and skiing communities span backgrounds and span generations.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Spiritual = religious." Spiritual value can be broader.
- "Aesthetic is just pretty." It includes awe and connection.
- "Cultural value is universal." Different groups hold different cultural meanings.
- "Tourism is always harmful." Well-designed tourism can support protection.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why values are not "extras".* They shape how people relate to and protect places; they affect law and management.
2. *Why climbing Uluru was closed.* Traditional Owners (Anangu) requested closure on cultural grounds; the National Park Board agreed in 2017; closure took effect in 2019.
3. *How aesthetic value supports mental health.* Awe reduces stress; visual quality boosts mood and attention.
4. *Cultural value beyond First Nations.* Sydney Harbour Bridge; bushwalking traditions in the Blue Mountains; agricultural heritage in Goulburn Valley.
5. *Practical respectful step.* Stay on marked paths; ask permission before photography; follow ranger or Traditional Owner direction.
6. *Why management names values explicitly.* It avoids losing what cannot be replaced; supports honest trade-off decisions.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Local landform.* A nearby river, hill, beach or park with personal or family meaning.
2. *Surf Coast Shire access principles.* Marked paths; Acknowledgement of Country; signage in multiple languages.
3. *Junior ranger messages.* Welcome with Acknowledgement of Country; safety on the trail; pack-out-what-you-pack-in.
4. *Photography at sacred sites debate.* For: education and remembrance. Against: cultural harm. Both sides agree on Traditional Owner-led decisions.
5. *Wadawurrung-led tour.* Listen; do not stray from the trail; do not take rocks or plants; follow visual-recording guidance.
