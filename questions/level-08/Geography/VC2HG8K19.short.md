# VC2HG8K19 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Patterns of international and internal migration in Australia and China and/or other countries, and their impacts on urban population growth.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Migration changes who lives where. International and internal both reshape cities. Australia: large international; China: large internal.

## What you are learning
- You are learning **patterns of international and internal migration** and their **impacts on urban population growth**.
- You should be able to see migration as a city-shaper.

## Core content

**International migration** crosses borders; **internal migration** moves within a country. **Net migration** = arrivals minus departures. Australia's international migration has included post-WWII European waves, Vietnamese refugees, skilled migration from India, China and the Philippines, and humanitarian arrivals from East Africa and West Asia. Internal: rural-to-urban; interstate (e.g., to Queensland); within-state (city-to-regional during COVID-19).

China's hukou-regulated internal migration has moved hundreds of millions of people from rural areas to coastal cities since the 1980s, driving the growth of Shenzhen, Shanghai and Beijing.

**Worked example.** Internal migration in China. Origin: rural inland provinces. Destination: eastern coastal megacities. Policy: hukou regulates settlement. Impact: workforce growth, housing pressure, services pressure, rural depopulation in some areas.

**Cultural context.** Many Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people maintain mobility between Country and cities. Migrant communities shape Melbourne suburbs: Vietnamese in Footscray; Indian in Tarneit; Chinese in Box Hill; Sudanese in Sunshine. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- International and internal reshape cities.
- Net migration plus natural increase = growth.
- Migration brings opportunity and pressure.
- Comparison reveals patterns.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Migration is one-way." Many move back or back-and-forth.
- "Migration causes all city growth." Natural increase also matters.
- "Aboriginal mobility is rural-only." Many are deeply connected to cities.
