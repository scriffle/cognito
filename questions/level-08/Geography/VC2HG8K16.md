## Core content

**1. Types of geomorphological hazards.** **Earthquakes** (sudden movement of plates or faults); **landslides** (mass movement on slopes); **tsunamis** (water waves generated by earthquakes, landslides or eruptions); **volcanic eruptions**; soil and coastal erosion that becomes hazardous. *Quick check:* What is one Australian example of each?

**2. Causes.** Plate tectonics drives earthquakes and volcanoes; rainfall and slope angle drive landslides; underwater earthquakes cause many tsunamis. Human factors (deforestation, building on slopes) increase vulnerability. *Quick check:* Why are landslides more common after fires?

**3. Impacts.** Direct: loss of life, injury, building damage, infrastructure loss. Indirect: displacement, economic loss, mental-health impact, long-term environmental change. Disaster scale depends on exposure and vulnerability. *Quick check:* What is one indirect impact often underestimated?

**4. Worked example: the 2021 Mansfield earthquake.** A magnitude 5.9 earthquake near Mansfield in north-east Victoria was felt across the state. Cause: movement on a fault deep below the Earth's surface. Impacts: building damage in Melbourne CBD (Chapel Street); some injuries; widespread disruption; aftershocks for months. Responses: emergency services activated; building inspections; updated building-code discussions; Geoscience Australia continued monitoring. *Follow the thinking:* cause → location → impacts → response → lessons.

**5. Phases of response.** **Mitigation** before (building codes, planning, education). **Preparedness** (drills, kits, warnings). **Response** (during) (rescue, shelter, communication). **Recovery** (after) (repairs, mental health, planning updates). Geoscience Australia and Emergency Management Victoria coordinate parts. *Quick check:* Why does mitigation often save more lives than rapid response?

**6. Australia's risk profile.** Australia has moderate earthquake risk (mostly intra-plate); landslide risk in mountainous and steep coastal areas; tsunami risk on parts of the coast. Compared with the Pacific Ring of Fire, exposure is lower but real. *Quick check:* Why is Australia not as quake-prone as Japan?

**7. Climate change and geomorphological hazards.** Climate change indirectly increases some hazards: heavier rain triggers more landslides; sea-level rise increases coastal erosion. Wildfires followed by storms produce mudflows. *Quick check:* What is one Australian example of fire-rain compounding?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Geoscience Australia monitors seismic activity; Emergency Management Victoria coordinates response; CFA, VICSES and FRV handle local emergencies. The 2021 Mansfield quake and ongoing landslide risk in eastern Victoria are key cases.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Oral histories in Australia record ancient flooding and volcanic events. Aboriginal-led land management (e.g., cultural burning) reduces compounding hazard risks. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual emergency communications improved after major events. Faith-based and community organisations play key recovery roles.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Australia has no earthquakes." It does; usually moderate but sometimes significant.
- "Disaster scale depends on the hazard alone." Exposure and vulnerability matter as much.
- "After-event recovery is the most important phase." Mitigation usually saves more lives.
- "Aboriginal stories of earth movements are myth." Many are historical observations.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Australian examples.* Earthquake (Mansfield 2021); landslide (post-fire mudflows in mountainous Victoria); tsunami (low-level Indian Ocean tsunami impact on WA coast 2004); volcanic eruption (no recent active eruptions but recent volcanism in Western Victoria geologically).
2. *Why landslides after fires.* Vegetation loss reduces soil stability; heavy rain washes weakened slopes.
3. *Often-underestimated indirect impact.* Mental-health strain and community fragmentation.
4. *Why mitigation saves more lives.* Prevents harm; rapid response only manages what happens.
5. *Why Australia is less quake-prone than Japan.* Australia sits within a plate; Japan sits on multiple plate boundaries.
6. *Fire-rain compounding example.* Black Summer 2019–20 followed by heavy rain caused mudflows and sediment impacts.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Felt a small earthquake.* Earthquake kit; family plan; know safest spots in your home.
2. *Mansfield earthquake-plan updates.* Building inspections; community drills; updated insurance advice.
3. *Junior emergency-management officer.* Multilingual communications; school partnerships; clear pre-event education.
4. *Hillside-building debate.* For: safety. Against: existing rights and community attachment. Both sides can be reconciled with planning rules and compensation.
5. *Multilingual earthquake information.* Translate alerts; partner with community organisations; use community-language radio.
