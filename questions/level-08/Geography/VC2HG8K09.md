# VC2HG8K09 — Geography

## Snapshot

- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Geography (Place and liveability).

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **influence of social connectedness and community identity** on **perceptions of the liveability of places**, including the **cultural connectedness of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples to Country and Place**.
- You are learning to read liveability through belonging as well as services.

By the end of this you should be able to:

- Discuss how identity shapes whether a place feels liveable.

## Core content

**1. Social connectedness.** **Social connectedness** is how strongly people feel linked to others around them: family, friends, neighbours, sports clubs, faith communities, schools, workplaces. It is one of the strongest predictors of perceived liveability. *Quick check:* Why might a small town feel more liveable than a big city for some people?

**2. Community identity.** **Community identity** is how a place sees itself: a beachside town, a multicultural suburb, a sporting club, a Country town with a Show. Identity grows from history, shared experiences and visible markers (places of worship, sports clubs, public art, festivals). *Quick check:* What is one visible marker of community identity in your area?

**3. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander connections to Country.** **Country** in First Nations contexts includes land, water, sky, plants, animals, stories, ancestors and responsibilities. Connection to Country is ongoing, central to identity and shapes how a place feels liveable for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people. The Yarra River Protection Act 2017 formally recognises Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung connection to Birrarung. *Quick check:* Why is "Country" capitalised in this context?

**4. Worked example: Footscray as a multicultural place.** Footscray's identity is shaped by Vietnamese, Sudanese, Italian and other communities; the Footscray Market; football traditions; the Maribyrnong River. Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country runs through it. Long-term residents and new arrivals feel different aspects of the place. Liveability perceptions depend on belonging, networks and recognition. *Follow the thinking:* observe markers → identify communities → describe identity → consider belonging.

**5. Liveability through belonging.** A well-serviced suburb can feel unliveable if you feel unwelcome; a less-serviced area can feel deeply liveable when you belong. Both perceptions are valid. *Quick check:* Why is "I love it here" worth more than "everything is convenient"?

**6. Tensions and change.** As places change (gentrification, migration, climate), community identity can come under pressure. Honest planning involves the people most affected. *Quick check:* What is one Victorian example of community change?

**7. Recognition matters.** Public recognition (place-naming, signage, Acknowledgement of Country, multilingual signage) strengthens belonging. The First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria's treaty work is one expression. *Quick check:* What is one daily example of recognition in Victoria?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Victorian Multicultural Commission, Reconciliation Victoria and local councils document community identity. Dual-named places (e.g., Birrarung/Yarra) are visible.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Bunurong/Boon Wurrung, Wadawurrung, Gunaikurnai and other Victorian peoples lead joint-management of Country and shape state-level reform through treaty negotiations. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Festivals (Lunar New Year, Diwali, Antipodes), faith centres, language schools and community organisations across Melbourne support community identity.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Community is just where you live." It includes faith, family, sport, language.
- "Belonging is just feeling." It influences mental health, behaviour and decisions.
- "Country is just land." It includes water, sky, stories and ancestors.
- "Recognition is symbolic." It also has practical and legal effects.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Small town vs big city liveability.* Closer ties, faster help, sense of belonging; trade-off with services and opportunities.
2. *Visible marker of community identity.* Festivals; sports clubs; language schools; murals; community centres.
3. *Why "Country" is capitalised.* It marks a specific First Nations concept distinct from general land.
4. *Why "I love it here" matters.* Belonging predicts staying and contributing more than convenience does.
5. *Victorian community change example.* Inner-Melbourne suburbs shifting through gentrification; growth-suburb identity forming through migration.
6. *Daily recognition example.* Welcome to Country at events; dual-named places; multilingual signage; faith leaders in public roles.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Three social ties.* Family; sport or hobby groups; school or workplace networks.
2. *Wyndham identity strategy.* Multicultural festivals; community storytelling project; public art reflecting local Traditional Owners.
3. *Junior community-development worker.* Surveys; in-person community meetings; mapping organisations and informal networks.
4. *Multilingual signs debate.* For: inclusion and belonging. Against: cost and uniformity. Both sides can be addressed with targeted bilingual signage in major languages.
5. *Wurundjeri-led recognition.* Support corporation as lead; ensure long-term funding; integrate in council communications and events.
