# VC2HG8K08 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** The influence of environmental quality on people's perceptions of the liveability of places.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Environment shapes daily life. Air, water, noise, heat, green. Uneven distribution shapes liveability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning the **influence of environmental quality on perceptions of liveability**.
- You should be able to see environment as part of what makes a place liveable.

## Core content

Environmental quality covers air (particulate matter, traffic, smoke), water (catchments, treatment), noise (traffic, aircraft, industry), urban heat islands, green space and biodiversity. Tree canopies and reflective surfaces reduce urban heat. Green space supports mental health, biodiversity and cooling.

Initiatives include Plan Melbourne's 20-minute neighbourhood, Greening the West canopy programs, EPA Victoria monitoring, cool-burning partnerships and water-sensitive urban design.

**Worked example.** Comparing two Melbourne suburbs. Eastern (Boroondara): high canopy, lower density, cooler microclimate, more parks, higher house prices. Western/northern (Wyndham, Hume): lower canopy cover, hotter days, expanding population. Liveability perception differs partly due to these factors.

**Cultural context.** Traditional Owner-led land management contributes to environmental quality through cool burning, waterway care and native species reintroduction. Multilingual communities help maintain community gardens and clean-up programs across Melbourne. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Environmental quality is multi-factor.
- Air, water, noise, heat, green.
- Environment and health are linked.
- Many initiatives can help.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Environment is just nature." It includes urban environments.
- "Air quality is the same across a city." It varies block by block.
- "Trees are decoration." Canopy provides cooling and health benefits.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why invisible-but-important air quality.* You cannot see PM2.5; health impacts accumulate.
- *Why some Melbourne suburbs are hotter.* Less canopy, more dark surfaces, denser construction.
- *Green-space mental-health benefit.* Lower stress, better mood, recovery from attention fatigue.
- *Why does chronic noise affect wellbeing?* Sleep disruption, stress, cardiovascular impact.
- *Short summary of Boroondara vs Wyndham/Hume.* Canopy and density differences drive temperature and amenity gaps; planning programs aim to close them in growth areas.
