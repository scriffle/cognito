## Core content

**1. What cities need to do.** Australian cities face: housing demand; transport pressure; heat and flooding from climate change; energy transition; service growth in outer suburbs; ageing population. No single strategy fixes everything. *Quick check:* Why is "build more housing" not a complete answer?

**2. Strategies for liveability.** Urban greening (canopy, parks); 20-minute neighbourhoods; transport investment (rail, buses, cycling); social-housing programs; community-centre and library investment; cultural-festival programs. *Quick check:* What is one strategy that supports both liveability and health?

**3. Strategies for sustainability.** Renewable-energy in public buildings; water-sensitive urban design; recycling and waste reduction; integrated transport; smart growth boundaries; rooftop solar; vehicle electrification. *Quick check:* What is one strategy that reduces emissions and saves money?

**4. Worked example: Melbourne's response to urban heat.** Plan Melbourne sets long-term direction. Plant tree-canopy targets in councils across the west and north; cool-roofs initiatives; integrated water management to support trees; heat-health alerts; daytime cooling-centre access; building-code reviews. Combined, they reduce heat-related illness during summer. *Follow the thinking:* identify problem → list strategies → combine → measure outcomes.

**5. Climate adaptation.** **Adaptation** is adjusting to climate-change effects: heat-tolerant tree species; stormwater capacity for more intense rain; coastal-erosion responses; planning rules in fire-prone and flood-prone areas. *Quick check:* Why does adaptation need to happen alongside emissions reduction?

**6. Equity in strategies.** Honest planning ensures benefits reach all suburbs, including outer growth areas. Vulnerable groups (elderly, low-income, those without air-conditioning) need special attention during heat events. *Quick check:* Why do equity-focused strategies often outperform broad ones?

**7. Indigenous-led and community-led responses.** Cool burning around urban fringes; native revegetation on Country; community gardens; multilingual environmental campaigns. *Quick check:* What is one community-led example you have seen?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Plan Melbourne, the Suburban Rail Loop, Victoria's net-zero target, Greening the West and council-level canopy plans are key responses.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Wadawurrung, Bunurong and other Traditional Owners lead joint-management and caring-for-Country programs in urban edges. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual climate-adaptation campaigns and community-led greening programs work across Melbourne's diverse suburbs.

## Watch out for these traps
- "More housing solves everything." It is part of the answer.
- "Trees are decoration." Canopy is essential infrastructure.
- "Adaptation alone is enough." Emissions reduction is also needed.
- "Only big projects matter." Small community projects compound.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why "build more housing" is incomplete.* Without transport, services, schools and amenity, housing alone is not enough.
2. *Liveability and health strategy.* 20-minute neighbourhoods (walkable amenity); urban greening (heat and exercise).
3. *Strategy reducing emissions and saving money.* Rooftop solar; efficient appliances; public-transport investment over time.
4. *Why adaptation alongside emissions reduction.* Some climate change is locked in; emissions reduction limits how much.
5. *Why equity-focused strategies win.* They reduce risk across more of the population and build community trust.
6. *Community-led example.* Community gardens; repair cafés; cultural events; multicultural climate-action groups.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *Three things in your suburb.* Plant a tree; take part in a community-clean-up; lobby council for better lighting or transport.
2. *Wyndham climate-adaptation priorities.* Tree planting; cooling-centre access; flood-management upgrades.
3. *Junior urban planner.* Walking-distance access to services; cycling infrastructure; community-amenity measures.
4. *Wealthy suburbs subsidising debate.* For: equity and shared climate risk. Against: rates fairness. Both sides can be reconciled with state-level grants.
5. *Sudanese-Australian heat-response initiative.* Provide a community-grant; co-design with elders; multilingual safety messaging.
