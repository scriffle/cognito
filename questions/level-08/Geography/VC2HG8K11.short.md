- You should be able to use the local toolkit.

## Core content

**Local scale** is neighbourhood, suburb or town. Common response types: infrastructure (parks, paths, lighting); services (community health, libraries); programs (gardens, neighbourhood watch); planning rules; social initiatives (festivals, multilingual outreach). **Community-led** responses are designed and delivered by residents (community gardens, repair cafés, men's sheds, Welcoming Cities initiatives). **Co-design** with residents and partners improves take-up and trust.

Aboriginal-led responses include place-naming, cultural-burning and urban greening with native species. Local responses complement but cannot replace state/federal action on structural issues.

**Worked example.** A Melbourne neighbourhood feels hot and disconnected. Council responses: plant trees, install drinking fountains, repaint pedestrian crossings. Community-led: community garden on a vacant lot, multilingual welcome signage, monthly street market. Combined effect: cooler, more connected, more visible neighbourhood.

**Cultural context.** Wurundjeri, Wadawurrung and other Traditional Owners lead place-based initiatives including dual naming, cultural-burning partnerships and urban greening. Multicultural community organisations (ECCV, Welcoming Cities) deliver local-scale projects. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Local responses are quick and visible.
- Many response types fit different issues.
- Co-design improves outcomes.
- Local cannot solve everything.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Local responses are decorative." They affect daily life.
- "Consultation = co-design." They are different.
- "Local solves all." Some issues need higher-scale action.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Why local-scale responses.* Visible and quick changes build trust and momentum.
- *Longest response type.* Major infrastructure; years.
- *Why community-led responses stick.* Locally owned; embedded in social networks.
- *Why "consulting at the end" is worse.* Decisions already made; residents feel ignored.
- *Short summary of the hot-street response.* Council greening plus community garden, multilingual signage and First Nations co-design produce layered improvement.
