# VC2HG8S07 — Geography (Short)

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** Create and present explanations and responses, using geographical knowledge, concepts and methods, and referring to sources.
- **Year / Area:** Year 8, Geography.
- **Big ideas:** Concepts plus methods plus sources. Audience first, format second. Honest communication earns trust.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **create and present explanations and responses** using **geographic concepts**, **methods** and **sources**.
- You should be able to communicate as a geographer.

## Core content

Geographic communication includes explanations, descriptions, evaluations and recommendations. Use concepts (space, place, interconnection, change, environment, sustainability, scale) to frame analysis. Name methods (fieldwork, GIS, surveys, statistics) so readers can check. Cite sources by author, year and access.

Choose format and tone for the audience: short video for peers, written submission for council, summary for family. Visuals (charts, infographics, maps) need titles, legends, sources and dates.

**Worked example.** Responding to "Should our suburb plant more trees?". Audience 1: school assembly — 90-second video with canopy data and three reasons. Audience 2: council — 200-word submission with one chart and two cited sources. Audience 3: family — one-page summary with photos and a call to action. Same explanation, three formats.

**Cultural context.** Cite First Nations authors directly (NITV, IndigenousX, AIATSIS). Multilingual presentation supports reach across Melbourne. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.

## Memorable takeaways
- Concepts plus methods plus sources.
- Audience first, format second.
- Cite by author, year, source.
- Honest communication earns trust.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Big words sound smart." Plain language sounds clear.
- "Long quotes prove the point." Use sparingly; reasoning is yours.
- "If it looks polished, it is right." Polish is not provenance.

## Model responses

Use these to check your thinking after you have had a go.

- *Description vs explanation.* Description says what; explanation says why.
- *Why does method naming build trust?* It lets readers check how you produced the result.
- *Common map error.* Missing legend or units; misleading colours.
- *Why is "Google says" not a citation?* It hides the source.
- *Short summary of the tree-planting response.* Same idea adapted to three audiences (peers, council, family) using concepts, methods and sources.
