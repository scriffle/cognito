# VC2HG8S06 — Geography

## Snapshot
- **Descriptor:** VC2HG8S06. Identify a strategy for action in relation to environmental, economic, social or other factors, explain potential impacts and develop appropriate actions.
- **Year level / Learning area:** Year 8, Geography (Concluding and decision-making).
- **Big ideas:**
  - Action follows conclusion.
  - Strategies are specific and practical.
  - Impacts span environment, economy and society.
- **Curriculum hooks:** Sustainability; Personal and Social Capability; Ethical Capability.

## What you are learning
- You are learning to **identify a strategy for action** in relation to **environmental, economic, social or other factors**, **explain potential impacts** and **develop appropriate actions**.
- You are learning to turn understanding into action.

By the end of this you should be able to:
- Define **strategy**, **action**, **impact** and **stakeholder** in your own words.
- Develop a specific strategy with three actions.
- Identify potential environmental, economic and social impacts.
- Justify why the strategy fits the issue.

## Core content

**1. Strategy and action.** A **strategy** is the overall plan; **actions** are the specific steps. Vague strategies fail; specific strategies get tested. *Quick check:* Why is "do better" a poor strategy?

**2. Three-factor lens.** Strong strategies consider **environmental** (impact on nature, climate), **economic** (cost, jobs, value), and **social** (community, equity) factors. Other factors (cultural, technological, political) may also apply. *Quick check:* Why think about all three together?

**3. Three-part action plan.** What (specific action), when (timeline), who (responsible). Without these, actions become wishes. *Quick check:* What is missing from "we should plant more trees"?

**4. Worked example: a strategy for a hot inner-Melbourne suburb.** Issue: rising summer heat, low canopy. Strategy: increase canopy and cool spaces over five years. Actions: (1) Council plant 2,000 native street trees by 2027; (2) install three cooling stations in libraries by 2026; (3) co-design a community-greening grant with residents in 2025. Impacts: environmental (cooler microclimate, biodiversity); economic (cost ~$5m capital; long-term savings); social (improved wellbeing, especially for vulnerable groups). *Follow the thinking:* identify issue → write strategy → list actions → predict impacts → check fit.

**5. Stakeholders.** Identify who benefits, who pays, who must agree. Engagement before implementation reduces resistance and improves design. *Quick check:* Why does early stakeholder engagement save time later?

**6. Honest limits.** Strategies cannot do everything. Name the limits and what they will not solve. Plan for review and adjustment. *Quick check:* Why is "set and forget" risky for strategies?

**7. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander-led actions.** Cool burning, native revegetation, dual naming, cultural mapping. These actions strengthen environmental, economic and social outcomes when led by Traditional Owners. *Quick check:* Why do First Nations-led actions often deliver more durable outcomes?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Plan Melbourne and council climate strategies use this approach. Departmental program evaluations (DEECA, DHHS) reflect three-factor thinking.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** First Peoples' Assembly of Victoria and Traditional Owner corporations lead joint-management actions. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Multilingual engagement and community-language partnerships strengthen action design in Melbourne's diverse communities.

## Memorable takeaways
- Strategy guides; actions deliver.
- Think environment, economy, society together.
- What, when, who.
- Engage stakeholders early.
- Plan for review.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Strategies fix everything." They influence outcomes; they do not guarantee them.
- "Cost is the only consideration." Social and environmental impacts matter.
- "If consultation is done, the strategy will succeed." Implementation also matters.
- "Set and forget." Strategies need review and adjustment.

## Model responses

Use these to check your own thinking after you have had a go.

**Quick checks (Core content)**

1. *Why "do better" is a poor strategy.* No specifics; no measure; no action.
2. *Why think all three factors together.* They interact; ignoring one creates blind spots.
3. *Missing from "plant more trees".* When (timeline), who (responsible) and how many (specific).
4. *Why early stakeholder engagement.* Builds support; surfaces concerns; reduces delays.
5. *Why "set and forget" is risky.* Conditions change; strategies must adapt.
6. *Why First Nations-led actions deliver durable outcomes.* Long-term observation, integrated practice, cultural authority.

**Try these scenarios**

1. *School sustainability plan.* (1) Audit electricity and water use this term; (2) launch a reusable-water-bottle campaign next term; (3) plant a sensory garden by end of year.
2. *Wyndham heat-response.* Plant 5,000 trees; open three cooling centres; multilingual heat-safety campaign. Impacts: cooler microclimate; capital cost; safer summers especially for vulnerable.
3. *Junior planner community-greening proposal.* Co-design workshops; grant rounds; outcome metrics.
4. *Strategy affecting a community.* Issues: consent and benefits flowing to community. Engage early; share data; allow review.
5. *Wurundjeri-led parkland strategy.* Step 1: contact the Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation. Step 2: support their lead. Step 3: amplify with credit and resources.

**Stretch task — example shape of a strong answer.** *Issue: outer-suburb heat in Melbourne.* Strategy: integrated cooling over five years. Actions: tree planting; cooling-centre access; community-led greening grants. Impacts: environmental (cooler microclimate, biodiversity); economic (cost ~$5m; long-term savings); social (improved wellbeing). Stakeholders: residents, schools, councils, Traditional Owners. Limits: depends on watering and care. Review: annual. Sources cited.
