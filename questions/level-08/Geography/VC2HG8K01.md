## Core content

**1. Water as a renewable resource.** A **renewable resource** is one that can be naturally replenished within a human timeframe. Water is renewable because the **water cycle** constantly recycles it. But locally, water can still be scarce when use outpaces supply. *Quick check:* Why is "renewable" not the same as "unlimited"?

**2. Forms of water in the cycle.** Liquid (rain, rivers, lakes, oceans, groundwater); solid (snow, ice, glaciers, hail); gas (water vapour, clouds). Each form has different properties and uses. *Quick check:* Which form sits in the atmosphere most of the time?

**3. The water cycle.** Evaporation lifts water from oceans, lakes and plants into the air. **Transpiration** moves water from plants. Clouds form by **condensation**. **Precipitation** brings water back as rain, snow or hail. **Runoff** moves it across land into rivers; some seeps into **groundwater**. The cycle is driven by the sun. *Quick check:* What is one step in the cycle a hot day speeds up?

**4. Catchments and flows.** A **catchment** is an area where rainfall drains to a single river system. The Murray-Darling Basin is Australia's largest. Flows connect uplands, plains, towns and coasts. *Quick check:* What is one Victorian catchment that supplies Melbourne?

**5. Worked example: water flow in the Yarra River.** The Yarra rises on Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country in the highlands east of Melbourne. Water flows from forests through the Yarra Valley wineries, past suburbs, to Port Phillip Bay. Each step changes water quality, temperature and use. Upstream forests filter; farms add nutrients; cities discharge stormwater. *Follow the thinking:* identify source → trace flow → notice changes → name uses.

**6. Flow connects and changes places.** Water moves nutrients, sediments and people. Floods carry topsoil; droughts shrink rivers; reservoirs store seasonal supply. Flow changes places visibly (floodplains) and invisibly (groundwater levels). *Quick check:* What is one invisible effect of a long drought?

**7. Human change to flows.** Dams, weirs, irrigation channels, stormwater drains and pipelines reshape natural flows. Some benefits (drinking water); some costs (habitat loss, salinity). *Quick check:* What is one trade-off in building a dam?

## Victorian and cultural context

- **Victorian anchor.** Melbourne Water manages catchments in the Yarra, Tarago, Thomson, Upper Yarra and Cardinia. The Western Treatment Plant at Werribee treats Melbourne's wastewater.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.** Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Bunurong/Boon Wurrung, Wadawurrung, Yorta Yorta and other Victorian peoples have managed waterways for tens of thousands of years. Eel-harvest aquaculture at Budj Bim (Gunditjmara Country) is a UNESCO World Heritage example. Specific cultural knowledge should only be read through published, community-approved material.
- **Multicultural Australian perspective.** Migrant communities arriving in Victoria bring water knowledge from many climates (Mediterranean, monsoon, semi-arid). Vietnamese-Australian, Italian-Australian and Lebanese-Australian gardens use diverse water practices.

## Watch out for these traps
- "Renewable means infinite." It means recyclable in human timeframes.
- "All water is fresh water." Most is salt; only a small fraction is freshwater accessible.
- "Floods are just disasters." They also build floodplains and bring nutrients.
- "Aboriginal water knowledge is folklore." It is observation-based, complex and contemporary.
